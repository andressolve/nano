# Settings and Objects

**World palette (inherited from Volume I, Mineral Ink):** ink line with
mineral-pigment washes, the blue-green landscape tradition lit like a film.
Malachite green and azurite blue for the living world, cinnabar red for the
monkey's things, gold leaf for the divine. This volume adds **the road**:
ochre, dust, and autumn rust, which takes over from the green as the book goes
west. Each location is a variation on that, except the three that must look
like a different world: the sea floor (azurite and crystal, no green, as
Volume I), the back house at night (indigo black and one lantern, no daylight
colour), and the Flowing Sand River (sand-gold and pale gray, no green and no
blue at all; water that looks like a desert).

Each location names one restrained accent that does dramatic work.

---

## Locations

### 1 · The Mountain of Five Elements · pp 1–3, 5–8
Volume I's last location, now in spring: five bare ridges like the fingers of
a hand pressed into the earth, moss and grass grown over their lower slopes, a
cleft at the base with the monkey's face in it, a gold paper seal on the
summit. Across a strip of sea, far off, the green peak of Flower-Fruit
Mountain. On p 7 the ridges split. From p 8 it is behind them, cracked.
**Accent:** the gold seal, the only writing in the landscape, until the monk's
hand is on it.

### 2 · The border road · pp 4, 10–13, 16–21, 23
A dirt road through a long dry valley going west, scrub pines, boulders,
dust; low hills on either side; the light changes from spring to summer as
they walk. Ochre and malachite, wide skies. The place says: there is nobody
out here, and it is very far.
**Accent:** a lone white milestone by the road on p 4, carved with the word
for west, seen again on p 17 when Wukong lands beside it.

### 3 · The floor of the Eastern Sea — Ao Guang's crystal palace · pp 14–15
Volume I's palace, unchanged: crystal and coral, white shell columns, a sand
floor lit from above, fish drifting through the hall. Azurite and crystal, no
green at all. Where the pillar stood in Volume I's treasury there is now an
empty socket. In the audience hall, on a screen behind the throne, the
painting.
**Accent:** the painting on the screen: an old man on a stone bridge, a shoe
falling, a boy reaching. Ink only, no colour, the only flat thing in the sea.

### 4 · The South Sea — Guanyin's cloud · p 22
A sea of pale jade under morning mist, a single small rocky island with a
bamboo grove, and above it a white cloud on which she stands. White, jade,
silver. The place says: nobody comes here angry, and he has.
**Accent:** the bamboo, perfectly still while he shouts.

### 5 · Eagle Grief Stream · pp 24–28
A deep, fast, steep-banked mountain stream in a gorge of gray cliffs, green
water so clear the bottom shows, and then not. Cold blue-green and white
foam. The place says: something lives here and is hungry.
**Accent:** the empty bridle floating on the water after p 24.

### 6 · Gao Village · pp 30–31, 36–37
A farming village in a valley of terraced rice and millet at dusk: earthen
walls, gray tile roofs, every door shut, and one big farmhouse with a gate
and a courtyard. Old Gao's front room: a table, two lamps, a feast of noodles
that the pig never got. Warm dusk gold and shut gray. The place says: these
people were prosperous and are now afraid.
**Accent:** the bar across every door.

### 7 · The back house · pp 32–34
A small room at the back of the Gao courtyard: a bed with curtains, a chest,
a window, a door with a lock on the outside. Night: indigo black, the pig's
lantern the only light. On p 34 there is a hole in the roof with stars in it.
The place says: somebody has been kept here.
**Accent:** the lock on the outside of the door.

### 8 · The Cloud Pathway hills and the pig's cave · p 35
Rolling dark hills under a moon, a cave mouth with a crooked stone door, a
broken roof tile still stuck in the pig's hair. Moonlight silver and black.
**Accent:** the rake dropped on the doorstep.

### 9 · The Flowing Sand River · pp 39–48
A river so wide the far side is a line, the colour of sand, slow and heavy as
mud, with no waves and no boats; low gray reed beds on the near bank, a stone
tablet. Sand-gold and pale gray; flat late light. On the far bank, from p 47,
a stand of willows and the road going on west.
**Accent:** the stone tablet on the bank, carved: FLOWING SAND RIVER ·
EIGHT HUNDRED MILES · NO FEATHER FLOATS.
