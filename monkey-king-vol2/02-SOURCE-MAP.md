**A source map with an empty cut list is not finished.** Cutting comes first:
before combining, reordering, or inventing, ask what can simply go.

## The cut list — named, unapologetic, famous material included

### Whole sequences cut

- **The Buddha's feast and Guanyin's four recruitments in a row** (ch. 8).
  In the source she meets Sha Wujing, Zhu Bajie, the dragon, and Wukong on one
  trip east. Cut to one: the monkey. The other three tell her offer to us in
  their own mouths when the word "pilgrim" reaches them, which turns three
  exposition scenes into three punchlines.
- **The Tang court frame** (ch. 9–12): Chen Guangrui murdered on the river,
  the baby's blood letter, the Dragon King of the Jing River and his wager
  with the fortune-teller, his execution in a dream, Emperor Taizong's trip to
  the Underworld, the Land and Water Mass, Guanyin at the Mass. Famous. Cut to
  one prose page: found on the river as a baby, raised by monks, chosen by an
  emperor, sent west. The book's story is the road, not the reason for it.
- **The tiger demons who eat the monk's companions** (ch. 13) and the
  hunter Liu Boqin. Cut. The companions turn back at the border. Sanzang
  arrives at the mountain alone and frightened, which is all the reader needs.
- **The tiger Wukong kills for a kilt** (ch. 14). Cut. He comes out in the
  rags of Heaven's armor, and the monk gives him his own spare robe: the first
  thing anyone has ever given him. The kilt is the one from Volume I.
- **Six robbers killed** (ch. 14). Changed: he beats them flat and lifts the
  staff over the last one, and the monk throws himself in the way. The quarrel
  is over what he was about to do. Wukong kills nobody in this volume.
- **Wukong's attempt to strike the monk after the band** (ch. 14). Kept, as
  rage, stopped by the second recitation before the staff comes down.
- **The Guanyin Temple, the abbot, the stolen robe, the fire, the Black Bear
  Demon** (ch. 16–17). Cut whole. Two chapters, a new realm of monks, a new
  villain, and a new object, all to make a point the robbers already make.
- **The fight at the Cloud Pathway Cave, the Crow's Nest Zen Master and the
  Heart Sutra** (ch. 19). Cut. The chase ends at the cave mouth with one word.
- **The Yellow Wind Demon and Lingji** (ch. 20–21). Cut whole: a monster who
  is not a recruit.
- **Moksa and the gourd** (ch. 22). Cut; see merges.
- **The names: Pilgrim Sun, the Tripitaka title, the Bodhisattva's religious
  names for the three recruits.** Sanzang gives one name to each of the two
  who need one (Bajie, Wujing). Wukong already has his.

### Characters cut

The Buddha, the Jade Emperor, Laozi, Erlang, Emperor Taizong, Chen Guangrui,
Yin Wenjiao, the Dragon King of the Jing River, the Ten Kings, the hunter Liu
Boqin, the abbot and monks of the Guanyin Temple, the Black Bear Demon, the
Crow's Nest Zen Master, the Yellow Wind Demon, Lingji, Moksa, the Dragon King
of the Western Sea, Gao Cuilan's voice (she remains, rescued, as scenery), the
robbers' names, the tigers.

### Characters merged

- **The Dragon King of the Western Sea + Ao Guang → Ao Guang.** One face does
  two jobs: sends Wukong back to the monk, and is the father of the dragon in
  the stream. When the dragon names his father, the reader already knows him,
  and so does Wukong.
- **The old woman on the road + Guanyin → Guanyin, undisguised.** The reader
  sees her give the monk the cap, so the reader knows what the cap is before
  Wukong does.
- **Moksa + Sha Wujing → Sha Wujing.** He makes the raft himself, from the
  skulls he wears. The penance and the bridge are the same object.

### Why the biggest cut pays for the volume

Cutting the Tang court (ch. 9–12) and the two non-recruit demons (ch. 16–17,
20–21) deletes a second world, an emperor, a dead father, an Underworld, two
villain designs, a temple, and a stolen object, and it fixes the shape of the
book: everything after the mountain is the road, everyone met on the road
joins it, and the book's only real opponent is the quarrel between the monk
and the monkey. The reader learns five new faces and keeps all of them.

## Protected claims

Carried from Volume I (adopted 2026-09-01): the book treats its source as a
Chinese myth cycle and not as costume. Names are standard pinyin (Sun Wukong,
Tang Sanzang, Guanyin, Zhu Bajie, Sha Wujing, Ao Guang), the settings are
drawn from Chinese landscape and architecture rather than a generic "Eastern"
look, and no character speaks in pidgin, proverb, or fortune-cookie aphorism.

## Sensitive material and its treatment

- **The band.** It is a magic headache, not torture: Wukong rolls on the road
  and roars, holds his head, and it stops the moment the words stop. Twice in
  the book, never for comedy at his expense. The monk is horrified at himself
  both times.
- **The robbers.** Human, armed, and meant it. They are scattered, not
  killed; the staff stops over the last one because the monk is under it.
- **Sha Wujing's skulls.** In the source he ate nine pilgrims and wears their
  skulls because they would not sink. Kept. It is the volume's darkest fact
  and its best payoff: the monk is the tenth pilgrim, the monster tells him
  the truth, and the monk takes him anyway. Staged without gore: nine pale
  skulls on a cord, the confession in words, the faces of the listeners.
- **Zhu Bajie's marriage.** He came to Gao Village as a hardworking young man,
  married the farmer's daughter, then went back to his pig shape and ate the
  village bare, and when the family wanted him gone he locked her in the back
  house. Comedy, with a girl who wants out and gets out on page.
- **The dragon eats the horse.** In one gulp, offstage below the water line.
- **Religious figures.** Guanyin and the monk are people in a myth with wants
  and tempers, not objects of doctrine. Nobody preaches. The scriptures are a
  destination, not a lesson.
- **Death.** Old Ma is dead before page 1. One line, from Wukong, on page 3.
