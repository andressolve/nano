# Sequence gate — pages 21–30

## Verdict

APPROVED

## Blind sequence read

The golden band turns Wukong's sense of betrayal into a threat against Sanzang. Sanzang uses the binding words, immediately regrets the pain, and admits that Guanyin taught them to him. Wukong confronts Guanyin, but she refuses to remove the band and sends him back to the frightened monk. At the fire, Sanzang apologizes for both the words and the deceptive gift; Wukong, unaccustomed to apology, accepts the companionship without pretending the conflict is finished.

On the road, a white dragon swallows Sanzang's horse. Wukong protects the monk, fights the creature, draws it out by insulting its father, and pins it down. Questioning reveals that the dragon was sent by Guanyin to wait for this westbound pilgrim and is Ao Guang's son. Wukong recognizes the creature's long isolation, demands restitution, and the dragon becomes Sanzang's new white horse. Sanzang thanks both of them. The horse then carries him faithfully into autumn, while its pauses over water and Wukong's hidden reaction preserve the cost of its estrangement from its father. At dusk the travelers reach Gao's shuttered farm, hear that a pig demon has imprisoned Gao's daughter, and Wukong commits to meeting it that night.

## Script comparison and sequence judgment

The visible story matches the batch script throughout. All dialogue and prose are present with the intended speakers and attribution. Wukong, Sanzang, Guanyin, Ao Guang, the dragon/white horse, and Old Gao remain unambiguous. The gold band, Wukong's staff, the swallowed bay horse and saddle, and the replacement white horse with saddle all carry consequentially across their page turns. Campsite, South Sea, stream gorge, autumn road, and Gao farm transitions are legible without relying on material outside the batch.

Wukong remains the focal through-line: coercion and anger lead to a difficult reconciliation, the horse conflict becomes recognition of another punished child, and the quiet walk ahead supplies the emotional aftermath before the next problem begins. Sanzang's apology and later thanks materially advance that relationship rather than resetting it.

Pacing is sound. Dramatic confrontation is broken by illustrated-prose reflection on pages 23 and 29 and by the spectacle page on page 24. Pages 25–28 form a sustained action-to-interrogation-to-resolution run rather than a wall of equivalent dramatic scenes. The seasonal compression on page 29 is purposeful passage of time, not missing story, and it preserves the horse's and Wukong's aftermath. Every major beat receives either an immediate consequence or a later emotional echo. Page turns consistently carry a live question, consequence, or new threat.

No material sequence, continuity, attribution, focal, pacing, or page-turn failure justifies a complete-page redraw.
