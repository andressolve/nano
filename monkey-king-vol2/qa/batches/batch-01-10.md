# Sequence gate — pages 1–10

## Blind sequence read

Five hundred years after Heaven trapped the Monkey King beneath the Mountain
of Five Elements, his monkeys still cross the sea to bring him peaches.
Guanyin offers him freedom if he guards a monk walking from the far east to
fetch holy books in the far west. Wukong accepts eagerly, then learns that the
monk must remove the seal himself, travel every mile on foot, and receive no
shortcut from Wukong.

Sanzang, a gentle but stubborn monk abandoned by his two human companions at
the edge of the empire, continues west with his bay horse despite his fear. At
the mountain, Wukong calls to him from the rock and directs him to the summit.
Sanzang makes the frightening climb, tests Wukong's nature with a prayer, and
peels away the gold seal. The mountain bursts open and Wukong rises free.

Sanzang initially fears that Wukong will eat him, but the two identify
themselves and Wukong declares himself Sanzang's bodyguard. Sanzang refuses
Wukong's offer to carry him west in a single somersault because the walking is
the pilgrimage. That night, Sanzang replaces Wukong's disintegrating heavenly
armor with his own spare robe and asks for nothing in return. Wukong, who has
only taken possessions or received tribute, recognizes it as his first gift.
They begin the slow road west together. Wukong looks east once toward his
monkeys, turns west, and at night lies under the stars beside Sanzang after
five hundred years without that freedom or company.

## Gate judgment

The visible sequence is causally complete and emotionally legible. Guanyin's
terms establish both the release mechanism and the walking constraint before
Sanzang arrives; Sanzang's persistence makes his climb credible; removal of
the seal directly causes Wukong's release; and the robe scene supplies a quiet,
consequential aftermath that changes their relationship before the journey
continues.

The rendered dialogue and prose match the batch script, with correct speakers
and attribution across page turns. Sun Wukong, Sanzang, and Guanyin are named
clearly and remain visually and narratively identifiable. The gold seal, five
ridged mountain, distant green mountain across the sea, bay horse, ringed
staff, failing armor, and gifted hemp robe retain their story functions across
the relevant transitions. The visual focus stays with Wukong's release and
the forming Wukong–Sanzang bond.

Pacing is varied rather than a wall of dramatic scenes: the batch alternates
full-page spectacle, dialogue scenes, and illustrated prose. Pages 4 and 10
compress long spans deliberately without obscuring a necessary causal beat.
The mountain's destruction receives immediate relational aftermath on page 8,
and Wukong's freedom receives deeper emotional aftermath through the robe on
page 9 and the eastward glance, westward turn, and night under the stars on
page 10. No page turn creates a material continuity or comprehension failure.

## Verdict

APPROVED
