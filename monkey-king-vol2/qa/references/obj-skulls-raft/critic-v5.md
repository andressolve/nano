## Mandatory findings

1. Both consequential states have the wrong skull count. The loose cord contains seven skulls—three on each side and one at the low center—rather than the locked four-one-four arrangement of nine. The raft contains eight skulls rather than nine. Redesign the left state with exactly four complete, separated skulls on each arm of the cord plus one at the low center, and redesign the right state with exactly nine complete skulls at equal angular intervals around the raft. Add no other skull-like forms.

2. The raft state does not keep every skull complete, unobscured, outside the bindings, and unsubmerged. Several skulls are tucked behind the outer structure or sunk into the water, and the top skull is presented mainly as an obscured rear cranium. Redesign the raft with a smaller inner reed disk and a dry exterior perimeter so all nine skulls sit wholly beyond the bindings, each full cranium, face, and jaw visible without overlap, crop, structural occlusion, or water crossing any skull.

3. The assembled raft reads as heavy concentric poles or logs with thick lashings and cross-braces, not a low raft woven from charcoal-brown and pale-gray river reeds. Replace the thick pole rings and timber-like radial framework with a thin, tightly interlaced reed mat whose alternating charcoal-brown and pale-gray strands are visibly woven; retain the low circular silhouette, one continuous pale-gold elliptical light band, and the localized sand-gold/pale-gray water patch.
