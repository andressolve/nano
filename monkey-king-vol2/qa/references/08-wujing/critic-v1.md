## Mandatory findings

1. **The nose violates the broad-flat structural lock.** In the close view and especially the strict profile, it is long, narrow, strongly projecting, and down-hooked. Redesign it in every view by shortening the projection by roughly one third, widening the bridge and nostril plane, and replacing the pointed descending tip with a blunt, flattened end. Preserve the long hollow-cheeked face around it.

2. **The face reads substantially older than the locked apparent age of about forty-five.** The dense forehead furrows, crow's-feet, deeply webbed cheek creases, sagging lower lids, and long hooked nose combine into an elderly face. Keep the hollow cheeks and heavy brow, but remove most fine forehead and eye wrinkles, tighten the lower lids and jaw contour, and reduce the deep cheek folds to one strong plane break per side so he reads as a hard-lived man in his forties rather than an old man.

3. **The required skull count is not securely locked in view (d).** Fewer than nine separate skulls can be resolved while the cord is being lifted; several disappear into the hair and beard, so the image cannot prove compliance with the Never rule. Recompose that view with the complete cord held clear of the hair, showing exactly nine distinct, clean white, fist-sized skulls with visible gaps between them and no skull remaining around the neck.
