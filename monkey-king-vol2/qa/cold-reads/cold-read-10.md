# Cold read — pages 1–10

APPROVED

## Retell of the story so far

Five hundred years ago, the Monkey King fought Heaven, lost, and was imprisoned inside a mountain formed by the hand that defeated him. Guanyin comes to him there. He first assumes she is another gawker, but she says she needs his help: a monk is traveling from the far east to fetch holy books from the far west, and the road is dangerous. She asks the Monkey King to guard him. He eagerly accepts, partly because he wants freedom and partly because his monkeys have been bringing him a peach every spring and he wants to get out before generations pass. Guanyin explains that the monk must climb the mountain and peel off its gold seal himself; the Monkey King may not simply fly him up, because the walking is the monk's journey. She also says other guardians will join later.

The monk is Sanzang. Long ago he was found as a baby in a basket on a river and raised by monks. The Tang emperor has now sent him west for the holy books. Two companions began the trip with him but abandoned him at the edge of the empire, leaving only his bay horse. Though fearful, he continues alone.

At the mountain, the imprisoned Monkey King calls Sanzang "Master" and explains that he has waited five hundred years to accompany a westbound monk. Sanzang is suspicious that the voice may belong to a monk-eating demon, but climbs anyway. He removes the gold seal after declaring that a true demon should remain trapped while an innocent prisoner should be released. The mountain explodes apart, freeing the Monkey King in his old armor.

Sanzang initially begs not to be eaten. The Monkey King says he is Sanzang's bodyguard and introduces himself as Sun Wukong, the Great Sage Equal to Heaven. Sanzang identifies himself and his mission. Wukong offers to carry him west by somersault, but Sanzang insists that every mile of a pilgrimage must be walked. That night Sanzang notices Wukong's failing old armor and gives him his spare robe, asking nothing in return. Wukong, who says everything he has ever owned was taken or demanded as tribute, is moved by the gift.

The next day Wukong finds Sanzang's walking pace almost impossibly slow. He repeatedly races ahead and back, covering the day's distance many times while Sanzang walks it once. From a hill he looks east toward the distant home of his monkeys, then chooses to face west. That night Sanzang prays while Wukong lies awake under the stars, free for the first time in five hundred years.

## Cast

- **Sun Wukong / the Monkey King / the Great Sage Equal to Heaven:** A golden-brown monkey with bright amber eyes, a long tail, and a spiky crest of hair. While imprisoned, only his head is visible through an opening in the rock. When freed he wears battered red-and-green scale armor, then changes into Sanzang's plain pale robe. He is powerful, impatient, funny, boastful, and unexpectedly vulnerable. His role is Sanzang's newly freed bodyguard and traveling companion.
- **Sanzang:** A young, slender, clean-shaven monk with a shaved head. He wears ochre robes with a red checked outer cloth, carries a ringed staff and woven pack, and travels with a bay horse. He is fearful but morally brave, gentle, stubborn, and committed to walking every mile of his pilgrimage. His role is to travel west and bring the holy books home.
- **Guanyin:** A serene woman in flowing white robes and veil, with an ornate gold headdress, jewelry, a willow branch, and a small vessel. She arrives on a cloud. Her role is to arrange Wukong's release and recruit him as Sanzang's protector; she also knows that further guardians are waiting ahead.
- **The bay horse:** Sanzang's brown pack horse and his only remaining companion before Wukong joins him.
- **Sanzang's former companions:** Two small, distant human figures seen walking away on page 4. They accompanied him to the edge of the empire and then turned back.
- **The monkeys across the sea:** Wukong's community, described rather than individually introduced. They have brought him a peach every spring during his imprisonment, and he looks back toward their mountain after gaining his freedom.

## Causal chain

1. The Monkey King fights Heaven and loses, so he is sealed beneath a mountain for five hundred years.
2. Sanzang is sent west for holy books, but his human companions abandon him, leaving him exposed on a dangerous road.
3. Guanyin needs a protector for Sanzang, so she offers the imprisoned Wukong a way out in exchange for guarding him.
4. Guanyin requires Sanzang to remove the seal himself, so Wukong must persuade the wary monk to climb the mountain.
5. Sanzang decides that justice requires testing the seal, removes it, and frees Wukong.
6. Wukong joins the pilgrimage as Sanzang's bodyguard, but their opposite ideas of travel immediately clash: Wukong wants magical speed, while Sanzang insists on walking.
7. Sanzang gives Wukong a robe with no price attached, creating genuine trust and giving Wukong an emotional reason to value the partnership beyond his release.
8. Wukong looks back toward home but turns west, choosing the pilgrimage.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

None. The location change from Wukong's mountain to Sanzang's earlier life on page 4 was immediately clear from the narration, and page 5 clearly returned to the mountain in the story's present. The distant figures on page 4 were readable as the companions who had turned back. I did not need to guess at any character identity or action.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

All narration and dialogue were legible at normal pace. Speech order and attribution were clear throughout, including the exchanges with off-panel Wukong on pages 5–6. The repeated "Master" address made Wukong's intended relationship to Sanzang clear before his full introduction. No text failed, and no balloon required backtracking to identify its speaker.

## What I expect next

I expect the mismatched pair to continue west, with Wukong's immense speed, power, and impatience repeatedly colliding with Sanzang's vulnerable, deliberate, ethical approach. The road will likely test whether Wukong can protect rather than dominate, and whether Sanzang can trust someone so frightening and unruly. Guanyin's mention of other waiting guardians sets up additional companions joining them. I also expect Wukong's monkeys and his eastward home to remain an emotional pull, while the gift of the robe becomes an early foundation for loyalty between monk and bodyguard.
