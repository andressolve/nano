## Blind read
At a mountain campsite, the monk tells the monkey that Heaven’s armor is falling off him and offers his spare robe. The large middle panel unmistakably owns the page, while the monkey owns the emotional movement: he asks what the robe will cost, explains that everything he has was taken or received as tribute, and admits that nobody has ever given him anything. The monk answers that the robe carries no debt and keeps sewing it. In the final panel, after the monk has fallen asleep, the monkey accepts the gift and quietly calls it a good robe. The turn is the monkey’s shift from guarded, transactional disbelief to private recognition of unbargained-for care.
