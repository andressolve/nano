## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: Four lines are visibly owned by the wrong speaker. “Calm down, old fish. I only want tea.” hangs from Ao Guang; “I heard you were under a mountain. Why are you out?” hangs from Wukong; “A monk let me out, so I could guard him to the west. I quit this morning. He called me a robber.” hangs from Ao Guang; and “You are a robber. You robbed me of the staff.” is placed with Wukong in a Wukong-only panel rather than hanging from Ao Guang.
- Material reader harm: The swapped ownership makes Ao Guang address himself as an old fish, makes Wukong ask himself why he is free, assigns Wukong's confession to Ao Guang, and makes Wukong deliver Ao Guang's accusation. This breaks the causal conversation and obscures the robber/staff reversal that supplies the page's dramatic turn.
- Redraw justification: The failure affects four consecutive lines across the dominant tea scene and closing exchange, including the setup to the final punchline. Because the visible mouths contradict the scripted speakers throughout the page's central relationship, the reader cannot recover the intended event reliably; that core narrative loss is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
