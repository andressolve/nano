VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
The Monkey King bursts into Ao Guang's underwater court and taunts him; Ao Guang owns the page visually from the throne and the dominant central panel, but the Monkey King drives the exchange. Their hostile reunion turns into an absurdly casual tea visit, then into an argument over the staff: Ao Guang calls him a robber, and the Monkey King distinguishes the monk's accusation from Ao Guang's while declaring that he quit guarding the monk.

## Visible text
- Monkey King's top-left balloon, tail pointing to his mouth: `Ao Guang! Dragon King of the Eastern Sea! Still got your throne?`
- Ao Guang's top-right balloon, positioned over Ao Guang: `No. No, no. Not you again. You took my iron pillar that held the sea down, and shrank it to a needle!`

- Monkey King's lower bottom-right balloon, placed beside him and connected to his preceding balloon: `That was different. That was you.`
- The name `Ao Guang` and the title `Dragon King of the Eastern Sea` are lettered only as dialogue in the Monkey King's opening address. No speaker name, label, tag, or other extra text is lettered.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The dialogue is not in the script's required causal order. `Calm down, old fish. I only want tea.` appears in the central tea scene after Ao Guang asks `Why are you out?`, while the required answer to that question, `A monk let me out... He called me a robber.`, is delayed until the bottom-right panel after Ao Guang has already said `You are a robber. You robbed me of the staff.`
- Material reader harm: The placement makes the tea request read as the answer to why Wukong is out and makes Ao Guang accuse Wukong before Wukong reports the monk's accusation. This breaks the intended setup-and-reply logic of both exchanges and weakens the final distinction between the monk calling Wukong a robber and Wukong's history of robbing Ao Guang.
- Redraw justification: This is a blocking exact-script and causal-reading-order failure across the arrival, dominant tea scene, and final exchange, not optional polish; preserving it would change the page's central joke and turn, so the material harm outweighs the risk of replacing the page.
