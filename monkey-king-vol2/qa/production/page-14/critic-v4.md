VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Wukong plunges into Ao Guang's undersea crystal court, scattering armed shrimp and crab guards, and greets the alarmed Dragon King. Ao Guang owns the page through the dominant central tea scene: the enormous dragon pours from a tiny pot while Wukong lounges opposite him with his feet up. The encounter turns when Ao Guang calls Wukong a robber; after explaining that he left the monk who used the same accusation, Wukong dismisses robbing Ao Guang as different because it was Ao Guang.

## Visible text
1. Wukong, balloon tail pointing to the plunging monkey: “Ao Guang! Dragon King of the Eastern Sea! Still got your throne?”
2. Ao Guang, balloon tail pointing to the Dragon King on his throne: “No. No, no. Not you again. You took my iron pillar that held the sea down, and shrank it to a needle!”
3. Ao Guang, balloon tail pointing to the Dragon King pouring tea: “I heard you were under a mountain. Why are you out?”
4. Wukong, balloon tail pointing to the reclining monkey in the tea scene: “Calm down, old fish. I only want tea.”
5. Ao Guang, balloon tail pointing to the Dragon King: “You are a robber. You robbed me of the staff.”
6. Wukong, balloon tail pointing to the reclining monkey: “A monk let me out, so I could guard him to the west. I quit this morning. He called me a robber.”
7. Wukong, balloon tail pointing to the reclining monkey: “That was different. That was you.”

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The visible sequence places “I heard you were under a mountain. Why are you out?” before “Calm down, old fish. I only want tea.” The lower arrangement also puts Ao Guang's “You are a robber. You robbed me of the staff.” in the left panel beside and before the monk explanation that is supposed to prompt it, so the required causal order is not preserved.
- Material reader harm: Ao Guang's question is answered by an unrelated calming line, and his decisive accusation can be read before Wukong explains that the monk called him a robber. The exchange's setup, reaction, and final comic distinction therefore no longer land in the scripted sequence.
- Redraw justification: The defect is embedded in the page's panel and balloon order rather than in optional polish; correcting it requires replacing the complete page to restore the causal conversation.

### Finding C3
- Observation: “Calm down, old fish. I only want tea.” hangs from the reclining Wukong in the tea scene, not from the plunging Wukong in the arrival.
- Material reader harm: The second arrival line is assigned to the wrong depicted Wukong moment, moving his disarming reply out of the entrance exchange and into Ao Guang's later question-and-answer beat.
- Redraw justification: The balloon is structurally attached to the wrong scene and mouth instance, so the scripted arrival and tea-stage attribution cannot both read correctly without replacing the full page.
