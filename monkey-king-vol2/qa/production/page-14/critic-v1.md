The final exchange reopens the staff theft and lands on the Monkey King’s blithe double standard. The Monkey King owns the page through his entrance, relaxed posture, and last-word comic logic.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: Four lines hang from the wrong speaker. “Calm down, old fish. I only want tea.” points to Ao Guang; “I heard you were under a mountain. Why are you out?” points to Wukong; “A monk let me out, so I could guard him to the west. I quit this morning. He called me a robber.” points to Ao Guang; and “You are a robber. You robbed me of the staff.” points to Wukong.
- Material reader harm: The central exchange and the opening line of the final exchange reverse speaker ownership, making Ao Guang appear to announce that he quit the monk and Wukong appear to interrogate and accuse himself. This breaks the scripted host–guest relationship and the causal setup for Wukong choosing the man he robbed as his confidant.
- Redraw justification: The error affects four of the seven balloons, including both sides of the dominant tea scene and the accusation that triggers the final joke. Correct attribution is essential to understanding the page, so the harm is substantial enough to replace the complete page.
