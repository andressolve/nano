A monk meets a skull-necklaced, red-haired river being in the shallows and answers the being's fear of rejection with acceptance. His acceptance causes the being to drop to hands and knees, and then both figures kneel as the being reveals a final prophecy about what it carries and the monk answers for the nine who came before him.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The monk's balloon in the dominant panel reads `I will take you.` The required string is `I will. I'll call you Wujing. You'll walk right behind me, at my back, every day. And I won't be afraid of you.`
- Material reader harm: The substitution removes the naming of Wujing and the monk's promise to keep him at his back every day without fear, so the central kneeling acceptance no longer delivers the scripted recruitment, named identity, or full trust that makes this the page's dramatic turn.
- Redraw justification: This is missing owner-controlled dialogue at the focal turn, not optional polish; the altered story event and omitted naming are substantial enough to justify replacing the complete page.
