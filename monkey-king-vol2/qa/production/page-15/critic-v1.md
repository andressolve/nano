VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
In an underwater palace, a dragon shows Monkey a painted-screen story: an old man throws a shoe from a bridge and a boy retrieves it repeatedly; the old man then teaches the boy, who becomes a great man. The dragon uses the tale to suggest that going back can be admirable and urges Monkey to go back now, far away. Monkey dismisses the tale as terrible but decides to return to his monk, conceding that the dragon was right; he rises so abruptly that his drink spills. The dragon asks in surprise whether he is leaving. Monkey owns the page's decisive change, while the dragon is the dominant visual presence and causes the turn through the story and pointed advice.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: “And then?” is lettered twice in the central panel: once in a small left balloon placed with Ao Guang and once in the right balloon belonging to Wukong. The lower-left panel also presents Wukong’s “I’m going back to my monk. Don’t tell anyone you were right.” before the lower-right panel presents Ao Guang’s “You’re leaving?”
- Material reader harm: The duplicated question adds unscripted dialogue, and the page’s normal left-to-right order makes Wukong’s answer arrive before the question that prompts it, breaking the exact causal exchange at the decisive turn.
- Redraw justification: These are errors in required dialogue and causal reading order at both the central story exchange and the page-ending decision, substantial enough to block release despite the risk of replacing the complete page.

### Finding C3
- Observation: The extra left-side “And then?” is owned by Ao Guang through placement, although the scripted “And then?” belongs only to Wukong; at the end, Ao Guang’s correctly attributed “You’re leaving?” is placed after Wukong’s answer rather than between Wukong’s two statements.
- Material reader harm: Ao Guang appears to ask the central question assigned to Wukong, and the closing speaker sequence reads as Wukong declaring his destination before Ao Guang reacts, so the intended prompt-and-answer relationship does not land.
- Redraw justification: The wrong apparent ownership of the extra central line and the inverted closing exchange materially alter the dominant dramatic relationship, justifying replacement of the full page.
