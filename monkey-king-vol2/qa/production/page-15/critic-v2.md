## Blind read
Ao Guang tells Wukong, through the ink painting, how an old man repeatedly tested a boy by throwing a shoe from a bridge, then taught him everything after the boy fetched it three times. The large central painting owns the page. Ao Guang turns the story into a hopeful suggestion that Wukong should go back and leave; Wukong rejects the story, rises with his tea slopping, and leaves for his monk while conceding that Ao Guang was right.

## Visible text
Wukong: “What’s that? An old man throwing his shoe at a boy?”

Ao Guang: “He dropped it off a bridge, and the boy fetched it. He dropped it again, and the boy fetched it again. Three times.”

Wukong: “And then?”

Ao Guang: “And then the old man taught him everything he knew, and the boy became the greatest man of his time.”

Ao Guang: “Going back is very admirable. You could go back. Now. Far away.”

Wukong: “That’s a terrible story. I hate it.”

Ao Guang: “You’re leaving?”

Wukong: “I’m going back to my monk. Don’t tell anyone you were right.”
