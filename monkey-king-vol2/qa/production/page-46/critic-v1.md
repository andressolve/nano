VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
A single full-page image shows a raft ringed with nine skulls carrying a mounted monk and three companions across a broad river at sunset. The raft and company own the page: they are centered and fully visible against the water. The narration supplies the turn that the nine skulls became the raft, carried the tenth pilgrim across, and vanished on the far bank.

## Visible text
Narration/prose at the upper left (no name is lettered):
Nine skulls became a raft that would not sink.
It carried the tenth pilgrim across, every mile of it,
on the water, and on the far bank it was gone.

## Findings

### Finding C2
- Observation: The company crosses on a conventional woven raft with skulls visibly lashed around its edge; the skulls do not read as having become a broad raft of pale light.
- Material reader harm: The image replaces the page's central supernatural transformation with a physical raft decorated by skulls, so the impossible crossing made possible by what the ogre carried does not land visually.
- Redraw justification: This is the dominant spectacle and causal turn of the full page, not a subordinate styling detail; preserving the current image would materially misstate how the crossing becomes possible.
