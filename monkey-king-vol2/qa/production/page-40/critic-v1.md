## Blind read
Pigsy complains that the river crossing is eight hundred miles with no boat, bridge, or noodle stall. Tripitaka asks why not, then asks Wukong to fly him across; Wukong owns the page in the dominant middle-panel close-up and turns the scene by refusing on the principle that this is Tripitaka's road to cross. Tripitaka repeats his question, recognises his own rule, and admits that he cannot swim. Pigsy suggests riding the former dragon across, but the white horse rears and refuses to enter the water.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The required Wukong line “I can’t, Master. I could fly a whole mountain over this river. But I can’t fly you over. Not one mile of your road.” is absent. In its place in the causal sequence, an additional Tripitaka “Why not?” appears in the top panel before Tripitaka’s request, so “Why not?” appears twice and the request is displaced into the dominant panel.
- Material reader harm: The page no longer presents Wukong’s explicit refusal before Tripitaka asks why. The exchange instead begins with an unprompted question and reaches Wukong’s principle without his stated refusal, breaking the scripted cause-and-response turn.
- Redraw justification: This changes multiple required dialogue strings and the central dramatic sequence, including complete loss of the line that establishes the page’s decisive refusal; the page cannot be released with the owner-controlled script materially incomplete and out of order.
