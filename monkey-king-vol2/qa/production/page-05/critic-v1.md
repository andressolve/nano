## Blind read
Tang Sanzang and three other monks arrive at a strange island and face a rocky mountain marked with gold above a cave. The Monkey King owns the active progression, moving from curiosity to scouting, striking the mountain, and leading the group toward the cavern. The page's dominant turn is the wide eruption: the whole mountain splits apart and reveals the hidden cave, changing the group's approach into entry.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: None of the seven required lines appears. The page instead contains ten unrelated captions and dialogue lines about reaching an island, splitting a mountain, and entering a cave.
- Material reader harm: The exact voice-from-the-rock exchange, Sanzang's questions, Wukong's offer, and the command to climb are entirely absent, so the scripted causal reading and page-turn setup cannot occur.
- Redraw justification: The complete lettering and underlying story action belong to a different scene; preserving this page cannot recover the required exchange.

### Finding C2
- Observation: The page is dominated by a wide view of a mountain splitting open. There is no panel of Sanzang bending face-to-face toward a grinning monkey in a rock cleft.
- Material reader harm: The intended dramatic meeting of the two faces never lands, and the eye is directed to landscape-scale destruction instead of their relationship.
- Redraw justification: The dominant composition and dramatic event must both be replaced to deliver the required reader event.

### Finding C4
- Observation: The Monkey King appears repeatedly as a full-bodied, freely moving armored figure; only his face is never shown trapped in the cleft with moss, pale muzzle, round gold eyes, and golden fur.
- Material reader harm: Wukong's imprisonment and startling discovery—the focal identity and consequential state of the scene—are replaced by an already-free companion.
- Redraw justification: This is a page-wide character-state error embedded in every appearance of Wukong, requiring a new scene rather than a local correction.

### Finding C5
- Observation: The mountain is repeated across several panels and is explicitly shown splitting into two peaks, with the final caption stating that it “stood in two.”
- Material reader harm: The intact Mountain of Five Elements and the high seal that motivates Sanzang's climb are replaced by a destroyed mountain and an already-open route.
- Redraw justification: The central object state, action, and resulting geography are the opposite of the script and occupy the page's dominant panel.

### Finding C6
- Observation: Four fully shaven monks travel together throughout the page rather than one young monk alone.
- Material reader harm: The extra monks erase Sanzang's isolated encounter and diffuse the fear and personal responsibility that must drive him to climb.
- Redraw justification: The group is built into the page's establishing, approach, and entry compositions, so restoring the lone-monk scene requires replacing the full page.
