## Page intent

## PAGE 40

Dramatic. The monk asks Wukong to fly him across on his cloud, just this once, and Wukong, not joking for once, refuses: he could fly a mountain over, but the road is the monk's, and a road you are carried over is not crossed. The dominant turn is that refusal, close on Wukong's face with the monk listening above him on the white horse. Then the monk recognises his own rule, and frightened, admits he cannot swim; the horse will not set a hoof in the sand-water. Bajie supplies the bookends: no noodle stall, and a hopeful idea about the horse. The reader turns because the river is wide and something under it has been listening.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 40 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Bajie on the left, by the tablet, staring out at the
river. Sanzang on the white horse beside him, turning to Wukong.

> **BAJIE:** "Eight hundred miles. No boat. No bridge. Not one noodle stall."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Wukong, you can fly. Fly me across on your cloud. Just this once."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong on the right, close, not joking for once, the gold band on his brow,
the sand river behind him. Sanzang on the left on the white horse, looking
down at him.

> **WUKONG:** "I can't, Master. I could fly a whole mountain over this river. But I can't fly you over. Not one mile of your road."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Why not?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Because it's your road. If I fly you over, you never crossed it."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Sanzang on the left, looking out at the river, frightened,
the white horse under him shying back from the sand-water. Bajie on the right,
pointing at the horse.

> **SANZANG:** "That's what I told you, the first day. Then I have to cross it myself, and I can't swim."
>
> **BAJIE:** "Your horse used to be a dragon! Ride him over!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Look at him. He won't put a hoof in it."

**Why you turn the page:** the river is very wide, and something under it has been listening.
