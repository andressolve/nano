## Page intent

## PAGE 28

Dramatic. Wukong lifts the staff away and the dragon's head rises, ashamed: its father, the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea, put it in the stream for setting fire to his best pearl, and Wukong had tea with him last week. Wukong, arms folded, asks what it will do about the horse; the dragon looks up the bank and offers itself to the monk as his horse instead. Then the great image: one creature turning in a spiral of white foam, the long white coils folding into a slim white horse in the shallows, the same pale-blue mane and sea-green eyes, the old saddle on its back. The monk reaches out to its nose and thanks it; Wukong scowls at the injustice; and the monk thanks Wukong too, for coming back to him. The dominant turn: the dragon names its father and becomes the horse. The transformation and the monk's thanks own the page.

**Location:** Eagle Grief Stream.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Wukong lifting the staff off; the dragon's head rising,

> **WUKONG:** "Who put you in a stream, worm?"
>
> **DRAGON:** "My father, the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea. I set fire to his best pearl. By accident, mostly."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Ao Guang? I had tea with him last week."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Wukong on the left, arms folded; the dragon on the right, looking up the
bank at the monk.

> **WUKONG:** "So you ate the horse. What are you going to do about it?"
>
> **DRAGON:** "I'm sorry I ate your horse, Master. Let me be your horse instead. The lady said I was to carry you."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** One creature changing in a spiral of white foam:
the dragon's long white coils unwinding and folding into a slim white horse's
body standing in the shallows, the pale-blue mane and the sea-green eyes the
same, the bay horse's saddle on its back. No whole dragon beside it: the last
of the tail is becoming the horse's tail. Sanzang on the left on the bank,
reaching out a hand to its nose. Wukong on the right, staff on his shoulder,
scowling at the horse.

> **SANZANG:** "Thank you."
>
> **WUKONG:** "He eats your horse, then he turns into one, saddle and all, and he gets a thank-you?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Thank you too, Wukong. For coming back to me."

**Why you turn the page:** the monk has a horse again, it is somebody's son, and the monkey has been thanked.

### C1 — Transcription fails
All eight strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The transformation and the thanks do not land
The transformation must own the page: one creature turning in white foam from dragon into a white horse with the same pale-blue mane and sea-green eyes, the monk reaching to its nose, Wukong scowling. "Thank you too, Wukong…" must read as the last line. A whole dragon beside the horse, or a horse that is not plainly the dragon, fails the turn.

### C3 — Attribution
"Who put you in a stream…", "Ao Guang?…" and "So you ate the horse…" are Wukong's; "My father…" and "I'm sorry I ate your horse…" the dragon's. "Thank you." and "Thank you too, Wukong…" are the monk's; "He eats your horse…" is Wukong's. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail. A balloon on the horse is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and state
Wukong: thin gold band, hemp robe, no cap. The horse never brown. The monk shaven, in saffron and red.
