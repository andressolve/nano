## Page intent

## PAGE 38

Illustrated prose. Weeks of road go by in one long image: the monk on the white horse, Bajie trudging behind under the luggage pole with his mouth open, complaining, and Wukong walking backwards in front of the pig, pointing at him and telling him to hurry. The dominant turn is that Wukong, once the worst-behaved creature in the universe, has become the big brother who bosses somebody else, and he does not mind it as much as he should. The prose carries the comedy and the warmth; the picture carries the three of them as a travelling family. The reader turns because the road is about to end at a river. What stays subordinate: the plain, the hills, the luggage itself.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the plain)*
> "Bajie complained about the road, the weather, the hills, the luggage, and his feet, in that order, every day. He ate whatever the villages gave them and then asked what was for dinner. He snored like a landslide.
>
> And Wukong, who had once been the worst-behaved creature in the universe, found that he was now the one telling somebody else to hurry up, stop eating, and keep his hands off the horse. When Bajie heard the horse had once been a dragon, he never stopped asking it to talk.
>
> 'Big brother,' Bajie called him, to annoy him. It did not annoy him as much as it should have."

**Why you turn the page:** the road ends at the edge of a river.

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right field: one prose field, three paragraphs, in order, with no extra text anywhere; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order are what must match. Any balloon, caption, or label on the page is extra text and blocking.

### C2 — the prose page and its picture
The page is illustrated prose: one dominant road image and a prose field over open sky. A field that covers a face or the pig and monkey's exchange of looks is blocking; ragged line breaks and field shape are nonblocking.

### C3 — the big brother
By eye, Wukong walks backwards in front of the pig and points at him, and the pig is visibly complaining under the luggage pole. If the reader cannot see who is bossing whom, the turn fails and it is blocking; exact pose and road detail are nonblocking.

### C4 — identity and state
Wukong is the small golden monkey in the hemp robe with the thin gold band; a cap, ragged armor, or a missing band is blocking. Bajie is the pig-headed man in black; the horse is white with a pale-blue mane, never bay. Background finish is nonblocking.
