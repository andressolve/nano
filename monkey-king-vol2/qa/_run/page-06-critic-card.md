## Page intent

## PAGE 6

A dramatic page. The climb, told in three steps: the monk stuck halfway up a ridge with the horse tiny below and the monkey shouting directions from the bottom; the monk alone at the windy summit, on his knees, eyes shut, his hand flat on the small gold seal, praying that it stay if the monkey is a demon and lift if he is not; and the seal peeling away in his fingers and lifting on the wind while the voice from below tells him to run. The page belongs to the monk's fear and his choice; its one dominant turn is the climb, the fear, and his hand on the seal. The monkey is only a voice from far below and is not seen. The reader turns because the seal is off and the mountain is about to let go.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 6 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. The two voice-from-below lines are owned by their tails pointing down toward the mountain; they need no visible face.

### C2 — The hand on the seal does not land
One panel owns the page by eye: the monk alone on his knees at the windy summit, eyes shut, his hand flat on the seal. If that moment is crowded out by the climb or the seal's flight, or the page is not a dramatic panel page, it is blocking.

### C3 — Attribution of the climb
"Left foot!" and "It's coming!" come from below, never from the monk; "I looked down." and the prayer come from the monk's mouth. A voice-from-below line hanging from the monk, or "I looked down." read before "Left foot!", is blocking.

### C4 — Seal state and the mountain
The seal is flat under his hand at the summit, then peeling and lifting in the last panel. A seal already gone before his hand touches it, two seals, or a mountain already split is blocking.

### C5 — The monk and his hands
A young, fully shaven, beardless monk in saffron and red, alone. A visible monkey on the ridge, a second figure, or a malformed hand on the seal is blocking; minor background anatomy is not.
