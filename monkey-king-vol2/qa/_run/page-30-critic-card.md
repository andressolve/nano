## Page intent

## PAGE 30

Dramatic page. At dusk the travellers reach a village where every door is barred, and Old Gao, peering through the crack of his gate with a lantern, tries to send them away until Wukong says he catches demons. Inside, in the lamplit front room over a feast of noodles nobody is eating, Old Gao owns the page: the strong young ploughman who married his youngest daughter turned into a pig with a rake, eats everything, and has locked her in the back house. The one dominant turn is the old man's story of the pig son-in-law, told in the front room. The monk is present and quiet; the pig is only told about, never seen. Wukong's last line turns complaint into a plan, and the reader turns because the pig is coming tonight and Wukong means to be waiting for him.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 30 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Old Gao's farmhouse in Gao Village.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Dusk. A lane of shuttered doors, and at the end a gate with a bar across it. Old Gao peering out
through a crack, holding up a lantern, at the monk on the white horse and
Wukong.

> **OLD GAO:** "Go away! This is Gao's farm, and we don't open the gate after dark! We've got trouble here!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "What kind of trouble? I catch demons."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Old Gao's front room, lamps lit, a feast of
noodles on the table. Old Gao wringing his hands on the left; the monk and
Wukong on the right.

> **OLD GAO:** "Three years ago a strong young man came and asked to marry my youngest daughter. He ploughed like ten oxen, so we said yes."
>
> **OLD GAO:** "Then his face changed. Ears. A snout. Now he's a pig with a rake, and he eats a cartload of rice a day!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Old Gao, close, desperate.

> **OLD GAO:** "We told him to go. He locked my daughter in the back house, and he comes back to her every night."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Every night? Good. Tonight he'll find me there."

**Why you turn the page:** there is a pig coming tonight, and Wukong has a plan.

### C1 — Transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. No object on this page carries scripted words.

### C2 — The old man's story owns the page
Dramatic mode. The eye finds Old Gao telling his story in the lamplit front room. Blocking if the gate scene outweighs the telling; panel balance is nonblocking.

### C3 — Attribution
"Go away!…", both front-room lines, and "We told him to go…" hang from Old Gao; "What kind of trouble?…" and "Every night? Good…" from Wukong, each by tail from its own mouth. A swapped owner is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and the pig's absence
Old Gao (old, stooped, gray fringe, moustache, indigo) never reads as the monk (young, shaven, saffron and red); Wukong wears the thin gold band, no cap. A pig drawn anywhere is blocking; door counts are nonblocking.

### C5 — Focal integrity
Speakers' faces and Old Gao's hands are whole; background anatomy is nonblocking.
