## Page intent

## PAGE 1

Spectacle. The volume opens on the Mountain of Five Elements in spring: five bare ridges like the fingers of a huge hand pressed into the earth, moss over their slopes, a small gold paper seal on the highest ridge. At the base, in a cleft of rock, the Monkey King's face, matted with moss, gold eyes with their thin red ring wide open. A small brown young monkey climbs away down the slope, looking back; under the face's chin sits one fresh peach. Far across a strip of sea, small and green, his home. One caption tells the reader why he is there. The dominant turn: five hundred years under a mountain, a face still awake, and a young monkey's peach. The reader turns because his eyes are open. The far mountain and the sea stay small; the face, the peach and the seal carry the page.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Spring. Five bare ridges like the fingers of a
huge hand pressed into the earth, grass and moss grown over their lower slopes,
a small gold paper seal on the highest ridge. At the base, in a cleft of rock,
a monkey's face: golden fur matted with moss, gold eyes with a thin red ring
around each iris, wide open. A small brown young monkey, bare, is climbing down
the slope away from him, looking back. On the rock under the face's chin sits
one fresh peach. Far across a strip of sea, small and green, another mountain.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the sky)*
> "Five hundred years ago, the Monkey King fought all of Heaven and lost. The great hand that beat him became this mountain."

**Why you turn the page:** he has been under there five hundred years, and his eyes are open.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text anywhere, tail-free over the sky. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. Any writing legible on the seal or elsewhere is extra text and blocking.

### C2 — The face under the mountain does not land
One image owns the page as spectacle: a mountain shaped like five fingers pressed into the earth, and at its base a monkey's face in the rock with its eyes open. If the face is missing, hard to find, eyes shut, or reads as a carving rather than a living monkey trapped in stone, it is blocking.

### C3 — The peach is lost
One peach sits on the rock under his chin and reads as a peach, with a small brown young monkey climbing away and looking back. A peach that cannot be found or read as fruit, or no young monkey, loses the page's tenderness and is blocking; its exact place on the rock is not.

### C4 — Wukong not himself, or out of state
A monkey's face, golden fur matted with moss, gold eyes with a red ring, never a human face. A gold band or cap on his head, or his body drawn free of the rock, is blocking. The young monkey must be small, brown and bare; a second golden or clothed monkey is blocking.

### C5 — The mountain already broken
The ridges stand whole with the small gold seal on the highest one. Split ridges, or the mountain drawn twice as two states, is blocking.
