## Page intent

## PAGE 35

Dramatic page. Under the moon the pig flees with his rake, a broken roof tile in his hair, protesting that he worked hard; Wukong, behind him on a cloud, swings the staff and says he ate everything too, and staff and rake clash. At the crooked stone door of his cave the pig turns at bay and asks who sent him; Wukong says Old Gao asked, but he is here for a monk going west. Then the pig drops the rake, ears lifting, face lighting up in wonder: the pilgrim. A lady in white told him to wait, and he forgot and got married. The one dominant turn is the pig's wonder at the pilgrim. The fight is busy and stays small. The reader turns because the pig was waiting for the monk too, and the monk is back at the farm.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 35 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the moonlit hills, at the mouth of the pig's cave.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Moonlight. The pig running with his rake, a broken roof
tile stuck in his hair; Wukong behind him on a cloud, staff swinging. Staff
and rake clashing.

> **PIG:** "Leave me alone! I worked hard! I ploughed every field in that valley!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "And then you ate everything that grew in them!"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The pig on the left, backed against a crooked stone door, rake up. Wukong on
the right, landing off his cloud, staff on his shoulder.

> **PIG:** "Who sent you, monkey? Heaven? Old Gao?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Old Gao asked me. But I'm here for a monk on his way to the west."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** The pig dropping the rake on the doorstep, ears
lifting, face lighting up in wonder.

> **PIG:** "A monk? Going west for the holy books? The pilgrim?"
>
> **PIG:** "A lady in white told me to wait for him! I was supposed to wait! I forgot! I got married!"

**Why you turn the page:** the pig was waiting for the monk too, and the monk is back at the farm.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

### C1 — Transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. No object on this page carries scripted words.

### C2 — The pig's wonder owns the page
Dramatic mode. The eye finds the pig's face lighting up as the rake drops, not the fight. Blocking if the chase outweighs the wonder. Fight choreography is nonblocking.

### C3 — Attribution
In the chase, "Leave me alone!…" is the pig's and "And then you ate…" Wukong's; at the door, "Who sent you, monkey?…" is the pig's and "Old Gao asked me…" Wukong's. Both closing lines are the pig's. Each hangs from its own speaker's mouth; a swapped owner is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and absence
Wukong is the small gold monkey with the band and the red staff; the pig the big pink-gray pig-headed man with the rake. No lady in white appears. A drawn Guanyin, or the two figures blurring, is blocking.
