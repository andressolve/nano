## Page intent

## PAGE 10

An illustrated-prose page. One wide image of a long road through empty hills under a huge sky: near the bottom, the small monk leading his bay horse; far ahead on a hilltop, the tiny monkey in his robe shading his eyes, and a gold streak racing back down the road toward the monk. The prose tells the first days: the monkey running ahead and back until he has walked the day's road ten times, the question of whether it is always this slow, one look back east toward his monkeys' mountain before he turns west, and the first night in five hundred years lying on his back looking at the stars. The one dominant turn is the first days, running ahead and back, and how slow a man is. The road owns the page. The reader turns because the road is slow and empty, until it isn't.

>
> 'Is it always this slow?' he asked. 'Yes,' said Sanzang. 'Every day?' 'Every day.'
>

>

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 10 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, as one prose field, with no other text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The slow road does not land
One image owns the page: a long empty road under a huge sky, the small monk near the bottom and the monkey far ahead. If the page reads as panels or a dramatic page rather than illustrated prose, or the distance between them is lost, it is blocking.

### C3 — Speed reads as two monkeys
The gold streak racing back must read as one monkey's speed. A second fully drawn monkey with face and robe is blocking; a blur with a hint of figure at its head is not.

### C4 — Prose over the figures
The prose field sits over open sky. A field covering the monk, the horse, the hilltop figure, or the streak is blocking.

### C5 — Band or cap
A gold band or cap on Wukong is blocking, if it can be seen at all at this scale.
