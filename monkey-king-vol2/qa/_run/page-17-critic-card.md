## Page intent

## PAGE 17

A dramatic page on the border road by a plain white milestone. Wukong lands in a puff of dust and tells the monk not to make a speech; Sanzang, startled on his stone, hides the cap behind his back and says he was not going to. The dominant turn is the two of them face to face, Wukong with arms folded and not quite looking at him, explaining his return only as a dragon and a picture of a shoe he will not talk about, and the monk saying simply that he is glad. Then they walk west again, Wukong a step ahead, boasting he hit nobody the whole time and had tea. The reader turns because he came back on his own, and the monk is still hiding the cap.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 17 — card

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement is owned even if its tail wanders. The milestone carries no words; lettering on it is blocking.

### C2 — the turn lands: back, face to face
The eye must find the two of them face to face by the milestone, Wukong arms folded and not quite looking at the monk, as the page's centre. Blocking if the face-off is not the main image or the return does not read. Exact proportion is nonblocking.

### C3 — attribution
"Don't make a speech" is Wukong's and "I wasn't going to." is Sanzang's; in the face-off the question and "I'm glad you came back." are Sanzang's and the shoe line is Wukong's; the walking line is Wukong's. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his. Blocking if "I'm glad you came back." reads as Wukong's.

### C4 — identity and the hidden cap
Wukong is the small golden monkey in the mended hemp robe; a band or cap on his head is blocking. Sanzang hides the plain cap behind his back on landing; the cap in plain view before Wukong, or handed over, is blocking.

### C5 — focal integrity
Speaking faces and folded arms are whole and correct; malformed focal hands are blocking.
