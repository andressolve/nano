## Page intent

## PAGE 37

Dramatic page. At the farmhouse gate the monk asks the kneeling pig whether he will behave, hears that he will try very hard, and names him Bajie, eight rules. On his feet with the luggage on a carrying pole, Bajie haggles for two rules and asks his father-in-law to keep his bed made; Old Gao says please don't. Then the daughter looks at Bajie for a long moment and swings the gate shut on him herself. Bajie's ears droop; Wukong grins beside him, and the monk on the white horse waits beyond. The one dominant turn is the name Bajie, the bed made, and her answer: the gate shutting, owned by her silent face. The reader turns because there are three of them now and a horse, and the road goes on.

### C1 — Transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. No object on this page carries scripted words.

### C2 — Her answer
Dramatic mode. The eye finds the daughter shutting the gate on Bajie herself, her face carrying the look. Blocking if the gate action does not read as hers. Gate detail is nonblocking.

### C3 — Attribution
"Will you behave yourself…" and "Then I'll call you Bajie…" are the monk's, "I'll try very hard." the pig's between them. "Eight? Couldn't it be two?…" is Bajie's, "Please don't." Old Gao's. "That's her answer, Pig…" is Wukong's. Each hangs from its own speaker's mouth; a swapped owner is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and state
Bajie carries the luggage pole; Wukong wears the gold band; the monk and the old farmer never read as one man; the daughter is silent and wears no gold. The monk's horse is white. A brown horse or a speaking daughter is blocking.
