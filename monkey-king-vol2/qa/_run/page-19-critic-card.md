## Page intent

## PAGE 19

A dramatic page at a camp by the road at night. By the fire the monk asks Wukong to promise never to strike a man again, even to save him, and Wukong laughs at the idea, pulls the needle from behind his ear, and swings the grown staff to show off, so close that its end sweeps past the monk's nose. The dominant turn is the request, the needle, and the words: frightened, eyes shut, palms pressed hard together, the monk says the three words, and behind him the monkey's hand flies to his head. The page belongs to the monk's fear in the last panel, which is the cause of everything; the staff is the provocation, not the subject. The reader turns because the monk spoke out of fright and something is closing on the monkey's head. The fire and the camp stay simple.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 19 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a camp by the road at night.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The fire. Sanzang on the left, earnest. Wukong on the
right in the cap, laughing.

> **SANZANG:** "Wukong, promise me something. Never strike a man again, not even to save me."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Promise that? The next robber takes your head off, and I stand there with my hands folded?"

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Wukong pulling the needle, and the staff growing out of his
fist, swinging it to show off, its end sweeping past the monk's nose. The monk
flinching back.

> **WUKONG:** "This keeps you alive, Master. Not promises."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Sanzang, frightened, eyes shut, palms pressed
together hard, saying the words. Wukong behind him, staff still up, one hand
flying to his head.

> **SANZANG:** "Gold, hold fast."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Ow! What's that? What did you do?"

**Why you turn the page:** the monk said the words because he was frightened, and something is closing on the monkey's head.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 19 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the five scripted strings is missing, altered, doubled, reordered, or unreadable from the 600 × 900 proof, or extra text appears; or a balloon cannot be assigned by tail and placement. A string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; a line directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The words do not land
A dramatic page whose dominant moment, found first by the eye, is the frightened monk, eyes shut, palms pressed hard together, saying the words, with Wukong's hand flying to his head behind him. Blocking if the staff swing owns the page instead of the monk's fear.

### C3 — Attribution
The promise request and "Gold, hold fast." must hang from the monk's mouth; "Promise that?…", "This keeps you alive…", and "Ow! What's that?…" from Wukong's. Each by its own tail, the request read before the answer.

### C4 — Identity and state
Wukong wears the hemp robe and the plain cloth cap. Blocking: no cap on Wukong, a gold band visible on his brow, or the staff not the straight red-and-gold iron staff (the monk's ringed staff confused with it).

### C5 — Focal generation integrity
Blocking: a duplicated named figure, gross anatomy on the monk's pressed palms or on the hand holding the staff, a staff with two holders, or text colliding with a face. Background is nonblocking.
