## Page intent

## PAGE 13

A dramatic page on the border road. Sanzang throws himself across the fallen robber under the raised staff, and Wukong shouts at him to move. The dominant turn is the quarrel: the monkey lowers the staff, furious, while the robbers run; the monk, kneeling and shaking, tells him he was going to break a man's head, hears that the road is eat or be eaten, and answers that he cannot walk with a robber. Close on Wukong's face, the hurt shows under the anger: he waited five hundred years for this man. Then the road is empty, a gold streak shoots into the sky, and the monk stands alone by the horse. The reader turns because the monk is now alone on a road that eats monks.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the border road, among boulders.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Sanzang throwing himself across the fallen robber, arms
out, the staff above them both.

> **SANZANG:** "No! Stop! You'll kill him!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "That's the idea! Move, Master!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Wukong lowering the staff, furious, the robbers
running off behind him. Sanzang kneeling up, shaking, facing him.

> **SANZANG:** "He's a man! You could have frightened him off. You were going to break his head!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "He had a chopper for your head! On this road it's eat or be eaten."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Then you're no better than he is. I can't walk with a robber."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Close on Wukong's face: the hurt under the anger.

> **WUKONG:** "I waited under a rock for five hundred years for you. Fine. Walk to the west by yourself."

**Panel 4 — 10%.** The empty road. A gold streak shooting up into the sky. The
monk alone by the horse.

**Why you turn the page:** he has left, and the monk is alone on a road that eats monks.

## Page 13 — card

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — the turn lands: the quarrel and the leaving
The eye must find the face-off of the kneeling monk and the furious monkey as the page's centre, then the hurt close-up, then the empty road. Blocking if the monk's body across the robber or the final empty road with the gold streak is missing, or the quarrel does not read. Exact proportion is nonblocking.

### C3 — attribution in the quarrel
"No! Stop!" is Sanzang's and "That's the idea!" is Wukong's; in the face-off, the "He's a man!" line and "I can't walk with a robber" line are Sanzang's, the chopper line between them is Wukong's, and each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his. Blocking if either of the monk's face-off lines reads as Wukong's or the reply reads before its prompt. The empty-road panel must carry no balloon.

### C4 — identity and state
Wukong is the small golden monkey in the mended hemp robe, with no band and no cap; a band or cap is blocking. Sanzang is the shaven young monk in saffron and red. Two Wukongs in one panel is blocking. The robbers read as ordinary beardless working men in cloth head-wraps and rough brown and gray, the same men across pages 11 to 13, the leader the tallest with the chopper; any robber reading as a monk (shaven head, robe) or as a man with a long gray moustache is blocking. The exact robber count in a melee and the details of their weapons are nonblocking.

### C5 — focal integrity
Faces of both speakers and Wukong's grip on the staff are whole and correct; malformed focal hands are blocking. Fleeing robbers' anatomy is nonblocking.
