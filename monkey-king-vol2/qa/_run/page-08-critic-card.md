## Page intent

## PAGE 8

A dramatic page. The monkey lands in the dust in front of the sprawled monk, who begs not to be eaten; the monkey is offended: he is the bodyguard. Then the quiet heart of the page: the Monkey King kneeling low in the dust before the astonished monk, naming himself with all his grand titles and thanking him for climbing, and the monk giving his own plain name and his errand. Last, the monkey springs up to look west and offers to somersault the monk there by supper; the monk refuses, because every mile must be his own, on foot, and the monkey grumbles that the lady said so too. The dominant turn is the kneeling, "Master", and "every step". The bow is the page's largest image. The reader turns because the fastest creature in the world has to walk.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 8 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The bow does not land
One panel owns the page by eye: the monkey kneeling low in the dust before the astonished monk. If the landing or the springing-up panel takes the eye instead, or the page is not a dramatic panel page, it is blocking.

### C3 — Attribution across the three exchanges
The monk owns "Please don't eat me.", "I'm Sanzang…" and "No. Every mile…"; the monkey owns the bodyguard line, the titles, the somersault offer, and "The lady said that too." Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth; a line under the wrong mouth, or a reply read before its prompt, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and costume
The monkey is gold, small, long-tailed, in green-gray armor rags, with no robe, no gold band, no cap. The monk is young, fully shaven, beardless, in saffron and red. Either broken is blocking.

### C5 — The cracked mountain
The mountain behind them is split, not whole, and drawn once. A whole mountain is blocking.
