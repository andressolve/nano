# Exact script — pages 1–10

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Spring. Five bare ridges like the fingers of a
huge hand pressed into the earth, grass and moss grown over their lower slopes,
a small gold paper seal on the highest ridge. At the base, in a cleft of rock,
a monkey's face: golden fur matted with moss, gold eyes with a thin red ring
around each iris, wide open. A small brown young monkey, bare, is climbing down
the slope away from him, looking back. On the rock under the face's chin sits
one fresh peach. Far across a strip of sea, small and green, another mountain.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the sky)*
> "Five hundred years ago, the Monkey King fought all of Heaven and lost. The great hand that beat him became this mountain."

**Why you turn the page:** he has been under there five hundred years, and his eyes are open.

## PAGE 2 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** A white cloud coming down out of a blue sky toward the
cleft. On it, small, a tall woman in white. In the foreground, the monkey's
face in the rock, eyes rolled up toward her, peach stone in his teeth.

> **WUKONG:** "If you've come to stare at me, get in line. Everyone in Heaven already had a turn."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** GUANYIN on her cloud, level with his face, close:
white hood-veil, a willow sprig lifted in her right hand, a white vase in her
left. Calm. The monkey's face on the right, scowling, a head of moss.

> **GUANYIN:** "I'm Guanyin. I haven't come to stare. I've come to ask you for something."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Ask a rock? Lady, I can't even scratch my own nose."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Guanyin bending a little toward him. Wukong's eyes going
narrow, interested.

> **GUANYIN:** "A monk is walking this way from the far east, to fetch the holy books from the far west."
>
> **GUANYIN:** "It's a long road, and it eats monks. Guard him the whole way there, and this mountain will open."

**Why you turn the page:** the way out has a price, and he has not heard all of it yet.

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong's face pushing out of the rock as far as it can go,
eager. Guanyin on the left on her cloud.

> **WUKONG:** "Yes! I'll do it! My monkeys swim the sea every spring to bring their king a peach. Kings get paid."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Five hundred years of it. Get me out before they run out of grandchildren!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Close on both: Guanyin left, still; Wukong right,
grinning, very pleased with himself.

> **GUANYIN:** "When he comes, he must peel off the gold seal himself."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Then I'll put him in the west by lunchtime. One somersault on my cloud."
>
> **GUANYIN:** "No. He goes on the ground, the whole road, every mile. If you fly him there, it's your journey, not his."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Wukong's face falling. Guanyin rising away on her cloud,
looking back down at him.

> **WUKONG:** "Walk? Do you know how slow people are?"
>
> **GUANYIN:** "I do. And you won't guard him alone. I've asked others to wait for him too."

**Why you turn the page:** a monk is coming, and somebody else is waiting for him.

## PAGE 4 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the border road.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Dawn on a dirt road going west into dry
mountains, a lone white milestone beside it. A young shaven-headed monk in a
saffron robe and red kasaya, a ringed staff in his hand, leading a plain bay
horse west. Far behind him, small, two figures walking back east the way they
came.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the sky above the mountains, never over the monk)*
> "Far to the east, long before, a basket had come down a river with a baby in it. The monks who pulled it out of the reeds raised him and named him Sanzang.
>
> He grew up gentle and stubborn. When the Emperor of Tang wanted someone to walk to the far west and bring back the holy books, Sanzang said he would go.
>
> Two companions and a bay horse went with him. At the edge of the empire, where the road went into the mountains, the two companions looked at it and turned back.
>
> Sanzang was frightened of almost everything. He kept walking."

**Why you turn the page:** the monk who is coming is alone, and afraid, and still walking.

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the foot of the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Sanzang leading the bay horse past the foot of the five
ridges. A balloon bursts out of a cleft in the rock on the right. He jumps,
both hands up.

> **WUKONG:** "MASTER! You're here at last!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Sanzang on the left, bending to peer into the
cleft, palms pressed together under his chin. On the right, filling the cleft,
the monkey's face: moss, gold eyes, a huge grin.

> **SANZANG:** "A monkey? Inside the mountain?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "I've waited five hundred years for a monk going west. You are going west?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "I am. How do you know that?"

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Sanzang on the left, looking up the ridges, very high, the tiny gold seal at
the top. Wukong's balloons rise from the cleft in the rock at the lower right
of the frame, his gold eyes visible in the dark of it.

> **WUKONG:** "A lady in white told me. Climb to the top and peel off the gold paper seal, and I'll guard you all the way."
>
> **SANZANG:** "It's so high. I can't even climb a ladder. And what if you're a demon who eats monks?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Then you'll find out! Climb anyway!"

**Why you turn the page:** he is going to climb, and he is terrified of heights.

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the ridges of the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Sanzang clinging to a ridge halfway up, robe snagged on a
rock, the horse tiny far below. A balloon from the cleft at the bottom of the
frame.

> **WUKONG:** "Left foot! No, your other left! And don't look down!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "I looked down."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The summit, wind. Sanzang on his knees, alone
against a big sky, his hand flat on the small gold paper seal. Eyes shut.

> **SANZANG:** "If he is a demon, let this seal stay where it is. If not, let him out to walk with me."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The seal peeling away in his fingers and lifting on the
wind. A balloon rising from the bottom of the frame.

> **WUKONG:** "It's coming! Now run down, Master, fast! I've waited five hundred years. I can wait till you're at the bottom!"

**Why you turn the page:** the seal is off, and the mountain is about to let go.

## PAGE 7 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The five ridges splitting apart with a roar, rock
and dust bursting up into the sky, and out of the middle, rising on the blast,
a small gold monkey in the dull green rags of old armor, arms flung wide,
tail out, laughing at the sky. In the lower left foreground, far off down the
slope, the tiny monk flat on his back beside the horse. The gold seal fluttering
away high on the wind.

> **SOUND** *(across the split rock)*
> "BOOM"

**Why you turn the page:** he is out, and the monk is on his back looking at him.

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the foot of the cracked mountain.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Wukong landing in the dust in front of the sprawled monk.
Sanzang scrambling backward on his elbows.

> **SANZANG:** "Please don't eat me."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Eat you? Master, I'm your bodyguard!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Wukong kneeling, forehead to the ground, in front
of the astonished monk. The cracked mountain behind them.

> **WUKONG:** "Sun Wukong. The Great Sage Equal to Heaven. Thank you for climbing."
>
> **SANZANG:** "I'm Sanzang. I'm walking to the far west, to bring the holy books home."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Wukong springing up, a hand shading his eyes to look west,
the monk getting to his feet behind him.

> **WUKONG:** "The far west! Climb on my back, and I'll somersault you there by supper."
>
> **SANZANG:** "No. Every mile of the road must be mine, on the ground. That's what a pilgrimage is."
>
> **WUKONG:** "The lady said that too. I was hoping you'd be easier."

**Why you turn the page:** the fastest creature in the world has to walk.

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a camp by the road at dusk.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** A small fire. Wukong in his rags, pieces of old armor
falling off him. Sanzang on the left, pulling a folded hemp robe out of his
bundle.

> **SANZANG:** "You can't walk to the west in Heaven's armor. It's falling off you. Here, wear my spare robe."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 55%.** Sanzang on the left, sewing up a tear at the left
shoulder of the plain robe with needle and thread while Wukong, on the right,
stands in it holding his arms stiffly out to the sides, not knowing what to do
with his hands.

> **WUKONG:** "What do I owe you for it?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Nothing. It's a robe."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Everything I've ever had, I took. My iron staff, the armor. Even the peaches are tribute. Kings get paid."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Nobody ever gave me anything."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Then hold still. This is the first, and I haven't finished sewing it."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Later. Wukong alone by the fire in the robe, the monk
asleep beyond him, touching the mended patch at his shoulder with two fingers.

> **WUKONG:** "It's a good robe."

**Why you turn the page:** somebody gave him something, and in the morning they have to walk.

## PAGE 10 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the border road.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A long road through hills under a wide sky. Near
the bottom, the small monk leading the bay horse. Far ahead on a distant
hilltop, a tiny figure in a hemp robe, a hand shading his eyes, and a gold blur
racing back down the road toward the monk.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky, never over the figures)*
> "The monk walked as fast as a man walks, which is hardly at all. Wukong ran ahead to the next hill and back, and to the next river and back, and by noon he had walked the whole day's road ten times.
>
> 'Is it always this slow?' he asked. 'Yes,' said Sanzang. 'Every day?' 'Every day.'
>
> Once, from the top of the first hill, he looked back east at a green mountain far across the sea, where his monkeys were. Then he turned west.
>
> At night the monk said his prayers, and the monkey lay on his back, which he had not been able to do for five hundred years, and looked at the stars."

**Why you turn the page:** the road is slow and empty, until it isn't.
