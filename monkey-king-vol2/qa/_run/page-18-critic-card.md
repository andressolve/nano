## Page intent

## PAGE 18

A dramatic page on the border road, at a resting place. The monk holds out a plain cloth cap and calls it something to go with the robe, and Wukong, who has never been given anything he did not take, is delighted: he pulls it on with both hands and poses for the monk. The dominant turn is the cap: a gift, and he puts it on. The page belongs to Wukong's happiness and to the monk's face, which does not smile; the reader sees the thin gold glint inside the brim and knows more than the monkey does. He strides ahead promising to be good all week, and the reader turns because he has put it on gladly and nobody has told him what it is. The road and the resting place stay plain and subordinate; the cap and the two faces carry everything.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 18 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the border road, at a resting place.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Sanzang on the left holding out the plain cloth cap.
Wukong turning, surprised.

> **SANZANG:** "I have something for you, to go with the robe."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Another present? For me?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong pulling the cap on with both hands,
delighted, posing for the monk. Inside the brim, the thin gold band, glinting.
Sanzang on the left, palms pressed together, not smiling.

> **WUKONG:** "I had a cap in Heaven with long feathers. Nobody gave me that one either. I took it."
>
> **SANZANG:** "It suits you."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong striding ahead in the cap, looking back, grinning.
The monk behind, eyes down.

> **WUKONG:** "Come on, Master! I'll be good all day today. Maybe all week."

**Why you turn the page:** he has put it on, gladly, and the monk has not told him what it is.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 18 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the five scripted strings is missing, altered, doubled, reordered, or unreadable from the 600 × 900 proof, or extra text appears; or a balloon cannot be assigned to the monk or to Wukong by tail and placement. A string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; a line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The gift does not land
The page must read as a dramatic page whose one dominant moment, found first by the eye, is Wukong pulling the cap on, delighted, while the monk does not smile. Blocking if the eye lands elsewhere or the monk's unsmiling face is lost.

### C3 — Attribution in the exchanges
"Another present? For me?" must hang from Wukong's mouth after the monk's offer; "I had a cap in Heaven…" from Wukong and "It suits you." from the monk, read in that order. Each by its own speaker's tail, not merely by sense.

### C4 — Identity and cap state
Blocking: a gold band visible on Wukong's brow outside the cap, a second cap, or the cap drawn as feathered or ornamented. A glint of gold inside the brim is required by the story and correct.

### C5 — Focal generation integrity
Blocking: a duplicated named figure, gross anatomy on Wukong's two hands at the cap or the monk's pressed palms, or text colliding with a face. Background detail is nonblocking.
