# Exact script — pages 31–40

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 31 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the back house in Old Gao's courtyard.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong snapping the lock off the outside of a small door. A
young woman in a pale green jacket running out past him into Old Gao's arms.

> **WUKONG:** "Go to your father. Your husband has a surprise coming."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Inside the back house: Wukong in the middle of
changing, half monkey, half the young woman, the gold band still on the brow.
Sanzang in the doorway on the left, worried.

> **SANZANG:** "Wukong, you won't hurt him?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Hurt him? Master, I'm going to marry him."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The bride sitting primly on the curtained bed in the pale
green jacket, hands folded. Only the thin gold band at the hairline is wrong.

> **WUKONG AS BRIDE:** "I'll only scare him. Probably. Now go and hide."

**Why you turn the page:** the bride is waiting in the dark for a pig.

---

## PAGE 32 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the back house at night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Indigo dark. The window flung open by a gust of
wind, and filling it, lit by the lantern in his own hand, a huge figure with a
pig's head and floppy ears, a nine-toothed rake on his shoulder and a red sash
across his belly, squeezing in. On the bed, the bride, her back to him.

> **PIG:** "Darling! I'm home!"

**Why you turn the page:** the pig is in the room, and his bride is not his bride.

---

## PAGE 33 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the back house at night.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The pig on the left, sitting on the bed, which sags; the
bride on the right, turned away.

> **PIG:** "Why so cold tonight, my little dumpling? I brought you a peach. I ate most of it on the way."
>
> **WUKONG AS BRIDE:** "My father says you're ugly."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The pig, wounded, a hand on his chest.

> **PIG:** "Ugly? I was a marshal in Heaven once! They threw me out for being drunk at a party, and gave me this face."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 40%.** The bride turning, sly. The pig frozen, ears
standing straight up.

> **WUKONG AS BRIDE:** "Father has hired someone to catch you. The Great Sage Equal to Heaven."
>
> **PIG:** "The Monkey King? The one who smashed up Heaven? He's under a mountain!"
>
> **WUKONG AS BRIDE:** "Not anymore."

**Why you turn the page:** the pig is terrified of a name, and the name is sitting next to him.

---

## PAGE 34 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the back house at night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The bride's head turned fully round: in the pale
green jacket, the grinning monkey face, gold eyes, the gold band. The pig
shrieking, ears flying, bursting straight up through the roof in a storm of
broken tiles, stars above.

> **WUKONG:** "Hello, dumpling!"
>
> **PIG:** "AAAAH!"

**Why you turn the page:** the pig is through the roof, and the monkey is after him.

---

## PAGE 35 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the moonlit hills, at the mouth of the pig's cave.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Moonlight. The pig running with his rake, a broken roof
tile stuck in his hair; Wukong behind him on a cloud, staff swinging. Staff
and rake clashing.

> **PIG:** "Leave me alone! I worked hard! I ploughed every field in that valley!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "And then you ate everything that grew in them!"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The pig on the left, backed against a crooked stone door, rake up. Wukong on
the right, landing off his cloud, staff on his shoulder.

> **PIG:** "Who sent you, monkey? Heaven? Old Gao?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Old Gao asked me. But I'm here for a monk on his way to the west."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** The pig dropping the rake on the doorstep, ears
lifting, face lighting up in wonder.

> **PIG:** "A monk? Going west for the holy books? The pilgrim?"
>
> **PIG:** "A lady in white told me to wait for him! I was supposed to wait! I forgot! I got married!"

**Why you turn the page:** the pig was waiting for the monk too, and the monk is back at the farm.

## PAGE 36 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Old Gao's courtyard at dawn.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The pig on his knees before the monk, forehead in the
dust. Old Gao and his daughter at the gate behind them. Wukong leaning on the
staff.

> **PIG:** "Master! I'm yours! I'll carry your luggage, I'll guard your camp, I'll eat almost nothing!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Wukong on the right, stepping in between and
jabbing a finger at the kneeling pig. Sanzang on the left, palms together,
calm.

> **WUKONG:** "Master, he locked a girl in a shed, and you're going to take him with us?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "I took you, didn't I? And I gave you a robe."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Close: Wukong gone quiet, two fingers touching the mended
patch at the left shoulder of his robe, looking away.

> **WUKONG:** "That was different. I'm better-looking."

**Panel 4 — 25%.** The pig turning on his knees toward the daughter at the
gate, ears down, ashamed.

> **PIG:** "I'm sorry I locked the door. I was afraid you'd run away when you saw my real face."

**Why you turn the page:** he has said sorry, and she has not answered yet.

## PAGE 37 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the gate of Old Gao's farmhouse.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Sanzang on the left, palms together, the pig on the right,
still kneeling, looking up.

> **SANZANG:** "Will you behave yourself on the road?"
>
> **PIG:** "I'll try very hard."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Then I'll call you Bajie. It means 'eight rules'. You'll need every one of them."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Bajie on his feet with the luggage loaded on a carrying
pole over his shoulder, turning back to Old Gao at the gate.

> **BAJIE:** "Eight? Couldn't it be two? Father-in-law, keep my bed made. If this doesn't work out, I'll be back."
>
> **OLD GAO:** "Please don't."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** The daughter at the gate, looking at Bajie for a
long moment, then swinging the gate shut on him herself. Bajie's ears
drooping. Wukong beside him, grinning, the monk on the white horse beyond.

> **WUKONG:** "That's her answer, Pig. Pick up your feet."

**Why you turn the page:** there are three of them now and a horse, and the road goes on.

# MOVEMENT VII — THE RIVER OF FLOWING SAND · pp 38–48

---

## PAGE 38 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the road west.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A dusty road across a yellowing plain. The monk
on the white horse; Bajie trudging behind with the luggage pole, mouth open,
complaining; Wukong walking backwards in front of him, pointing.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the plain)*
> "Bajie complained about the road, the weather, the hills, the luggage, and his feet, in that order, every day. He ate whatever the villages gave them and then asked what was for dinner. He snored like a landslide.
>
> And Wukong, who had once been the worst-behaved creature in the universe, found that he was now the one telling somebody else to hurry up, stop eating, and keep his hands off the horse. When Bajie heard the horse had once been a dragon, he never stopped asking it to talk.
>
> 'Big brother,' Bajie called him, to annoy him. It did not annoy him as much as it should have."

**Why you turn the page:** the road ends at the edge of a river.

---

## PAGE 39 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A river so wide its far side is lost in haze,
the colour of sand, flat and heavy, no boats, no waves. Gray reeds on the near
bank. In the foreground, a tall stone tablet. Beside it, tiny, the monk on the
white horse, the pig, and the monkey, staring.

> **PRINTED TABLET** *(carved in the stone, three lines)*
> "FLOWING SAND RIVER"
> "EIGHT HUNDRED MILES"
> "NO FEATHER FLOATS"

**Why you turn the page:** there is no bridge and no boat, and the monk cannot fly.

---

## PAGE 40 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Bajie on the left, by the tablet, staring out at the
river. Sanzang on the white horse beside him, turning to Wukong.

> **BAJIE:** "Eight hundred miles. No boat. No bridge. Not one noodle stall."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Wukong, you can fly. Fly me across on your cloud. Just this once."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong on the right, close, not joking for once, the gold band on his brow,
the sand river behind him. Sanzang on the left on the white horse, looking
down at him.

> **WUKONG:** "I can't, Master. I could fly a whole mountain over this river. But I can't fly you over. Not one mile of your road."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Why not?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Because it's your road. If I fly you over, you never crossed it."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Sanzang on the left, looking out at the river, frightened,
the white horse under him shying back from the sand-water. Bajie on the right,
pointing at the horse.

> **SANZANG:** "That's what I told you, the first day. Then I have to cross it myself, and I can't swim."
>
> **BAJIE:** "Your horse used to be a dragon! Ride him over!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Look at him. He won't put a hoof in it."

**Why you turn the page:** the river is very wide, and something under it has been listening.
