## Page intent

## PAGE 5

A dramatic page. The lone monk, leading his bay horse past the foot of five bare ridges, is startled out of his skin by a voice from a crack in the rock: the monkey, buried to the face, has been waiting for him. The page belongs to the meeting of two faces, the frightened young monk bending to peer in and the moss-matted monkey grinning out of the stone. Its one dominant turn is the voice and the face, and the request: climb up and peel off the seal. The monk asks the sensible questions, including whether this is a demon who eats monks; the monkey has no reassurance to give except "climb anyway". What stays subordinate: the horse, the far sea, the tiny gold seal at the very top, which must be seen as high. The reader turns because this terrified man is going to climb.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the foot of the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Sanzang leading the bay horse past the foot of the five
ridges. A balloon bursts out of a cleft in the rock on the right. He jumps,
both hands up.

> **WUKONG:** "MASTER! You're here at last!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Sanzang on the left, bending to peer into the
cleft, palms pressed together under his chin. On the right, filling the cleft,
the monkey's face: moss, gold eyes, a huge grin.

> **SANZANG:** "A monkey? Inside the mountain?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "I've waited five hundred years for a monk going west. You are going west?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "I am. How do you know that?"

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Sanzang on the left, looking up the ridges, very high, the tiny gold seal at
the top. Wukong's balloons rise from the cleft in the rock at the lower right
of the frame, his gold eyes visible in the dark of it.

> **WUKONG:** "A lady in white told me. Climb to the top and peel off the gold paper seal, and I'll guard you all the way."
>
> **SANZANG:** "It's so high. I can't even climb a ladder. And what if you're a demon who eats monks?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Then you'll find out! Climb anyway!"

**Why you turn the page:** he is going to climb, and he is terrified of heights.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 5 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The meeting does not land
One panel owns the page by eye: the monk bending to the crack and the grinning monkey face in the rock, face to face. If the eye lands first on the horse or the landscape, or the page reads as anything but a dramatic panel page, it is blocking.

### C3 — Attribution across the two exchanges
"MASTER!", the "five hundred years" line, the "lady in white" line and "Climb anyway!" hang from the face in the rock; "A monkey?", "I am." and "It's so high" hang from the monk. A monkey line hanging from the monk, or a reply readable before the line it answers, is blocking.

### C4 — The face in the rock
Only the monkey's face shows in the cleft: golden fur, pale muzzle, round gold eyes, moss. A gold band, a body standing free, or a human face is blocking.

### C5 — The mountain and the seal
The mountain is whole and drawn once; the small gold seal is visible far up at the top. A split mountain, a second mountain, or no seal anywhere is blocking; the seal's exact size or shape is not.

### C6 — The monk himself
A young, fully shaven, beardless monk in saffron and red. Hair, a beard, white robes, or a second monk is blocking.
