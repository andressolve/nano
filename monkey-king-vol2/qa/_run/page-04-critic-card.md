## Page intent

## PAGE 4

Illustrated prose. The border road at dawn: a dirt road running west into dry mountains, a lone white milestone beside it, and a young shaven-headed monk in a saffron robe and red kasaya, ringed staff in hand, leading a plain bay horse west. Far behind him, small, two figures walk back east. The prose tells who he is: a baby in a basket on a river, raised by monks and named Sanzang, gentle and stubborn, who said he would walk to the far west for the holy books, and whose two companions turned back at the edge of the empire. He is frightened of almost everything, and he keeps walking. The dominant turn: the monk who is coming is alone. The reader turns because he is afraid and still walking. The two turning figures stay small and far; the road and the lone monk own the page.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 4 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, as one prose field, with no other text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. Legible writing on the milestone is extra text and blocking.

### C2 — The lone monk does not land
One image owns the page as illustrated prose: a young monk small against a long road into dry mountains, walking west with his horse, alone. If he reads as one of a group, or the page reads as a panel page, it is blocking.

### C3 — Prose field over the figure
The prose field sits over sky above the mountains. A field covering the monk's face, his hands or the horse is blocking; one crossing distant ridges is not.

### C4 — The monk not himself
A young beardless man with a fully shaven head, saffron robe and red kasaya, ringed wooden staff, leading a plain brown horse. A white robe, veil, hair, a white or blue-maned horse, or a second monk is blocking.

### C5 — The companions turned back
Two small figures far behind walk away east. If they are missing, or walk beside him so he is no longer alone, it is blocking; their faces and clothes are nonblocking.
