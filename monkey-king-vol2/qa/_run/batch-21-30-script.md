# Exact script — pages 21–30

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 21 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the camp by the road at night.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong on his knees, panting, pulling at the gold band with
both hands. It does not move.

> **WUKONG:** "It won't come off. It's grown into my head!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Wukong springing up with the staff raised over the
monk. Sanzang on the left, terrified, standing his ground, palms together.

> **WUKONG:** "You tricked me! You gave me a present, and it was a trap!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Gold, hold fast."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Wukong down on his knees again, the staff dropped. Sanzang
stopping, shaking.

> **WUKONG:** "Stop! I've stopped! I put it down!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "I'm sorry. I'm sorry!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Who taught you those words?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "The lady in white. Guanyin."

**Why you turn the page:** he knows whose band it is, and he is going to find her.

## PAGE 22 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the South Sea, above Guanyin's island.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A rocky island with a still bamboo grove in a pale jade
sea. Wukong landing on a rock below, pointing up at Guanyin on her cloud.

> **WUKONG:** "You tricked me! You let me out from under one mountain and put another one on my head!"
>
> **GUANYIN:** "Yes."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Guanyin calm on the left; Wukong on the right,
jabbing at the gold band on his brow.

> **GUANYIN:** "You were about to break a man's head, and the only way your monk could stop you was to lie down under your staff."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I went back to him, on my own, before your hat!"
>
> **GUANYIN:** "I know you did. But now he can stop you with three words, before you do a thing that can't be undone."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong turning away. Guanyin watching him go.

> **WUKONG:** "Then take it off."
>
> **GUANYIN:** "When you reach the west, it will come off by itself. Go back to him. He's frightened, and he's alone."

**Why you turn the page:** the band stays, and the monk is alone in the dark.

## PAGE 23 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the camp by the road at night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The fire low. The monk on the left, knees drawn
up, face wet. Wukong on the right, just landed, the gold band catching the
firelight. Both awake.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dark sky above the fire, never over either face)*
> "The monk was still awake when Wukong came down.
>
> 'I'm sorry,' said Sanzang. 'I said the words because I was frightened of you, not because you had done anything. And I called the cap a present when I knew what it was. That was worse.'
>
> Nobody in Heaven had ever told him they were sorry. Wukong did not know where to look.
>
> 'Everybody's frightened of me,' he said. 'That's the point of me.'
>
> 'Then I'll try not to be,' said the monk. 'And I'll only say the words if I must.'
>
> 'You'll have to say them a lot,' said Wukong, and sat down on the other side of the fire."

**Why you turn the page:** they are still together, and the road goes on into the mountains.

# MOVEMENT V — THE DRAGON IN THE STREAM · pp 24–29

---

## PAGE 24 · *spectacle*

**Location:** Eagle Grief Stream.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A deep green mountain stream in a gorge of gray
cliffs. Bursting up out of the water in a column of white foam, a long slim
white dragon, jaws closing over the bay horse, saddle and all. On the bank,
Wukong leaping back with the monk tucked under one arm. On the water, the
empty bridle.

> **SOUND** *(beside the dragon's jaws)*
> "GULP"

**Why you turn the page:** the monk's horse is gone, and the dragon is still in the water.

---

## PAGE 25 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Eagle Grief Stream.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Sanzang on the left on the bank, pointing at the empty
bridle floating away. Wukong on the right, furious, the staff already in his
hand.

> **SANZANG:** "My horse! It ate my horse, saddle and all!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Stand back, Master. Nobody eats your horse while I'm here."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Underwater, green and white: Wukong swinging the
staff at the coiling white dragon, bubbles everywhere.

> **WUKONG:** "Come out and fight, you overgrown eel!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong bursting out of the water on the right, spitting, the dragon's tail
vanishing into a dark hole under the bank behind him. Sanzang on the bank on
the left, calling down.

> **WUKONG:** "It's hiding in a hole. I can't swing a staff in a hole!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Then leave it be, and come out."
>
> **WUKONG:** "It ATE your HORSE!"

**Why you turn the page:** he can't get at it, and he is not going to let it go.

## PAGE 26 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Eagle Grief Stream.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong standing on a rock at the edge of the water, hands
cupped round his mouth, yelling down at the stream. The monk behind, hands
over his ears.

> **WUKONG:** "Hey, worm! Your mother was a tadpole, and your father's a puddle!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The white dragon erupting out of the stream in
fury, jaws wide. Wukong leaping to meet it, staff up.

> **DRAGON:** "Don't you DARE talk about my father!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong on the bank, the dragon's neck pinned flat under the
staff, its long body thrashing in the water.

> **WUKONG:** "Got you. Now we talk."

**Why you turn the page:** the dragon is pinned, and it has something to say about its father.

## PAGE 27 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Eagle Grief Stream.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Close: the dragon's young face under the staff, sea-green
eyes wide. Wukong leaning on the staff on the right.

> **DRAGON:** "Let me go! I'm not just an eel! A lady in white told me to wait here for a pilgrim going west!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "A pilgrim? A monk, going west?"
>
> **DRAGON:** "Yes!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Wukong pointing back up the bank at Sanzang, who
gives a small, weak wave. The dragon's eyes following.

> **WUKONG:** "THAT monk. And you ATE his HORSE."
>
> **DRAGON:** "Oh no."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The dragon on the left, miserable, chin in the water. Wukong on the right,
leaning on the staff, unimpressed.

> **DRAGON:** "I was hungry. I've been in this stream for years and years, and nobody told me the pilgrim would have a horse."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Years? Try five hundred, under a rock. Nobody brought me a horse."

**Why you turn the page:** the monster was waiting for the monk, and ate his horse first.

## PAGE 28 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Eagle Grief Stream.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Wukong lifting the staff off; the dragon's head rising,
ashamed.

> **WUKONG:** "Who put you in a stream, worm?"
>
> **DRAGON:** "My father, the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea. I set fire to his best pearl. By accident, mostly."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Ao Guang? I had tea with him last week."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Wukong on the left, arms folded; the dragon on the right, looking up the
bank at the monk.

> **WUKONG:** "So you ate the horse. What are you going to do about it?"
>
> **DRAGON:** "I'm sorry I ate your horse, Master. Let me be your horse instead. The lady said I was to carry you."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** One creature changing in a spiral of white foam:
the dragon's long white coils unwinding and folding into a slim white horse's
body standing in the shallows, the pale-blue mane and the sea-green eyes the
same, the bay horse's saddle on its back. No whole dragon beside it: the last
of the tail is becoming the horse's tail. Sanzang on the left on the bank,
reaching out a hand to its nose. Wukong on the right, staff on his shoulder,
scowling at the horse.

> **SANZANG:** "Thank you."
>
> **WUKONG:** "He eats your horse, then he turns into one, saddle and all, and he gets a thank-you?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Thank you too, Wukong. For coming back to me."

**Why you turn the page:** the monk has a horse again, it is somebody's son, and the monkey has been thanked.

## PAGE 29 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the hills above the gorge.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Autumn hills, red trees along the road. The monk
riding the white horse, Wukong walking beside them, staff on his shoulder. The
horse looking back over its shoulder at a stream far below.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the hills)*
> "The white horse never spoke again. It carried the monk up out of the gorge and over the hills, and it never stumbled, and it never ate anything that was not grass.
>
> Summer turned to autumn. Sometimes, crossing a stream, the horse would stop and look down into the water for a long moment, and the monk would let it.
>
> 'Do you think his father knows?' Sanzang asked.
>
> 'I'll tell him,' said Wukong, 'next time I need tea.' Then he walked ahead for the rest of that afternoon, so the monk would not see his face."

**Why you turn the page:** the road comes down into a valley where every door is shut.

# MOVEMENT VI — THE BRIDE OF GAO VILLAGE · pp 30–37

---

## PAGE 30 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Old Gao's farmhouse in Gao Village.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Dusk. A lane of shuttered doors, and at the end a gate with a bar across it. Old Gao peering out
through a crack, holding up a lantern, at the monk on the white horse and
Wukong.

> **OLD GAO:** "Go away! This is Gao's farm, and we don't open the gate after dark! We've got trouble here!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "What kind of trouble? I catch demons."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Old Gao's front room, lamps lit, a feast of
noodles on the table. Old Gao wringing his hands on the left; the monk and
Wukong on the right.

> **OLD GAO:** "Three years ago a strong young man came and asked to marry my youngest daughter. He ploughed like ten oxen, so we said yes."
>
> **OLD GAO:** "Then his face changed. Ears. A snout. Now he's a pig with a rake, and he eats a cartload of rice a day!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Old Gao, close, desperate.

> **OLD GAO:** "We told him to go. He locked my daughter in the back house, and he comes back to her every night."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Every night? Good. Tonight he'll find me there."

**Why you turn the page:** there is a pig coming tonight, and Wukong has a plan.
