## PAGE 41 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The flat sand-coloured water exploding upward: a
gaunt blue-gray ogre with a wild red beard, a necklace of nine pale skulls,
a crescent-bladed spade swinging down at the monk on his horse. Bajie throwing
himself in the way, rake up, blocking the blade. Wukong on the far side, staff
coming out.

> **SOUND** *(where the blade hits the rake)*
> "CLANG"

**Why you turn the page:** the monster wants the monk, and the pig is in the way.

## PAGE 42 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** On the bank, Wukong on the left, hopping with the staff,
unable to reach; Sanzang on the right beside the white horse. Out on the
sand-coloured water, nothing but a slow swirl where the pig and the ogre have
gone under, and the tip of a rake.

> **WUKONG:** "Bring him up, Bajie! It's sand, not water. I can't see a thing in it!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "You fought the dragon under the water!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "That was water! This is soup!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Under the sandy water, murky gold: Bajie's rake
against the ogre's spade, the nine skulls swinging on their cord, Bajie
fighting well.

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Bajie flopped on the bank, exhausted, the river flat and
empty again. The monk and Wukong standing over him.

> **BAJIE:** "He won't come up. Every time I get him near the bank, he dives again."
>
> **SANZANG:** "He's hiding from something. Nothing that big hides unless it's frightened."

**Why you turn the page:** they are stuck, and the pig is thinking.

## PAGE 43 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Bajie on the left, sitting up, scratching under one big ear, an idea
arriving. Wukong on the right, leaning on the staff.

> **BAJIE:** "Wait. The lady in white told me to wait for the pilgrim. Big brother, did she tell you too?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Me, and the horse. Don't look so pleased, Pig."
>
> **BAJIE:** "Then what if she told the river monster to wait for the pilgrim too?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Bajie on his feet at the edge of the flat river,
hands cupped, bellowing. Wukong and the monk behind him, hands over their ears.

> **BAJIE:** "HEY! YOU IN THE RIVER! THE PILGRIM IS HERE! THE MONK GOING WEST! HE'S RIGHT HERE!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The flat water. Slowly rising out of it, a red-bearded head,
eyes on the monk.

> **OGRE:** "The pilgrim?"

**Why you turn the page:** the monster has come up, and it is looking at the monk.

## PAGE 44 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The ogre standing in the shallows on the right, spade
lowered, facing the monk on the left. Bajie hiding behind the horse.

> **OGRE:** "Are you the tenth one?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "The tenth what?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The ogre, close, one hand closed over a skull on
his necklace. The monk's face beyond his shoulder.

> **OGRE:** "Nine monks came to this river before you, going west. I was hungry, and I was a monster, and I ate them."
>
> **OGRE:** "Their skulls wouldn't sink, so I wear them."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The ogre on the left, still talking, looking at the monk.
Sanzang on the right, frightened, standing his ground.

> **OGRE:** "Then the lady in white came and told me: wait for the tenth one."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Then why did you swing at me?"
>
> **OGRE:** "Under the sand I hear feet, not faces, and I swing at the first one."
>
> **OGRE:** "Up here I saw the Monkey King, and I thought he'd come for me."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Close: Wukong, his hand tight on the staff, speaking low to
the monk without taking his eyes off the ogre.

> **WUKONG:** "Master. Say the word and he goes back in the river. Your choice, not mine."

**Why you turn the page:** the monster has told the monk the worst thing, and the monk has to answer.

## PAGE 45 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Sanzang getting down off the white horse and stepping
toward the ogre, alone, frightened, one hand out.

> **SANZANG:** "You told me the worst thing about yourself first. Nobody made you."
>
> **OGRE:** "Will you take me, knowing it?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The ogre on his knees in the shallows, head
bowed; Sanzang standing over him, palms together.

> **SANZANG:** "I will. I'll call you Wujing. You'll walk right behind me, at my back, every day. And I won't be afraid of you."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wujing, the cord of nine skulls off his neck, kneeling to
lay the first skull on the sand-water, where it glows pale gold as it touches.
Sanzang beside him, palms together, head bowed.

> **WUJING:** "The lady said one more thing. One day, what I carry will carry the pilgrim."
>
> **SANZANG:** "For the nine who came before me."

**Why you turn the page:** he has taken off the skulls, and the river is still eight hundred miles wide.

## PAGE 46 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Sunset over the sand-coloured river. The nine
skulls laid on the water in a ring have become a broad raft of pale light.
On it, crossing: the monk on the white horse, Wujing poling with his spade,
Bajie clutching the luggage, Wukong at the front, a hand shading his eyes.

> **CAPTION** *(upper sky)*
> "Nine skulls became a raft that would not sink. It carried the tenth pilgrim across, every mile of it, on the water, and on the far bank it was gone."

**Why you turn the page:** they are across, all of them, and it is night.

## PAGE 47 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a camp on the far bank of the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Night, willows, a fire. Bajie snoring with his head on the
luggage. Wujing sitting up on watch. The white horse grazing. Sanzang on the
left, turning to Wukong on the right.

> **SANZANG:** "Wukong, the day of the robbers, you'd already left me. Why did you come back?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "I told you. A dragon showed me a picture of a shoe."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Close on Wukong in the firelight, the gold band
glinting, looking into the fire.

> **WUKONG:** "An old man kept dropping his shoe off a bridge, and a boy kept going back to fetch it. Three times."
>
> **WUKONG:** "And I thought, if a boy can go back for a shoe, I can go back for a monk."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Sanzang smiling. Wukong scowling at him across the fire.

> **WUKONG:** "Don't be glad. You're slower than a shoe."

**Why you turn the page:** it is morning, and the road west is in front of them.

## PAGE 48 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the road west from the Flowing Sand River.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Morning. A long road going west into blue
mountains. Coming up it, small: the monk on the white horse, Wujing and
Bajie carrying the luggage pole between them. Far ahead, on top of a hill,
Wukong in his hemp robe, gold band on his brow, one hand shading his eyes,
looking back at them.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the mountains)*
> "In the morning they went on. The white horse carried the monk. Wujing carried one end of the luggage pole, and Bajie carried the other and complained about it.
>
> Wukong ran ahead to the top of the next hill, shaded his eyes, and looked west. He could have been there in one somersault. It was very far, and full of things that ate monks, and the gold band was still on his head.
>
> He looked back at his monk, the slowest of them all, coming up the road.
>
> Then he waited."

**Why you turn the page:** there is no next page. The road goes on west, and he is waiting for them.
