## PAGE 1 — Five hundred years

### Reader turn
Under a mountain shaped like a great hand, the Monkey King's face is still awake after five hundred years, and a young monkey has just left him a peach.

### Ordered moments
1. One full-page spectacle image, the whole page. Spring on the Mountain of Five Elements: five bare ridges like the fingers of a huge hand pressed into the earth, grass and moss grown over their lower slopes; on the highest ridge, a small gold paper seal, plain, glinting. At the base, lower centre-right, in a cleft of gray rock, only a monkey's face: golden fur matted with moss, gold eyes with a thin red ring around each iris, wide open. On the rock just under his chin sits one fresh peach, round, pink-blushed, with a green leaf, clearly a peach. On the slope to the left, a small brown young monkey climbs down and away, looking back over its shoulder at the face. Far across a strip of blue sea, small and green, another mountain. The caption sits upper left over open sky, tail-free, never over the seal, the face or the peach.

### Exact strings
CAPTION: `Five hundred years ago, the Monkey King fought all of Heaven and lost. The great hand that beat him became this mountain.`

Render exactly this 1 string once, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page. The seal carries no readable writing.

### Character locks
Wukong, from the attached sheet, face only, framed by stone (under the mountain, pp 1–6): short heart-shaped face, pale hairless muzzle and cheek patches inside golden-tawny fur, wide mouth closed, large round wide-set gold eyes with the thin red ring, low brow, two small upright cupped monkey ears at the sides, golden ruff matted with moss. A monkey, never a human face; ears never long, sharp or elf-like. The sheet's views (a)–(c) show a gold band he does not have yet; draw no band. Silent. The young monkey: small, brown, bare, young, no clothing, no gold; silent.

### Consequential exclusions
No band, no cap, no robe, no body, arms or tail visible: only the face in the cleft. The mountain is whole: the attached setting plate is a reference sheet showing several states side by side; it is never copied as a group. Draw only the whole, unsplit mountain, once. Exactly one peach. No other monkey, no person, no cloud-rider; Guanyin and the monk are absent. No balloons, no sound, no labels.

## PAGE 2 — Someone has come to ask

### Reader turn
Guanyin comes down on her cloud to the monkey in the rock and offers him a way out: guard a monk on the long road west.

### Ordered moments
1. Panel one, about 25%. A white cloud coming down out of a blue sky toward the cleft; on it, small, a tall woman in white, upper left. Foreground right, large, the monkey's face in the rock, eyes rolled up toward her, a peach stone held at the corner of his teeth, mouth visible. His balloon beside his mouth, tail to it.
2. Panel two, the dominant panel, about 50% of the page. Guanyin on her cloud on the LEFT, level with his face, close: white hood-veil, willow sprig lifted in her right hand, white vase in her left, calm. The monkey's face in the rock on the RIGHT, scowling, a head of moss. Her balloon upper left, tail to her mouth; his reply middle right, clearly lower, tail to his mouth.
3. Panel three, about 25%. Guanyin on the left bending a little toward him; his eyes on the right going narrow, interested, mouth closed and silent. Her two balloons stacked upper left and middle left beside her mouth, both tails to her mouth.

### Exact strings
WUKONG: `If you've come to stare at me, get in line. Everyone in Heaven already had a turn.`
GUANYIN: `I'm Guanyin. I haven't come to stare. I've come to ask you for something.`
WUKONG: `Ask a rock? Lady, I can't even scratch my own nose.`
GUANYIN: `A monk is walking this way from the far east, to fetch the holy books from the far west.`
GUANYIN: `It's a long road, and it eats monks. Guard him the whole way there, and this mountain will open.`

Render exactly these 5 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
Wukong, from the attached sheet, face only in the cleft (under the mountain, pp 1–6): short heart-shaped face, pale muzzle and cheek patches, wide mouth, large round wide-set gold eyes with a thin red ring, low brow, small upright cupped ears, golden ruff matted with moss. Never a human face. The sheet's views (a)–(c) show a gold band he does not have yet; draw no band. Guanyin, from her sheet: tall, serene, long oval face, high smooth brow, long-lidded downcast eyes, fine arched brows, small red mouth, red dot between the brows, pale skin; long white hood-veil over black hair dressed high, small gold seated-figure crown at the hood's front; white robes, gold-and-jade necklace; willow sprig in her right hand, white vase in her left; always on a small white cloud. Never a shaven head, saffron, a kasaya, or a staff.

### Consequential exclusions
No band, cap, robe, body or tail on Wukong: only his face. The peach is eaten; only its stone in his teeth in panel one. The mountain is whole: the attached setting plate is a reference sheet showing several states side by side; it is never copied as a group. Draw only the whole, unsplit mountain. The monk is absent. No other figure. The seal carries no readable writing; no sound, no captions.

## PAGE 3 — Every mile on the ground

### Reader turn
Wukong says yes at once, and Guanyin gives him the rule that wipes off his grin: the monk walks, every mile, and Wukong will not be the only guard.

### Ordered moments
1. Panel one, about 25%. Guanyin on the LEFT on her cloud, still and silent. Wukong's face on the right pushing out of the rock as far as it can go, eager, mouth wide. His two balloons stacked upper right and middle right beside his mouth, both tails to his mouth.
2. Panel two, the dominant panel, about 45% of the page. Close on both: Guanyin left, still; Wukong right, grinning, very pleased with himself. Reserve three balloon lanes in the sky before placing the faces. A–B–A: Guanyin's first line upper left, tail to her mouth; Wukong's reply middle right, lower, tail to his mouth; Guanyin's last line lower left, clearly third, tail to her mouth.
3. Panel three, about 30%, two stacked strips. Upper strip: Wukong's face alone in the rock, on the right of the frame, grin falling, mouth visible, his balloon beside his mouth. Lower strip: Guanyin alone on the left, rising away on her cloud and looking back down, mouth visible, her balloon beside her mouth.

### Exact strings
WUKONG: `Yes! I'll do it! My monkeys swim the sea every spring to bring their king a peach. Kings get paid.`
WUKONG: `Five hundred years of it. Get me out before they run out of grandchildren!`
GUANYIN: `When he comes, he must peel off the gold seal himself.`
WUKONG: `Then I'll put him in the west by lunchtime. One somersault on my cloud.`
GUANYIN: `No. He goes on the ground, the whole road, every mile. If you fly him there, it's your journey, not his.`
WUKONG: `Walk? Do you know how slow people are?`
GUANYIN: `I do. And you won't guard him alone. I've asked others to wait for him too.`

Render exactly these 7 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
Wukong, from the attached sheet, face only in the cleft (under the mountain, pp 1–6): short heart-shaped face, pale muzzle and cheek patches, wide expressive mouth, large round wide-set gold eyes with a thin red ring, low brow, small upright cupped ears, golden ruff matted with moss. Never a human face. The sheet's views (a)–(c) show a gold band he does not have yet; draw no band. Guanyin, from her sheet: long oval face, high smooth brow, long-lidded downcast eyes, small red mouth, red dot between the brows; white hood-veil with small gold crown, black hair beneath; white robes, gold-and-jade necklace; willow sprig in right hand, white vase in left; on a small white cloud, never on the ground. Never a shaven head, saffron, or a staff.

### Consequential exclusions
The gold paper seal is a plain blank strip of gold paper: no characters, glyphs, red marks, or writing of any kind on it; writing on the seal is extra text.
No band, cap, robe, body or tail on Wukong: only his face. No peach. No flying cloud for Wukong is drawn; the somersault is only words. The mountain is whole and drawn from words as gray mossy rock and sky; the seal, if glimpsed, carries no readable writing. The monk and the young monkeys are absent. No other figure, no sound, no captions.

## PAGE 4 — The monk who is coming

### Reader turn
The monk who is coming is alone at the edge of the empire, frightened of almost everything, and still walking west.

### Ordered moments
1. One illustrated-prose page whose dominant image is about 65% of the page. Dawn on a dirt road going west into dry ochre mountains, scrub pines and boulders, a lone white milestone beside the road. A young shaven-headed monk in saffron robe and red kasaya walks west toward the right, mid-ground, ringed staff in his right hand, the bay horse's lead rope in his left: exactly two hands, one action, walking. Straw hat on his back, cloth bundle over his shoulder. Far behind him on the road, small, two figures walk back east toward the left.
2. One matte parchment prose field across the sky above the mountains, compose it first, four short paragraphs; never over the monk, his face, his hands, the horse or the milestone.

### Exact strings
PROSE FIELD 1: `Far to the east, long before, a basket had come down a river with a baby in it. The monks who pulled it out of the reeds raised him and named him Sanzang.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `He grew up gentle and stubborn. When the Emperor of Tang wanted someone to walk to the far west and bring back the holy books, Sanzang said he would go.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `Two companions and a bay horse went with him. At the edge of the empire, where the road went into the mountains, the two companions looked at it and turned back.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `Sanzang was frightened of almost everything. He kept walking.`

Render exactly these 4 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page. The four paragraphs are one prose field.

### Character locks
Sanzang, from the attached sheet, its view (d) leading the bay horse: a young man of about twenty-five, slender and upright; long oval beardless face; high round fully shaven head, no hair, no topknot; straight dark brows; dark almond eyes a little wide; small closed mouth; fair skin; saffron ankle-length robe under a red kasaya with a gold-thread grid over the left shoulder; straw sandals; dark wooden staff with a pewter loop of six rings on top. Never a veil, white robes, a willow, a vase or a cloud (Guanyin); never armed. The horse: plain bay, brown with dark mane, never white, never blue-maned. The two companions: tiny, plain gray-brown travelling clothes and hats, faces unreadable, never monks' robes, never saffron.

### Consequential exclusions
Wukong, Guanyin and the mountain are absent. The milestone carries no legible writing. Exactly one monk and one horse; the two companions stay small and far behind. No balloons, captions or sound.
