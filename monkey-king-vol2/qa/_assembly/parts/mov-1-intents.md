## PAGE 1

Spectacle. The volume opens on the Mountain of Five Elements in spring: five bare ridges like the fingers of a huge hand pressed into the earth, moss over their slopes, a small gold paper seal on the highest ridge. At the base, in a cleft of rock, the Monkey King's face, matted with moss, gold eyes with their thin red ring wide open. A small brown young monkey climbs away down the slope, looking back; under the face's chin sits one fresh peach. Far across a strip of sea, small and green, his home. One caption tells the reader why he is there. The dominant turn: five hundred years under a mountain, a face still awake, and a young monkey's peach. The reader turns because his eyes are open. The far mountain and the sea stay small; the face, the peach and the seal carry the page.

## PAGE 2

Dramatic. At the mountain, a white cloud comes down out of a blue sky with a tall woman in white on it, and the monkey's face in the rock rolls its eyes up at her, a peach stone in his teeth, and tells her to get in line. Close and level with his face, calm on her cloud, she names herself, Guanyin, and says she has come to ask him for something; he scowls that she is asking a rock. Then she bends a little closer and tells him about a monk walking west for the holy books, on a road that eats monks, and the price: guard him the whole way, and the mountain opens. His eyes go narrow and interested. The dominant turn: Guanyin arrives with the offer. The reader turns because the way out has a price and he has not heard all of it. The sky and ridges stay subordinate to the two faces.

## PAGE 3

Dramatic. At the mountain, Wukong's face pushes out of the rock as far as it can go: yes, he will do it, after five hundred years of peaches from his monkeys. Close on both, he grins and promises to put the monk in the west by lunchtime with one somersault; Guanyin, still, tells him the monk must peel off the seal himself, and then gives the rule: the monk goes on the ground, every mile, because flying him there would make it Wukong's journey, not his. Wukong's face falls. Rising away on her cloud, she adds that he will not guard the monk alone; others are waiting too. The dominant turn: his yes meets the rule of the road. The reader turns because a monk is coming and somebody else is waiting for him. The landscape stays subordinate to the two faces and the falling grin.

## PAGE 4

Illustrated prose. The border road at dawn: a dirt road running west into dry mountains, a lone white milestone beside it, and a young shaven-headed monk in a saffron robe and red kasaya, ringed staff in hand, leading a plain bay horse west. Far behind him, small, two figures walk back east. The prose tells who he is: a baby in a basket on a river, raised by monks and named Sanzang, gentle and stubborn, who said he would walk to the far west for the holy books, and whose two companions turned back at the edge of the empire. He is frightened of almost everything, and he keeps walking. The dominant turn: the monk who is coming is alone. The reader turns because he is afraid and still walking. The two turning figures stay small and far; the road and the lone monk own the page.
