## Cold read

I read it start to finish from "# MOVEMENT I" before opening any other book document. This is the story as a first-time reader gets it.

The Monkey King has been pinned under a five-ridged mountain for five hundred years. His monkeys still swim the sea to bring him a peach. Guanyin comes down on a cloud and offers him a way out: guard a monk walking to the far west, on foot, every mile. He can't fly the monk there, "it's your journey, not his." She has asked others to wait too.

The monk is Sanzang, a foundling, frightened of almost everything. His two companions turn back and he keeps walking. He climbs the mountain anyway and peels off the seal. The mountain bursts and Wukong kneels to him.

That night Sanzang gives him a spare robe and mends it on him. "Nobody ever gave me anything." Then come the robbers. Wukong is about to brain the leader, Sanzang throws himself across the man and calls Wukong a robber, and Wukong leaves.

Under the sea, the Dragon King Ao Guang nervously serves him tea and shows him a painting: a boy who fetched an old man's shoe three times. Wukong hates the story and goes back. Meanwhile Guanyin has given the weeping monk a cap with a gold band in it and three words.

Wukong comes back, and Sanzang gives him the cap without saying what it is. When Wukong swings the staff past his nose, Sanzang panics and says the words. The band tightens. Wukong flies to Guanyin ("You tricked me!" / "Yes."), then comes back to a monk who apologises.

The three recruits follow:
- **The white dragon** eats the horse. It turns out to be Ao Guang's exiled son, told to wait for the pilgrim, and it becomes his horse.
- **The pig-husband of Gao Village.** Wukong poses as the bride and the pig goes through the roof. He too was told to wait and forgot. He becomes Bajie, and the girl shuts the gate on him.
- **The ogre of the sand river**, who ate nine monks. Bajie shouts him up and he confesses. The monk takes him, and the nine skulls become the raft across.

At the last fire Wukong finally tells the shoe story. In the morning he runs ahead and waits for the slowest of them.

**Where I stopped on the first read:**
- **p7:** Sanzang is at the summit on p6 and flat on his back at the foot on p7. I paused over how he got down.
- **p22:** "he couldn't stop you." I stopped and reread, because I had just watched him stop Wukong on p13. This is Finding 1.
- **p28:** "For coming back." Coming back from where? I had to work out which return he meant, and Wukong's reaction is missing.
- **p35:** "Neither." The "bride" had just claimed Old Gao hired the Great Sage. It was a small snag.
- **p42:** why the pig can fight in the sand-soup when the monkey can't. I guessed "pigs like mud."
- **p44:** "This is your choice, not mine." Choice of what? I inferred it.

## Is it alive?

Yes. It is the best script I have audited for this workspace. Wukong's voice is unmistakable on every page he's on: loud, funny, hurt underneath. The book is built from scenes where people want things from each other, not from errands.

**Movement I.** "If you've come to stare at me, get in line. Everyone in Heaven already had a turn" is a protagonist with appetite in the first balloon. The rule (walk, don't fly) is planted as a bargain he dislikes, not as a moral. The p4 prose ending, "Sanzang was frightened of almost everything. He kept walking," earns the monk in four sentences.

**Movement II.** The robe scene on p9 is the heart of the book. "What do I owe you for it?" / "Nothing. It's a robe." Then "This is the first, and I haven't finished sewing it," and then him alone, touching the patch: "It's a good robe." That is subtext, enacted and not narrated. I felt it. The p6 climb is funny ("your other left" / "I looked down") and brave at once.

**Movement III.** The heat is real. Wukong is right that the robber would have killed the monk, Sanzang is right that he was about to kill a man, and "I waited under a rock for five hundred years for you" is a wound, not a speech. Ao Guang is the comic find of the book ("That was different. That was you."). His real want is to get the monkey out of his palace, and it drives the shoe parable without anyone preaching it.

**Movement IV.** This is the strongest movement. The reader knows the cap is a trap and watches Wukong put it on gladly: "Nobody gave me that one either." Sanzang's "It suits you," said not smiling, is a lie by omission with real heat. Sanzang is wrong on the page (p19, the words said in fright), Guanyin's "Yes." lands like a door, and the p23 apology ("That was worse") is the monk's growth enacted. One line on p22 undermines the logic of the confrontation (Finding 1).

**Movement V.** This is the lightest movement, but the "your father's a puddle" / "Don't you DARE talk about my father!" exchange plants the exiled son. It pays off in Ao Guang's empty coral seat and in "I'll tell him, next time I need tea." That is warm and earned. The transformation lands. Wukong's reaction to being thanked does not (see the notes).

**Movement VI.** Pure pleasure. "I brought you a peach. I ate most of it on the way." "Not anymore." "Hello, dumpling!" The pig has a voice from his first line. His apology to the girl, and her answer of shutting the gate herself, gives a silent woman a real choice. "I took you, didn't I? And I gave you a robe" uses the book's central object as an argument, and Wukong's touch of the patch is the best piece of unspoken acting in the script.

**Movement VII.** The inversion on p40 works: the monkey who wanted to fly the monk now refuses, and the monk is the one asking. Bajie gets his heroism twice, blocking the blade and then thinking of the shout. Wujing's confession ("Their skulls wouldn't sink, so I wear them") and Sanzang's answer ("You told me the worst thing about yourself first. Nobody made you") quietly rhyme with the hidden cap. The p47 shoe payoff ("You're slower than a shoe") and "Then he waited." end the book on character, not plot.

**Presence tests across the book:**
- **Logistics and aphorism:** no logistics dialogue anywhere. No aphorism dialogue: the rule-lines on p3, p8 and p40 are bargains and refusals spoken to a person, in character.
- **Attribution-strip:** passes; all six speaking voices are distinct.
- **Appetite and page-turns:** the protagonist is never serene. Every page-turn reason is a question, threat or appetite.

## Mandatory findings

**1. Page 22, panel 2. The leash is justified with a premise the reader saw disproved, and "he" has two possible meanings.**

- **Failure:** Guanyin says "You were about to break a man's head, and he couldn't stop you." She follows it with "But now he can stop you, before you do a thing that can't be undone."
- **What the reader saw:** on p13 the monk did stop him. He threw himself across the robber, and Wukong lowered the staff.
- **Reader harm:**
  - If "he" is the monk, the line contradicts the scene just shown.
  - If "he" is "a man" (the nearest antecedent, the robber), then "now he can stop you" no longer parallels it, and the second line makes no sense.
  - Either way, the reader must stop and repair the moral argument at the centre of the band movement. That argument is the book's stated plot ("the quarrel is the plot"), and it happens at the exact moment Wukong has the stronger case. A muddled justification makes Guanyin read as arbitrary rather than hard. This is an antecedent failure (§7.4) in a consequential argument, not a stylistic nit.
- **Required fix:** state what she actually means, with a clear subject. For example:
  > GUANYIN: "You were about to break a man's head, and the only way your monk could stop you was to lie down under your staff."
  > …
  > GUANYIN: "I know you did. But now he can stop you with three words, before you do a thing that can't be undone."

  Both balloons stay under the 24-word maximum, and the page stays within budget.

Findings beyond 1: NONE.

## Nonblocking observations

1. **p28 into p29, Wukong's thanks.** "Thank you too, Wukong. For coming back." does fulfil his secret want (to be given something he didn't take), but:
   - "Coming back" has three candidates: from the robbers, from Guanyin, or out of the water.
   - Wukong has no visible reaction, and p29 moves straight to the horse.

   Suggested fix: "For coming back to me," plus either a reaction note in panel 3 (caught off guard, scowl slipping) or one sentence in the p29 prose, e.g. "Wukong walked ahead all that afternoon, so the monk would not see his face."
2. **p44, panel 4.** "Master. This is your choice, not mine." is the payoff of Wukong's restraint (pp 13, 19, 22), and it rests on a hand on the staff and a choice with no object. Naming the object in his own voice would make the payoff unmistakable without explaining it. For example: "Master. Say the word and he goes back in the river. Or don't. This one's your choice, not mine."
3. **p6 into p7.** The monk descends between the panels. Wukong could cover the gap and gain a character beat: "I've waited five hundred years. I can wait till you're at the bottom. Run!"
4. **p35.** "Neither." sits awkwardly against the bride's "Father has hired someone to catch you." Something like "Old Gao asked. But I'm here for a monk going west" would remove the snag.
5. **p42.** Nothing says why the pig can fight in sand-soup when the monkey can't. One Bajie line would do it, e.g. "Mud? I love mud!", or a hint on p33 ("a marshal of Heaven's river").
6. **p44 and p45, the nine monks.** Sanzang never acknowledges them. A gesture or a single line when the first skull touches the water (palms together, "For the nine who came before me") would give their deaths their aftermath and deepen the raft.
7. **Movement I and the mandate's wound clause.** The "nobody gave me anything I didn't take" wound is only seeded in Movement I, through the tribute peach. p9 pays it in full, so there is no reader harm. A half-line on p3 ("…bring their king his peach. Kings get paid.") would meet the mandate's once-per-movement requirement literally.
8. **p1.** "The great hand that beat him" is evocative but unowned for a reader who hasn't read Volume I. It is fine as mystery; naming it ("the Buddha's great hand") is an option.

Once Finding 1 is fixed, I expect this script to clear a fresh gate. The verdict is REVISE only because approval must be unconditional.
