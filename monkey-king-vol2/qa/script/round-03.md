VERDICT: REVISE

# Script gate, round 3: Monkey King, Volume II: The Road West

## Cold read

The Monkey King has been pinned under a mountain for five hundred years. A woman in white, Guanyin, offers him a way out: guard a monk walking to the far west, on foot, every mile, and the mountain will open. She warns him he will not be the only guard. The monk, Sanzang, is a foundling raised by monks, frightened of almost everything, and he is still walking after his companions turn back. He climbs the mountain in terror, peels off the seal and lets the monkey out. The monkey kneels to him. That night Sanzang gives him his spare robe and sews it on him, and Wukong, who has taken everything he ever owned, does not know what to do with his hands.

The robbers bring the first quarrel. Wukong nearly breaks a man's head, Sanzang throws himself across the man and calls Wukong a robber, and Wukong flies off. He goes to Ao Guang, the dragon he once robbed, who gives him tea out of pure fear and shows him a painting of a boy who keeps fetching an old man's shoe. Wukong hates the story and goes back. Meanwhile Guanyin has given the weeping monk a cap with a gold band in it and three words to say. The monk hands it over as a present, uses the words in a panic, and Wukong is banded. Wukong has it out with Guanyin. That night the monk apologises. Then come three recruits, each of whom was told to wait for the pilgrim. A dragon eats the horse and becomes the horse. A pig courts a bride who turns out to be Wukong. A river ogre has eaten nine monks, tells the truth about it, and is taken in. They cross the river on a raft made of the skulls. By the fire Wukong finally says why he came back, and in the morning he waits for the slowest of them.

I could tell this back after one read, and I did not need Volume I.

**Where I stopped:**
- **p22.** I reread p16. Guanyin gave the words for when he is "about to do a terrible thing." Here she says the band is for the next time he *leaves*. I could not see how a spoken word stops a monkey who is already over the horizon.
- **p28 panel 3.** As staged, the panel shows a dragon spiralling up *and* a white horse in the shallows. I had to wait for p29's "never spoke again" to be sure they are the same creature.
- **p36.** Sanzang says "I took you, didn't I? And I gave you a robe." That is the hardest thing anyone says to Wukong in the movement. I looked for his face and the page had already moved to the pig.
- **p42 to p44.** Sanzang says the ogre is "hiding from something," because "nothing that big hides unless it's frightened." I carried that question through two pages and nobody answered it.
- **Small snags:**
  - p9: "The staff" comes before any staff exists.
  - p11: "my first day as a bodyguard," but p10 has already covered several days.
  - p37: "Pick up the luggage," but he is already carrying it.

## Is it alive?

Yes. It is alive, often funny, and in places it moves me. This is a script with a voice, not a correct one.

- **I (pp 1–4).** A strong opening. "Get me out before they run out of grandchildren!" gives Wukong appetite on page 3. Guanyin's "it's your journey, not his" plants the book's argument without a sermon. The p4 prose ending, "Sanzang was frightened of almost everything. He kept walking.", earns its place.
- **II (pp 5–10).** The best movement. "Left foot! No, your other left!" / "I looked down." is pure character. The robe scene on p9 is the heart of the book: "Nobody ever gave me anything." / "Then hold still. This is the first, and I haven't finished sewing it." Wukong touching the patch alone is enacted, not narrated. I felt it.
- **III (pp 11–17).** Real heat. Sanzang's "Then you're no better than he is" is hard and fair. The Ao Guang scenes run on subtext: "Going back is very admirable. You could go back. Now. Far away." Ao Guang is pushing him out the door while handing him the lesson. "I didn't hit anybody the whole time I was gone. I had tea." is a lovely close.
- **IV (pp 18–23).** Sanzang lies ("It suits you") and is wrong once, on the page. Then he apologises, and owns both the words and the false gift. "Everybody's frightened of me. That's the point of me." is the wound spoken in Wukong's own voice. The only weak point is the band's stated purpose on p22 (Finding 1).
- **V (pp 24–29).** Good comedy. "Your mother was a tadpole" leading to "Don't you DARE talk about my father!" turns an insult into a reveal. "Try five hundred, under a rock" keeps Wukong's wound alive. The transformation itself is not secured in words (Finding 2).
- **VI (pp 30–37).** The funniest movement: "I brought you a peach. I ate most of it on the way." / "Hello, dumpling!" / "Eight? Couldn't it be two?" The daughter shutting the gate is a real act of choice. But the movement's best line, "I took you, didn't I?", lands on Wukong and the script never shows it landing. That leaves VI as the one movement where his wound is not visible on the page (Finding 3).
- **VII (pp 38–48).** The river refusal on p40 is the payoff the book was built for: Wukong is now the one holding the rule. The ogre's confession has real weight ("I was hungry, and I was a monster, and I ate them."), and "at my back, every day. And I won't be afraid of you." repays p23. The fire on p47 and "Then he waited." on p48 make a quiet, earned ending.
- **Voice.** Strip the names and you can still tell who is talking. Wukong is loud, Sanzang is plain and polite, Ao Guang is polite on the edge of panic, Bajie talks about food, Wujing is flat and honest. I found no logistics dialogue and no aphorisms. "On this road it's eat or be eaten" is Wukong's own creed, spoken in a fight, so it passes.

## Mandatory findings

**1. p22: the band's purpose contradicts p16 and does not work mechanically.**
- **Defect.** On p16 Guanyin says: "If ever he is about to do a terrible thing, say the words." On p22 she says: "You left him at the first hard word. Next time you might not go back… The band is for the next time."
- **Reader harm.** The central device of the book now has two different purposes. The second one makes no sense: words spoken by a monk on the road cannot stop a monkey who has already flown away. A thinking reader stops and asks what the band is for and how it would stop him leaving. They have to work it out themselves, and that is the failure this gate exists to catch.
- **Fix.** Make Guanyin's reason on p22 match p16 and the p13 robber scene, and make it something Sanzang can actually use while Wukong is standing in front of him. For example: "I know. You went back. But you were about to break a man's head, and he couldn't stop you. Now he can, before you do the thing you can't undo." Keep her "Yes." and "I know."

**2. p28 panel 3: the dragon becoming the horse is never said, and the staging shows two creatures.**
- **Defect.** The staging reads: "The dragon rising out of the water in a spiral of white foam, and in the shallows, where it was, a slim white horse." The only spoken setup is "I'm to carry him."
- **Reader harm.** As written, this renders a dragon and a horse in the same frame. A first-time reader can fairly conclude the dragon left and summoned a horse. The causal step only becomes clear in hindsight on p29 ("never spoke again"), which this method treats as a fail, not a pass. Sanzang's "Thank you." also loses its object until the reader has sorted this out.
- **Fix.**
  - Restage the panel as one creature changing: the white coils unwinding into a horse's body, with the blue mane and sea-green eyes carrying through. No complete dragon should appear beside the horse.
  - Put the change into words in Wukong's mouth, which also improves his joke: "He eats your horse, then he turns into one, and he gets a thank-you?"

**3. p36 panel 2 to 3: Wukong's reaction to "I took you, didn't I? And I gave you a robe." is missing.**
- **Defect.** Sanzang answers Wukong's objection by calling back p9, the emotional core of the book. The next panel goes straight to the pig's apology. Wukong is not staged reacting, and he has no line.
- **Reader harm.** This is the consequential beat of the quarrel, and it gets no aftermath. The reader cannot tell whether Wukong was shamed, moved, or unconvinced, so the objection just evaporates. It is also Movement VI's only chance to make Wukong's wound visible. The volume mandate says that wound ("nobody ever gave me anything I didn't take") must appear at least once per movement, and VI otherwise has none.
- **Fix.** Add a beat, either at the end of panel 2 or as a small inset before the apology. Wukong goes quiet, two fingers touching the mended patch at his left shoulder (the gesture from p9). Optionally give him a grudging line in his own voice, for example: "…That was different. I'm better-looking." Then the pig's apology.

**4. p42 panel 3 to p44: Sanzang's "He's hiding from something" is never answered.**
- **Defect.** Sanzang says: "He's hiding from something. Nothing that big hides unless it's frightened." It is presented as an insight, and the turn rests on it. On p44 the ogre explains why he swung at the monk ("I can't see faces… I come up hungry") but never why he kept diving away.
- **Reader harm.** The reader is handed a question (frightened of what?) and carries it into the confession, where it is quietly dropped. Without its answer, the monk's one moment of insight looks like a dead end.
- **Fix.** Pay it off on p44 in the ogre's flat voice, after "Then why did you swing at me?" For example: "And every time the pig brought me near the bank, I saw the Monkey King standing on it. Nine monks. I thought he had come for me." This answers Sanzang, gives Wukong's reputation a job, and fits the ogre's "says the worst thing about himself first." Alternatively, cut Sanzang's line and give him one that points at Bajie's idea, but the payoff version is better.

## Five-word audit (43 balloons)

All 43 pass, subject to Finding 2 for p28 "Thank you."

- Every one is either a complete thought or a direct answer to the line before it, with one visible referent.
- The intentional mysteries pass:
  - p44 "Are you the tenth one?" is answered at once by "The tenth what?" and then the confession.
  - p27 "Oh no." is explained by the line before it.
- Callbacks land:
  - p34 "Hello, dumpling!" calls back p33.
  - p40 "Pigs float." plays against the tablet's "NO FEATHER FLOATS."
- p22 Guanyin's "Yes." answers "You tricked me!" and is the most forceful line on the page.

## Nonblocking observations

- **p9.** "The staff, the armor" names a staff the reader has not seen yet (it first appears on p11). "My iron staff" or "the iron staff" would be clearer.
- **p11.** "It's my first day as a bodyguard" contradicts p10's several days on the road ("Every day?"). Try "my first fight as a bodyguard."
- **p15 panel 3.** Ao Guang is "hopeful" (that Wukong will leave) while "glancing at the small empty coral seat" (his exiled son). Two intentions are on one glance. Make the glance at the seat a separate, wistful beat so the plant reads as longing rather than relief.
- **p37 panel 3.** "Pick up the luggage" while he is already carrying it. Either stage the pole slipping off his shoulder when his ears droop, or change the line to "Pick up your feet."
- **p42 panel 1.** "Waist-deep" fighting, visible from the bank, undercuts Wukong's reason for not helping, since he could cloud over and strike. Stage it as the two heads going under, with only a swirl of sand on the surface.
- **p43 appetite.** The pig has the idea, and Wukong's only reply is the flat "Me, and the horse." Give Wukong the insult of being upstaged: a grumble, or "Don't look so pleased, Pig."
- **p44 panel 3.** Wukong's restraint toward a man who ate nine monks is the growth this book is about. Stage what it costs him: his hand tight on the staff while he says "This is your choice, not mine."
- **Movement I wound.** It is faint, carried mostly by "Everyone in Heaven already had a turn." The prologue gets by on this, since p9 is close behind.
- **p3 text load.** About 100 words in 7 balloons over 3 panels, the heaviest dramatic page. It fits within the protected-lane allowance; the builder should reserve the lanes.
- **Mode balance.** 65 / 15 / 21 (dramatic / prose / spectacle), with the mandate's floor of seven prose pages met. Acceptable under this volume's stated allowance.
- **Architecture.** Every page has one mode, one turn, one dominant panel and no more than two locations. No functional crowds and no page included out of obligation. Every recruit earns their place by changing what Wukong or Sanzang chooses.

**Findings: 4.** The fixes are small and cheap, and once they are made this script is close to approval.
