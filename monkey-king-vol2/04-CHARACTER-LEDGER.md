# Character Ledger — 8 locks, hard ceiling

Identity hierarchy, in order of priority:
**1.** face shape and apparent age · **2.** hair and facial-hair mass ·
**3.** body silhouette and habitual posture · **4.** costume value pattern ·
**5.** colour accent, prop, repeated gesture.

> Costume and colour may reinforce a character; they may never be the only
> cues. Separation is built through skull, jaw, nose, eye set, hairline,
> apparent age, body mass and posture — the traits that survive grayscale and
> thumbnail reduction. **Every page prompt names both the positive lock and the
> nearest forbidden lookalike.**

The cast divides into four silhouettes that must never blur: **the monkey**
(small, gold, never still); **two humans** (one young, shaven, robed and
upright; one old, stooped, a farmer); **two who are divine and calm** (a
woman in white on a cloud; a vast robed dragon-lord); and **three beasts on
the road** (a slim white dragon who becomes a white horse, a big-bellied pig
in black, a gaunt blue-gray river ogre with a red beard). The danger pairs are
the monk and Guanyin (both robed, both calm), the white dragon and Ao Guang
(both dragons), and the pig and the ogre (both monsters with long-handled
weapons who join in the last third).

## 1 · SUN WUKONG, the Monkey King — apparent age: a youth, ageless

**Anchor:** Volume I, `../monkey-king-vol1/refs/approved/09-wukong-tempered.png`
(face, eyes) and `01-wukong.png` (body, tail, posture). Same monkey, five
hundred years older and not one day wiser.

**Wants (volume):** from Guanyin, out. From the monk, to be let do things his
way, fast. From the road, to be over. **Wants (secretly):** to be given
something he did not have to take, and then to deserve it.

**Moral position:** believes he is guarding the monk. Is actually learning,
from the first person who ever needed him, what it is to stay. Nobody says
this.

**Arc:** under the mountain → promises → freed → given a robe → saves the
monk by nearly killing a man → called a robber → leaves → comes back on his
own → banded → rages → is apologized to → stays → bosses a dragon, a pig, and
an ogre → admits he cannot carry the monk → walks.

**Structural lock (continuity lock, clarified for Volume II):** a monkey, not a man. Short
heart-shaped face, pale hairless muzzle and cheek patches against a
golden-tawny fur; a wide mouth; large round eyes set wide, **gold with a thin
red ring of fire around each iris** (from the furnace, Volume I); low brow;
two small upright monkey ears at the sides of the head, cupped, their rims
rounded or turning through a soft peak, the top at or a little above the brow
line; never long, never narrow and sharp-tipped like an elf's or a fox's, never
floppy (Bajie's ears hang; Wukong's stand); a golden face ruff continuous with a pronounced swept-back crown and
rear ruff of separated golden fur tufts, longest behind the ears and held as
the same silhouette in every view. The swept mass is monkey fur, not human
hair, and the pale heart-shaped face stays clear inside it. Small and wiry, a head shorter
than any human in the book, long arms, a long tail always in frame. Never
stands square: one foot up on something, crouched, mid-leap, or sitting on a
thing meant for sitting under.
Habitual gesture: a flat hand shading his eyes to look far away.

**Costume states (three, all in the reference plan):**
(a) *Under the mountain / released* (pp 1–8): the golden scale armor of
Volume I gone dull and green with age, hanging in rags over the red tunic;
the tiger-skin kilt; no cap; fur matted with moss and dust.
(b) *Pilgrim* (pp 9–19): the monk's spare robe, a plain undyed hemp
travelling robe, knee length, belted with a cord, sleeves rolled, worn over
the tiger-skin kilt, with a mended patch at the left shoulder. Bare feet.
(c) *Pilgrim, banded* (pp 20–48): state (b) with a thin plain **gold band**
around the brow, just above the eyes, flush to the fur, no ornament. After
p 20 it is on every page he appears and never comes off.
The staff (unchanged): a straight constant-diameter red-lacquered iron
cylinder, blunt gold end caps, tight flush gold banding only beside the caps;
a tiny red-and-gold needle behind his right ear when not in use, lying close
and parallel to the skull, with one capped tip allowed to show just beyond the
rear ruff as a single red-and-gold tick.

**Never:** gray or silver fur, a stooped back, a stick. A human face. A
monk's shaven head, a monk's kasaya. A cap after p 20. The band never jeweled,
never wide, never a crown, never a different colour from the staff's gold.
Never tall, narrow, sharp-tipped, or elf-like ears; never smooth human coiffure or
long hanging hair. The swept crown must read as fur continuous with the cheek
ruff. The stored needle is never doubled, oversized, or shaped like a horn.
The staff never has mid-shaft collars, rings, a tapered point, a blade, the
monk's ringed staff head, or the pig's rake teeth.

**Voice:** says exactly what he thinks to exactly the person he should not,
in short sentences, and laughs in the middle of them. Calls the monk
"Master," sometimes as a joke.

## 2 · TANG SANZANG, the monk — apparent age: about twenty-five

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** to reach the west and bring the scriptures home, without
anyone being hurt on the way for his sake. **Wants (secretly):** not to be
alone on the road; to be brave once.

**Moral position:** believes nothing may be killed, even to save him. Is
actually right about the robbers and wrong about the band, and the book does
not tell him which is which; he works it out on page 23.

**Arc:** alone at the border → climbs the mountain afraid → frees the monkey
→ gives him a robe → stops the staff with his body → is left → is given the
band → uses it in panic → is horrified → apologizes → rides a dragon → hires
out his monkey to a farmer → takes in a pig → tells the monkey to carry him,
and hears no → takes in the monster who ate nine pilgrims → walks in front.

**Structural lock:** a young man, slender, upright, of middle height (Wukong
comes to his shoulder). Long oval face, smooth and beardless; a high round
fully shaven head with no topknot and no hair at all; straight dark brows;
calm dark almond eyes that widen when frightened, which is often; a straight
nose; a small closed mouth. Fair skin. Stands straight with his weight even
and his hands together at his chest; walks with a staff. Habitual gesture:
palms pressed together under his chin, praying or panicking, or both.

**Costume:** a saffron-yellow travelling robe to the ankles, under a red
kasaya with a gold-thread grid over the left shoulder; straw sandals; a
broad round straw hat on his back on the road. The monk's staff: a dark wooden
staff as tall as he is, topped with a pewter loop that carries six small
rings, which jingle. A cloth bundle over his shoulder until the luggage is
somebody else's job.

**Never:** a beard, any hair, a topknot (that would be a Daoist). White
robes, a crown, a willow branch, a vase, a cloud under his feet, a halo, a
feminine face (Guanyin). Never armed. Never smiling while frightened. His
staff never reads as Wukong's staff (no red lacquer, no gold caps, the ring
loop on top always visible).

**Voice:** polite, plain, and stubborn; frightened sentences that end in a
firm full stop. Never preaches; asks.

## 3 · GUANYIN, the Bodhisattva — apparent age: ageless, a woman of forty

**Anchor:** new. Not in Volume I (cut there).

**Wants (volume):** the monk to reach the west alive, and the monsters she
has gathered to be turned into his guardians. **Wants (secretly):** for the
monkey to stay without the band.

**Moral position:** believes she is giving five lost creatures a second
chance. Is actually also putting a leash on one of them, and does not
pretend otherwise.

**Arc:** the offer at the mountain → the cap on the road → "you tricked me" /
"yes" → absent, but every recruit quotes her.

**Structural lock:** a tall serene woman. Long oval face, high smooth brow,
long-lidded downcast eyes, arched fine brows, a small red mouth, a dot of red
between the brows; pale skin. Black hair dressed high and covered by a long
white hood-veil that falls to her shoulders and back, a small gold
seated-figure crown at the front of the hood. Long-limbed, very still; she
stands or sits on a small white cloud, never on the ground. Habitual
gesture: a willow sprig held lightly, lifted, in her right hand; a small
white porcelain vase in her left.

**Costume:** flowing white robes and white veil; a gold-and-jade necklace;
nothing else coloured.

**Never:** a shaven head, saffron or red robes, a kasaya, a staff, a hat
(the monk). Never on the ground, never hurrying, never fighting. Never the
Buddha's curled head or saffron robe (Volume I).

**Voice:** brief, warm, and exact. Answers the question she was asked,
including the uncomfortable ones.

## 4 · AO GUANG, Dragon King of the Eastern Sea — apparent age: old, vast

**Anchor:** Volume I, `../monkey-king-vol1/refs/approved/06-ao-guang.png`.

**Wants (volume):** never to see the monkey again. Failing that, for him to
leave quickly and take a lesson with him. **Wants (secretly):** his exiled
son forgiven, which he cannot ask for.

**Moral position:** believes he is being polite to a dangerous guest. Is
actually doing the most useful thing anyone does for Wukong in the whole book,
out of fear.

**Arc:** horrified → hospitable → the painting → relieved; and, named, the
father of the white dragon.

**Structural lock (inherited verbatim):** a dragon in the shape of a lord: a
long horse-like dragon head with a scaled blue-green face, two branching
antlers, long white whiskers that float as if underwater, round golden eyes
with slit pupils, a beard of fins. Human body, enormous: seated, he appears
about twice the height of a standing man, and Wukong comes to less than half
of him. Sits on a coral throne and does not get up. Habitual gesture: fingers
drumming on the throne's arm.

**Costume:** robes of deep sea-green with a pattern of waves, a coral crown,
a pearl at the throat.

**Never:** a human face. Never ordinary-man scale. Never a serpent's body
(the white dragon is the serpent; Ao Guang is the lord).

**Voice:** grand, weary, and very polite right up to the edge of panic.

## 5 · THE THIRD PRINCE, the white dragon / the white horse — young

**Anchor:** new. Two forms, both locked; one sheet each.

**Wants (volume):** to be forgiven and let out of the stream. **Wants
(secretly):** to be useful to someone his father would hear about.

**Moral position:** believes he is waiting faithfully for the pilgrim. Is
actually starving, and ate the first horse that came by, which belonged to
the pilgrim.

**Arc:** eats the horse → fights → hides → is pinned → names his father →
becomes the horse → carries the monk, silent, to the end.

**Structural lock — dragon form:** a slim serpentine dragon, white as river
foam, long and lithe, the length of a stream bend; a short narrow young
dragon face with no beard and no fins at the jaw; small pale-gold horns, two
short unbranched spikes, never antlers; large round sea-green eyes, not
gold; a pale-blue mane along the spine; four small clawed legs; no robes, no
human body ever.
**Structural lock — horse form:** a slim white horse with a pale-blue mane
and tail, the same sea-green eyes, carrying the monk's saddle (dark red
leather, brass fittings). Unmistakably the dragon: the eyes and the blue mane
are the carriers.

**Never:** antlers, whiskers, a beard of fins, blue-green scales, gold eyes,
robes, a crown, a human body (Ao Guang). Never brown, never dappled (the
monk's first horse, which is a plain bay).

**Voice:** young, quick, ashamed, eager. Speaks only as a dragon; the horse
never speaks.

## 6 · ZHU BAJIE, the pig — apparent age: thirties, a big man gone soft

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** dinner, his wife, a nap, and to be thought handsome.
**Wants (secretly):** to have been somebody once (he was a marshal in
Heaven, thrown out for being drunk at a party; one line, his own).

**Moral position:** believes he is a good husband and a hard worker who has
been badly treated. Is actually a pig who ate a village, and is right that he
worked hard for the first year.

**Arc:** courting the bride in the dark → terrified of the Great Sage →
through the roof → caught → *the pilgrim?* → kneels → leaves his bed made →
carries the luggage and complains → the hero of the river, by shouting.

**Structural lock:** a pig-headed man, big and round. A pig's head: long
snout with a wide pink-gray nose disc, two small lower tusks, enormous
floppy ears that hang to his jaw, small black eyes, bristly black hair on a
pink-gray hide. A huge round belly, short thick legs, broad sloping
shoulders; taller than the monk by a head and twice as wide. Slouches with
his belly forward. Habitual gesture: patting his belly, or fanning himself
with one ear.

**Costume:** a black cotton tunic open over the belly, a blue sash, black
trousers; when courting, a borrowed red bridegroom's sash. His weapon: a
nine-toothed iron rake on a long wooden shaft. From p 36, the luggage on a
carrying pole over his shoulder.

**Never:** fur, a tail in frame, a muzzle like Wukong's, gold anything. A
blue-gray face, a red beard, skulls, a crescent spade (Wujing). Never thin,
never upright.

**Voice:** loud, whining, flattering, and funny; talks about food at the
worst moments; brave exactly once.

## 7 · SHA WUJING, the river monster — apparent age: forties, gaunt

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** the end of his punishment. **Wants (secretly):** to be
forgiven by the one person with the right to refuse.

**Moral position:** believes he is a monster and will always be one. Is
actually the most honest creature in the book.

**Arc:** rises and grabs → fights the pig in the river → will not come up →
"the pilgrim?" → confesses → is taken → makes the raft → carries the luggage
pole on the other end.

**Structural lock:** a tall gaunt ogre, bony and long-limbed, a head taller
than the pig and half as wide. A long hollow-cheeked face, slate blue-gray
skin, deep-set round red-brown eyes under a heavy brow ridge, a broad flat
nose; a wild bristling red beard and wild red hair; a wide grim mouth. Stands
hunched forward from the shoulders, long arms. Habitual gesture: a hand
closed over one skull of the necklace, as if counting.

**Costume:** a dark sand-brown sleeveless tunic, a sash of river reeds, bare
arms and feet. Around his neck, clear below the beard, a shallow open cord
holds **exactly nine** separate pale skulls the size of fists: four on each
side and one at the center, with a visible gap between neighbours. His weapon:
a long staff with a flat crescent-moon blade at one end and a small spade at
the other.

**Never:** a pig's head, ears, a belly, a rake, black clothes (Bajie). Never
a dragon's face, scales, antlers, or green (Ao Guang). Never a monk's shaven
head or robes. The story says nine, and the text carries the number. **Art
tolerance (ruling 2026-10-16, after eleven reference candidates across three
sheets miscounted):** a spaced cord of eight to ten clean skulls reads as nine
at reader scale and passes on every sheet, plate and page; fewer than eight or
more than ten is blocking; never bloody.

**Voice:** few words, low and flat, no jokes. Says the worst thing about
himself first.

## 8 · OLD GAO, the farmer — apparent age: about sixty-five

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** the pig out of his house and his daughter out of the
back room. **Wants (secretly):** his son-in-law back the way he was in the
first year, when the fields were never so well ploughed.

**Moral position:** believes he was swindled by a monster. Is actually also
a man who married his daughter to a stranger for his strong back.

**Arc:** shuts the door on the travelers → hears "demon-catcher" → explains
→ watches → gets his daughter back → is asked to keep the bed made.

**Structural lock:** a small stooped old man, thin and wiry, shorter than
the monk. A round wrinkled sun-browned face, bald on top with a fringe of
gray hair at the sides tied in a small gray knot at the back, a thin gray
moustache that hangs past his chin and a wisp of gray chin beard; anxious
narrow eyes. Habitual gesture: wringing his hands, or holding a lantern up.

**Costume:** a faded indigo farmer's jacket and trousers, a cloth belt, cloth
shoes; a paper lantern at night.

**Never:** a shaven head with no fringe, robes, a kasaya (the monk). Never
young, never tall.

**Voice:** fast, anxious, and aggrieved, with lists of what the pig ate.

## Scenery (no locks, no balloons)

The young monkey on p 1: brown, bare, young (Volume I's rule: every monkey
but Wukong and Old Ma is brown, bare, and young). The six robbers (pp 11–13):
locked as an ensemble on `13-robbers`; no balloons. The monk's two companions and his bay
horse (p 4 only). Gao Village's people, shut behind doors. **The Gao daughter**
(pp 31, 36–37): a young woman in a pale green jacket, hair in two loops; she is
on two pages and therefore gets a reference sheet, but never speaks. Sea-floor
servants: shrimp and crab soldiers, as Volume I.

## Reserved design space

No background or unnamed figure may inherit a complete identity stack:

- golden fur + gold band + hemp robe → **Wukong**. No other monkey wears anything.
- shaven head + saffron robe + red kasaya + ringed staff → **Sanzang**. No other monk appears.
- white hood-veil + willow sprig + vase + cloud → **Guanyin**
- antlers + whiskers + sea-green wave robes + coral crown → **Ao Guang**
- white serpent + pale-blue mane + sea-green eyes → **the Third Prince**
- pig head + belly + black tunic + rake → **Bajie**
- blue-gray skin + red beard + nine skulls + crescent spade → **Wujing**
- bald with gray fringe and knot + long gray moustache + indigo jacket → **Old Gao**
- paired hair loops + young round face + pale-green jacket + no gold → **the Gao daughter**
- that stack + the thin gold brow band → **Wukong as the bride**
- white fine-boned horse + pale-blue mane and tail + sea-green eyes + dark-red brass-fitted saddle → **the Third Prince as the horse**
- bay coat + black mane and tail + the first horse's saddle and bridle → **Sanzang's first horse** (pp 4–24 only)
- cloth head-wrap + rough brown/gray clothes + cudgel or chopper → **the six robbers** (ensemble lock `13-robbers`, pp 11–13)

## Collision matrix — pairs requiring adversarial boards

| Pair | Risk | Carried by |
|---|---|---|
| Sanzang / Guanyin | **highest** | head (bare shaven dome vs white hood-veil with gold crown), sex and face (young man, plain brow vs woman, red dot, red mouth), robe (saffron and red vs pure white), ground (sandals on earth vs cloud), prop (ringed staff vs willow and vase), posture (palms together vs one hand lifted) |
| White dragon / Ao Guang | high | body (serpent vs robed lord), face (short young, no beard vs long horse-like with fin beard), horns (two short spikes vs branching antlers), colour (white with blue mane vs blue-green), eyes (sea-green vs gold), scale |
| Bajie / Wujing | high | head (pig vs ogre), mass (round vs gaunt), skin (pink-gray vs slate blue-gray), hair (black bristle vs wild red), clothes (black vs sand-brown), weapon (rake vs crescent spade), necklace (none vs nine skulls) |
| Sanzang / Old Gao | medium | age (25 vs 65), head (fully shaven vs bald with fringe and knot), face (smooth vs moustache and wrinkles), height and posture (upright vs stooped), clothes (saffron and red vs indigo) |
| Wukong (b) / Wukong (c) | continuity, not collision | the band must read at proof scale on every page from p 20 and must never be mistaken for the cap or a crown |
| White horse / the monk's first horse | continuity | white with blue mane and green eyes vs plain bay; the first horse is seen only on pp 4–24 |

The highest-risk pair is the monk and Guanyin, who are both robed, both calm,
and on the same page at p 16. They must survive grayscale and face crops as
two different people at a glance; the adversarial board tests it.
