# Volume Mandate — Monkey King, Volume II: The Road West

The craft authority for this book is the workspace method in `../../method/`,
starting with `00-CRAFT-MANDATE.md`. This file carries only what is specific to
this volume. **It relaxes no method rule.** If a future editor adds a clause
that lowers a number, widens a tolerance, or softens a gate, that clause must
name the rule it relaxes and why, in the clause itself.

## The adaptation stance — say it first

> This volume is an adaptation. Fidelity to the source is not a goal, not a
> tiebreaker, and not a defense. Every element earns its place by what it does
> for this reader in this book, or it is cut.

## The specific failure this volume must avoid

**The monster-of-the-week reel, and the saint and his bodyguard.**

From chapter 13 on, the source is a road of interchangeable stops: arrive,
monster, capture, rescue, leave. Adapted faithfully it becomes a catalogue of
demons the reader has to learn and forget. Worse, the two leads harden into
fixed types: a monk who is merely good and merely helpless, and a monkey who
is merely a weapon. Neither one changes, so nothing is at stake.

The countermeasure is fixed here and enforced at the script gate:

- **Every monster on this road is a recruit.** No demon appears to be beaten
  and forgotten. The dragon, the pig, and the river monster are each a
  creature who was told to wait for a pilgrim, and each fight ends in a
  joining. The robbers are human and are the only foes who stay foes.
- **The quarrel is the plot.** The real opposition is between Wukong and
  Sanzang: the monk's horror of violence against the monkey's certainty that
  the world eats the soft. Every movement turns on the two of them, and the
  monk must be frightened, stubborn, wrong once, and brave once, on the page.
- **The road cannot be skipped.** Wukong can cross the world in one leap and
  cannot carry the monk a single step of it. The rule is planted in
  Movement I and paid off at the river. It is the book's whole argument and
  nobody states it as a moral.
- **Wukong is never above it.** The Volume I blocker stands: he is loud,
  greedy, hurt, delighted, insulted, on every page he appears. The wound under
  the noise in this book — *nobody ever gave me anything I didn't take* — must
  be visible at least once per movement.
