# The Invisible Forces, Book Two — Settings

Seven recurring locations. Each settles a consistent register so location prompts stay coherent across pages.

---

## 1. Cambridge — Trinity College room (post-*Principia*)

**Used by:** Page 1 (1687, after *Principia* lands), Page 2 (1693 breakdown), Page 13 (knighthood, Trinity courtyard exterior).

- **Era:** 1687–1705.
- **Lighting:** Cold daylight through tall leaded windows; candle amber after dark; the breakdown page is candlelit only.
- **Materials:** Dark oak panelling, plaster walls, heavy oak desk and chair, threadbare carpet, leather-bound books on shelves.
- **On the walls/floor:** Geometric diagrams pinned to a board; loose papers; a small portrait of Charles II.
- **Weather:** Winter or late autumn; cold light, no leaves on the court tree visible through the window.
- **For P2 (breakdown):** spilled ink across a letter, an overturned cup, unanswered correspondence stacked, the candle guttering. The DOOR closed in shadow.

## 2. Royal Mint — Tower of London

**Used by:** Page 5 (Recoinage), Page 8 (Mint interrogation, Prototype 1), Page 9 (Tyburn-warrant page).

- **Era:** 1696–1699 and ongoing through the volume.
- **Lighting:** Hard cold daylight from high small windows; candlelight at interrogation desk; coining-press furnace amber.
- **Materials:** Massive iron coining presses, brass scales, heavy oak ledger-desks, sealed wood-and-iron chests, stone walls, low arched ceilings.
- **On the walls/floor:** Stacks of clipped and new coin; piles of bright milled shilling blanks; clamps and dies; the royal cypher carved into a beam.
- **Atmosphere:** Smoke, hot metal, sweat, the smell of brass and oil. Mint workers in shirtsleeves in background. Newton's interrogation chamber is a smaller room off the main floor — quieter, candlelit, heavy table, ledger and depositions.
- **For P9 (Tyburn-warrant):** Newton at his Mint study desk; tall narrow window in the wall showing distant gallows silhouettes against a pale dawn sky over London rooftops. Dawn pewter light, candle still lit. The death warrant — a folded paper with a black seal — open on the desk in front of him.

## 3. Royal Society chamber — Crane Court / Gresham

**Used by:** Page 11 (RS presidential election Nov 1703), Page 15 (Flamsteed seizure), Page 18 (*Commercium Epistolicum* secret drafting), Page 23 (Royal Society fellows attending the funeral procession).

- **Era:** 1703–1727.
- **Lighting:** Disciplined daylight through tall leaded windows; brass candelabra on the long table for evening meetings.
- **Materials:** Long polished oak meeting table, oxblood leather chairs, dark oak panelling, Turkey carpet, the Royal Society silver mace on the table, framed portraits of past presidents on the walls.
- **On the walls/floor:** Astronomical instruments (a brass telescope on a tripod, an armillary sphere), shelves of bound *Philosophical Transactions*, a sealed leather-bound minute-book.
- **Newton's chair:** at the head of the long table, oxblood velvet with a high back.

## 4. Newton's London house / Kensington study

**Used by:** Page 14 (Catherine arrives), Page 17 (Leibniz at his Hanover desk — mirrored as split scene reference), Page 20 (the private Newton, alchemy and chronology), Page 22 (deathbed), Page 23 (Jerusalem Chamber lying-in-state).

- **Era:** 1696–1727.
- **Lighting:** Daylight on the domestic scenes (P14); deep Kensington-night blue with a single candle on the private/alchemy and deathbed pages.
- **Materials:** Polished walnut desk, deep blue or oxblood velvet upholstery, dark wood floor with Turkey rug, brass candlesticks, oak bookshelves loaded with manuscripts and bound volumes, alchemical apparatus (a crucible, a small furnace, glass vessels, a balance) on a separate side table.
- **On the walls/floor:** Newton's own copy of the *Principia* closed on the desk; loose manuscript pages of biblical chronology with prophetic diagrams; sealed letters; the Kneller portrait of Newton (small) on the side wall in P14 only.
- **For P20:** alchemical furnace amber glow; a single candle on the main desk; the biblical chronology page open with a hand-drawn diagram of ancient kingdoms or the prophecies of Daniel; the *Principia* in the background.
- **For P22 (deathbed):** the bedroom adjoining the study, a tall canopy-bed, white linen, dark wood headboard, single bedside candle, Catherine seated on a low chair reading aloud from a small book, the closed Bible on the bedside table.

## 5. Greenwich Observatory garden

**Used by:** Page 16 (Flamsteed's 1716 bonfire only).

- **Era:** 1716.
- **Lighting:** Late afternoon or dusk, autumn; the bonfire is the brightest light source; long cold shadows on the lawn.
- **Materials:** Lawn, low stone wall of the Observatory garden, the Observatory building silhouetted behind, a small iron brazier or simple bonfire arrangement with the catalogue pages being burned.
- **On the ground:** Stacked copies of *Historia Coelestis* beside the fire, some already in the flames, pages curling.

## 6. London coffee-house / Exchange Alley

**Used by:** Page 6 (Newton undercover collecting depositions), Page 21 (South Sea Bubble).

- **Era:** 1697 (P6), 1720 (P21).
- **Lighting:** Warm interior light, candle-and-fireplace amber, late afternoon daylight from windows opening onto the street.
- **Materials:** Long communal tables, wooden benches, pewter mugs, clay pipes, copies of broadsheets nailed to the wall, a high counter with a coffee-pot.
- **For P6:** dim Mint-investigation register — Newton in a plainer dark coat (not the wig) sitting across from an informant, a ledger in front of him, the coffee untouched.
- **For P21:** the South Sea Company's broker-pamphlet pinned to the wall, the share-price chalkboard with crossed-out numbers, Newton seated alone with a folded newspaper and an untouched coffee.

## 7. Westminster Abbey — Jerusalem Chamber and the nave

**Used by:** Page 23 (closing-as-invention finale).

- **Era:** 28 March 1727.
- **Lighting:** High cold Abbey light through stained glass; candles on the bier; the Jerusalem Chamber lit by tall stained windows; the procession lit by gray London daylight outside.
- **Materials:** Cold pale Abbey stone, the high vault, the choir stalls, the stained glass, the heavy oak doors of the Jerusalem Chamber, gold leaf on the altarpiece, the black mourning cloth on the bier.
- **On the walls/floor:** The Royal Society fellows in mourning dress arranged near the bier; the pallbearers (the Lord Chancellor, Dukes of Montrose and Roxburgh, Earls of Pembroke, Sussex, and Macclesfield) in dark robes and white lace; Newton's casket draped with cloth-of-state.
- **Crowd at the Abbey doors:** A small attentive crowd; Voltaire among them, a single young French face in plainer continental dress, watching.

## Recurring objects across the volume

These objects recur and should look the same on every appearance:

- **The *Principia* (1687).** A heavy leather-bound quarto. Appears closed on Newton's desk in P1, P20, and as small vignette in P23.
- **The *Opticks* (1704).** A smaller plain quarto in tooled leather. Appears as title page on P10 and P12, and as small vignette in P23.
- **The *Commercium Epistolicum* (1712-13).** A thin quarto with a long Latin title page. Appears on P18 in Newton's hand.
- **A counterfeit shilling.** Recurs across P5, P7, P8, P9 — same coin, with the same shallow impression.
- **The Mint die.** Recurs on P5 (Recoinage), P7 (Chaloner at his press), P8 (interrogation evidence).
- **Newton's quill and ink.** Same dark inkwell, same quill. P1, P2, P10, P18, P20, P22.
- **A single tall candle in brass.** P2, P20, P22.
- **The death warrant.** P9 — folded paper with black seal.
- **The shell-of-state on the casket.** P23 — Westminster Abbey scene.

Lock these in the prompts when they appear.
