# The Invisible Forces, Book Two — Style Guide

The Book Two style is the Book One style, advanced in time. **Do not re-derive it.** Copy verbatim into every page prompt.

## Style Block (COPY VERBATIM TO EVERY PROMPT)


## Visual identity shift from Book One

Book One was Lincolnshire, Cambridge, plague-year orchard, the young scholar's room. **Book Two is London-civic-power and old-age private-night.**

- The dominant Book Two interiors are: **the Mint chamber at the Tower of London** (brass and iron, coining presses, ledgers), **the Royal Society chamber** (long table, oxblood-and-oak panelling, leaded windows, fellows in wigs), **Newton's London study/Kensington** (deep candlelight, manuscripts, alchemy apparatus, biblical chronology pages, the closed *Principia* on the desk), and **Westminster Abbey** (Jerusalem Chamber stone, the Abbey nave).
- The dominant exteriors are: **London streets at dawn** (the Tyburn-warrant page), the **Trinity Cambridge quad** (knighthood, briefly), Greenwich Observatory in distance for Flamsteed.
- **Newton ages from 44 to 84 across the book.** The visual register tracks this. The young-scholar look does not survive Book One; do not regress it.

## Atmosphere notes

- **Solitude is structural.** Even at the Royal Society or the Mint, Newton is often the only sharply rendered figure; everyone else is in the painted middle-ground.
- **Power is in the paperwork.** Ledgers, depositions, warrants, sealed letters, manuscripts, printed title pages — these are the visual subjects of the public-life pages. Violence stays distant.
- **Color is rare and meaningful.** The *Opticks* prism page (P12) is the only saturated-spectrum page in the book. Bonfire orange (P16, Flamsteed burns the catalogue) is its only rival. Otherwise the palette is restrained.
- **Newton is not theatrical.** The aged Newton is severe, alert, and self-contained. Do NOT give him broad expressions. He is a man who has spent forty years keeping his face still.

## Newton continuity markers across ages (carried forward from Book One)

1. **Narrow long face**, more drawn with age.
2. **High forehead**, increasingly visible above the wig once he adopts it post-knighthood.
3. **Slightly downturned mouth**, habitually compressed.
4. **Heavy-lidded watchful eyes**, gray-brown.
5. **Long prominent nose**, more pronounced with age.
6. **Long precise hands**.
7. **Hair:** in Book Two, dark hair gradually graying / hidden under the formal **Augustan full-bottom wig** for public-civic scenes (Master of the Mint, Royal Society, knighthood, Catherine's domestic scenes, Leibniz war). In private night scenes (1693 breakdown, the Kensington alchemy page), the wig is OFF and Newton is in his cap or bareheaded with thin gray hair. **Use this contrast deliberately:** public Newton in the wig; private Newton in his cap.

### Caption box treatment

- Off-white parchment caption boxes with dark serif text, readable.
- **Full-width caption bands** for T4–T5 hero pages (top sets the moment, bottom closes it). 50–80 words each is shippable.
- **In-scene caption boxes** anchored to upper-left for setup and lower-right for resolution on lower-density pages.

### Speech bubbles

- Round, off-white, dark serif text, tail explicitly described in the prompt ("tail pointing to the LEFT figure").
- Under 15 words.

### Signage and primary-source rendering

- Period title pages, warrants, letters, broadsheets render reliably on gpt-image-2 when described as physical objects with their text quoted verbatim.
- The *Opticks* title block on P12 and the *Commercium Epistolicum* title page on P18 are the two primary-source-as-artwork hits.

## Anti-drift reinforcement (if a page drifts toward softness, cartoon, or generic-educational)

```
STYLE REINFORCEMENT: NOT a children's book. Serious mature graphic novel. Austere late-17th and early-18th-century English historical realism. Realistic human proportions. Restrained palette. Cool London window-light, candlelit shadow, brass-and-iron Mint surfaces, oxblood-and-oak Royal Society panelling, Kensington-night deep blue.
```

Monitor especially for drift on:
- **Old Newton** — the model will try to soften him into a kindly grandfather. Hold the severe, watchful, self-contained register.
- **The Mint pages** — the model will try to make them adventurous. They should feel weighty, bureaucratic, and dangerous in the paperwork.
- **The prism page** — the model will over-saturate the spectrum. Keep colors precise and restrained, anchored to the diagram.
- **The closing Abbey page** — the model may go pompous or overly celestial. The death is dignified and quiet; the Abbey is cold stone.

## What this should NOT look like

- Children's book illustration
- Manga or anime styling
- Modern glossy concept art sheen
- Flat educational infographic aesthetics
- A jolly Newton in a powdered wig
- Saturated rainbow spectrum on every page just because *Opticks* is in the book
- A young Newton (he is 44–84 across the volume)
