## Window

**1687 → 20 March 1727** (~40 years; Newton age 44 → 84).

Closes with the death at Kensington, body lying in state in the Jerusalem Chamber, burial in Westminster Abbey, Voltaire watching from the crowd.

**IN this volume:**
- Post-*Principia* exhaustion and the 1693 breakdown (the "black year").
- Move to London. Warden of the Mint (1696), Master of the Mint (1699).
- The Great Recoinage and the war on counterfeiters. William Chaloner — Newton's nemesis-by-paperwork — caught, prosecuted by Newton personally, hanged at Tyburn 1699.
- *Opticks* published 1704 (had been sitting in the drawer since Hooke's death freed it).
- Knighthood 1705 (the first scientist knighted).
- Royal Society presidency 1703 → death. Reign as autocrat. Halley's loyalty. Flamsteed's catalogue seized and published over Flamsteed's protest.
- The Leibniz priority war (1708–1716). The *Commercium Epistolicum* committee Newton secretly chaired. Leibniz's death 1716; Newton outliving the rival.
- Catherine Barton — Newton's beloved niece who kept his house in London; the unspoken center of his late domestic life.
- Decades of alchemy and biblical chronology, mostly private; the man Keynes later called "the last of the magicians."
- South Sea Bubble 1720 — Newton invested, lost ~£20,000 (an immense sum), said the famous "I can calculate the motion of heavenly bodies but not the madness of people."
- Death March 1727. Westminster Abbey.

**NOT in this volume** (already covered in Book One — do not retread):
- Woolsthorpe, the apple, plague year, the prism, the *Principia*. These are Book One material. Refer back in a single early caption ("twenty years before, he had built the world from gravity and light…") — do not re-stage.

**Editorial note:** This is a darker book than Book One. The young Newton was a discoverer; the old Newton was a power-wielder, a heretic in private, a relentless prosecutor, a quarreler. Do not soften this into a "great old man" story. The interesting beat is that the same obsessive mind that solved gravity then turned its full force onto counterfeiters and rivals. That's the human spine.

## Safety-filter watch-outs

Book Two has **execution and prosecution** content (Chaloner hanged at Tyburn 1699). This is the Newton equivalent of the da Vinci anatomy page. Apply the same fix:

- **Anchor on the artifact, not the body.** The execution page should be Newton reading the death warrant in his study at the Mint, with the gallows abstractly indicated in a window, in the distance, at dawn — NOT a foreground hanging.
- **The Mint chapter's violence is in the paperwork.** Lead with the counterfeit coin, the ledger, the warrant, Newton's interrogation notes. Not the body.
- **The 1693 breakdown.** Newton's mercury-poisoning / mental crisis episode. Anchor on the candlelit study, the spilled ink, the unanswered letters, the closed door. Do NOT describe self-harm or anything the safety filter will read as such.

## Research dossier — REQUIRED before scripting

**Do not rely on model knowledge for Newton's late life.** The model knows the broad outline but will get dates, relationships, and specific Mint-prosecution facts wrong. Before writing any script:

1. Create `~/Documents/nano/newton-vol2-research/source-dossier.md`.
2. Pull at least two independent sources. Floor: solid Wikipedia pass + Westfall's *Never at Rest* (or equivalent biographical source for the late life). Better: primary sources for the Mint records and the *Commercium Epistolicum*.
3. Verify every date, every place, every quote. The "I can calculate the motion of heavenly bodies but not the madness of people" quote is widely attributed but the wording varies — find the closest verified version.
4. Verify the order: the breakdown precedes the Mint move; the *Opticks* publication waits for Hooke's death; the Leibniz war runs in parallel with the Royal Society presidency; the South Sea Bubble comes late.
5. Decide IN/OUT for: the alchemy (private, weird, central to who he was), the Arian biblical chronology (heretical, also central), Catherine Barton's relationship with Halifax (interesting, contested, kid-appropriate framing needed).
