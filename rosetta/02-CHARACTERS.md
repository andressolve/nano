# 02 — CHARACTERS

Recurring cast = 4 humans (one in two age phases) + 1 object-character (the Stone).
Per the refs-not-prose rule every one of them gets locked pixels before any page is
generated. Locks below never name the famous figures — the visual is the lock; names
live in the script and filenames only.

## Lock blocks

### CHAMPOLLION — BOY (~12, Grenoble years, pages P5 only)
Slight French boy of about twelve, olive-toned skin, unruly jet-black hair falling over
his forehead, dark brown eyes large and intense, strong dark eyebrows, thin face with
high cheekbones. Worn dark-green schoolboy coat over a rumpled white shirt, ink-stained
fingers. Realistic child anatomy. NOT cute, NOT mascot proportions, NOT oversized eyes.

### CHAMPOLLION — MAN (~31, Paris 1822, P8–P17)
Intense French scholar in his early thirties, olive-toned skin, thick jet-black hair worn
longish and swept back with side-whiskers (no beard, no moustache), dark brown deep-set
burning eyes, strong black eyebrows, high cheekbones, slightly gaunt face. Shabby dark
frock coat over a high-collared white shirt with a loosely knotted black cravat;
ink-stained fingers. Lean build, restless forward-leaning posture.

### JACQUES-JOSEPH — the elder brother (~44 in 1822, P5, P13)
Steady French man of letters in his mid-forties, olive-toned skin, dark hair receding at
the temples and cut short, neat short side-whiskers, calm brown eyes, fuller and squarer
face than his younger brother, composed upright posture. Respectable brown tailcoat,
buff waistcoat, white cravat neatly tied. The reliable one: tidy where his brother is
rumpled.

### THOMAS YOUNG — the rival detective (~46–48, P6–P7, P14–P15)
Polished English gentleman-scholar in his late forties, fair ruddy skin, short
powdered-looking light-brown hair brushed forward in the Regency manner, clean-shaven
round intelligent face, alert grey eyes, composed confident half-smile. Immaculate dark
blue tailcoat with brass buttons, cream waistcoat, crisp white high cravat. Upright,
unhurried, precise — everything about him orderly.

### THE STONE (object-character, P2–P3, P7, P10, P14, P17 inset)
Irregular upright slab of dark grey-black granodiorite with a subtle dark-pink granite
vein at the upper break, top edge and upper-left corner BROKEN OFF at an angle, lower
right corner chipped. Three bands of incised text: hieroglyphs at top (visibly
incomplete, cut off by the break), dense cursive demotic in the middle (the widest,
best-preserved band), tight ancient Greek capitals at the bottom. Text incised, lighter
grey against the dark polished surface. About 112 cm tall. Never rendered whole or
unbroken — the break IS the plot.

## Master cast plate — ONE single generation (user directive)

`refs/ref_cast_plate.png` — 1536×1024, `generate_image`, quality high.

Prompt skeleton (final assembly at production): STYLE BLOCK + REGISTER GUARD + —
> Character reference plate, five subjects in a row against a plain warm parchment
> background, museum-lineup style, each subject large and clearly lit, thin hand-written
> name label below each. From left: (1) [BOY lock]; (2) [MAN lock] — the same person
> twenty years later, same jet-black hair and burning dark eyes; (3) [JACQUES-JOSEPH
> lock]; (4) [YOUNG lock]; (5) [STONE lock], shown at true relative height (waist-high
> slab on a low plinth). Faces LARGE and readable; three-quarter view; neutral expressions.
> No text other than the five name labels.

Casting gate: age right, era right (Empire/Regency, not Victorian), realistic, distinct
(the two brothers clearly kin but the elder tidier/fuller-faced; Young unmistakably NOT a
Champollion — different coloring, different grooming), register matches §1. The BOY and
MAN must read as the same person aged. The Stone must show the break and three bands.
