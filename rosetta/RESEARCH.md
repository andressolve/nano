# RESEARCH — The Riddle of the Stone (Champollion & the Rosetta Stone)

Web-verified 2026-07-05. Every date, name, and quote below was checked against at least
two independent sources. The script must not go beyond what is recorded here without
re-verification.

## The mystery itself

- **Last known hieroglyphic inscription:** the Graffito of Esmet-Akhom, temple of Philae,
  carved **24 August AD 394** by the priest Nesmeterakhem/Esmet-Akhom, with a figure of the
  god Mandulis and a prayer that the inscription last "for all time and eternity."
  Shortly after, likely no one alive could read hieroglyphs. → ~1,400 years of silence
  until 1822. (Wikipedia "Graffito of Esmet-Akhom"; British Museum decipherment timeline.)
- Knowledge of the script was fully lost; medieval/Renaissance Europe read hieroglyphs as
  **pure symbols** — mystical picture-ideas, not language. **Athanasius Kircher** (mid-1600s)
  published confident "translations" that were speculative guesswork (though he was first to
  assign one correct phonetic value, the water sign = *mu*, and he championed Coptic).

## The Stone

- **Found mid-July 1799** (July 15 traditionally cited) at **Fort Julien** near **Rashid
  (Rosetta)** in the Nile Delta, by French soldiers strengthening fortifications during
  Napoleon's Egyptian campaign. Spotted by **Lieutenant Pierre-François Bouchard**. It had
  been reused as building material in the fort's wall.
- **Granodiorite stele** carrying a **decree issued at Memphis, 196 BC**, for
  **King Ptolemy V Epiphanes** (a boy-king of the Greek dynasty), in **three registers**:
  hieroglyphic (top), **demotic** (middle — the everyday cursive script of later Egypt),
  **Ancient Greek** (bottom). Same text, minor variations.
- **The stone is broken.** The upper (hieroglyphic) register is mostly lost — only 14 lines
  survive, none complete; lower right corner of the Greek also missing. This is WHY the stone
  alone could not finish the job: by 1824 "the Rosetta Stone, with its limited hieroglyphic
  text, had become irrelevant for further progress" (Wikipedia, Decipherment article).
- **1801:** French capitulate at Alexandria; stone ceded to the British. In the
  **British Museum since 1802**. But **copies and lithographs circulated across Europe** —
  the race was run on paper.
- **Cleopatra's name is NOT on the Rosetta Stone.** The decisive second name came from the
  **Philae obelisk** (below). Important plot point — do not let the script imply the stone
  contained everything.

## False trails / early partial progress

- **Silvestre de Sacy** (Paris, Champollion's own teacher) attacked the demotic register,
  identified name-groups (Ptolemy, Alexander, Arsinoe), published 1802, gave up.
- **Johan David Åkerblad** (Swedish diplomat), same year: proposed a 29-sign demotic
  alphabet, about half later proven right; also gave up. Both were trapped by assuming
  demotic was purely alphabetic.
- The reigning assumption for hieroglyphs proper remained: **symbols, not sounds.**

## Thomas Young

- London physician-polymath (also proved the wave nature of light — usable for grounding
  him as "the cleverest man in England" style framing, keep sober). Worked on the scripts
  **1814–1819**.
- Genuine contributions: recognized **demotic derived from hieroglyphic** (one family of
  scripts, not unrelated); noted there are **too few distinct hieroglyphs** for
  one-sign-per-word; read the **cartouche** ovals as royal names; assigned phonetic values
  to the **Ptolemy** cartouche — proposed **13 values: 6 right, 3 partly right, 4 wrong**.
  Correctly decoded Egyptian numerals. ~80 correct hieroglyphic word correlations.
- Published summary: **"Egypt," anonymous supplement article, Encyclopaedia Britannica, 1819**.
- **Young's ceiling:** he believed phonetic signs were used **only to write foreign
  (non-Egyptian) names** — the old symbol theory still ruled the native script for him.
- Later did decipher **demotic** substantially (Rudiments of an Egyptian Dictionary,
  posthumous 1831); John Ray: Young "probably deserves to be known as the decipherer of
  demotic."
- Died **1829**; relations with Champollion "varied between cordial and contentious."

## Champollion — life

- Born **Figeac, 23 December 1790**; father a book dealer. Died **Paris, 4 March 1832**,
  age 41 (stroke; after the Egypt expedition).
- Raised/educated largely by his elder brother **Jacques-Joseph Champollion-Figeac**,
  a Grenoble intellectual — lifelong supporter, the person he ran to on 14 Sept 1822.
- **Fourier anecdote:** at ~11 in Grenoble, prefect **Joseph Fourier** (the mathematician)
  showed him his Egyptian collection; told the hieroglyphs could not be read, the boy
  declared he would be the one to read them ("In a few years I will be able to").
  **Provenance: biographer's account (Hartleben tradition), historicity uncertain — frame
  as "the story is told."** Fourier's patronage afterward is fact.
- Languages: Latin, Greek, Hebrew, Arabic, Syriac, Aramaic, Persian, Sanskrit, Chinese
  (studied), and crucially **Coptic** — the last stage of the ancient Egyptian language,
  written in Greek letters, surviving in Egyptian church liturgy. His edge over Young.
- Professor of ancient history at Grenoble **1809** (age 18). Political trouble in the
  Restoration years; lost his position; **moved to Paris in 1821** to his brother.
- **As late as 1821 Champollion himself believed NONE of the three scripts were phonetic.**
  (Wikipedia, Decipherment article.) The detective's own blind spot — use it.

## The breakthrough sequence (all 1822)

1. **January 1822 — the Bankes obelisk.** Found at **Philae 1815** by **William John
   Bankes**, shipped to England; its **Greek base** names **Ptolemy and Cleopatra**; a
   cartouche on the shaft presumed = Cleopatra (Bankes pencilled the guess on lithographs
   he circulated; he later resented Champollion's non-attribution — keep the credit honest:
   Bankes guessed it first). Champollion saw a copy in January 1822.
2. Champollion had also worked out the **demotic spelling of "Cleopatra"** from the
   **Casati papyrus** (Greek–demotic), and could convert demotic signs to their hieroglyphic
   ancestors — so he could **predict** what Cleopatra should look like in hieroglyphs.
3. **The cross-check:** Ptolemy (from the Rosetta Stone) vs Cleopatra (from the obelisk).
   Shared letters **P, O, L** sit exactly where they should; **E** appears once in
   Cleopatra, doubled in Ptolemy's fuller spelling; **T differs** — explained as
   **homophones** (two signs, same sound). The alphabet held. He then read cascade
   cartouches: **Alexander, Berenice, Caesar, Trajan…** — all Greco-Roman rulers.
4. **The residual doubt (real, load-bearing):** every cracked name was foreign. Young's
   theory — phonetics as a special tool for foreign names only — was still standing.
5. **14 September 1822.** Champollion receives fresh drawings by architect
   **Jean-Nicolas Huyot** of **Abu Simbel** — a temple far older than the Greeks. One
   cartouche, four signs: **sun disc + unknown sign + folded cloth + folded cloth**.
   Sun in Coptic = **"Re."** Folded cloth = **S** (known from Ptolemy). Middle sign
   deduced = **M** → **Re-m-s-s = Ramesses** ("Re gave him birth," Coptic *mise* = to give
   birth). Second cartouche: **ibis (the god Thoth) + same m-s** → **Thutmose**. A native
   pharaoh from ~3,000 years before, written with sounds: **the phonetic principle was
   Egyptian from the start, not a foreign patch.**
6. He ran to Jacques-Joseph's office at the Institut de France, cried
   **"Je tiens mon affaire !"** ("I've got it!" — some sources render "Je tiens l'affaire !";
   use *mon affaire* per Hartleben) and **collapsed — the story says he kept his bed for
   days.** **Provenance: Hartleben's 1906 biography; historicity debated — frame as "the
   story is told."**
7. **27 September 1822 — the *Lettre à M. Dacier*** read at the **Académie des Inscriptions
   et Belles-Lettres** (completed 22 Sept). **Thomas Young was present in the audience**
   (fact). The letter claimed the phonetic alphabet for **Greco-Roman cartouches** and only
   *hinted* that phonetic signs might go back much earlier. Often called the founding
   document of Egyptology.
8. **1824 — *Précis du système hiéroglyphique*.** The full system. Verbatim Champollion
   (translated): *"Hieroglyphic writing is a complex system, a script all at once
   figurative, symbolic and phonetic, in one and the same text, in one and the same
   sentence, and, I might even venture, one and the same word."* Hundreds of words,
   grammar outline.

## The priority dispute (handle honestly, both quotes verified)

- Young, 1823, published *An Account of Some Recent Discoveries…* subtitled **"Including
  the Author's Original Hieroglyphic Alphabet, As Extended by Mr Champollion."**
- Champollion: **"I shall never consent to recognise any other original alphabet than my
  own, where it is a matter of the hieroglyphic alphabet properly called."**
- Young privately (after the Lettre): quoted the French saying that "it's the first step
  that counts," but added: **"if he did borrow an English key, the lock was so dreadfully
  rusty, that no common arm would have strength enough to turn it."**
- Champollion claimed he had reached his results without seeing Young's 1819 article;
  scholarly opinion **remains divided** on whether that was true. Do not adjudicate.
- Fair modern verdict, historian **R. B. Parkinson** (British Museum): *"Young discovered
  parts of an alphabet — a key — but Champollion unlocked an entire language."*

## Aftermath

- **1826:** curator of the new Egyptian department at the **Louvre** (under Charles X).
- **1828–29:** the Franco-Tuscan **expedition to Egypt** — Champollion finally sees Egypt,
  reads the monuments in place, Nile mouth to the second cataract.
- **Verbatim, letter to Bon-Joseph Dacier from Wadi Halfa, Nubia, 1 January 1829:**
  **"…notre alphabet est bon"** — *"our alphabet is good"* — it applies equally to monuments
  of the Romans and the Ptolemies and, more importantly, to the far older native ones.
  (Gallica/egyptologue.fr, Lettres de Nubie.) Perfect closing artifact.
- **1831:** first chair of Egyptian history and archaeology created for him in Paris.
- **4 March 1832:** dies in Paris, age 41. Brother Jacques-Joseph edits and publishes his
  grammar and dictionary posthumously.
- **Honest coda:** Champollion was not fully right either — **Karl Richard Lepsius (1837)**
  showed many signs spell **two or three consonants** (biliterals/triliterals), refining
  the system. By the 1850s Egyptian texts could be genuinely translated.

## Cartouche fact-checks for on-page clue work (safe, well-attested glyph facts)

- **Cartouche** = the oval rope ring around royal names. (Gloss on first use.)
- **Lion sign = L**, shared by Ptolemy and Cleopatra readings.
- **Folded-cloth sign = S**, doubled at the end of Ramesses' cartouche.
- **Sun disc = Ra/Re** (Coptic *re* = sun).
- **Ibis = the god Thoth** (in Thutmose's cartouche).

## Editorial cautions

- Frame Fourier promise + the faint as "the story is told" (Hartleben tradition).
- Keep Bankes's prior guess on Cleopatra's cartouche in the record.
- Show Young's 6-right/3-partly/4-wrong scoreboard honestly; credit his demotic work.
- Do not claim Champollion read "everything" after 1822 — the Précis (1824) is the system;
  Lepsius (1837) corrected it; translation matured by the 1850s.
