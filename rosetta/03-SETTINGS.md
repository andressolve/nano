# 03 — SETTINGS

Recurring locations. Egypt scenes are the sun-bleached counter-world (ochre, hard light);
Europe scenes are the nocturnal case-world (ink blue, lamplight amber).

## 1. Philae temple, Egypt — AD 394 and 1829 (P1, P17)
Island temple complex on the Nile. Massive sandstone pylons and columns covered edge-to-
edge in incised hieroglyphs and god-figures in sunk relief. AD 394: late-afternoon amber,
one aged priest with a chisel at a gate wall, the river beyond. 1829 (and Abu Simbel-like
walls): the same stone under full sun, ochre and bone-white, shadows knife-sharp.
Decorative background glyphs need no fidelity; only exhibit cartouches are locked.

## 2. Fort Julien / Rashid (Rosetta), Egypt — July 1799 (P2)
Half-ruined Ottoman fort being rebuilt by French soldiers: mud-brick and rubble walls,
wooden scaffolds, picks and baskets, dust in hard morning light. Napoleonic soldiers in
dusty blue coats, muskets slung (never aimed). The Nile delta flat and green-grey beyond.

## 3. Grenoble — Fourier's salon & the boy's world, ~1802 (P5)
Prefect's residence: warm candlelit room, Egyptian antiquities on a table (papyrus rolls,
a small stela, scarabs), heavy drapes, Empire furniture. Also a corner of the boy's attic
study: sloped ceiling, one candle, grammar books piled.

## 4. Young's study, London, ~1814–1819 (P6–P7)
Bright, orderly Regency study: tall sash windows, daylight, neat bookshelves, papers in
perfect stacks, brass instruments (prism, compass), a lithograph copy of the stone's
inscriptions flat on a wide desk. Everything precise, cool light — the counter-image of
Champollion's clutter.

## 5. Champollion's garret room, Paris, 1821–1822 (P8–P9, P11–P13 launch)
Cramped attic study near the Institut: one dormer window over Paris rooftops, a narrow
desk drowning in papers — lithographs of inscriptions, Coptic word-lists pinned to the
wall, candle stubs, ink pots. Night palette, single amber lamp. The case-wall look: pinned
cartouche copies connected by pencil lines.

## 6. Institut de France, Paris — 14 & 27 September 1822 (P13–P14)
Jacques-Joseph's office: tall shelves, manuscripts, a long table. The Académie session:
the domed neoclassical hall, tiered seats of dark-coated academicians, a lectern, high
cold daylight. Empire-period dress throughout.

## 7. The evidence table (recurring inset device, not a place)
Case-file splash backdrop for P7/P10/P12/P16: a plain dark tabletop or cork board with
cream exhibit cards pinned under lamplight, gold pins, hand-inked labels. Exhibits are
the locked cartouche plates.

## Era guardrails
France/England 1799–1832: Empire/Regency dress, candle and oil-lamp light, quill pens,
lithograph prints. NO Victorian props, no photographs, no gas street lighting indoors,
no modern typography on documents. Egypt 1799/1829: no tourists, no excavation machinery.
