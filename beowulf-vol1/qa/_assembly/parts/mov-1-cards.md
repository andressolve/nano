## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two title-card lines and the caption must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. The title lines are printed title text over the sky, not a balloon. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The listening does not land
The page must read as one spectacle image: a distant hall blazing gold across a cold black fen, and something huge crouched in the near reeds with ember eyes turned toward it. If the reader cannot find both the far hall and the eyes, or the creature reads as facing the reader rather than the hall, the turn fails.

### C3 — The creature shown whole or wrong
Grendel must stay partly in shadow, a gaunt hairless grey-green back and long clawed arm, never whole and fully lit. A fully lit full figure, hair, wings, or a balloon or tail from the creature is blocking; reed and water texture are nonblocking.

### C4 — Extra presence
No person anywhere on the page. A visible human figure is blocking.

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All seven strings, four captions and three spoken lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. A line owned by placement (directly beside its speaker, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The watching does not land
The feast must own the page as a hall full of joy, and the last strip must show two coal-red eyes in the dark outside the open doors. If the eyes are missing, unreadable, or inside the hall among the feasters, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution in the toast
"Drink, all of you!…" hangs from Hrothgar's mouth; "Then I give it to the king…" from Wealhtheow's, read after his; "A hall this fine…" from Unferth's. The poet, the old friend and the eyes have no balloons. A swapped or shared tail, or a balloon from the poet or the eyes, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Hrothgar: very thin, waist-length white beard, gold circlet, crimson. Wealhtheow: auburn braids under a white veil, green gown, the great gold cup. Unferth: lean, short black pointed beard, black and silver. The old friend must not read as a second Hrothgar; a waist-length white beard on him is blocking. Repeated background faces are nonblocking.

The reader must see the emptied, wrecked hall and the tracks leading out, hear Unferth's promise of swords, and end on the queen's question and the king's bitter answer. If the closing exchange reads before Unferth's promise, or the wreck is unreadable, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"Thirty of my men…" is Hrothgar's; "Then tonight nobody sleeps…" is Unferth's; "And if swords can't stop it?" hangs from Wealhtheow's mouth and "Then I built the greatest hall…" from Hrothgar's, after hers. ### C4 — Bodies or blood
The men are gone, not shown. Any body, blood, wound, or remains is blocking; overturned benches and trailing blankets are the scene.

### C5 — Identity
Hrothgar thin with a waist-length white beard and circlet; Wealhtheow auburn-braided in green; Unferth lean with a short black pointed beard in black and silver; the old friend bald with a grey braid. Confusing the old friend with Hrothgar is blocking.

## Page 7 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two chronicle fields and four labels must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Each label must sit on its place: Danes over Denmark, Geats over southern Sweden, Lejre beside the hall drawing, England at the left edge. The illuminated W is the first letter of "Who", not an extra letter. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — Every claim reads as written
The page's facts (about fifteen hundred years ago, Danes in Denmark, Geats in southern Sweden, Lejre, post-holes of great halls almost fifty metres long, the poem made in England) must read exactly as written. A changed number, place, or name is blocking.

### C3 — Not visibly a chronicle
The whole page must read at a glance as a page from an old chronicle: ivory vellum, ruled margin, brown ink and watercolour, an illuminated interlace initial in red and blue with gold. A painted oil scene, a story character, a balloon, or a caption box is blocking.

### C4 — The map does not carry the idea
The map must own the page, showing Denmark and southern Sweden across a sea, a sea-road between them, and England to the west. Geats placed in Denmark or labels swapped is blocking; coastline inaccuracy is nonblocking.
