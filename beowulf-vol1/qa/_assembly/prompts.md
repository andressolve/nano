# Builder-only page prompts

## PAGE 1 — Something in the fen

### Reader turn
Far across a black fen a great hall blazes with music, and something enormous crouched in the reeds is listening to it and hating it.

### Ordered moments
1. One full-page panel, the whole page. A wide black fen at night under a cold rising moon: dark still pools, reeds, mist lying in long low strips. Far off, on a hill in the top third of the page, a long timber hall with a gold-shingled roof blazes with warm light from its open doors; thin threads of light and the faint drifting shapes of music float from it across the fen. Everything is cold blue-black and silver except that distant gold and two coal-red eyes.
2. In the lower foreground, low among the reeds, something enormous crouches with its back to us: only the curve of a huge stooped, hairless grey-green back with the ribs and spine ridges showing, one very long arm resting in the water with hooked black claws, and the side of a long bald skull with two eyes glowing like embers, turned away from us toward the hall. Most of it is lost in shadow and reeds; it is never seen whole and never fully lit. The eyes and the far hall are the two focal points.
3. The title card is lettered over the open night sky at the top, above the hall: two lines, a large title and a smaller subtitle under it, in a dignified carved-looking book-title lettering, no box.
4. The caption sits lower left over plain dark water, a tail-free caption field, clear of the creature and its eyes.

### Exact strings
TITLE CARD (line 1): `BEOWULF`
TITLE CARD (line 2): `The Hall, the Mere, and the Dragon`
CAPTION: `In the fens of Denmark, where nobody went after dark, lived something that hated the sound of music.`

Render exactly these 3 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page. The two title-card lines are printed title text over the sky, not a balloon or a sign.

### Character locks
Grendel (crouched, seen from behind, partly shadowed): a gaunt stooped man-shape far larger than a man, hairless, grey-green wet-stone hide scaled on the arm and shoulder, every rib and vertebra showing, an arm so long the hand trails in the water, huge hooked black claws, a long bald skull with a heavy brow ridge, ember-glowing eyes. He is silent: no balloon, no tail.

### Consequential exclusions
No person anywhere: no figures in the hall doorway, no walkers on the fen. Grendel never shown whole, never front-lit, no face turned to the reader, no open jaws, no hair, no wings. No blood. No glowing magic beyond the two eyes and the hall light. No blue cloth anywhere. No balloons; no words on any object; no page number, no credits.

## PAGE 2 — The first cup

### Reader turn
Heorot's feast is the happiest hall in the world, and outside the open doors two coal-red eyes are watching it.

### Ordered moments
1. Dominant panel, about 50% of the page, top. Inside Heorot by firelight: a long hearth burning down the middle, carved pillars, round shields hung on the walls, benches crowded with feasting warriors. At the far end, on his high seat, LEFT of centre, Hrothgar stands with both arms raised to the hall, mouth open. Close beside him on the RIGHT stands Wealhtheow, the great gold cup held high in both hands. On a low stool at the king's feet, lower right, sits Unferth, silent in this panel. Behind the king's shoulder an old bald warrior with a grey braided beard laughs. The caption sits top left over the dark rafters. Hrothgar's balloon upper left beside his mouth; Wealhtheow's balloon on the right beside her mouth, clearly lower than his.
2. Panel two, about 25%: Unferth alone on the RIGHT, rising halfway off his stool and lifting his own cup with a showman's flourish, pleased with himself. Caption across the top of the panel; his balloon below it beside his mouth.
3. Panel three, about 25%, bottom: two beats in one strip read left to right. LEFT: the king's poet, an old grey-bearded man, singing by the fire with a small harp. RIGHT: the open doorway and the blackness beyond the light, and low down in it two coal-red eyes, nothing else visible. One caption over the poet; one caption over the darkness.

### Exact strings
CAPTION: `King Hrothgar of the Danes had built the greatest hall in the world. He named it Heorot.`
HROTHGAR: `Drink, all of you! Tonight Heorot belongs to everyone in it. Wealhtheow, my queen, the first cup is yours to give.`
WEALHTHEOW: `Then I give it to the king who built this hall.`
CAPTION: `At the king's feet sat Unferth, his spokesman, who always had the cleverest thing to say.`
UNFERTH: `A hall this fine deserves a fine speech, my lord. Luckily, I've written one.`
CAPTION: `The king's poet sang of how the world was made.`
CAPTION: `Out in the dark, something heard every note.`

Render exactly these 7 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
Hrothgar: tall, very thin, slightly stooped; long narrow face, sunken cheeks, deep-set kind grey eyes, long straight nose; a flowing white beard to the waist, thin white hair loose to the shoulders under a plain gold circlet; dark crimson robe with gold border, grey fur mantle. Wealhtheow: tall and perfectly upright; long oval face, level dark brows, steady hazel eyes, firm mouth; dark auburn braids wound with gold thread under a white veil; forest-green gown, two oval gold brooches joined by amber beads; the great gold cup in both hands. Unferth: lean, wiry, narrow face, hollow cheeks, heavy-lidded dark eyes, close black hair, short black pointed beard, one-sided smile; black tunic with silver-embroidered collar, black cloak, silver arm-ring; at his hip his sword Hrunting, a silver-wire hilt, sheathed and not focal. The old friend (unnamed; not attached, drawn from words exactly as his sheet): completely bald with a sun-freckled scalp, a long grey beard in one thick braid bound with a bronze ring, a broad square lined face with a flattened nose, solid and shorter than the king, dull brown tunic with a grey fur collar over old mail; silent.

### Consequential exclusions
No Beowulf, no Grendel's body: only two eyes in the dark. The poet and old friend have no balloons. Background Danes: middle-aged, brown/grey/rust, full beards; no blue, no pointed black or long white beards. No other text.

## PAGE 3 — The doors burst

### Reader turn
The iron-barred doors of Heorot burst inward and a huge wrong shadow falls across the sleeping hall.

### Ordered moments
1. The prose field sits across the top of the page over the dark rafters and smoke, on a matte parchment field, full width inside the margins, nothing behind it but rafters.
2. Dominant illustration, about 70% of the page, below the field. The great double doors of Heorot, bound with iron bands, bursting inward toward us in a storm of oak splinters; the iron bar snapped. The long hearth-fire in the foreground bent flat by the blast, sparks streaming. In the doorway, against cold moonlight, only an enormous shadow falling across the floor and benches, man-shaped and wrong: too tall, arms too long, hands ending in hooks, and two coal-red eyes in the dark of the doorway. The body itself is not shown, only its shadow and the eyes.
3. Along both sides, sleeping warriors on the benches are dark shapes under blankets, nobody yet awake, faces unseen.

### Exact strings
PROSE FIELD 1: `That night, when the feast was over and the warriors lay asleep on the benches, it came up out of the fen. Later, people gave it a name: Grendel.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `The doors of Heorot were barred with iron. Grendel touched them with one claw, and they burst inward like kindling. The fire in the long hearth bent flat. He was across the hall before the first man woke.`

Render exactly these 2 strings once each, as two paragraphs of the one prose field, in this order; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
Grendel appears only as a shadow and two ember eyes: a gaunt hunched giant shape, arms nearly to the floor, hooked claws. Silent: no balloon. Sleepers: anonymous dark shapes under rough blankets.

### Consequential exclusions
No one is harmed, seized, or dragged in the picture; no blood, no body. Grendel's body, face and hide are not drawn. No Hrothgar, Wealhtheow, Unferth or Beowulf. No balloons, no sound effects, no captions. No horned helmets. No other text.
