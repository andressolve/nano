# Visual-continuity gate — pages 41–50

## Verdict

APPROVED

## Review

Pages 41–50 remain in the same book and in the approved register. Page 41 carries the forge's hearth-gold light, heavy materials, peat-brown ground, and cast-shadow logic into the established Northern Saga treatment. Page 42 shifts cleanly into the Vellum Chronicle register with warm ivory vellum, iron-gall brown lettering, ruled ornament, and the red-and-lapis interlace initial. Pages 43–50 return coherently to the cold slate-grey headland and barrow, with the dragon's fire supplying the only strong warm light during the fight.

Old Beowulf remains the locked massive, weathered king: short white beard, tied white hair, broken broad nose, cleft chin, faded deep-blue cloak, twisted gold neck-ring, old mail, boar-crested helmet, iron shield, and Naegling all remain readable through the sequence. Wiglaf remains slight and narrow-faced, with dark curls, freckles, too-large mail, russet underlayer, iron cap, and yellow linden shield. Their scale and silhouette separation remain clear in shared panels. The dragon retains its bronze-black ember-seamed scales, ragged wings, horned head, serpent character, and fire logic. The forge and barrow remain recognizable against their approved setting locks.

Contrast, black level, visible paint treatment, material weight, and overall finish are coherent across the batch. The informal balloon lettering and rounded cream balloons remain consistent on dialogue pages; prose and caption lettering remains deliberately serifed. No page looks as if it belongs to another production.

Nonblocking variation: Beowulf's helmet is absent in the final panel of page 49 after being present earlier in the fight, then appears set down on page 50. The change is momentary, does not confuse identity or action, and does not justify redrawing a climactic page.

No revision findings.
