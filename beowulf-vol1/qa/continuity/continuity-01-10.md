# Continuity gate — pages 1–10

## Verdict

APPROVED

## Review

Pages 1–10 remain visibly part of the same book. The Northern Saga register is
stable across the story pages: cool slate and sea-grey exteriors, peat-brown
wood and earth, hearth-gold interiors, weighty blacks, visible painted texture,
and simple high-contrast staging. Page 7 changes register as intended and
matches the approved Vellum Chronicle anchor in vellum temperature, ruled
margin, brown ink, watercolour map treatment, and red-and-lapis interlace.

Lettering is consistent by mode. Story narration uses the same dark serif on
warm rough-edged panels; dialogue uses the same dark hand-lettered weight and
cream organic balloons. Balloon outlines, tails, and panel borders do not drift
enough to suggest a different production system.

Character recognition holds:

- Grendel remains the same bald, long-armed, clawed, ember-eyed man-shape on
  pages 1 and 3. Smoke and doorway shadow soften his ribbed/scaled anatomy on
  page 3, but this is lighting and concealment rather than identity drift.
- Hrothgar remains thin and stooped, with loose white hair, circlet,
  waist-length beard, crimson robe, and grey fur on pages 2, 4, 5, and 10.
- Wealhtheow retains her long oval face, auburn braids, white veil, forest-green
  gown, brooches, and amber on pages 2, 4, and 10.
- Unferth retains his lean face, cropped black hair, pointed beard, and
  black-and-silver costume on pages 2, 4, and 10.
- Hrothgar's oldest friend on page 4 matches the bald head, grey braided beard,
  broad build, mail, and brown-grey clothing of his approved lock without
  colliding with Hrothgar.
- Young Beowulf remains exceptionally tall and broad, clean-shaven, tawny-haired,
  blue-eyed, and marked by the reserved deep sea-blue cloak on pages 6, 8, 9,
  and 10. His face, proportions, costume, brooch, sword, and carried boar helmet
  remain recognizable across changes of angle and expression.

The recurring settings also hold. The fen, Heorot exterior and interior, Geat
harbour and striped-sail ship, and Danish white cliffs retain their approved
forms and material language. Changes to Heorot across feast, attack, morning
aftermath, long abandonment, and renewed occupation read as story state rather
than setting drift.

No page in this group falls outside the book's established visual system, and
no drift confuses character identity, location, object state, or reading.
