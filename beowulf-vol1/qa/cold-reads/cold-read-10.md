# Cold read — pages 1–10

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without outside documentation. I could follow who everyone was, why Beowulf chose to come, what he intends to do, and why the coming night matters.

## Retell of the story so far

In the Danish fens, a creature named Grendel hates the sound of music coming from Heorot, the magnificent hall built by King Hrothgar. Inside, Hrothgar and Queen Wealhtheow preside over a feast. Hrothgar's spokesman, Unferth, makes a cutting toast, while a poet sings of the king's achievement. Grendel listens from outside.

After the feast, when the warriors are asleep, Grendel smashes through Heorot's barred doors and kills thirty men. Hrothgar is devastated. Unferth answers the attack by filling the hall with armed men, but swords do not stop Grendel. The creature keeps returning until no one will sleep in Heorot. For twelve winters the great hall stands empty at night while the aging Hrothgar waits for help.

Across the sea among the Geats, the story reaches Beowulf. He was mocked as a slow, lazy child, but he has grown into an imposing young warrior. Beowulf knows that Hrothgar once saved his father's life and paid a debt that gave him a home. He decides it is now his turn to repay that kindness by fighting Hrothgar's monster. Fourteen men volunteer to go with him.

A map explains that the Danes live in what is now Denmark and the Geats across the water in what is now southern Sweden. Beowulf and his men sail to Denmark. A mounted Danish guard confronts them on the beach, then directs them to the gold-roofed hall after Beowulf identifies himself and explains his purpose.

At Heorot, Hrothgar recognizes Beowulf and remembers helping his father. Beowulf asks for one night in the hall and promises to kill Grendel. Because Grendel uses no sword, Beowulf declares that he will fight barehanded too. Hrothgar welcomes him, but Unferth plainly resents the newcomer receiving the king's favor.

## Cast

- **Beowulf:** A tall, powerfully built young Geat with long blond hair, chain mail, and a blue cloak. He is the volunteer champion who crosses the sea to repay Hrothgar's help to his father and kill Grendel.
- **King Hrothgar:** An elderly Danish king with very long white hair and beard, a gold circlet, and red-and-gold robes. He built Heorot, once helped Beowulf's father, and has been broken down by twelve years of Grendel's attacks.
- **Queen Wealhtheow:** A poised woman with reddish-brown hair, a green gown, white veil, and gold jewelry. She is Hrothgar's queen, serves the ceremonial first cup, shares his grief, and challenges the claim that more swords will solve the danger.
- **Unferth:** A lean, dark-haired man with a pointed beard, black clothing and armor, and a sword. He is Hrothgar's spokesman: clever and sarcastic, confident in armed force, and jealous of Beowulf's reception.
- **Grendel:** A huge, dark, roughly humanoid creature with long arms, clawed hands, and glowing red eyes. He hates the hall's music, breaks into Heorot, kills sleeping men, and cannot be stopped by ordinary swords.
- **Hrothgar's oldest friend:** A bald, white-bearded veteran in fur and mail who stands close beside and comforts the king after the massacre. He is identified by role but not named.
- **The poet:** A round-faced man in a plain brown robe who sings and plays a small harp in Heorot.
- **The Danish coast guard:** A helmeted, mounted spearman who challenges the armed strangers, accepts Beowulf's explanation, and directs the Geats to Heorot.
- **The fourteen Geats:** Beowulf's armed companions and shipmates. They volunteer to cross the sea and accompany him to the hall, but none is individually identified yet.

## Causal chain

Hrothgar builds Heorot and fills it with communal celebration and music. Grendel hates that music, enters the hall after the feast, and kills thirty sleeping men. The Danes' armed defense fails, so fear empties Heorot every night for twelve years. Sailors carry the story across the sea to the Geats. Beowulf hears it and, because Hrothgar once saved his father, chooses to repay the debt by confronting Grendel. He recruits fourteen companions, sails to Denmark, passes the coast guard's challenge, and reaches Hrothgar. Since Grendel carries no sword, Beowulf commits himself to an equal barehanded fight. Unferth's jealousy creates an additional human conflict just before the coming test.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

I did not need to backtrack or reread any story page. Page 7 pauses the forward action for historical and geographic context, but the map made the Danes/Geats relationship clearer and I returned to the voyage without confusion.

The only small inference was that the enormous red-eyed figure on page 1 is Grendel before page 3 names him. The repeated red eyes, the narration, and the later entrance make that identification secure rather than confusing. I did not misidentify any recurring character.

Unferth's first sardonic line is attributable to the seated dark-haired man before the next panel explicitly identifies him; later dialogue, narration, and his repeated appearance confirm the attribution. The exchanges among Hrothgar, Wealhtheow, Unferth, the guard, and Beowulf remained clear.

## What I expect next

I expect Unferth to challenge or belittle Beowulf before the night begins. Then Hrothgar's people will leave Beowulf and the Geats in Heorot as bait. Grendel will enter expecting another easy slaughter, and Beowulf will surprise him by meeting him barehanded. The repeated emphasis on swords failing makes physical strength, courage, and Beowulf's grip feel like the coming answer.
