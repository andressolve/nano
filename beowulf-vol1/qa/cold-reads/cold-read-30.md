## Retell of the story so far

King Hrothgar of the Danes has built Heorot, a magnificent hall where his people feast, drink, and hear songs. The noise enrages Grendel, a huge creature living in the nearby fens. One night Grendel breaks into the hall and carries off thirty sleeping men. Hrothgar's spokesman Unferth promises to defend Heorot, but neither his swords nor anyone else's can stop the monster. For twelve winters, the Danes abandon the hall after dark while their aging king waits for help.

Across the sea among the Geats, a young warrior named Beowulf hears the story. He was mocked as a boy for being too large and slow, but he knows that Hrothgar once protected his father and decides it is now his turn to repay that debt. Fourteen men volunteer to sail with him to Denmark.

Hrothgar welcomes Beowulf and lets him defend Heorot for a night. Beowulf announces that because Grendel carries no sword, he will fight without one. Unferth tries to humiliate him by recalling a childhood swimming race that Beowulf supposedly lost. Beowulf explains that a storm separated the swimmers and that he fell behind because he spent days killing nine sea-monsters. He then accuses Unferth of being brave only with words. Queen Wealhtheow asks Beowulf to protect the hall where her sons have never been able to sleep, and he publicly vows to kill Grendel or die there.

The Danes leave Heorot to Beowulf and his Geats. Grendel enters, seizes the nearest Geat, and reaches for another, but Beowulf catches his arm. The other Geats' swords cannot cut the monster's hide. Beowulf holds on until he tears off Grendel's arm at the shoulder. Grendel escapes to the moor to die, and his severed arm is hung above Heorot's doors. Hrothgar embraces Beowulf as a son. The king and queen reward him with treasure and ask him to remain a friend to their young sons. That night the Danes finally sleep inside Heorot while Beowulf sleeps in a separate guest house.

Grendel's mother comes to avenge her son. She enters Heorot, reclaims his severed arm, and kills Hrothgar's oldest friend before escaping. Beowulf learns what happened the next morning and asks to be shown the monsters' lake. Hrothgar leads the Danes and Beowulf's thirteen surviving companions to the black water. Unferth lends Beowulf his famous sword, Hrunting, and Beowulf dives in after Grendel's mother.

Water-snakes surround him as he sinks. Grendel's mother seizes him and confronts him in her submerged stone hall. Hrunting, which has never failed before, cannot cut her. Beowulf throws it aside and tries to fight with his hands. Her knife cannot pierce his mail, and during the struggle he sees an enormous sword made by giants hanging on the wall. Page 30 ends as he lifts it to strike her.

## Cast

- **Beowulf:** A very tall, massively built young Geat with long blond hair, blue eyes, chain mail, and a blue cloak. He is the arriving hero: confident, physically exceptional, loyal to his father and companions, and determined to repay Hrothgar's old kindness.
- **King Hrothgar:** The elderly Danish king, with very long white hair and beard, a gold circlet, red robes, and a fur mantle. He built Heorot, has endured twelve years of Grendel's attacks, and becomes a grateful father figure to Beowulf.
- **Queen Wealhtheow:** Hrothgar's queen, with long reddish-brown hair under a white veil and a green-and-cream gown. She performs the hall's ceremonial welcome, worries about her sons, and asks Beowulf to protect them.
- **Unferth:** Hrothgar's lean, dark-haired, sharp-featured spokesman, dressed in black and armed with a sword. He is initially jealous and antagonistic toward Beowulf, but later lends him Hrunting and asks him to return it.
- **Grendel:** A towering, long-armed humanoid monster with black-gray scaled hide, claws, red eyes, and sharp teeth. He hates the music and life of Heorot, terrorizes it for twelve winters, and is mortally wounded when Beowulf tears off his arm.
- **Grendel's mother:** A large female water-creature with dark scaled skin, yellow-green eyes, claws, and long weedlike hair. She avenges Grendel, retrieves his arm, kills Hrothgar's friend, and fights Beowulf in the hall beneath the lake.
- **Hrothgar's oldest friend:** An elderly, bald, gray-bearded Danish warrior in fur and mail. He comforts Hrothgar after the first massacre and is later killed by Grendel's mother.
- **Hrothgar and Wealhtheow's sons:** Two young brown-haired boys. They have never before slept in their father's hall because of Grendel; Beowulf promises to be their friend.
- **The king's poet:** A round-faced brown-haired singer who plays a small harp or lyre. He preserves and reshapes the warriors' deeds as songs.
- **The Geats:** Beowulf's fourteen volunteer companions, bearded warriors in mail with shields and swords. One is seized when Grendel enters Heorot, leaving thirteen to accompany Beowulf to the lake.
- **The Danish coast guard:** A mounted, helmeted spear-bearer who challenges the newcomers on the beach, accepts Beowulf's purpose, and directs the Geats to Heorot.

## Causal chain

Heorot's music enrages Grendel → Grendel kills thirty Danes and defeats every attempt to guard the hall → Heorot is abandoned nightly for twelve years → sailors carry the story to the Geats → Beowulf remembers Hrothgar's aid to his father and sails to repay the debt → Hrothgar entrusts the hall to him → Beowulf's bare-handed grip overcomes the protection that defeated swords → Grendel loses his arm and flees to die → the Danes celebrate and sleep in Heorot again → Grendel's mother attacks in revenge while Beowulf is elsewhere → she kills Hrothgar's oldest friend and retreats to the lake → Beowulf pursues her with Unferth's sword → Hrunting fails → Beowulf resorts to his hands and then finds the giant-made sword.

## What I expect next

I expect the giant-made sword to succeed where Hrunting failed, allowing Beowulf to kill Grendel's mother. I also expect him to find proof of Grendel's death in the underwater hall—possibly Grendel's body or head—and bring a trophy back to Hrothgar. Because Unferth specifically asks for Hrunting back, I expect Beowulf to recover or return the sword despite its failure. The Danes will celebrate again, but the title's promised dragon suggests that this victory will close only the first part of Beowulf's life and that a later, more costly conflict still lies ahead.
