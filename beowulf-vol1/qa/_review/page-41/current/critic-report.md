## Blind read
Wiglaf asks his lord whether he was ever afraid. Beowulf admits that he was afraid every time, then explains that public boasting left him no way to run. Wiglaf invokes his family's loyalty and asks to accompany him tomorrow; Beowulf permits him to come with ten men and watch, but reserves the dragon for himself. Beowulf owns the page through the central close-up, the confession, and the decisive final terms. The turn is his movement from admitting fear and explaining his code to granting Wiglaf bounded permission to accompany him.
