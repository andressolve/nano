VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Beowulf and his Geats sail through a rough sea in a longship. Beowulf unmistakably owns the page: he stands elevated at the prow, smiling into the wind and spray, while the other Geats row behind him. The turn comes on the second day when Denmark's white cliffs appear across the water, changing the voyage into an arrival.

## Visible text
Narrative prose: “Beowulf and his Geats sailed at once. The ship ran before the wind
with foam at her neck, like a flying bird, and Beowulf stood at the prow
the whole way, grinning into the spray. He had never been so far from home.
He had never seen so happy.”

Narrative prose: “On the second day, the Geats saw white cliffs rise out of the sea. Denmark.”

The names “Beowulf” and “Denmark” and the group name “Geats” are lettered only as part of the narrative prose. No speaker name, label, tag, or other extra text is lettered. There are no balloons or sound cues.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The first prose paragraph reads “He had never seen so happy.” The exact script requires “He had never been so happy.”
- Material reader harm: The substituted word makes the sentence visibly ungrammatical and changes the required account of Beowulf's happiness, so the exact prose does not survive the desktop proof.
- Redraw justification: Exact readable prose is a release requirement, and this wrong word sits in the page's central statement of Beowulf's joy; the blocking text error outweighs the risk of replacing the complete page.
