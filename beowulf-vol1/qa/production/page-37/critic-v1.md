## Blind read
Fifty winters pass. Beowulf’s uncle and then his cousin die, the Geats ask Beowulf to become their king, and his long, generous reign keeps them safe. Beowulf owns the page: now an old, white-haired, white-bearded king, he stands alone on the steps of a timber hall above a peaceful coastal settlement and harbour, looking out to sea while touching the gold ring at his neck. The change is the whole-lifetime leap from warrior to aged king, with the page’s calm suggesting that his long peace is poised to turn.

Fifty winters went by.

Beowulf’s uncle, the king, was killed in a war across the sea, and then his son after him. The Geats came to Beowulf and asked him to be their king, and he was, for all the long years after. He sent his ships to any friend who asked, and no enemy dared to attack the Geats.

Beowulf was an old man now, his hair and beard white. But he was still the biggest man in any room, and he still wore Queen Wealhtheow’s gold ring.
