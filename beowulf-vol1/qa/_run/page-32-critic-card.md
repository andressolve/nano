## Page intent

## PAGE 32

Dramatic. Late afternoon on the shore: far up the cliff path, small, the Danes ride away, and only Beowulf's Geats still sit by the black water, staring at it. Then the page's great image: Beowulf bursts up out of the water holding the golden hilt high in one fist and Hrunting in the other, the Geats leaping to their feet, and he asks, grinning, whether they thought he wasn't coming back. They haul him out laughing and pull off his helmet; dripping, he lifts Hrunting and asks to be taken to Hrothgar, because he has a sword to give back. The dominant turn: Unferth is about to get his failed sword back.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 32 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The caption and both of Beowulf's lines must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The return does not own the page
The page must read as dramatic, and the eye must find Beowulf bursting out of the black water, hilt high in one fist and the sword in the other, his Geats leaping up. If the waiting strip outweighs the return, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
Beowulf owns both lines; each must hang from his mouth by tail. A Beowulf line read from a Geat, or any balloon or laugh line on a Geat, is blocking.

### C4 — The two objects
Exactly one golden hilt with no blade and one patterned sword in his hands. A whole giant's sword, or the hilt and sword confused, is blocking. The exact number of Geats is nonblocking.

### C5 — Identity
Beowulf recognisable: clean-shaven, cleft chin, broken nose, white left-brow notch, sea-blue cloak, gold neck-ring; bareheaded once the helmet comes off, tawny hair tied back. Unrecognisable is blocking.
