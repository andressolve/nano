# Exact script — pages 51–57

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 51 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the hoard chamber.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 80%.** Inside the barrow's stone chamber: WIGLAF,
small, stands knee-deep in gold — cups, dishes, rings, old helmets eaten by
rust — gathering an armful. High over the hoard hangs a banner woven all of
gold, glowing in the dark with its own light, lighting his astonished face.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "Inside the barrow, Wiglaf found gold everywhere — and a banner woven all of gold that shone in the dark by its own light."

**Why you turn the page:** he has to carry it out to a dying man.

## PAGE 52 · *dramatic*

**Location:** beside the barrow.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** WIGLAF, on the right, kneels with gold heaped at OLD
BEOWULF's feet. Beowulf, on the left, looks at it and smiles.

> **BEOWULF:** "Good. That's for our people. They'll need it when I'm gone."

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Beowulf takes off the twisted gold ring and presses it
into Wiglaf's hand; the boar helmet lies between them.

> **BEOWULF:** "Take my ring, and my helmet, and my mail. You're the last of our family now, Wiglaf. Keep our name alive."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Build me a mound high on the headland, where sailors will see it and call it Beowulf's Barrow."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 35%.** Beowulf's eyes are closed, his head back
against the stones, the sea wind in his white hair. Wiglaf holds his hand.
The helmet rests in the grass.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "And the king of the Geats died, with the sea wind in his hair."

**Why you turn the page:** the men who ran away are coming back.

---

## PAGE 53 · *dramatic*

**Location:** beside the barrow, evening.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The ten men creep out of the trees, shields
lowered, heads down. WIGLAF stands to meet them, on the right, the gold ring
around his neck. Behind him, OLD BEOWULF lies covered to the chest with his faded
blue cloak, his face peaceful.

> **WIGLAF:** "Now you come back. He gave you everything you own. And when he needed you, you ran."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Wiglaf, on the right, very quiet. The ten men, on the
left, cannot look at him.

> **WIGLAF:** "You'll keep your lives. But you've lost your names. Nobody will ever sing about you."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wiglaf turns to the gold heaped by the barrow door and
lays the old king's broken sword on top of it. His other hand touches the gold ring at his neck.

> **WIGLAF:** "He won this gold for his people. Not one of you will ever wear a single ring of it."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "He gave me this one with his own hand."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "The rest he paid for with his life. I don't think any of us should keep it."

**Why you turn the page:** the Geats have to say goodbye to their king.

---

## PAGE 54 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the headland, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A great funeral fire on the headland, hung
with helmets and shields, flames and smoke climbing into a star-filled sky.
The Geats stand in a ring around it, small and dark against the fire. One
woman with her hair bound up stands closest, singing, her face lifted. WIGLAF
stands near her, wearing the gold ring.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the smoke)*
> "They built a great fire on the headland and hung it with helmets and shields. A woman of the Geats sang: who will keep us safe now?"

**Why you turn the page:** the king is gone, and his people have to remember him.

---

## PAGE 55 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the headland, ten days later.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A tall grassy barrow newly raised on the
highest point of the headland over the sea. Twelve riders circle it, cloaks
streaming, singing. WIGLAF rides at their head, the boar-crested helmet on,
the gold ring at his neck.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the sky)*
> "Beowulf had wanted the gold for his people. But Wiglaf told them how their king had died, and the Geats would not keep gold he had paid for with his life. For ten days they built a mound on the headland, high, where it could be seen from far out at sea. Inside it they laid the ashes of their king and all the dragon's gold, and it lies there still.
>
> Then twelve riders rode around the mound, singing. They sang that of all the kings in the world, he was the gentlest, the kindest to his people, and the most eager for fame."

**Why you turn the page:** the king is buried, and his name is all that is left.

---

## PAGE 56 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the open sea below the headland, years later.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A longship under a striped sail far out on a
gold evening sea. A sailor at the prow points up at the high cliff, where a
green mound stands against the sunset. The others turn to look.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the page, over the sky)*
> "And ever since, sailors far out on the whale-road have looked up at that cliff and said: there is Beowulf's Barrow."

**Why you turn the page:** the story is still being told — and the reader wants to know how it reached them.

## PAGE 57 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** How do we still know this story, after more than a thousand years?

**Sources:** British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius A.xv (the Nowell Codex, copied by two scribes, dated about 975–1025); the Ashburnham House fire, 23 October 1731; G. J. Thorkelin's transcripts (1787) and first edition (1815); J. R. R. Tolkien, "Beowulf: The Monsters and the Critics" (1936) and *The Hobbit* (1937), chapter 12, Bilbo's stolen cup; Tolkien, *Letters*, no. 25 (1938), on *Beowulf* as one of his "most valued sources".

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A page of a chronicle on warm ivory vellum with
a ruled margin. The dominant image is a museum study in ink and watercolour
of an open page of the Beowulf manuscript: dense dark lines of old English
handwriting, the edges of the page browned, scorched and crumbled away in
places. A red-and-lapis interlace initial with a touch of gold begins the
first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(above the manuscript, on plain vellum)*
> "How do we still know Beowulf's story? Because of one book. Every word of the poem comes from a single copy, written out by two scribes in England about a thousand years ago."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(beside the manuscript, on plain vellum)*
> "In 1731, the library where it was kept caught fire. The book was saved, but its edges were scorched, and over the years crumbs broke away, taking some words with them forever. You can see it today in the British Library in London."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 3** *(below the manuscript, on plain vellum)*
> "A professor named J. R. R. Tolkien loved this poem all his life. In his book The Hobbit, a thief creeps into a sleeping dragon's hoard and steals a golden cup. Sound familiar?"

> **LABEL** *(beside the manuscript)*
> "the Beowulf manuscript, about 1000"

**Why you turn the page:** the book is over, and the reader knows the story came to them through fire too.
