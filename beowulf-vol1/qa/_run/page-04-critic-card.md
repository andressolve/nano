## Page intent

## PAGE 4

Dramatic. Grey morning in Heorot: benches overturned, blankets trailing, a cup on its side, the doors hanging off their hinges, huge clawed tracks in the mud leading away toward the fen. No bodies anywhere; the men are simply gone. Hrothgar stands grey-faced on his staff, his oldest friend gripping his shoulder, and cries out that he slept through it in his own house. Unferth, sword already drawn, steps up: tonight every sword in Denmark will wait in this hall. Then, close, the queen asks what happens if swords can't stop it, and the king answers bitterly that he built the greatest hall in the world for that thing to walk into. The dominant turn: the Danes are going to fight it with swords, and the queen doubts that swords will be enough. The empty, wrecked hall owns the page; the queen's question is the last word. The reader turns to see whether the swords hold.

**Location:** Heorot, the morning after.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Heorot in grey morning light: benches

> **CAPTION** *(top left)*
> "In the morning, thirty men were gone from Heorot. None of them was ever seen again."
>
> **CAPTION** *(beside the old bald warrior)*
> "Beside the king stood his oldest friend, who had fought next to him in every battle of his life."
>
> **HROTHGAR:** "Thirty of my men. Thirty! And I slept through it, in my own house."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Unferth steps forward, on the right, sword held high.

> **UNFERTH:** "Then tonight nobody sleeps, my lord. I'll fill this hall with every sword in Denmark. Let it come back and meet them."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Wealhtheow, on the left, and Hrothgar, on the
right, looking at the clawed tracks.

> **WEALHTHEOW:** "And if swords can't stop it?"
>
> **HROTHGAR:** "Then I built the greatest hall in the world for that thing to walk into."

### C1 — Transcription fails
All six strings, two captions and four spoken lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The doubt does not land
The reader must see the emptied, wrecked hall and the tracks leading out, hear Unferth's promise of swords, and end on the queen's question and the king's bitter answer. If the closing exchange reads before Unferth's promise, or the wreck is unreadable, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"Thirty of my men…" is Hrothgar's; "Then tonight nobody sleeps…" is Unferth's; "And if swords can't stop it?" hangs from Wealhtheow's mouth and "Then I built the greatest hall…" from Hrothgar's, after hers. The old friend has no balloon; the caption naming him sits by him. A swapped or shared tail in the closing exchange is blocking.

### C4 — Bodies or blood
The men are gone, not shown. Any body, blood, wound, or remains is blocking; overturned benches and trailing blankets are the scene.

### C5 — Identity
Hrothgar thin with a waist-length white beard and circlet; Wealhtheow auburn-braided in green; Unferth lean with a short black pointed beard in black and silver; the old friend bald with a grey braid. Confusing the old friend with Hrothgar is blocking.
