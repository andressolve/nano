### C1 — Transcription fails
All four lines must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line stacked directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The offering does not own the page
The page must read as dramatic, and the eye must find Unferth holding his sword out across his palms to Beowulf at the black water. If the offer cannot be seen, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the exchange
Unferth owns the offer and the closing line; Beowulf owns both thank-you and the message for the king. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his; a Beowulf line read from Unferth, or a balloon on Hrothgar, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
