## Page intent

## PAGE 44

Dramatic. On the headland, old Beowulf gets slowly to his feet and puts on his boar helmet, telling Wiglaf that an old king once warned him fire, the sword or old age would come for him, and now it is all three at once. He turns to his ten men and orders them to wait on the hill; Wiglaf steps forward and argues that the king would send an army to help anyone else. Beowulf's answer comes without looking back: fifty years of songs say he never needed anyone, and he is too old to start. The dominant turn: the old king is walking alone toward the dragon. The last image, one small armed figure going down the slope toward the steaming stone door, owns the page; the argument above it is the reason he goes alone.

sword Naegling drawn. He speaks without looking back.

> **BEOWULF:** "For fifty years they've sung that I never needed anyone, Wiglaf. I'm too old to start now. Stay."

### C1 — Transcription fails
All five spoken lines must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of any repeated letter; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The walk alone does not land
The reader must finish on one small armed figure going down the slope alone toward the steaming stone door. If that image is lost under the talk, or he is followed by anyone, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"An old king told me once…" and "He was right. It's all three, all at once." are Beowulf's. "Wait here on the hill…" is Beowulf's; "My lord, you'd send an army…" is Wiglaf's, read after it. "For fifty years they've sung…" is Beowulf's, though his back is turned. Each must hang from its own speaker by tail; a swapped or shared tail, or a balloon on the silent men, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Old Beowulf huge, white-bearded, faded blue, gold ring. Wiglaf slight, beardless, dark curls, yellow shield, no ring, no boar helmet. Either broken is blocking.
