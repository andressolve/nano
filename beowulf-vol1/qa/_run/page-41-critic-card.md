## Page intent

## PAGE 41

Dramatic. Night in a stone forge shed lit orange. A smith hammers a great round shield of dark iron on the anvil while old Beowulf and Wiglaf sit on a bench watching the sparks. Wiglaf gathers his courage and asks whether Beowulf was ever afraid. "Every time." In the forge light the old face remembers: a boy everyone laughed at, a boast in a king's hall that everyone heard, and after that no running away. Wiglaf leans in and asks to come tomorrow, as his father promised their family would; Beowulf, with a ghost of his old grin, says he may come and watch, but the dragon is his. The dominant turn: tomorrow the old king walks out to the dragon, and Wiglaf will be watching. The confession owns the page; the forging stays behind it.

**Location:** the forge, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** A stone forge shed lit orange; a smith
(unnamed) hammering a great round shield of dark iron on the anvil. OLD
BEOWULF sits on a bench, on the left, watching the sparks. WIGLAF sits beside

>

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wiglaf, on the right, leaning in; Beowulf, on the left,
with a ghost of his old grin.

> **WIGLAF:** "Before he died, my father said our family always stands by you. Let me come tomorrow."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Come, then, and bring ten men. You can watch. But the dragon is mine."

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 41 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All six spoken lines must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of any repeated letter; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The confession does not land
The old king's admission that he was afraid every time, and his memory of the boast that left him no way back, must read as the heart of the page, with the forge behind them. If the smith's work owns the eye, or "Every time." reads as anyone's but Beowulf's, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"My lord, can I ask you something?…" is Wiglaf's; "Every time." is Beowulf's, read after it. "When I was a boy…" and "Everyone heard me…" are Beowulf's. "Before he died, my father said…" is Wiglaf's; "Come, then, and bring ten men…" is Beowulf's, read after it. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail; a swapped or shared tail is blocking. The smith is silent.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Old Beowulf: huge, white hair tied back, short white beard, crooked nose, notched left brow, faded blue, the gold ring. Wiglaf: slight, beardless, dark curls, freckles, too-big mail. A Wiglaf with fair hair or a beard, or the smith resembling either, is blocking.

### C5 — The iron shield
Exactly one round dark iron shield, on the anvil. A wooden or painted shield on the anvil, or a second iron shield, is blocking; sparks and forge detail are nonblocking.
