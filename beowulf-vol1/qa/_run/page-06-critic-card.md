## Page intent

## PAGE 6

Dramatic. Across the sea, in the Geats' harbour of turf-roofed houses and beached longships, two sailors tell the monster story to a crowd, one making claws of his hands. On an upturned boat sits Beowulf, a young man, huge and still rangy, tawny hair tied back, a deep sea-blue cloak, his grin gone, very still. The captions say that as a boy the whole country laughed at him and nobody expected anything of him. He stands, frowning, and says King Hrothgar once saved his father's life by paying a debt nobody else would pay. Then he jumps up on the boat, grinning, fist raised, and asks who is coming to Denmark with him; hands go up, and his companions say yes. The dominant turn: the boy everyone laughed at is going to fight the thing no army could stop. Beowulf owns the page; the sailors and crowd stay subordinate. The reader turns to put him on the sea.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Geats' harbour, across the sea.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A harbour of turf-roofed houses and beached
longships under a grey sky. On the dock, a pair of sailors (unnamed) tell
their story to a crowd, one making claws of his hands. Sitting on an
upturned boat, listening, is BEOWULF, nineteen: very tall and broad but
still rangy, a broad open clean-shaven face, a crooked nose, a notch scar
through his left eyebrow, long tawny-gold hair tied back at the nape, a
deep sea-blue cloak. His grin is gone. He is very still.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the sky)*
> "In the land of the Geats, across the sea, the sailors' story reached a young man named Beowulf."
>
> **CAPTION** *(below it)*
> "When he was a boy, the whole country had laughed at him: too big, too slow, always asleep by the fire. Nobody expected anything of Beowulf."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Beowulf stands up, on the left, frowning, to the sailors
on the right.

> **BEOWULF:** "King Hrothgar? Years ago that king saved my father's life. He paid a debt for him that nobody else would pay."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Beowulf jumps up onto the upturned boat, grinning now,
one fist raised, to a crowd of young Geat warriors around him.

> **BEOWULF:** "Now it's my turn. I'm going to Denmark to fight his monster. Who's coming with me?"
>
> **CAPTION** *(bottom right, over the raised hands of the crowd)*
> "Fourteen of them said yes."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy everyone laughed at is going to fight the thing no army could stop.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 6 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All five strings, three captions and two of Beowulf's lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, his lines owned by his mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The decision does not land
The reader must see Beowulf go from very still and listening to standing up, then up on the boat grinning with a fist raised and hands rising around him. If he reads as serene or modest throughout, or the final call is lost in the crowd, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
Both spoken lines hang from Beowulf's mouth; the sailors and the crowd have no balloons. A balloon from a sailor, or Beowulf's call hanging from someone in the crowd, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Beowulf: huge, young, clean-shaven, tawny hair tied back, notched left brow, crooked nose, the only figure in deep sea-blue; no gold neck-ring. A bearded Beowulf, a second figure in blue, or a neck-ring is blocking. The number of raised hands is nonblocking.
