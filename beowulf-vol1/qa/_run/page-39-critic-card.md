## Page intent

## PAGE 39

Spectacle. In the dark of the barrow one huge molten-gold eye opens; then the dragon's head hangs low over the hoard, sniffing at the small gap where the cup used to be. The caption says it knew every cup and knew one was gone. Then the page breaks open into the night sky over the Geats' coast: the dragon in flight, wings spread, pouring a river of fire onto the villages, and on the rise the old king's great hall going up in flames. ## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 39 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both captions must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of any repeated letter; the words and their order are what must match. Captions carry no tail and belong to no mouth.

### C2 — The burning hall does not own the page
The dragon pouring fire on the coast with the king's great hall going up in flames must own the page. If the hall is not findable as burning, or the eye and sniffing panels outweigh it, the turn fails.

### C3 — Cause before effect
The eye opening and the head at the gap in the gold must read before the flight and fire, so the reader knows why it came out. A missing gap, or the burning read first, is blocking; the gap's exact form is nonblocking.

### C4 — The dragon reads wrong
Winged serpent, horned crocodile jaw, molten-gold eye, bronze-and-black ember-seamed scales, in both the chamber and the sky. A different creature between panels, or a balloon or tail from the dragon, is blocking.

### C5 — Death drawn
No body, figure in the flames, or wound anywhere; the cost is the burning hall. Blocking if drawn.
