## Page intent

## PAGE 21

Dramatic. Morning under the hanging claw. Old King Hrothgar holds Beowulf by both shoulders with tears in his eyes: twelve winters he prayed, and God sent him a boy from across the sea; from this day he will love him like a son. Beowulf, grinning, is a head taller. Then the grin goes: the hall is the king's again, but Beowulf wanted all his men home, and behind him the Geats stand round one empty bench with a folded cloak laid on it. Last, Unferth alone under the claw, looking up, cup forgotten in his hand, with nothing to say for once. The dominant turn: tonight there will be a feast in Heorot for the first time in twelve winters. The king's embrace owns the page; the empty bench is the cost; Unferth's silence is the aftershock.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 21 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All five strings, four spoken lines and one caption, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth or field by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The embrace does not land
Dramatic mode: Hrothgar holding Beowulf by the shoulders, in tears, under the hanging claw must own the page. If the embrace or the claw above is not found, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"Twelve winters I prayed for this…" and "Beowulf, from this day…" hang from Hrothgar's mouth. "Your hall is yours again…" and "But I wanted to bring all my men home…" hang from Beowulf's mouth, after Hrothgar's. A swap is blocking.

### C4 — The cost
Behind Beowulf, his men stand round one empty bench with a folded cloak on it. A body, blood, or no empty bench is blocking; the exact number of Geats is nonblocking.

### C5 — Identity and separation
Hrothgar: very thin, waist-length white beard, gold circlet, crimson. Beowulf: a head taller, clean-shaven, cleft chin, broken nose, notched brow, mail and deep sea-blue, no neck-ring yet. Unferth: lean, pointed black beard, black and silver, silent, looking up at the claw. A short-bearded Hrothgar, a bearded Beowulf, or a balloon for Unferth is blocking.
