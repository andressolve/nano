### C1 — Transcription fails
The two prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth or field by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The claw does not land
Illustrated-prose mode: the Danes crowding in and looking up at the claw hung high inside Heorot over the doors must own the page. If the claw is missing, hung outside, or not the thing everyone looks at, the turn fails.

### C3 — The lake
The second image shows riders stopped at a black churning lake with clawed tracks leading in. Anything rising from the water, or a monster seen, is blocking; the number of riders is nonblocking.

### C4 — Named presence and gore
No named character on the page and no blood on the claw. A figure who reads as Beowulf, Hrothgar, or the queen, or blood or torn flesh on the claw, is blocking.
