## Page intent

## PAGE 1

Spectacle. The book opens on a black fen at night under a cold rising moon: dark pools, reeds, mist lying in long strips. Far off on a hill a great hall blazes with gold light and drifting music. In the foreground, low among the reeds, something enormous crouches with its back to us: only the curve of a huge stooped grey-green back, a long arm resting in the water, and two eyes glowing like coals, turned toward that hall. The title sits in the night sky above the hall; one caption says that something in the fens hated the sound of music. The dominant turn: something in the dark is listening to that hall, and it hates what it hears. The creature stays half-seen and silent; the gulf between the cold dark and the distant gold owns the page. The reader turns to see what it will do.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the fen below Heorot, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A wide black fen under a cold rising moon:
pools of dark water, reeds, mist lying in long strips. In the foreground,
low among the reeds, something enormous is crouched — only the curve of a
huge stooped grey-green back, a long arm resting in the water, and two eyes
glowing like coals, turned away from us toward the horizon. Far off, on a
hill in the top third of the page, a great hall blazes with light from its
open doors, its gold roof catching the firelight. Thin threads of light and
the shapes of music drift from it across the fen. Everything is blue-black
except that distant gold and the two coals.

> "BEOWULF
>
> The Hall, the Mere, and the Dragon"

> **CAPTION** *(lower left, over dark water)*
> "In the fens of Denmark, where nobody went after dark, lived something that hated the sound of music."

**Why you turn the page:** something in the dark is listening to that hall, and it hates what it hears.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two title-card lines and the caption must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. The title lines are printed title text over the sky, not a balloon. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The listening does not land
The page must read as one spectacle image: a distant hall blazing gold across a cold black fen, and something huge crouched in the near reeds with ember eyes turned toward it. If the reader cannot find both the far hall and the eyes, or the creature reads as facing the reader rather than the hall, the turn fails.

### C3 — The creature shown whole or wrong
Grendel must stay partly in shadow, a gaunt hairless grey-green back and long clawed arm, never whole and fully lit. A fully lit full figure, hair, wings, or a balloon or tail from the creature is blocking; reed and water texture are nonblocking.

### C4 — Extra presence
No person anywhere on the page. A visible human figure is blocking.
