## Page intent

## PAGE 25

Dramatic. Dawn in the wrecked hall. Hrothgar sits on the edge of the empty bench with his friend's dropped sword across his knees. Beowulf walks in cheerful and sleepy, innocent, asking whether the night was peaceful. The old king looks up with tears in his beard and tells him everything in three hard sentences: his oldest friend is dead, there were two of them, the tracks go into the black lake, and his sons slept here because Beowulf swore they could. The page belongs to the moment the grin leaves Beowulf's face; he kneels, takes the old man's hands, and says what a hero says: go after the killer, show me the lake. The dominant turn: Beowulf is going to the lake. The empty place above the doors stays in the background, never a focus.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 25 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All five lines must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line stacked directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The grin does not leave
The page must read as dramatic, and the eye must find the cheerful entrance meeting the grieving king, then the change in Beowulf as he kneels and takes the old man's hands. If Beowulf still looks cheerful at the end, the turn to the lake fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the exchange
Beowulf owns the greeting and the last line; Hrothgar owns the three middle lines about his friend, the two monsters, and his sons. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his; a Hrothgar line read from Beowulf, or a reply readable before the greeting, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Beowulf: young, huge, clean-shaven, cleft chin, broken nose, white notch through the left brow, tawny hair tied back, sea-blue cloak, gold neck-ring. Hrothgar: very thin, waist-length white beard, gold circlet, crimson. Either unrecognisable, or a missing neck-ring on Beowulf, is blocking.

### C5 — Continuity in the hall
The claw is gone; if it still hangs above the doors, it is blocking. No body, no blood. Background hall detail is nonblocking.
