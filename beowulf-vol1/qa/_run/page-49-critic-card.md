## Page intent

## PAGE 49

Spectacle. The dragon's jaws close on the old king at the neck and shoulder, seen only as the jaws and his helmet in silhouette against the fire. Then Wiglaf, shieldless, darts in low under the flames and drives his short sword up into the dragon's belly between the ember seams, and the fire in its throat goes out. Last, Beowulf draws the long knife from his belt and swings; the blow lands beyond the panel's edge and the great head falls away. Three captions carry it. The dominant turn: the dragon is dead, and the old king has been bitten. The boy's stroke under the fire owns the page. No blood, no wound and no severed part is ever seen.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 49 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All three captions must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of any repeated letter; the words and their order are what must match. Captions carry no tail and belong to no mouth.

### C2 — The boy's stroke does not own the page
Wiglaf darting in low under the fire, driving his short sword up at the belly while the throat-fire dies, must be what the eye finds. If the bite or the knife outweighs it, the turn fails.

### C3 — Harm drawn
Blood, a wound, a severed head, or torn flesh anywhere is blocking; the bite stays a silhouette and the final blow lands beyond the panel's edge.

### C4 — Identity and order
Wiglaf shieldless, slight, beardless, dark curls; old Beowulf with boar helmet and white beard. The bite, the thrust, and the knife must read in that order. A misordered page, or a yellow shield on Wiglaf, is blocking.
