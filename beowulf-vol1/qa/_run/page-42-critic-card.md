## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 42 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Why does a dragon sleep on a pile of gold inside an old grave?

**Sources:** *Beowulf* 875–900 (the scop's song of Sigemund and the dragon), 2231–2277 (the hoard laid in the barrow); *Völsunga saga* (13th century), Sigurd and Fafnir; the Sutton Hoo ship burial (excavated 1939, Mound 1, about 625); the Staffordshire Hoard (found 2009 by Terry Herbert; about 4,600 items and fragments, mostly gold and silver war-gear).

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A page of a chronicle on warm ivory vellum with
a ruled margin. The dominant image is an ink-and-watercolour cutaway of a
grassy grave mound on a headland, its inside opened like a museum drawing:
a stone chamber heaped with gold — cups, a helmet, a shield boss, rings — and
curled around it, drawn in thin ink like a figure in an old manuscript, a
small serpent-dragon. A red-and-lapis interlace initial with a touch of gold
begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(above the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "Why does a dragon sleep on gold? In the old stories of the North, dragons guarded treasure hidden in the graves of people who died long ago, and only a hero could win it. The poet at Heorot sang of one: Sigemund. In later stories it was his son, Sigurd, who killed the dragon Fafnir."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(below the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "And people really did bury treasure with their kings. In 1939, under a mound at Sutton Hoo in England, diggers found the shape of a whole ship packed with a king's gold. In 2009, a man with a metal detector found thousands of pieces of gold war-gear in a field in Staffordshire."

> **LABEL** *(on the mound)*
> "a barrow"

> **LABEL** *(at the chamber)*
> "the treasure chamber"

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what the old king is walking toward — a hoard in a grave, and the thing that guards it.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 42 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two chronicle fields and two labels must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. "a barrow" sits on the mound; "the treasure chamber" sits at the opened chamber. The illuminated W is the first letter of "Why", not an extra letter. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of any repeated letter; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — Every claim reads as written
Sigemund, Sigurd and Fafnir, Sutton Hoo with its year and its ship, Staffordshire with its year and its metal detector, must read exactly as written. A changed name, year, or place is blocking.

### C3 — Not visibly a chronicle
The whole page must read at a glance as a page from an old chronicle: ivory vellum, ruled margin, brown ink and watercolour, an interlace initial in red and blue with gold. A painted oil scene, a story character, a balloon, or a caption box is blocking.

### C4 — The cutaway does not carry the idea
The opened grave mound with gold inside and a small ink dragon curled around it must own the page. A mound with no visible treasure, or no dragon, is blocking; the exact contents of the heap are nonblocking.
