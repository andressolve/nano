## Page intent

## PAGE 53

Dramatic. Evening by the barrow. The ten men who ran creep out of the trees, shields lowered, heads down. Wiglaf, the gold ring now at his neck, stands to meet them; behind him old Beowulf lies covered to the chest by his faded blue cloak, his face peaceful. Wiglaf speaks alone: they come back now, after running; they keep their lives but lose their names. Then he lays the old king's broken sword on the heaped gold by the barrow door, touches the ring at his neck, and says none of them will wear the gold, the old king paid for it with his life. The dominant turn: the Geats have to say goodbye to their king. Wiglaf owns every line; the ten men stay ashamed and silent. The reader turns to the farewell.

> **WIGLAF:** "Now you come back. He gave you everything you own. And when he needed you, you ran."

> **WIGLAF:** "You'll keep your lives. But you've lost your names. Nobody will ever sing about you."

lays the old king's broken sword on top of it. His other hand touches the gold ring at his neck.

> **WIGLAF:** "He won this gold for his people. Not one of you will ever wear a single ring of it."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "He gave me this one with his own hand."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "The rest he paid for with his life. I don't think any of us should keep it."

### C1 — Transcription fails
All five strings, every one Wiglaf's, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by Wiglaf's mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. A line placed directly under his previous balloon is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The reckoning does not land
The reader must see the men who ran come back ashamed and Wiglaf, alone and wearing the old king's ring, refuse them the gold, with the old king lying at peace behind. If Wiglaf does not plainly own the page, or the shamed men read as a welcome, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
Every balloon hangs from Wiglaf. A tail to any of the returning men, or a balloon that reads as their reply, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and object state
Wiglaf: slight, beardless, dark curls, freckles, the gold ring at his neck, no boar helmet. Old Beowulf lies covered to the chest by the faded blue cloak, face peaceful. The sword laid on the gold is broken, a grip with only a short stub of blade; a whole sword is blocking. The returning men's exact number is nonblocking; a blue-cloaked, white-bearded, or dark-curled boy among them is blocking.

### C5 — Integrity
No blood, no wound on the old king, no dragon. Wiglaf's hands carry the two actions in the last panel cleanly; a third hand is blocking, background hands are nonblocking.
