### Register — *Northern Saga*

Northern Saga.A painted northern saga in the tradition of the great book illustrators of a century ago: opaque oil on toned canvas board with visible, confident brushwork, a warm peat-brown underpainting showing through, thick impasto in the highlights, and strong simple shapes that read from across a room. Earth, sea and timber: peat brown, slate grey, sea grey, pine green, raw umber, oxide red, bone white, iron grey, and hearth-gold. Light like a stage: inside, the gold and red of hearth-fire and torchlight with real cast shadows and smoke under the rafters; outside, cold silver moonlight, sea fog, and flat grey northern daylight. One deep sea-blue is reserved for one character only, the hero Beowulf — bright on the young man, faded on the old; nothing else in the world is that blue. Materials painted with weight: riveted mail rings, rough undyed wool, grey fur, carved and tarred oak, gold shingles, wet black rock, peat water, reeds, iron, leather, amber beads, twisted gold, salt spray. Faces painted with character and weather, never pretty. Not smooth prestige-oil realism. No glossy concept-art surfaces, no airbrushed skin, no photographic rendering, no anime proportions, no children's-book softness, no plastic 3D render, no fantasy-game armour, no horned helmets, no glowing magic effects except where named. No blood, no wounds, no gore. No signature, monogram, artist's mark, or watermark in any corner.

Beowulf alone wears deep sea-blue. The land is the North about 500: Heorot gold-roofed on its green hill and hearth-lit inside; the fen and moor cold blue-black under mist and moon; grey-green sea and white cliffs; the Geats' coast and its hall, later burned; the grassy headland and its steaming barrow. **Two places must look like different worlds:** the hall beneath the mere (murky green-black water, then an air-filled cave lit by a strange pale fire, old giant-work arches), and the dragon's hoard (a stone chamber of heaped gold lit by a glowing golden banner). **The Chronicle pages are a different medium altogether** (see below).

### Hierarchy and staging

One dominant image plainly owns the page; the share named in the prompt is a construction target, and the generator should build a clear hierarchy rather than a stack of equal bands. When the page's turn is a quiet beat (an answer, a sentence, a face changing) and another panel carries action or a crowd, the quiet panel is still the largest image on the page by far, close and tall, and the busy panel is a shallow strip seen from far back with small figures; this generator favors action tableaux and must be told otherwise. At most two small reaction insets unless causality needs a strip. A clear top-to-bottom reading path. The first speaker in a panel stands on the LEFT; opposed speakers stand on opposite sides with each balloon on its owner's side; in a three-line exchange, A upper on A's side, B middle on B's side, A's second line lower on A's side; a reply is never readable before the line that prompts it and sits clearly lower than it, never level. Every balloon's tail ends beside its own speaker's mouth; a tail into open space, into a crowd, or toward an animal is a defect. Every visible speaker's mouth is visible and its tail corridor is clear of hands, props, and bystanders. Any exchange of two short lines in one panel where either line could plausibly be spoken by either character (a question and its answer, a check and its confirmation, a one-word address) is staged as two single-speaker beats, the questioner alone with mouth visible and the balloon beside that mouth, then the answerer alone the same way, as stacked strips or a strip cut into two panels read left to right; a shared two-shot with crossing tails is the most common attribution failure on this generator. A focal action uses exactly two hands, named. Consequential objects that the story counts (five of something, three of something) are drawn in a spaced row so the reader can count them, and the prompt says "count them". Sound cues render the exact letters given, with no added punctuation or extra words; the run length of a repeated letter is not a defect. Words painted on a story object (a banner, a plaque, a sign) are object text: they must read exactly as scripted, and the same object seen in two panels may carry them twice. Nothing else is lettered: no laugh lines, no invented captions, no labels. Vertical accumulation for time, consequence, descent, isolation, aftermath. Unnamed figures never carry a complete reserved identity stack: background Danes and Geats are men of middle age in brown, grey, rust and undyed wool, with brown, grey or dark-blond hair and full beards; never a named character's look (each prompt names the reserved looks). Hrothgar's oldest friend (unnamed, no balloon) is an old bald warrior with a grey braided beard. **Deaths are real and never drawn:** no blood, no wound, no body, no eating, no severed anything; a man taken by a monster is an empty bench, a trailing blanket, a dropped sword; a blow lands between panels or beyond a panel's edge; Beowulf's death is closed eyes and a hand held. Grendel's torn-off claw is a monster's limb — a long dark scaled arm with hooked claws, its shoulder end always lost in shadow, no blood or flesh. **The monsters never speak:** no balloon, no tail, no caption in their voice. Grendel is always partly in shadow, never seen whole and fully lit. The swords are never confused: each page's prompt names which sword is present and its state; a melted or shattered sword is never drawn whole again. No horned helmets anywhere; helmets are iron caps or boar-crested.

### Chronicle pages (Mode 4) — a different medium, same lettering law

A page whose mode is *chronicle* is not a story page. It is **a page from a chronicle**: the whole canvas is warm ivory vellum with a ruled brown-ink margin, drawn in iron-gall brown ink with fine pen hatching and transparent watercolour wash, with one illuminated initial in the Northern insular style — a large letter built of red and lapis-blue interlace knotwork with a touch of raised gold leaf, the kind drawn in English monasteries about the year 1000. No oil paint, no full-bleed painted scene, no story character, no balloon, no caption box. The illuminated initial is the first letter of the first word of CHRONICLE FIELD 1, made large and decorated, with the rest of that word following it in the field; it is never an extra letter. CHRONICLE FIELD text is lettered in the prose serif (36–42 px, 38–52 characters a line, left-aligned, calm ragged edge) in dark brown ink directly on the plain vellum, with generous space around it and nothing drawn behind it. LABEL strings are short hand-lettered ink labels sitting beside their map point, date, or object, at the small-label level, never smaller than about 32 px. The one dominant image (a map, a museum study of a real object, a cutaway of a hall or a grave mound, a page of an old manuscript) owns the page; the fields sit on plain vellum beside or below it. The reader must know at a glance that they have stepped out of the story.
