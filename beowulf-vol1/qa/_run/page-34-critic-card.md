## Page intent

## PAGE 34

Dramatic. Late night, the hall empty except for two men by the hearth. Old Hrothgar turns the golden hilt in the firelight, its carving of giants and great waves, and tells young Beowulf that the giants who made it were strong too, and a flood took every one of them. Then, leaning in close and serious, he warns him not to let pride grow: strength is lent for a little while, and sickness, the sword, fire or old age come for everyone. Beowulf, rubbing the gold ring at his neck, nods politely at the fire and says "Yes, my lord," and the caption tells us he didn't hear a word. The page belongs to the old king's warning and the young man's polite, absent face; the empty hall stays dark around them. The dominant turn: the warning has been given, and it is time to go home.

### C1 — Transcription fails
All six strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line directly under its speaker's previous balloon is owned even if its tail wanders. Hilt carving that reads as letters is extra text.

### C2 — The warning does not land
The eye must find the old king alone with Beowulf by the fire, turning the hilt, then leaning in to warn him, and Beowulf nodding at the fire without listening. If the warning reads as public, or Beowulf looks moved, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
All four warning lines are Hrothgar's, in order; "Yes, my lord." is Beowulf's. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his; a Hrothgar line tailed to Beowulf, or the reply read before the warning, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Hrothgar: very thin, waist-length white beard, gold circlet, crimson. Beowulf: young, massive, clean-shaven, tawny hair tied back, crooked nose, white left-brow notch, sea-blue cloak, gold neck-ring. A short-bearded Hrothgar or a bearded or ringless Beowulf is blocking.

### C5 — The hilt and the hands
The golden hilt has no blade and is held in exactly two of Hrothgar's hands; a whole giant's sword or a third hand on it is blocking.
