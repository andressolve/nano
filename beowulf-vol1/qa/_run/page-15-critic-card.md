## Page intent

## PAGE 15

Dramatic. Late afternoon in Heorot. Queen Wealhtheow carries the great gold cup high down the long hall, past every bench of Danes, and last of all to the stranger, who rises to meet her. She offers it, but her eyes go past him to her two small sons on the bench: men have sworn great things here before, and her boys have never slept a night in their father's hall. Beowulf takes the cup in both hands and holds it up to the whole hall: tomorrow night they will; tonight he wins or dies in this hall. The dominant turn: he has sworn it in front of everyone, and night is coming. The queen's walk with the cup owns the page; her glance at the boys is the hinge; the oath is the answer. The reader turns into the night.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 15 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four strings, one caption and three spoken lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth or field by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The oath does not land
The page must read as the queen's walk down the hall with the great gold cup, ending in Beowulf lifting it to the whole hall as he swears. If the cup never reaches him, or the oath reads as private rather than said to everyone, the turn fails. Judge by what the eye finds first, not by area.

### C3 — Attribution in the exchange
Both lines about the sons and the sworn things hang from Wealhtheow's mouth; "Then tomorrow night they will…" hangs from Beowulf's mouth and reads after hers. A Wealhtheow line tailed to Beowulf, or a Beowulf line tailed to the queen, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and state
Beowulf: tall tawny-haired clean-shaven young man with the cleft chin, broken nose and notched left brow, in mail and deep sea-blue cloak, no gold neck-ring. Wealhtheow: auburn braids, white veil, forest green, the gold cup. Her eyes going to two small boys must read. A neck-ring on Beowulf, a beard, or a second woman in green is blocking; repeated faces among the Danes are nonblocking.
