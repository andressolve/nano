## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 19 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The sound cue and the caption must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth or field by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. The howl is a sound cue, never a balloon.

### C2 — The victory does not land
Spectacle mode: the wrestling in the shaking hall must own the page, and the second image must show Beowulf in the doorway holding the long scaled arm while Grendel flees far down the hill. If the reader cannot tell Grendel has gone and his arm stayed, the turn fails.

### C3 — Gore
The arm's shoulder end is lost in shadow; no blood, torn flesh, or wound on the arm or on Grendel. Any of these is blocking.

### C4 — The arm is wrong
A long dark scaled arm with hooked claws, longer than Beowulf is tall. A human-looking arm or a small paw is blocking.

### C5 — Identity and state
Beowulf in his tunic only, no mail or sword, cleft chin and notched brow; Grendel hairless and partly in shadow, no balloon. A sword in Beowulf's hands is blocking.
