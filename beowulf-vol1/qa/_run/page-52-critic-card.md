## Page intent

## PAGE 52

Dramatic. Beside the barrow, Wiglaf kneels with gold heaped at old Beowulf's feet, and the old king looks at it and smiles: it is for their people. Then he takes off the twisted gold ring and presses it into Wiglaf's hand, the boar helmet between them, and names the boy the last of their family, asking for a mound high on the headland that sailors will call Beowulf's Barrow. Last, the quiet panel that owns the page: the old king's eyes closed, his head back against the stones, the sea wind in his white hair, Wiglaf holding his hand; one caption says he died. The death is closed eyes and a held hand, nothing more. The dominant turn: the men who ran away are coming back. The reader turns to see what Wiglaf will say to them.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 52 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four strings, three spoken lines and one caption, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of any repeated letter; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The death does not land
The last panel must own the page by eye: the old king's eyes closed, head back against the stones, wind in his white hair, Wiglaf holding his hand, and the caption saying he died. If the reader cannot tell he has died, or the death reads before the ring is given, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"Good. That's for our people…", "Take my ring, and my helmet, and my mail…" and "Build me a mound…" all hang from old Beowulf's mouth; Wiglaf speaks no line. Any of these hanging from Wiglaf is blocking.

### C4 — The ring passes
The twisted gold ring is at Beowulf's neck, then pressed into Wiglaf's hand, then Wiglaf's. Two rings at once, the ring still on Beowulf after he gives it, or no visible handing-over is blocking. The boar helmet lies in the grass; on anyone's head it is blocking.

### C5 — Identity and integrity
Old Beowulf: huge, short white beard, crooked nose, notched left brow, faded blue. Wiglaf: slight, beardless, dark curls, freckles. Confusing the two is blocking. Blood, a wound, or a bite mark is blocking; the hands in the ring panel must be one clear exchange.
