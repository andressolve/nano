## Page intent

## PAGE 5

Illustrated prose. Time passes in one long breath. Heorot stands at night under snow, its gold roof white, its doors shut, not one light in it, while below the hill small houses huddle with barred shutters and a thin glow in each. Then, by day, inside the cold empty hall, old Hrothgar sits alone in his high seat, gaunter and older, staring at the dead hearth. The prose tells it plainly: Unferth's swords failed, whoever stayed after dark was gone by morning, soon nobody stayed, the king waited winter after winter for anyone to help, and sailors carried the story across the sea. The dominant turn: the story is crossing the sea, looking for someone. The dark snowbound hall owns the page; the lonely king is its echo. The reader turns to find who hears it.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 5 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All three prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The long wait does not land
The dark snowbound hall must own the page, unlit above lit little houses, and the lower image must show the old king alone in the empty hall by day. If the hall shows light, figures crowd it, or the king has company, the turn fails.

### C3 — Not illustrated prose
The page must read as illustrated prose: one parchment prose field over plain sky, never over the king's face, and no balloons or captions. A balloon on the page is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Hrothgar: very thin, older, waist-length white beard, gold circlet, crimson robe, in his high seat. A short-bearded or heavy king is blocking.
