## Page intent

## PAGE 33

Dramatic. Evening in Heorot, the hall full of Danes, the old king beaming on his high seat with the golden hilt from under the lake. Beowulf, wearing the queen's gold neck-ring, finds Unferth and holds Hrunting out across both palms, exactly as Unferth once offered it to him at the lake, and thanks him: it's a good sword. Unferth will not take it; ashamed, he says everyone knows it failed. Beowulf says it again, louder, so the whole hall hears, and calls him generous. Unferth takes the sword back and bows his head over it, with nothing to say. The page belongs to the two former rivals and the sword passing between them; the king and the watching Danes stay subordinate. The dominant turn: the rivals are friends, and the old king has something to say to Beowulf alone. The reader turns to hear it.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 33 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All five strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The sword does not pass
The eye must find Beowulf holding Hrunting out across both palms to Unferth, then Unferth holding it, head bowed. If the sword never visibly passes, or the page reads as a quarrel rather than a gift, the turn fails; the king and the Danes stay subordinate.

### C3 — Attribution in the exchange
"Your sword, Unferth…" and "Then everyone here…" are Beowulf's; "A good sword?…" is Unferth's and reads before the reply. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his; a swapped or shared tail, or a balloon on Hrothgar, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Beowulf: young, huge, clean-shaven, tawny hair tied back, crooked nose, white left-brow notch, sea-blue cloak, gold neck-ring. Unferth: lean, black hair, short pointed black beard, black and silver. A bearded or ringless Beowulf, or the two confusable, is blocking; repeated Danish faces are nonblocking.

### C5 — Object state
Hrunting is one blade with wavy water-patterns; the king holds only a golden hilt. A whole giant's sword, a second Hrunting, or a claw over the doors is blocking.
