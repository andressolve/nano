## Page intent

## PAGE 10

Dramatic. Heorot by day. Old King Hrothgar leans out of his high seat with both hands reaching to the young stranger, remembering the debt he paid for Beowulf's father; Beowulf answers that this is why he has come and asks for one night in the hall. ## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 10 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Heorot, by day.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Heorot in daylight, the high seat at the far
end. HROTHGAR leans forward from it, both hands reaching out. BEOWULF stands
before him on the left, helmet under his arm, the fourteen Geats behind.
WEALHTHEOW stands beside the king. UNFERTH sits on his stool at the king's
feet, watching.

> **HROTHGAR:** "Beowulf! I knew your father. When his own people drove him out, I paid his debt and gave him a home."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "He told me, my lord. That's why I'm here. Give me one night in your hall, and I'll finish Grendel for you."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close on Beowulf, grinning, holding up his two enormous
empty hands.

> **BEOWULF:** "They say he never carries a sword. Then neither will I. I'll fight him with these."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Unferth at the king's feet: heavy-lidded eyes
narrowing, the corner of his mouth lifting in a slow sneer.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "The king's spokesman, Unferth, didn't like anyone the king smiled at."

**Why you turn the page:** the young stranger has boasted in front of the whole hall, and Unferth is about to make him pay for it.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 10 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. The caption is tail-free.

### C2 — The boast does not land
The welcome must own the page: the old king reaching out with both hands from his high seat to the young stranger. Then Beowulf holds up his two empty hands, and last Unferth's face narrows into a sneer under the caption naming him. If the empty hands are not plainly empty, or Unferth's sneer is missing, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution in the welcome
"Beowulf! I knew your father…" hangs from Hrothgar's mouth; "He told me, my lord…" from Beowulf's, clearly after it; "They say he never carries a sword…" from Beowulf's in the close. Unferth, Wealhtheow and the Geats are silent; a balloon on any of them, or a swapped or shared tail, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Hrothgar: very thin, waist-length white beard, gold circlet, crimson. Beowulf: huge, clean-shaven, tawny hair tied back, crooked nose, left-brow notch, deep sea-blue. Unferth: lean, black hair, short black pointed beard, black and silver. Wealhtheow: auburn braids, green, veil. Any two of them reading alike is blocking; repeated Geat faces are nonblocking.

### C5 — Continuity
No gold neck-ring on Beowulf, no cup in the queen's hands, nothing hung above the doors. Any of these present is blocking.
