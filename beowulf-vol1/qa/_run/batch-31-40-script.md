# Exact script — pages 31–40

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 31 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the hall beneath the mere.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Light floods the cave like sunrise. BEOWULF
stands alone in it, holding up the giant's sword — and its long blade is
melting away like an icicle in the sun, bright drops falling and vanishing.
Hrunting lies on the floor behind him. Of her, only a dark mass of weed-hair
at the panel's edge, still.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "She fell. Light filled the hall under the lake like sunrise. And the giant's blade began to melt, like ice in spring."

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Close on Beowulf's hand: only a heavy golden hilt left,
carved with tiny pictures. In his other hand, he has picked up Hrunting.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "Soon only the golden hilt was left in his hand."

**Why you turn the page:** he has won, and he is still at the bottom of the lake.

## PAGE 32 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the shore of the mere, late afternoon.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Far up the cliff path, small, the Danes riding away.
On the bank below, the thirteen Geats sit by the black water, staring at it.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "On the shore, the Danes waited until the afternoon. Then they gave up, and went home. Only the thirteen Geats were still there."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** BEOWULF bursts up out of the black water,
holding the golden hilt high in one fist and Hrunting in the other; the Geats
leaping to their feet.

> **BEOWULF:** "What's the matter with all of you? Did you think I wasn't coming back?"

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The Geats haul him out onto the rocks, laughing, pulling
off his helmet. Beowulf, dripping, lifts Hrunting.

> **BEOWULF:** "Take me to Hrothgar. I've got a sword to give back."

**Why you turn the page:** Unferth is about to get his failed sword back.

# MOVEMENT V — THE OLD KING'S WARNING · pp 33–36

---

## PAGE 33 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Heorot, evening.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The hall full of Danes, watching; on his high
seat HROTHGAR, holding the golden hilt, is beaming. BEOWULF, on the left, holds Hrunting out across both palms to UNFERTH, on the right —
exactly the way Unferth offered it to him at the lake.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "That evening Beowulf told the whole hall what had happened under the lake. Then he went to find Unferth."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Your sword, Unferth. Thank you for the loan. It's a good sword."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Unferth, on the right, doesn't take it. He looks
ashamed.

> **UNFERTH:** "A good sword? It failed you. Everyone here knows it failed you."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Then everyone here can hear me say it again. It's a good sword, and you're a generous man to lend it."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Unferth takes Hrunting and bows his head over it.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "Unferth took back his sword, and for the second time in his life, he couldn't think of a single thing to say."

**Why you turn the page:** the rivals are friends, and the old king has something to say to Beowulf alone.

## PAGE 34 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Heorot, late night, by the hearth.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The hall empty except for two men by the
fire. HROTHGAR, on the left, holds the golden hilt in his long hands, turning
it to the firelight: carved on it are tiny giants and great waves. BEOWULF,
on the right, sits on the bench, young and enormous, rubbing the gold ring
at his neck.

> **HROTHGAR:** "This hilt is older than any king. The giants who made it were strong too, Beowulf."
>
> **HROTHGAR:** "And a great flood took every one of them."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close on Hrothgar, leaning in, serious.

> **HROTHGAR:** "Listen to an old man. Don't let pride grow in you. Your strength is yours for a little while."
>
> **HROTHGAR:** "Then sickness, or the sword, or fire, or old age will come for you. They come for everyone."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Beowulf, on the right, nodding politely, looking at the
fire, already thinking about something else.

> **BEOWULF:** "Yes, my lord."
>
> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "Beowulf was nineteen. He nodded, and didn't hear a word."

**Why you turn the page:** the warning has been given, and it is time to go home.

## PAGE 35 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the Danish shore, and the Geats' harbour.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The white-cliffed beach at morning, the ship
ready. HROTHGAR holds BEOWULF in his arms like a son, his long white beard
against the young man's shoulder, weeping. Wealhtheow and Unferth stand
behind. The thirteen Geats load horses and chests of gold.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The Geats' harbour: Beowulf, grinning, handing gold cups
and arm-rings out from a chest to a crowd of Geats — the same crowd that
once laughed.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the morning sky)*
> "In the morning, at the shore, the old king held Beowulf like a son and wept. He knew they would never see each other again.
>
> The ship sailed home low in the water, so loaded was it with gold and horses. And Beowulf gave nearly all of it away. In the land of the Geats, nobody ever called him the slow boy by the fire again."

**Why you turn the page:** the young hero is home and famous, and he has forgotten the warning.

## PAGE 36 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Why did Unferth's sword have a name, as if it were a person?

**Sources:** *Beowulf* 1455–1464 (Hrunting), 2680 (Naegling), 303–306 and 1448–1454 (boar-figures on helmets); pattern-welding in early medieval swords (Hilda Ellis Davidson, *The Sword in Anglo-Saxon England*, 1962); the Sutton Hoo helmet (British Museum 1939,1010.93), with boars' heads at the ends of its eyebrows and a flying creature formed by its nose and brows; the Benty Grange boar-crested helmet (7th century).

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A page of a chronicle on warm ivory vellum with
a ruled margin. Two museum studies in ink and watercolour, side by side: on
the left, a long double-edged sword blade standing upright, its surface
showing swirling herringbone patterns like flowing water; on the right, the
Sutton Hoo helmet as reconstructed — an iron face-mask with eyebrows that
end in little gilded boar heads, a nose and brows forming a creature with
spread wings. A red-and-lapis interlace initial with a touch of gold begins
the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(above the studies, on plain vellum)*
> "Why did a sword have a name, like Hrunting? Because a good sword was almost a person. A smith twisted rods of iron together and hammered them into one blade, over and over. When it was polished, the blade showed patterns like flowing water. A sword like that was worth a fortune, and it was handed down from father to son."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(below the studies, on plain vellum)*
> "Helmets were treasures too. The one found in a king's grave at Sutton Hoo, in England, has a winged creature across its face and little boars' heads at the ends of its eyebrows, like the boar-helmets in the poem."

> **LABEL** *(beside the blade)*
> "a pattern-welded blade"

> **LABEL** *(beside the helmet)*
> "the Sutton Hoo helmet, about 625"

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what a named sword meant — and the story is about to jump fifty years.

# MOVEMENT VI — FIFTY WINTERS · pp 37–42

---

## PAGE 37 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Geats' coast, fifty years later.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A sturdy timber hall on a rise above the
Geats' harbour, peaceful, smoke from its roof, fields and ships below. On
its steps stands BEOWULF, about seventy: still the biggest man in sight but
heavy now, shoulders rounded, long white hair tied back at the nape, a short
close-trimmed white beard, the same crooked nose and notched left eyebrow, a
faded blue cloak, Wealhtheow's twisted gold ring at his neck. He watches the
sea, rubbing the ring with his thumb.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the sky)*
> "Fifty winters went by.
>
> Beowulf's uncle, the king, was killed in a war across the sea, and then his son after him. The Geats came to Beowulf and asked him to be their king, and he was, for all the long years after. He sent his ships to any friend who asked, and no enemy dared to attack the Geats.
>
> Beowulf was an old man now, his hair and beard white. But he was still the biggest man in any room, and he still wore Queen Wealhtheow's gold ring."

**Why you turn the page:** fifty peaceful years are about to end.

## PAGE 38 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the headland and the barrow, night.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A high grassy headland over the sea at night, an ancient
grave mound with a dark stone doorway; a ragged young runaway (unnamed)
scrambling into it, looking back over his shoulder in terror.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "On a headland by the sea, a runaway hiding from a beating crawled into an ancient grave mound."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 70%.** Inside the stone chamber: a mountain of gold —
cups, dishes, helmets, rings — and coiled around and over it, asleep, an
enormous dragon: a serpent body thick as an oak, bronze-and-black scales with
faint ember seams glowing between them, folded ragged wings, a long horned
head resting on the gold. The tiny runaway tiptoes at the edge, lifting a
single golden cup.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "He found a dragon asleep on a mountain of gold. He took one golden cup, and ran."

**Why you turn the page:** the dragon is going to wake up.

## PAGE 39 · *spectacle*

**Location:** two — the barrow, and the Geats' land, night.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Close: one huge molten-gold eye opening in the dark.

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The dragon's head low over the hoard, sniffing at a
small gap in the gold where the cup had been.

> **CAPTION** *(across panels 1 and 2)*
> "The dragon knew every cup in its hoard. It knew one was gone."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 60%.** The night sky over the Geats' coast: the dragon
in flight, wings spread, pouring a river of fire down onto the villages. On
the rise, Beowulf's great hall is going up in flames.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "When night fell, it came out burning. And the first great hall to burn was the king's own."

**Why you turn the page:** the old king's own hall has burned.

## PAGE 40 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the ruins of Beowulf's hall, dawn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Dawn. The burned black timbers of Beowulf's
hall, smoke still rising. OLD BEOWULF stands in the ashes, huge and still.
Running up to him, on the right, soot on his face, comes WIGLAF, seventeen:
slight and lanky, short dark curly hair, freckles, a too-big mail shirt, a
round yellow wooden shield.

> **CAPTION** *(top left)*
> "In the morning, old Beowulf, who had fought the fire all night, stood in the ashes of his hall."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "My lord! The dragon has burned half the coast."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "A runaway came to us. He stole a cup from its treasure, and he knows where it sleeps."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Beowulf, on the left, looks at the boy as if seeing him
properly for the first time.

> **BEOWULF:** "You're Wiglaf, my cousin's boy. You were a baby the last time I looked."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Tell the smith to make me a shield all of iron. Wood burns. And find me that runaway."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wiglaf, on the right, eager; Beowulf, on the left,
shaking his head.

> **WIGLAF:** "I'll gather the army!"
>
> **BEOWULF:** "No army. I killed Grendel with my own two hands. I'll kill this thing myself."

**Why you turn the page:** the old king means to fight the dragon alone.
