## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 12 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All three prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text anywhere; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Every number word in the prose (five nights, two more nights, the seventh morning, nine sea-monsters) must read as written. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The true story does not land
One image must own the page: a boy in a mail shirt in a black storm sea, sword in his fist, fighting a huge tusked sea-beast whose coils hold his legs, with another boy's head small and far off. If the beast or the boy's struggle cannot be found, or the far-off rival is missing, the turn fails.

### C3 — The boy is not young Beowulf
He must read as a big boy, younger than the hero of the surrounding pages, with the same crooked nose and tawny hair tied back, beardless, in mail with no cloak. A grown man, a deep sea-blue cloak, a gold neck-ring, or a helmet is blocking.

### C4 — Violence drawn
No blood, no wound, no dead or cut beast. Blocking if present. Wave and lightning detail and the beast's exact anatomy are nonblocking.
