## Page intent

## PAGE 26

Illustrated prose. One great image owns the page: a narrow goat-path along grey crags with a small line of riders on it, the king, the Danes, Beowulf in helmet and mail with his Geats; and below them the lake, black and steaming in a cleft of rock under dripping trees, a waterfall pouring in, water-snakes on the rocks, and at the water's edge a hunted stag standing with hounds behind it, refusing to jump. ## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 26 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The lake does not carry the dread
The page must read as illustrated prose with one great image: riders small on a cliff path, and below them the black steaming lake with its waterfall, water-snakes, and a stag at bay refusing to jump. If the stag cannot be found, or the lake reads as an ordinary pond, the turn fails.

### C3 — Prose over busy art
The prose field sits on calm ground. A field laid over the stag, the riders, or the water so that words or the stag are lost is blocking.

### C4 — Identity at small scale
Beowulf must be findable among the riders: the tallest, in mail and helmet, the only one in sea-blue. Another rider in sea-blue is blocking; the exact number of Geats is nonblocking.
