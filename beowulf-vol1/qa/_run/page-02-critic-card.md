## Page intent

## PAGE 2

Dramatic. Inside Heorot by firelight, a feast: the long hearth, carved pillars, shields on the walls, crowded benches. King Hrothgar, very thin with a white beard to his waist, raises his arms and tells the hall to drink, and gives the first cup to his queen; Wealhtheow, holding the great gold cup in both hands, gives it back to the king who built the hall. Behind him his oldest friend laughs. Then Unferth, the king's sly spokesman on his low stool, rises with his cup and promises a speech he has already written. Last, in one strip, the old poet sings by the fire while, outside the open doors in the blackness, two coal-red eyes watch. The dominant turn: the happiest hall in the world doesn't know it is being watched. The joy of the feast owns the page; the eyes stay small, low and outside. The reader turns because the thing outside has heard every note.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 2 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Heorot, night, a feast.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Inside Heorot by firelight: a long hall with a
fire burning down the middle, carved pillars, shields hung on the walls,
benches crowded with feasting warriors. At the far end on his high seat,
KING HROTHGAR — tall, very thin, a long flowing white beard to his waist, a
gold circlet, a crimson robe — raises his arms to the hall. Beside him
stands QUEEN WEALHTHEOW, tall, auburn braids under a white veil, a green
gown, holding a great gold cup in both hands. On a low stool at the king's
feet sits UNFERTH, lean and dark with a short pointed black beard, in black
and silver. Behind the king's shoulder, an old bald warrior with a grey
braided beard (unnamed) laughs.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the dark rafters)*
> "King Hrothgar of the Danes had built the greatest hall in the world. He named it Heorot."
>
> **HROTHGAR:** "Drink, all of you! Tonight Heorot belongs to everyone in it. Wealhtheow, my queen, the first cup is yours to give."
>
> **WEALHTHEOW:** "Then I give it to the king who built this hall."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Unferth, on his stool on the right, rises halfway and
lifts his own cup to the hall with a showman's flourish, pleased with
himself.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "At the king's feet sat Unferth, his spokesman, who always had the cleverest thing to say."
>
> **UNFERTH:** "A hall this fine deserves a fine speech, my lord. Luckily, I've written one."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Two beats in one strip. Left: the king's poet, an old
man with a small harp, singing by the fire. Right: outside the open doors,
in the blackness beyond the light, two coal-red eyes, low down, watching.

> **CAPTION** *(over the poet)*
> "The king's poet sang of how the world was made."
>
> **CAPTION** *(over the darkness outside)*
> "Out in the dark, something heard every note."

**Why you turn the page:** the happiest hall in the world doesn't know it is being watched.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All seven strings, four captions and three spoken lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly beside its speaker, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The watching does not land
The feast must own the page as a hall full of joy, and the last strip must show two coal-red eyes in the dark outside the open doors. If the eyes are missing, unreadable, or inside the hall among the feasters, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution in the toast
"Drink, all of you!…" hangs from Hrothgar's mouth; "Then I give it to the king…" from Wealhtheow's, read after his; "A hall this fine…" from Unferth's. The poet, the old friend and the eyes have no balloons. A swapped or shared tail, or a balloon from the poet or the eyes, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Hrothgar: very thin, waist-length white beard, gold circlet, crimson. Wealhtheow: auburn braids under a white veil, green gown, the great gold cup. Unferth: lean, short black pointed beard, black and silver. The old friend must not read as a second Hrothgar; a waist-length white beard on him is blocking. Repeated background faces are nonblocking.
