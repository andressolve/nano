## Page intent

## PAGE 24

Spectacle, across two places. Out of the black lake at night rises a second monster: Grendel's mother, larger than her son, a long wolfish jaw, pale green eyes, black-green weed-hair streaming water. The page belongs to her rising. Then Heorot: the Danes asleep under the hanging claw, a vast dark shape in the doorway reaching up to it, the king's oldest friend sitting up and half-drawing his sword. A moment later the bench is empty, a sword lies on the floor, the place above the doors where the claw hung is bare, and the hall is on its feet with torches while the queen holds her two small sons, safe. The dominant turn: there is a second monster, and it has taken the king's friend. The taking happens between the panels; nothing is shown. She never speaks. Beowulf is not here.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 24 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both captions must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, tail-free, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. Any balloon, tail, or sound given to the monster is blocking.

### C2 — The second monster does not own the page
The page must read as spectacle, and the eye must find first the creature rising from the black lake. If the hall panels outweigh her, or she cannot be told from Grendel (she has weed-hair, a long wolf jaw and green eyes; he is bald with ember eyes), the turn fails.

### C3 — The taking does not read
The reader must see the claw hanging and a dark shape reaching for it, then an empty bench, a dropped sword, and the bare place above the doors. If the claw is still hanging in the last panel, or the reader cannot tell someone was taken, it is blocking.

### C4 — Death drawn
No blood, wound, body, or struggle on screen. A visible body or wound is blocking; the friend simply gone is correct.

### C5 — Identity of the friend and the queen
The old friend is bald with a grey braided beard and must not read as the king (long white beard, gold circlet). The queen and her two boys are safe. A friend who reads as Hrothgar is blocking; the exact number of Danes is nonblocking.
