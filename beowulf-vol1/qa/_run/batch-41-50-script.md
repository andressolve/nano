# Exact script — pages 41–50

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 41 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the forge, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** A stone forge shed lit orange; a smith
(unnamed) hammering a great round shield of dark iron on the anvil. OLD
BEOWULF sits on a bench, on the left, watching the sparks. WIGLAF sits beside
him, on the right, gathering his courage.

> **WIGLAF:** "My lord, can I ask you something? Were you ever afraid? Of Grendel, or the lake, or anything?"
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Every time."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close on Beowulf's old face in the forge light,
remembering.

> **BEOWULF:** "When I was a boy, everyone laughed at me. So I crossed the sea and boasted in a king's hall."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Everyone heard me. After that, I couldn't run away."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wiglaf, on the right, leaning in; Beowulf, on the left,
with a ghost of his old grin.

> **WIGLAF:** "Before he died, my father said our family always stands by you. Let me come tomorrow."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Come, then, and bring ten men. You can watch. But the dragon is mine."

**Why you turn the page:** tomorrow the old king walks out to the dragon, and Wiglaf will be watching.

## PAGE 42 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Why does a dragon sleep on a pile of gold inside an old grave?

**Sources:** *Beowulf* 875–900 (the scop's song of Sigemund and the dragon), 2231–2277 (the hoard laid in the barrow); *Völsunga saga* (13th century), Sigurd and Fafnir; the Sutton Hoo ship burial (excavated 1939, Mound 1, about 625); the Staffordshire Hoard (found 2009 by Terry Herbert; about 4,600 items and fragments, mostly gold and silver war-gear).

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A page of a chronicle on warm ivory vellum with
a ruled margin. The dominant image is an ink-and-watercolour cutaway of a
grassy grave mound on a headland, its inside opened like a museum drawing:
a stone chamber heaped with gold — cups, a helmet, a shield boss, rings — and
curled around it, drawn in thin ink like a figure in an old manuscript, a
small serpent-dragon. A red-and-lapis interlace initial with a touch of gold
begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(above the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "Why does a dragon sleep on gold? In the old stories of the North, dragons guarded treasure hidden in the graves of people who died long ago, and only a hero could win it. The poet at Heorot sang of one: Sigemund. In later stories it was his son, Sigurd, who killed the dragon Fafnir."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(below the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "And people really did bury treasure with their kings. In 1939, under a mound at Sutton Hoo in England, diggers found the shape of a whole ship packed with a king's gold. In 2009, a man with a metal detector found thousands of pieces of gold war-gear in a field in Staffordshire."

> **LABEL** *(on the mound)*
> "a barrow"

> **LABEL** *(at the chamber)*
> "the treasure chamber"

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what the old king is walking toward — a hoard in a grave, and the thing that guards it.

## PAGE 43 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the headland above the barrow.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** The grassy headland over the grey sea. The
barrow below, a stream running out of its stone door, steaming. OLD BEOWULF
sits alone on a rock in mail, the iron shield leaning against his knee, the
boar-crested helmet beside him, looking out to sea. Behind him on the slope,
ten armed men and WIGLAF with his yellow shield wait; the ragged runaway
points a shaking finger at the barrow.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the grey sky)*
> "The runaway led Beowulf and his men to the headland, shaking so hard he could barely point. Steam rose from the stream that ran out of the barrow's stone door.
>
> Old Beowulf sat down on a rock and looked at the sea for a long time. He thought about the boy by the fire, and Grendel's claw, and the black lake. And he thought about old Hrothgar by the hearth in Heorot, all those years ago: sickness, or the sword, or fire, or old age."

**Why you turn the page:** the old king has remembered the warning, and he is about to say it out loud.

## PAGE 44 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the headland above the barrow.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** OLD BEOWULF, on the left, getting slowly to his feet,
putting on the boar-crested helmet. WIGLAF, on the right, watching him.

> **BEOWULF:** "An old king told me once that one day fire, or the sword, or old age would come for me."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "He was right. It's all three, all at once."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Beowulf, on the left, turns to the ten men; Wiglaf,
on the right, steps forward.

> **BEOWULF:** "Wait here on the hill, all of you. This isn't your fight."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "My lord, you'd send an army to help anyone else. Let us help you."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 40%.** Beowulf walks down the slope alone toward the
steaming stone door, small in the frame, the iron shield on his arm, his
sword Naegling drawn. He speaks without looking back.

> **BEOWULF:** "For fifty years they've sung that I never needed anyone, Wiglaf. I'm too old to start now. Stay."

**Why you turn the page:** the old king is walking alone toward the dragon.

## PAGE 45 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the barrow door.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** OLD BEOWULF at the barrow's stone door, head thrown back,
roaring a challenge into it.

> **BEOWULF:** "Come out, dragon! Come out and fight the king of the Geats!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 70%.** The DRAGON bursts out of the barrow in a river
of fire, wings spreading, ember seams blazing. Beowulf crouches behind the
iron shield; the flames split around it on both sides.

**Why you turn the page:** the fire is on him.

---

## PAGE 46 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the barrow door.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** OLD BEOWULF strikes Naegling against the dragon's neck;
sparks fly; the blade scores a scale and stops.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "Naegling, his old sword, bit into the dragon, but not deep enough."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 65%.** The dragon's fire wraps all the way around
Beowulf; the iron shield glows red; he is a dark shape inside the flame,
still standing.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "And the fire came again."

**Why you turn the page:** the old king is burning, and his men are watching.

---

## PAGE 47 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the headland above the barrow.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The slope: all ten men are running for the
woods, dropping their spears. WIGLAF, alone, has turned on them,
on the right, shouting after them.

> **WIGLAF:** "Where are you going? We promised him!"
>
> **WIGLAF:** "In his hall, when he gave us these helmets and swords, we promised to stand by him if he ever needed us!"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Their backs disappearing into the trees.

> **WIGLAF:** "He needs us now!"
>
> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "Not one of them turned around."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Wiglaf, alone, lifting his yellow shield, running down
the slope into the smoke toward the fire.

> **WIGLAF:** "Then I'll go by myself."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is running into the fire.

## PAGE 48 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the barrow door.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** A blast of fire: WIGLAF's yellow wooden shield bursts into
flame and he flings it away.

**Panel 2 — 30%.** OLD BEOWULF, on the left, hauls him behind the iron
shield, furious and astonished. Wiglaf, on the right, shaking.

> **BEOWULF:** "I told you to stay on the hill!"
>
> **WIGLAF:** "You told me you were afraid every time. So am I. I came anyway."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Beowulf swings Naegling with all his strength
into the dragon's head — and the old blade shatters into pieces.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "Beowulf struck with all his strength, and Naegling broke in pieces. His arm was too strong for any sword."

**Why you turn the page:** the old king has no sword left.

---

## PAGE 49 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the barrow door.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The dragon's great jaws close on OLD BEOWULF at the neck
and shoulder; we see the jaws and his helmet in silhouette against the fire,
nothing more.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "The dragon's teeth closed on the old king's neck."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** WIGLAF darts in low under
the fire and drives his short sword up into the dragon's belly between the
ember seams. The fire in its throat goes out.

> **CAPTION** *(across the panel)*
> "Wiglaf struck low, under the fire, into its belly."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Beowulf draws the long knife from his belt and swings it;
the blow lands beyond the panel's edge. The dragon's head falls away.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "Beowulf drew his knife and finished it."

**Why you turn the page:** the dragon is dead, and the old king has been bitten.

## PAGE 50 · *dramatic*

**Location:** beside the barrow.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** OLD BEOWULF sits against the barrow's stones,
helmet off, his face grey, his white hair loose. WIGLAF kneels beside him,
on the right, bathing his face with water from the stream. The dragon is a
dark dead coil in the background.

> **BEOWULF:** "Its teeth were poisoned. I can feel it in me. Sickness too, then. Old Hrothgar left nothing out."
>
> **WIGLAF:** "Don't talk like that, my lord. Please."

**Panel 2 — 50%.** Beowulf, on the left, grips the boy's arm and looks at
him properly.

> **BEOWULF:** "Fifty years I never needed anyone, Wiglaf. Today I did, and you came."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Now go into the barrow, quickly. Bring out the treasure. I want to see what I've won for our people before I die."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy has to go into the dragon's grave.
