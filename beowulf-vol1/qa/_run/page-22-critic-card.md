## Page intent

## PAGE 22

Dramatic. The victory feast: Heorot full of light and people, every bench crowded, gifts laid before the high seat for Beowulf, a golden banner, a boar-crested helmet, a mail shirt, a sword, horses through the open doors. The old poet sings with his harp; the king's two small sons stare up at Beowulf; the king's oldest friend raises his cup to him. Then Queen Wealhtheow holds out a heavy twisted gold neck-ring and asks him to be kind to her small sons, since their father is old. Beowulf puts the ring on, lays one huge hand gently on the smaller boy's head, and promises to be their friend as long as he lives. The dominant turn: the hall is singing again, and the king's poet has made a song about Beowulf. The feast owns the page; the ring and the promise are the quiet weight beneath it.

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four strings, two captions and two spoken lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth or field by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The feast does not land
Dramatic mode: Heorot full of light and people, gifts before the high seat, the poet singing, must own the page. If the hall does not read as joyful and crowded, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"Wear this ring, Beowulf…" hangs from Wealhtheow's mouth; "I'll be their friend as long as I live, my lady." from Beowulf's, after hers. A swap, or a balloon for the poet, the boys, or the old friend, is blocking.

### C4 — The ring
One heavy twisted gold neck-ring: in the queen's hands as she offers it, and at Beowulf's neck when he lays his hand on the boy's head. A missing ring, or two rings, is blocking.

### C5 — Identity
Beowulf clean-shaven with cleft chin, broken nose, notched brow, deep sea-blue cloak; Wealhtheow auburn, veiled, in green; the old friend bald with a grey braided beard, never like Hrothgar. A second figure in deep sea-blue is blocking; repeated faces in the crowd are nonblocking.
