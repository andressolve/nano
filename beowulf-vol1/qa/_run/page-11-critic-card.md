## Page intent

## PAGE 11

Dramatic. Heorot by day. Unferth rises from his stool with a cup in his hand and turns to the whole hall like a performer, and asks whether this is the boy who swam out to sea racing another boy and lost. He enjoys it: a week in the water, beaten by a boy, so what will Grendel do to him? The benches roar with laughter. Beowulf, arms folded, is still grinning, but his eyes are not, and he tells Unferth he has had a lot of mead and offers to tell how the race really went. The dominant turn: Beowulf is about to tell the true story. Unferth's mockery owns the page; Beowulf's cold grin is the reply the reader waits for.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 11 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text and no laugh lines; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The mockery does not land
Unferth, risen with a cup in his hand and playing to the hall, must own the page; then the benches laughing, and Beowulf still grinning with cold eyes. If Unferth reads as angry rather than enjoying himself, or Beowulf reads as hurt or cowed, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution in the taunt
The first three lines ("Beowulf. I know that name…", "Seven nights in the water, and you lost.", "If you couldn't beat a boy…") hang from Unferth's mouth; "Unferth, my friend, you've had a lot of mead…" hangs from Beowulf's. A taunt on Beowulf's side or tail, or the reply readable before the taunts, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Unferth: lean, narrow sharp face, close-cut black hair, short black pointed beard, black and silver. Beowulf: huge, young, clean-shaven, tawny hair tied back, crooked nose, left-brow notch, deep sea-blue cloak. A beard on Beowulf or tawny hair on Unferth is blocking; repeated faces on the benches are nonblocking.

### C5 — Continuity
No gold neck-ring on Beowulf; nothing hung above the doors. Present is blocking.
