## Page intent

## PAGE 17

Spectacle. Out of the moonlit moor, through strips of mist, Grendel strides toward the hall on the hill, seen from behind and below: twice a man's height, gaunt, hunched, arms nearly dragging, one coal-red eye catching the light. A hooked claw touches the iron band of the barred door. Then the doors burst inward: Grendel, half in shadow, eyes blazing, in the doorway; the nearest bench is empty, its blanket trailing to the floor where a Geat slept a moment ago; and his claw reaches for the big shape under a cloak on the next bench. The captions say he was too fast. The dominant turn: the next sleeper is Beowulf. Grendel's approach on the moor owns the page; the empty bench says the death without showing it. The reader turns to see who is under that cloak.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 17 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both captions must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth or field by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The approach does not land
Spectacle mode: Grendel crossing the moonlit moor toward the hall must own the page, seen from behind and below, gaunt and huge. Then the doors burst inward. If the reader cannot tell the monster is coming to the hall, the turn fails.

### C3 — The taking is drawn
The taken Geat is only an empty place and a trailing blanket. Blood, a body, a man in Grendel's grip, or anything eaten is blocking.

### C4 — The next sleeper
On the next bench a big shape under a cloak must read as the one Grendel reaches for next. If the reach reads at the empty bench or at nothing, the page's hook fails.

### C5 — Grendel's identity
Hairless, gaunt, grey-green, ember eyes, hooked claws, always partly in shadow, never whole and fully lit. Hair, wings, a balloon, or a tail toward him is blocking.
