# Exact script — pages 11–20

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 11 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Heorot, by day.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** UNFERTH, on the right, has risen from his stool,
cup in hand, turning to the whole hall as if to an audience. BEOWULF on the
left, arms folded.

> **UNFERTH:** "Beowulf. I know that name. Aren't you the boy who swam out into the open sea, racing another boy, just to show off?"

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Unferth, enjoying himself, on the right.

> **UNFERTH:** "Seven nights in the water, and you lost."
>
> **UNFERTH:** "If you couldn't beat a boy in a swimming race, what do you think Grendel will do to you?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The benches roar with laughter. Beowulf, on the left, is
still grinning, but his eyes aren't.

> **BEOWULF:** "Unferth, my friend, you've had a lot of mead. Let me tell you how that race really went."

**Why you turn the page:** Beowulf is about to tell the true story.

## PAGE 12 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the open winter sea, years before.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A black, heaving winter sea at night. In the
foreground, a big boy of fourteen in a mail shirt — BEOWULF, younger, the
same crooked nose and tied-back tawny hair — swims with a sword in his fist
while a huge tusked sea-beast rises out of the waves beside him, its coils
wrapping his legs. Far off, small, another boy's head in the water, moving
away on the swell. Lightning over the waves.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the storm sky)*
> "It was true: when they were boys, Beowulf and his friend swam out into the open sea for a dare, each in a mail shirt, each with a sword. For five nights they swam side by side.
>
> Then a storm drove them apart, and something rose out of the deep, wrapped itself around Beowulf and dragged him down to the bottom of the sea. He killed it with his sword. More came, for two more nights. By the seventh morning he had killed nine sea-monsters, the sea was quiet, and the waves carried him ashore far to the north.
>
> Nobody at home believed him. All anyone remembered was that Beowulf lost."

**Why you turn the page:** Unferth has just heard what really happened, in front of everyone.

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Heorot, by day.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** BEOWULF, on the left, spreads his hands to the
hall, mock-apologetic, enormous and grinning. UNFERTH, on the right, has
stopped smiling.

> **BEOWULF:** "So that's how I lost the race. I was busy killing sea-monsters."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Beowulf takes one step toward Unferth and stops grinning.
Very still, very plain.

> **BEOWULF:** "Here's the truth, Unferth. If you were half as fierce as your words, Grendel would never have dared come back to this hall."
>
> **BEOWULF:** "Tonight the Danes are going to see what a Geat can do."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The hall erupts: warriors pounding the tables, shouting.
On his high seat, Hrothgar laughs out loud. Unferth
sits down on his stool, his face dark, saying nothing.

**Why you turn the page:** Beowulf has won the hall, and now he has to keep his word.

## PAGE 14 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** What was a hall like Heorot really like, and why did everybody sleep in it?

**Sources:** *Beowulf* 64–85 (the building of Heorot), 1232–1250 (warriors sleeping on the benches with shields above their heads), 35 and 352 (*bēaga brytta*, "ring-giver"); hall excavations at Lejre and Gudme (Denmark) and the reconstructed halls at West Stow and Cheddar (England); mead, Old English *medu*, a drink fermented from honey.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A page of a chronicle on warm ivory vellum with
a ruled margin. The dominant image is an ink-and-watercolour cutaway of a
great timber hall, its roof lifted away: a long hearth down the middle with
smoke rising through a hole in the roof, benches along both walls, shields
and spears hung above them, a high seat at the far end, great double doors.
Tiny figures feast at one end; at the other, the same benches are covered
with straw mattresses. A red-and-lapis interlace initial with a touch of
gold begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(above the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "What was a hall like Heorot? For the people of the North, a great hall was the heart of the kingdom. A fire burned down the middle of the floor. Warriors feasted on the benches, drank mead made from honey, and at night pulled out straw mattresses and slept right there, with their shields on the wall above them."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(below the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "The king was called the ring-giver. He handed out gold rings, and every warrior who took one promised to fight for him. So a hall was the one warm, bright place in a dark country, full of music, gifts and promises. That is why a monster breaking in at night was the most frightening story anyone could tell."

> **LABEL** *(on the cutaway, at the fire)*
> "the long hearth"

> **LABEL** *(on the cutaway, at the far end)*
> "the high seat"

> **LABEL** *(on the cutaway, over the mattresses)*
> "benches by day, beds by night"

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what a cup and a promise mean in a hall — and the queen is coming down the hall with the cup.

## PAGE 15 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Heorot, late afternoon.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** WEALHTHEOW walks down the long hall holding the
great gold cup high in both hands, past the benches of Danish warriors, toward
the stranger. BEOWULF rises from his bench to meet her, on the right.

> **CAPTION** *(top left)*
> "Late that afternoon, Queen Wealhtheow carried the great gold cup down the hall, to every warrior in turn, and last of all to the stranger."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Wealhtheow, on the left, holds out the cup to Beowulf,
but her eyes go past him to two small boys on the bench by the high seat.

> **WEALHTHEOW:** "I thank God someone has come. But men have sworn great things in this hall before, Beowulf."
>
> **WEALHTHEOW:** "My sons have never slept one night in their father's hall."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Beowulf, on the right, takes the cup in both hands and
holds it up to the hall.

> **BEOWULF:** "Then tomorrow night they will, my lady. I swear it here, in front of everyone: tonight I win, or I die in this hall."

**Why you turn the page:** he has sworn it in front of everyone, and night is coming.

## PAGE 16 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** Heorot, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The empty hall in the dying red light of the
hearth. The great doors are shut and barred. The fourteen Geats lie asleep on
the benches under their cloaks, shields on the wall above them. In the
foreground, BEOWULF lies on his back on a bench in his tunic, no mail, no
sword, his huge empty hands resting on his chest — eyes wide open, watching
the doors.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the dark rafters)*
> "At sunset, King Hrothgar did something he had never done in twelve winters: he gave his hall to another man for the night. Then he led the Danes away, and the great doors shut behind them.
>
> The Geats lay down on the benches. Not one of them thought he would see home again. Beowulf took off his mail shirt, handed his sword to a friend, and lay down with nothing in his hands at all.
>
> The fire burned low. He did not close his eyes."

**Why you turn the page:** he is waiting in the dark with no sword, and Grendel always comes.

## PAGE 17 · *spectacle*

**Location:** two — the moor, and Heorot, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The moor under the moon, mist lying in strips.
GRENDEL strides out of it toward the hall on the hill, seen from behind and
below: twice the height of a man, gaunt and hunched, hairless, grey-green
hide like wet stone, arms so long his hooked claws nearly brush the grass. His
head turns; one coal-red eye catches the light.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the moon)*
> "Out of the moor, under the mist, came Grendel."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Close: one hooked black claw touches the iron band of
Heorot's barred door.

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Inside: the doors burst inward. Grendel in the doorway,
half in shadow, ember eyes blazing. At the nearest bench, a blanket trailing
to the floor where a sleeping Geat lay a moment ago — the place is empty. On
the next bench, a big shape lies under a cloak, and Grendel's claw is reaching
for it.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "He was too fast. Before Beowulf could move, Grendel had snatched the Geat on the nearest bench, and reached for the next."

**Why you turn the page:** the next sleeper is Beowulf.

## PAGE 18 · *spectacle*

**Location:** Heorot, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Close and huge: an enormous human hand clamped
around Grendel's scaled wrist. BEOWULF, rising off the bench in his tunic,
teeth bared in a grin, holds the monster's arm. Grendel's coal eyes, above,
go wide.

> **BEOWULF:** "Looking for me?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Grendel heaves back toward the door, dragging Beowulf
off his feet; Beowulf plants his heels and doesn't let go. A bench splinters
under them.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "Grendel had never in his life felt a grip like that one. For the first time, he was afraid."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The Geats awake, hacking at Grendel with their swords;
the blades bounce off his hide in showers of sparks.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "The Geats' swords bounced off his hide like sticks off a stone."

**Why you turn the page:** no sword can hurt him, and Beowulf is the only thing holding him.

## PAGE 19 · *spectacle*

**Location:** Heorot, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The hall shaking: a pillar cracked, benches
flying, the hearth scattered in sparks. BEOWULF, braced with one foot against
a pillar, both arms locked around Grendel's arm; GRENDEL wrenching away toward
the doorway with everything he has, head thrown back in a howl.

> **SOUND** *(across the top)*
> "AAAAAOOOOOOH!"

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The doorway, a moment later. Grendel is a shape fleeing
into the dark, hunched over one shoulder, already far down the hill. In the
doorway stands Beowulf, breathing hard, holding a long dark scaled arm with
hooked claws, longer than he is tall. Its shoulder end is lost in shadow.

> **CAPTION** *(bottom of the panel)*
> "Grendel tore himself free and ran howling into the dark to die. But he left his arm behind."

**Why you turn the page:** the monster is beaten, and the Danes don't know it yet.

## PAGE 20 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — Heorot, morning, and the black lake on the moor.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Morning inside Heorot: Danes crowding in
through the broken doors, pointing up. High on the inside wall above the
great doors, under the roof-beams, hangs Grendel's claw — the long dark scaled arm, hooked claws downward, its
shoulder end in shadow.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** Riders on the moor stopped at the edge of a black lake in
a cleft of grey rock; the water heaving and churning; huge clawed tracks
leading down into it.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the morning sky)*
> "In the morning the Danes came running up the hill. Inside Heorot, high over the doors where everyone could see it, hung Grendel's claw.
>
> Men followed the monster's tracks across the moor, all the way to a black lake in the rocks. The water there heaved and churned, and nothing came up. Riding home, they sang about Beowulf the whole way."

**Why you turn the page:** the king is about to meet the young man who saved his hall.
