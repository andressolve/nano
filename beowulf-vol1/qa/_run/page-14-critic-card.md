## Page intent

## PAGE 14

Chronicle. The story steps aside and the page becomes a page from an old chronicle: ivory vellum, brown ink and watercolour, an illuminated interlace initial. One dominant image carries the idea: a cutaway of a great timber hall with its roof lifted away, a long hearth down the middle with smoke rising through the roof, benches along both walls with shields and spears above them, a high seat at the far end, great double doors; tiny figures feast at one end while at the other the same benches are laid with straw mattresses. The fields answer what a hall was, why everyone slept in it, and why the king was called the ring-giver. The dominant turn: the reader knows what a cup and a promise mean in a hall, and the queen is coming down the hall with the cup. No story character appears.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 14 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** What was a hall like Heorot really like, and why did everybody sleep in it?

**Sources:** *Beowulf* 64–85 (the building of Heorot), 1232–1250 (warriors sleeping on the benches with shields above their heads), 35 and 352 (*bēaga brytta*, "ring-giver"); hall excavations at Lejre and Gudme (Denmark) and the reconstructed halls at West Stow and Cheddar (England); mead, Old English *medu*, a drink fermented from honey.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A page of a chronicle on warm ivory vellum with
a ruled margin. The dominant image is an ink-and-watercolour cutaway of a
great timber hall, its roof lifted away: a long hearth down the middle with
smoke rising through a hole in the roof, benches along both walls, shields
and spears hung above them, a high seat at the far end, great double doors.
Tiny figures feast at one end; at the other, the same benches are covered
with straw mattresses. A red-and-lapis interlace initial with a touch of
gold begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(above the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "What was a hall like Heorot? For the people of the North, a great hall was the heart of the kingdom. A fire burned down the middle of the floor. Warriors feasted on the benches, drank mead made from honey, and at night pulled out straw mattresses and slept right there, with their shields on the wall above them."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(below the cutaway, on plain vellum)*
> "The king was called the ring-giver. He handed out gold rings, and every warrior who took one promised to fight for him. So a hall was the one warm, bright place in a dark country, full of music, gifts and promises. That is why a monster breaking in at night was the most frightening story anyone could tell."

> **LABEL** *(on the cutaway, at the fire)*
> "the long hearth"

> **LABEL** *(on the cutaway, at the far end)*
> "the high seat"

> **LABEL** *(on the cutaway, over the mattresses)*
> "benches by day, beds by night"

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what a cup and a promise mean in a hall — and the queen is coming down the hall with the cup.

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two chronicle fields and three labels must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Each label must sit by its point: the long hearth at the fire, the high seat at the far end, "benches by day, beds by night" over the mattresses. The illuminated W is the first letter of "What", not an extra letter. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — Every claim reads as written
The page's facts (the fire down the middle of the floor, mead made from honey, straw mattresses on the benches at night, shields on the wall above, the king called the ring-giver, a gold ring taken for a promise to fight) must read exactly as written. A changed or missing claim is blocking.

### C3 — Not visibly a chronicle
The whole page must read at a glance as a page from an old chronicle: ivory vellum, ruled margin, brown ink and watercolour wash, an illuminated interlace initial in red and blue with gold. A painted oil scene, a story character, a balloon, or a caption box is blocking.

### C4 — The cutaway does not carry the idea
A hall cutaway must own the page, with a long central hearth and smoke rising, benches along both walls with shields above, a high seat at the far end, feasting at one end and mattresses at the other. If the reader cannot see that the same benches are both tables and beds, the idea fails. Small figure detail is nonblocking.
