## Page intent

## PAGE 28

Spectacle. Beowulf leaps from the rocks into the black water with Hrunting in his fist, the Geats on the bank behind him. Then the page's great image: deep green-black water, Beowulf sinking for a long time, his mail glinting, strange tusked water-beasts butting and snapping at his armour from every side while his eyes stay fixed downward. At the bottom, out of the dark, two huge webbed and clawed hands seize him, with pale green eyes and a cloud of weed-hair above them. The dominant turn: she has him, at the bottom of the lake. Nobody speaks. The water carries the page.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 28 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both captions must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, tail-free, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The descent does not own the page
The page must read as spectacle, and the eye must find Beowulf sinking through dark water among snapping beasts. If the leap or the seizing outweighs the descent, the page loses its long fall.

### C3 — She does not have him
In the last image two webbed clawed hands must seize him from below, with green eyes and weed-hair above. If the reader cannot tell he has been caught, or the creature reads as Grendel (bald, ember eyes), it is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and integrity
Beowulf is recognisable in helmet and mail with the sea-blue cloak and gold neck-ring. No blood, no bite landing. A bloody wound is blocking; beast anatomy is nonblocking.
