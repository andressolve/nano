## Page intent

## PAGE 29

Spectacle. A cave-hall under the lake, full of air, lit by a strange pale fire, old carved arches, weapons on the walls. Grendel's mother flings Beowulf onto the stone floor. Then the page's great image: Beowulf back on his feet, swinging Hrunting with all his strength at her head, and the patterned blade ringing off her in a shower of sparks, not biting. Last, he hurls Hrunting away across the floor and squares up to her with his bare hands, shouting that he will do it with his hands. The dominant turn: the sword has failed, and she is stronger than her son. She never speaks; no blood anywhere.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 29 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The caption and Beowulf's line must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The failure does not land
The page must read as spectacle, and the eye must find the patterned blade ringing off her head in sparks without biting. If the sword appears to cut her, or the blow is lost, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
Beowulf owns his only line; it must hang from his mouth by tail. A tail toward her, or any balloon or sound given to her, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Beowulf recognisable: clean-shaven, cleft chin, broken nose, sea-blue cloak, gold neck-ring. She has weed-hair, a long wolf jaw, green eyes, blue-black hide, never bald. Either unrecognisable is blocking.

### C5 — The thrown sword
In the last image Hrunting is away from his hands and his hands are empty. No blood or wound anywhere. A sword still in his grip, or a second sword taken up, is blocking.
