# Sequence gate — pages 11–20

## Uninterrupted read

Unferth publicly mocks Beowulf for losing a seven-night swimming race. Beowulf answers that the apparent loss was really a fight for survival against sea-monsters, then turns the insult back on Unferth and wins the hall's confidence. A chronicle interlude establishes Heorot as the kingdom's shared home, sleeping place, and center of reciprocal promises. Queen Wealhtheow then carries the hall's cup to Beowulf; her fear for her sons gives the coming fight a human cost, and Beowulf swears before everyone that he will win or die.

At night Hrothgar entrusts Heorot to Beowulf and leads the Danes away. The Geats sleep while Beowulf waits unarmed. Grendel crosses the moor, breaks into the hall, snatches the nearest Geat, and reaches for the next sleeper. Beowulf catches his arm and frightens him for the first time. The Geats' swords cannot pierce the monster, leaving Beowulf's grip as the only effective weapon. Beowulf tears off Grendel's arm, and the wounded monster flees into the dark to die. In the morning the arm hangs above Heorot's doors as public proof. Riders follow Grendel's tracks to a churning black lake and return singing of Beowulf, closing the victory with celebration while leaving the lake ominous.

## Gate judgment

The batch is causally and emotionally continuous. The taunt, answer, public vow, night watch, attack, reversal, victory, and morning proof each motivate the next turn. The rendered text and attribution match the batch script across transitions. Beowulf, Unferth, Wealhtheow, Hrothgar, the Geats, and Grendel remain legible in role and identity. The cup, hall interior, barred doors, Grendel's severed arm, tracks, and black lake carry their consequences clearly across page turns.

Pacing is varied and controlled: dramatic confrontation gives way to illustrated recollection, chronicle context, an intimate oath, a suspenseful prose hold, sustained spectacle, and aftermath. The explanatory pages deepen the stakes without turning the batch into compressed summary, and the fight receives enough space for its reversals. No consequential beat lacks an aftermath, and no focal or page-turn failure causes material reader harm.
