## Blind sequence record

Hrothgar embraces Beowulf as a son after the victory, while Beowulf mourns the man he could not save and Unferth is silenced beneath Grendel's severed arm. That night Heorot celebrates again: Hrothgar rewards Beowulf, the poet sings, Wealhtheow gives him a gold ring and asks him to befriend her sons, and Beowulf promises that he will. A chronicle interlude explains the scop, the lyre, and kennings before the story turns back to the sleeping hall.

Grendel's mother rises from the lake in grief and raids Heorot, reclaiming her son's arm and carrying off Hrothgar's oldest friend while Beowulf sleeps in his guest house. At dawn Beowulf arrives cheerfully, discovers Hrothgar's loss, hears of the mother and her lake, and vows to pursue her. Hrothgar, the Danes, Beowulf, and the Geats ride to the dreadful mere. At its edge Unferth lends Beowulf Hrunting; Beowulf thanks him, gives final instructions in case he dies, and their former hostility resolves into respect.

Beowulf dives, sinks through attacking water-beasts, and is seized by Grendel's mother. In her hall beneath the lake, Hrunting fails to wound her, so he throws it aside and fights bare-handed. Her knife cannot pierce his mail. He sees a giants' sword on the wall, lifts it, and swings as the batch ends before the result is shown.
