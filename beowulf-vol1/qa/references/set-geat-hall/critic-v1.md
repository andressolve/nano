## Mandatory findings

1. The intact hall and burned hall occupy opposite sides of one continuous bay, so they read as two halls existing at the same time rather than the same hall in two states. Redesign the plate as two spatially discontinuous state studies without a dividing border: use the same camera angle and match the foundation footprint, ridge length, doorway position, wall-post spacing, and both gable-end terminals exactly; in the burned state remove the roof and walls but leave the corresponding foundation and charred post stumps in those same positions. Do not connect the two states with one continuous shoreline, harbour, or village panorama.

2. The intact building is a tall, ornate landmark that dominates the settlement and therefore reads closer to “the greatest hall in the world” than to the specified smaller, sturdier Geat hall. Make its primary silhouette compact and low-slung: lower the ridge and front gable by roughly one third relative to the footprint, shorten the visible wall run to a few stout bays, thicken the wall and corner posts, and simplify the front carving to restrained structural timberwork rather than a towering ornamental facade. Preserve those exact compact proportions in the burned state.

3. The burned state is still actively aflame in multiple places, while the required continuity state is the hall already burned to black timbers with smoke over the villages. Remove all open orange flames from the ruined hall and nearby buildings; render a cold blackened frame with small smouldering wisps and one broad grey smoke drift passing over a sparse distant village. The ruined state must read as aftermath, not a second hall burning concurrently beside the intact one.

4. The plate devotes most of its structure to a harbour, docks, multiple boats, and a large village, duplicating the separate Geat-harbour-and-ship design space and weakening the hall lock. Crop both studies around the hall so the building and its matched footprint occupy the majority of each state; retain only a narrow strip of grey-green sea and a few distant turf roofs as coastal context, and remove the foreground docks and all boats.

At thumbnail and in grayscale, however, the current two-hall reading becomes stronger because both structures remain simultaneous high-contrast silhouettes; finding 1 is therefore blocking.
