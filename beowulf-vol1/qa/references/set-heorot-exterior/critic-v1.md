## Mandatory findings

1. The plate's location marker is wrong: a sea inlet, tidal rocks, coastal mountains, and a cluster of turf-roofed houses make this read as the separately locked Geat harbour rather than Heorot on its rise above the moor. Redesign the entire ground and horizon as open Danish moorland—peat-brown earth, low heath/reeds, mist in strips, and no visible sea, harbour, coastal village, or mountain-walled inlet—while retaining the long high timber hall, gold-shingled roof, and paired curling dragon-head gables as the primary silhouette.

2. The dominant lighting and accent are wrong: the huge orange sunset turns the whole sky, roof, terrain, and water into gold, so hearth-gold is no longer a restrained location accent and the exterior does not carry the specified cold northern light. Replace the sunset with cold silver moonlight or flat grey northern daylight; keep the exterior timber and moor in slate, sea-grey, peat-brown, and raw umber, and confine warm hearth-gold to a small glow through the hall openings. Remove the two freestanding door braziers so an unlisted recurring exterior fixture is not locked into later pages.
