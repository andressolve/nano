## Mandatory findings

1. The ordinary swords are structurally too close to the giant's sword in total length. Beowulf's plain sword, Hrunting, and Naegling each read at roughly three-quarters of the whole giant sword's tip-to-pommel length, so the giant weapon does not achieve the specified enormous, far-longer-than-any-other scale at thumbnail or after a long absence. Keep the whole giant sword at exactly its present maximum size and keep the detached golden hilt at its present scale; uniformly reduce each of the far-left plain sword, Hrunting, and far-right Naegling independently until its total tip-to-pommel length is 60–65% of the whole giant sword's total tip-to-pommel length, then re-space all five studies with open peat ground while preserving their left-to-right order.

## Nonblocking observations

- The plate is unframed and contains no lettering, labels, runes, signature, watermark, figures, hands, or scabbards.
- All five required studies are present in the correct order. The giant sword is whole in the third study; the fourth study ends cleanly under the guard with no blade remnant.
- The warm peat ground, weighted iron and gold materials, visible painted texture, restrained palette, and absence of reserved deep sea-blue fit the Northern Saga register.
- The plain sword, Hrunting, and Naegling have distinct primary silhouettes in grayscale: plain straight blade and compact guard; wavy pattern-welded blade and wire grip; worn narrow blade and broad down-curved gold guard.
