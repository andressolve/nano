# Foundation - Part Two: The Encyclopedists

## Purpose

This volume continues the expanded *Foundation* adaptation fifty years after
Part One. It gives the first Seldon crisis enough room for Francisco and
Sebastian to understand the political puzzle: Terminus has no army, almost no
natural resources, and no usable Imperial protection, yet the knowledge that
makes it vulnerable also makes it impossible for one neighboring kingdom to
own safely.

Part One is complete in `../foundation-expanded-part1/` and must not be
overwritten. The earlier compressed `../foundation/` adaptation remains useful
provenance, not a production lock.

## Scope

- Source coverage: Part II, "The Encyclopedists," from Isaac Asimov's
  *Foundation*.
- Length: cover + 28 landscape pages.
- Viewpoint: Salvor Hardin, first Mayor of Terminus City.
- Arc: Hardin sees the Empire's retreat in missing metal shipments, obsolete
  atomics, and empty diplomatic language; the Encyclopedia Board refuses to
  act; Hardin replaces it without bloodshed; Seldon's first Vault message
  reveals the Foundation's real purpose; and a balance among the Four Kingdoms
  prevents Anacreon from owning Terminus.
- Ending: Anacreon's base is withdrawn, Terminus remains independent, and the
  Foundation begins looking outward. The religion-of-science system belongs to
  Part Three and is not developed here.

## The One-Sentence Window

A frontier mayor must save a weaponless colony from annexation by proving that
its most dangerous weakness - being the last source of atomic knowledge - is
also the one thing its rival kingdoms cannot let any enemy possess.

## Governing Adaptation Decision

This is an expanded Asimov adaptation, not an Apple TV+ continuity blend.

Do not add the television Vault null field, Phara, an Anacreon occupation of
Terminus City, the *Invictus*, the Genetic Dynasty, Demerzel's television role,
or a large action war. Anacreonian troops may land on empty Terminus land as a
visible consequence of the ultimatum, but the crisis is resolved through
politics and balance of power, not a battlefield victory.

The screen adaptation may inform scale, physical danger, and emotional clarity.
It does not supply events, mechanics, or character arcs to this continuity.

## What Collapse Looks Like Here

Part One showed Trantor's future ruin as a mathematical projection. Part Two
shows collapse already happening at the Periphery without requiring a ruined
city:

1. A royal governor stops using the Imperial title and crowns himself king.
2. Metal shipments and messages stop crossing a former Imperial border.
3. Old warships and uniforms survive after the science inside them is lost.
4. A nuclear accident is answered by restricting atomics instead of training
   technicians.
5. An Imperial chancellor offers ceremony and a treaty, but no ship, engineer,
   or enforceable promise.
6. The Encyclopedia Board preserves old discoveries while forgetting how
   discovery works.

The visible idea is that civilization can look grand while the systems joining
it together have already failed.

## Structure

- **Pages 1-4 - A colony became a city:** the fifty-year jump, Terminus's
  resource poverty, the new generation, the Four Kingdoms, and Hardin's first
  warning to Pirenne.
- **Pages 5-10 - The envoy's slip:** Rodric's ceremonial arrival, the proposed
  military base and tribute, the threat to take land, and Hardin's plutonium
  test proving that Anacreon has lost atomic science.
- **Pages 11-16 - The Empire answers with words:** Hardin forces a place at the
  Board table, Dorwin displays scholarship without experiment, and symbolic
  analysis reduces Imperial reassurance to nothing.
- **Pages 17-22 - Six days:** Anacreon's ultimatum, the Board's paralysis,
  Hardin's deduction about the missing psychohistorians, and Yohan Lee's
  bloodless transfer of civic power.
- **Pages 23-25 - The first Vault message:** Seldon reveals the Encyclopedia
  cover, the logic of constrained crises, the fall already underway, and the
  Foundation's role in the Second Empire.
- **Pages 26-28 - The obvious solution:** the Board yields, the coup is complete,
  Hardin confronts the Four Kingdoms with the same atomic fact, and Anacreon
  withdraws under a three-kingdom ultimatum.

## Editorial Standard

Every major scene must contain:

1. Orientation in place, time, and participants.
2. An immediate question or objective for Hardin.
3. Evidence presented through action or exchange.
4. A visible change in understanding or power.
5. Reaction and consequence.
6. A clear bridge to the next scene.

Foundation-specific rules:

- Translate political abstractions into visible systems: empty cargo racks,
  cut routes, a failing reactor diagram, a treaty stripped to logic, four
  rival vectors around Terminus.
- Explain not only what Hardin notices but how the evidence changes his next
  move.
- Hardin is perceptive, not omniscient. He doubts his own reading of Seldon
  until the Vault opens.
- Pirenne is wrong but not foolish. His life's work is real and valuable; his
  error is treating its official purpose as more important than the living city
  that makes it possible.
- Psychohistory predicts population-scale pressures. Seldon's recording does
  not give personal instructions or magically react to the room.
- Keep enough native page text for a young reader to follow the causal chain.
  Political and technical pages may use T4-T5 density when the lettering can
  remain clear.
- The Foundation remains the long-range protagonist while Hardin carries this
  volume.

## Canon and Source Locks

The following facts were re-checked on 2026-07-20 against chapter-by-chapter
summaries, the novel's publication record, and the established compressed
adaptation research:

- The story occurs fifty years after the Foundation's establishment on
  resource-poor Terminus; the first Encyclopedia volume is still about five
  years from publication.
- Lewis Pirenne chairs the Board of Trustees. The Board governs the planet;
  Salvor Hardin is the first Mayor of Terminus City and initially needs Board
  authority.
- Anacreon's Royal Governor declares himself king and cuts Terminus off from
  metal shipments and the remaining Imperial route.
- The Periphery has broken into four nearby kingdoms: Anacreon, Smyrno, Konom,
  and Daribow. Anacreon is the strongest and has recently fought Smyrno.
- Anselm haut Rodric proposes Anacreonian military bases, taxes, and ultimately
  land under the language of protection.
- Terminus has almost no metals, little agriculture, no military, and no useful
  export except its concentrated scientific expertise.
- Hardin's plutonium question is a deliberate test. Terminus no longer needs
  plutonium for its advanced atomics; Rodric's surprise proves that Anacreon
  has lost working atomic knowledge.
- Hardin uses public pressure and the *Terminus City Journal* to obtain a place
  at Board meetings.
- Lord Dorwin is an Imperial Chancellor, an antiquarian with curled hair,
  elaborate manner, a snuff box, and an affected loss of the letter R.
- Dorwin compares ancient authorities instead of testing their claims himself.
  He acknowledges declining nuclear maintenance and treats the Periphery as
  unimportant.
- Logical analysis of Dorwin's five days of reassurance and the Imperial treaty
  produces no enforceable promise of protection.
- Anacreon's final ultimatum demands control, bases, and taxes within a week;
  the Vault is due to open one day earlier, on the Foundation's fiftieth
  anniversary.
- Jord Fara trusts that Seldon's recording will provide instructions. Hardin
  argues that the Foundation was intentionally left without psychohistorians
  who might discover the Plan too early.
- Hardin and Yohan Lee organize a bloodless coup against the Board.
- The first Vault recording reveals that the Encyclopedia was a cover, not the
  true purpose; the Foundation will face crises that constrain it to one viable
  path toward a Second Galactic Empire.
- Terminus is isolated, resource-poor, and the only nearby power retaining
  atomics. The combination makes the balance-of-power solution possible.
- Hardin brings Smyrno, Konom, and Daribow against exclusive Anacreonian control
  of Terminus. Anacreon's occupation is withdrawn without a Foundation battle.
- The science-as-religion policy develops during the following thirty years and
  belongs to Part Three, "The Mayors."

## Visual Standard

Use the finished Part One pages as the strongest source of truth: serious
cinematic mid-century science-fiction realism, controlled inked contours,
painterly airbrush/cel color, realistic anatomy, monumental environments, cream
captions, and clean off-white balloons.

Part Two introduces a frontier register:

- ochre plains, pale sky, low ivory settlement blocks, practical teal machinery;
- enormous empty land around a compact city;
- Anacreonian maroon, black, brass, heraldic display, and obsolete heavy ships;
- Imperial lavender, gold, polished ceremony, and no practical help;
- the Vault's pale blue-white light as the only luminous exception.

Canvas remains exactly 1536x1024, 3:2 landscape. Every story word is baked into
the raster. There are no blank balloons, HTML/SVG lettering, or post-production
repairs.
