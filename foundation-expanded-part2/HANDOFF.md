## Story Boundary

Provisional source boundary: Part II, **The Encyclopedists**, from Isaac Asimov's
*Foundation*.

The expanded volume should cover:

- an explicit fifty-year jump from the exile generation to settled Terminus;
- Salvor Hardin as the new viewpoint lead;
- Lewis Pirenne, the Encyclopedia Board, and their faith in the official mission;
- the Four Kingdoms formed from the Empire's retreating Periphery;
- Anacreon's pressure on small, strategically valuable Terminus;
- Anselm haut Rodric's visit and the evidence that atomic knowledge is being lost;
- Lord Dorwin's ceremonial learning and the absence of usable Imperial help;
- Hardin's political move against the Board;
- the first Time Vault opening and Seldon's recorded message;
- the revelation that the Encyclopedia was a cover for the Foundation's real historical purpose;
- the first crisis resolved through balance among the Four Kingdoms, not battlefield victory.

The ending should leave Hardin in control of a Foundation that finally looks
outward, with Anacreon's immediate pressure checked but the next phase not yet
solved. Do not drift into the later religion-of-science crisis; that belongs to
Part Three, **The Mayors**.

## Continuity Locks from Part One

- Part One ends with colony ships leaving a still-intact Trantor.
- Seldon expects roughly one more year of life and does not travel to rule Terminus.
- The settlers believe they are preserving knowledge through the Encyclopedia.
- A second Foundation exists at Star's End, but Part Two should not explain its hidden work.
- Psychohistory models large populations and probabilities; it does not predict individual behavior or operate as magic.
- Visible collapse means routes, protection, expertise, and repair capacity disappearing before civilization looks fully ruined.
- The Apple TV+ continuity remains outside this adaptation.

The opening must orient Francisco and Sebastian clearly: how much time passed,
what the settlers built, what they believe their job is, what Imperial protection
has vanished, and why Hardin sees danger before the Board does.

## Book/Screen Boundary

## Dramatic Spine to Develop

The complete script in `04-SCRIPT.md` implements this causal sequence:

1. **A colony became a city.** Fifty years of work make Terminus look stable.
2. **The Empire withdrew.** Four local kingdoms now occupy the political vacuum.
3. **A visitor reveals decay.** Anacreon retains Imperial display but has lost atomic expertise.
4. **The Board waits for authority.** Pirenne trusts the Emperor, the treaty, and Seldon's plan more than present evidence.
5. **Dorwin proves no rescue is coming.** Scholarship has become repetition without experiment, and Imperial promises contain no action.
6. **Anacreon applies pressure.** Terminus has knowledge but no fleet and little time.
7. **Hardin acts politically.** He removes the passive Board without turning the story into a gunfight.
8. **The Vault opens.** Seldon reveals that the Encyclopedia was the means, not the true end.
9. **The trap becomes visible.** Geography and rival kingdoms leave one workable path.
10. **Balance prevents conquest.** Terminus survives because no kingdom can let another possess it.

Each major scene must show not only what happens, but what evidence changes a
character's understanding and why the resulting choice matters.

## Visual Continuity

Use the finished Part One pages as the strongest register reference:

- serious cinematic mid-century science-fiction realism;
- inked contours with painterly cel shading;
- teal, amber, scarlet, ivory, and space-black;
- realistic anatomy and monumental but readable environments;

Part Two needs a distinct Terminus register: ochre frontier land, pale sky, low
functional settlement architecture, practical interiors, and a small population
surrounded by enormous empty space. Imperial and Anacreonian visitors should
bring impressive uniforms and old grandeur whose underlying systems are failing.

expanded-edition production locks. Generate and approve a coherent new reference
