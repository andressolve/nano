# 03 — SETTINGS

Recurring locations. Each gets era, lighting, materials, and mood so prompts stay
consistent across pages.

## Soho streets — Broad Street & neighbours (P1–P3, P8–P11, P13)
1854 London, narrow cobbled streets lined with tall soot-darkened brick terraces and
small shopfronts, iron railings, gas lamps not yet lit in daytime scenes. A public
water pump stands at the corner with iron handle and stone trough. Fog-grey sky, damp
cobblestones reflecting weak daylight. Later pages: same streets shuttered, handcarts
loaded with belongings, black crepe on some door-knockers, otherwise empty — dread
conveyed by absence, not bodies.

## Snow's study (P6–P7, P14)
A physician's study/consulting room: dark wood desk piled with ledgers and a
Registrar-General's list of deaths, an oil lamp casting warm amber light, a wall map
of Soho pinned up, medical instruments in a glass-fronted cabinet, a physician's black
bag by the door. Books on shelves behind. Quiet, orderly, lamplit.

## The Vestry board room (P12)
A formal mid-Victorian meeting room: long dark table, half a dozen gentlemen in dark
coats seated around it, tall windows with heavy drapes, a portrait or civic crest on
the wall, gas or oil lamps. Snow stands at one end with the map propped on an easel.
Formal, slightly tense atmosphere.

## The General Board of Health / Farr's office (P4–P5, P18)
A government office: shelves of bound reports and ledgers, a large wall table of
mortality figures, tall sash windows, orderly and official. Farr at a writing desk with
columned paper, quill or steel pen, an oil lamp. Cooler, more clinical lighting than
Snow's study — official rather than intimate.

## The brewery & the workhouse (P9, prose-only incidental figures)
Brewery: large wooden vats, barrels, a well with its own pump handle inside a covered
yard, workers in rolled shirtsleeves. Workhouse: plain institutional courtyard, a
separate well, inmates in drab uniform dress at a distance. Both shown briefly,
non-named figures, to establish "their own water, spared."

## Hampstead cottage doorway (P10, incidental)
A modest cottage on a quiet lane well outside London's crowded core — greener,
brighter, less fog than Soho — establishing visually that this death has no local
cause in common with the outbreak except the water itself.

## St Luke's parish, Whitehead's rounds (P15–P16)
Narrow tenement stairwells and doorways in the same Soho streets, seen from a
different, gentler angle — Whitehead is welcomed in where a stranger might not be.
The cesspool exhibit (P16) is examined outdoors at the front area/basement well of a
Broad Street house: brickwork, a narrow gap, a cross-section diagram inset rather than
an excavation scene.

## Florence, Italy — a sunlit contrast (P19 inset only)
A bright, sunlit Italian room, tall shuttered windows, a brass microscope on a wooden
table — the volume's one moment of warm daylight contrast to London's fog and lamplight,
signalling the story has left England.

## Broadwick Street today (P19 closing)
A modern London street: the same corner, now paved and busy, a red granite kerbstone
marking the historic pump site and a black cast-iron memorial pump nearby, a
period-signed pub visible in the background. Present-day clothing and cars kept minimal/
background-blurred so the historical throughline reads as the point, not a street-photo.
