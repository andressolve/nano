# Settings — The Name of the Rose, Book One

Recurring locations. Each setting block is pasted verbatim into page prompts that use that location, ensuring visual continuity across the volume.

---

## A. The abbey approach (P2, P21)

> **THE ABBEY APPROACH.** The approach to a wealthy Benedictine abbey high in the Italian Apennines, late November 1327. A narrow stone-paved road switchbacks up a snowy mountainside through stands of dark pine, the snow knee-deep beside the road, the road itself partly trampled. Above, the abbey complex sits on a rocky shoulder: a Romanesque chapel with a squat bell-tower, a low rectangular cloister, the cellarer's outbuildings — and dominating everything, the **Aedificium**, a massive three-storey octagonal stone tower with four square corner-towers (see Aedificium ref). Snow on every roof. Low grey winter sky. Crows on the gateposts. Faint smoke from one or two chimneys. The world is silent except for the wind.

---

## B. The abbey courtyard (P3, P4, P21 finale)

> **THE ABBEY COURTYARD.** The inner courtyard of the abbey, paved with worn limestone slabs cleared of snow. Bordered by the chapel on one side, the long low refectory on another, the cellarer's storehouses on the third, and the towering **Aedificium** (octagonal Romanesque tower, four corner-towers, narrow slit windows) on the fourth. Open arcade with slim round-arched columns running around two sides. Snow piled against the walls. A central well-head with a wrought-iron crank and a wooden bucket. Cold thin winter light. Habited monks visible going about errands in the middle distance.

---

## C. The scriptorium (P5)

> **THE SCRIPTORIUM.** The first floor of the **Aedificium** — the scriptorium where the abbey's monks copy and illuminate manuscripts. A long high-ceilinged hall lit by tall round-arched windows down both long sides, with afternoon winter light flooding in across rows of slanted oak writing-desks. Each desk has: a sheet of cream **vellum** (calfskin) pinned with small lead weights, a small ceramic inkpot of black iron-gall ink, a quill in a brass holder, a knife for scraping mistakes, a horn cup of pumice powder for smoothing. Some desks have small ground crystal lenses on stands for fine illumination work. On the walls between windows: open wooden cabinets of pigment jars — **lapis blue** (the most expensive), **vermilion red**, **gold leaf** in small bound books, **lampblack**, **verdigris green**, **ochre yellow**. Monks at work bent silently over their pages. The air smells of ink and beeswax. Stone floor scattered with dry rushes.

## D. The labyrinth library interior (P12)

> **THE LABYRINTH LIBRARY.** The top floor of the **Aedificium**, accessed only by a hidden stair behind the scriptorium's altar. A series of small stone rooms, each three paces across, connected by narrow round-arched doorways. Lit only by the single candle William and Adso carry — everything beyond ten paces in any direction is darkness. Floor-to-ceiling oak shelves crammed with leather-bound codices and rolled scrolls and stacked unbound quires. Carved Latin inscriptions over each doorway naming the region whose books are kept beyond (LEONES — Africa; AEGYPTUS — Egypt; HIBERNIA — Ireland; ROMA; GALLIA; etc — only some visible per scene). **Mirrors** set into some walls in unexpected places so the candlelight throws disorienting reflections that make rooms look like rooms that aren't there. Dust motes hanging in the candlelight. The air thick with old vellum. The geometry of the rooms wrong somehow — every passage seems to bend the same way. A labyrinth.

---

## E. The herbarium / infirmary (P20)

> **THE HERBARIUM.** The herbarium of the abbey's master herbalist Severinus, in a low stone building off the cloister. A long low room with two small round-arched windows letting in thin grey winter light. Walls lined with hundreds of small clay jars (each labeled in Latin) and twists of dried plants hanging in bundles from the ceiling beams. A heavy oak worktable in the centre covered with mortars and pestles of various sizes, small glass vials, a brass scale with weights, a small open codex of plant illustrations. Severinus's leather apron hangs on a peg by the door. The air is strong with herbs — rosemary, dried lavender, something acrid underneath. Dim warm light from a single oil lamp on the table.

---

## F. The great kitchen at night (P16)

> **THE GREAT KITCHEN AT NIGHT.** The vast vaulted kitchen of the abbey at midnight. Two enormous Romanesque hearths at one end, banked coals glowing dark red. Long oak preparation tables in the centre. Iron hooks above hung with cured hams, strings of garlic and dried herbs. A side door — the cellarer's door, leading to the storerooms — standing slightly ajar to the night, a strip of dim blue snow-light showing through. **One small oil lamp on a wall hook gives the only direct light** — the rest is firelight and shadow. A loaf of dark rye bread, a half-wheel of cheese, a clay jug on the table. The smell of yeast, woodsmoke, salt-pork.

---

## G. The cloister at midday (P18)

> **THE CLOISTER AT MIDDAY.** The covered cloister walk of the abbey at noon. A square open central courtyard with a small garden of bare winter herbs and a stone fountain, surrounded on all four sides by a covered arcade with slim Romanesque columns supporting round arches. Cold thin winter sun in the central garden, deep cool shadow under the arcade. The stone floor of the walk worn smooth by centuries of sandalled feet. A single bird call. Otherwise silence — even the wind doesn't reach here.

---

## H. The chapter house (P24)

> **THE CHAPTER HOUSE.** The chapter house of the abbey — a square stone room with a high vaulted ceiling, used for assemblies and judgments. Stone benches built into the four walls. A heavy oak lectern in the centre. **A single large round-arched window high in one wall** throwing one shaft of grey winter light onto the floor and lectern. The rest of the room in deep gloom lit only by tall iron candle-stands at each corner with thick beeswax candles. Today the assembled Benedictine monks line the benches in silence, faces in shadow under their hoods. The Franciscan visitors stand on one side in their brown habits. The papal Dominican delegation stands on the other in their white-and-black. The atmosphere is of a courtroom.

## Old-Adso writing-cell (referenced only — frame opener P1)

For the chapter-break / frame opener, the inset miniature shows:

> **OLD ADSO'S CELL, decades later.** A small stone cell in the Benedictine abbey at Melk in Austria. A tiny round-arched window high in the wall showing pale dawn. A simple oak writing-desk against the wall. Beeswax candle. Quill, inkpot, stack of vellum sheets. An old, **old** monk seated at the desk in black Benedictine habit — clearly Adso many decades after the events of the story, beard now long and white, hands gnarled with age, writing. His face concentrated, sad, alive. This is the man writing the manuscript we are reading.

(This is a one-time setting used only inside the historiated initial on P1's illuminated chapter-break page. The rest of Vol 1 happens in 1327.)
