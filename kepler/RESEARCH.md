## 1. Birth & Childhood (1571–1584)

- Born **27 December 1571**, Weil der Stadt, a small free imperial city in Swabia (Württemberg region). [Wikipedia, MacTutor, SEP — unanimous]
- **Self-computed conception:** In his own family horoscope Kepler computed his conception to **16 May 1571 at 4:37 a.m.** (his parents married 15 May 1571) and the pregnancy at **224 days, 9 hours, 53 minutes** — a seven-month premature baby. [Astro-Databank/Boockmann, Kepler's horoscope collection; widely cited from Caspar] This is real and delightfully in-character: he calculated *everything*.
- Premature and sickly; **smallpox around age 3–4** left him with damaged ("crippled") hands and permanently weakened eyesight. [Wikipedia, Caspar via secondary sources]
- **Father: Heinrich Kepler** — mercenary soldier, repeatedly left the family to fight (including for Catholic forces against Protestants), finally disappeared for good around **1588**, presumed died abroad. Kepler's own family-horoscope description: "a man vicious, inflexible, quarrelsome and doomed to a bad end" (Koestler's translation, *The Sleepwalkers* — label as Koestler's rendering if used).
- **Mother: Katharina Kepler (née Guldenmann)**, born **8 Nov 1547** in Eltingen/Leonberg; daughter of **Melchior Guldenmann, innkeeper and mayor** (Schultheiss) of Eltingen. Herbalist/healer who ran an inn. [Wikipedia: Katharina Kepler]
- **Great Comet of 1577:** age 6, "taken by his mother to a high place to look at it." [Wikipedia — Kepler's own recollection] **Lunar eclipse of 1580:** age 9, called outdoors by his parents; he recalled the moon "appeared quite red." [Wikipedia] Both scenes are documented from Kepler's own notes — safe to draw.

## 2. Education (1577–1594)

- German schools in Weil, then Latin school in **Leonberg** (family moved there 1576); convent school at **Adelberg (1584)**; higher seminary at **Maulbronn (1586)**. [Wikipedia, SEP]
- **University of Tübingen**, entered the Stift (Protestant seminary) **September 1589**; **Magister Artium 1591**; then theology studies. Intent: **Lutheran clergyman**. [Wikipedia, SEP]
- **Michael Mästlin (1550–1631)** taught the official Ptolemaic astronomy publicly but introduced select students, Kepler among them, to the **Copernican system** privately. Kepler became a convinced Copernican at Tübingen. [MacTutor, SEP]
- **1594 diversion:** before finishing theology, Kepler was assigned (via Tübingen's recommendation) to replace the deceased mathematics teacher at the Protestant Stiftsschule in **Graz**, Styria. He took the post reluctantly — arrived **April 1594**, age 22, also serving as district calendar-maker (his calendars' weather/politics predictions had some lucky hits, boosting his local reputation). [Wikipedia, SEP]
- HEDGE: MacTutor's summary mentions a "poor mathematics grade in first year" — this conflicts with the standard account (Caspar) of Kepler as a top student. Do not use.

## 3. Graz (1594–1600)

- **The classroom epiphany, 19 July 1595:** while drawing a diagram of Jupiter–Saturn conjunctions for a class, he saw that inscribed/circumscribed circles of a polygon stand in fixed ratios — maybe geometry dictates the cosmos. Failing with flat polygons, he moved to 3D: the **five Platonic solids** nested between spheres give exactly **six planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn). [Wikipedia, APS "This Month in Physics History" — date consistently verified]
- Published as ***Mysterium Cosmographicum* (Tübingen, 1596)** — the **first published defense of Copernicanism** by a professional astronomer. The polyhedral idea was **WRONG**, but: (a) it asked the modern question — *why* this number of planets at *these* distances; (b) sent to Tycho Brahe, it opened the door to Prague. Kepler never abandoned it (2nd edition 1621). [Wikipedia, SEP]
- **Marriage: 27 April 1597**, to **Barbara Müller (1573–3 July 1611)**, daughter of wealthy mill owner Jobst Müller; she was ~23 and **already twice widowed**, with a young daughter (Regina). She brought property into the marriage. [Wikipedia, Deutsche Biographie]
- **Counter-Reformation:** Archduke Ferdinand ordered Protestant teachers and preachers out of Graz (**edict September 1598**); Kepler was briefly exempted (his reputation, Jesuit contacts), but in **summer 1600**, refusing to convert to Catholicism, he was **expelled for good** (left Graz for Prague, arriving October 1600). [Wikipedia, SEP — exact day of final expulsion varies by source; "August 1600" is the common form. Keep to month-level.]

## 4. Tycho Brahe (1600–1601)

- **Tycho Brahe** (14 Dec 1546 – 24 Oct 1601): Danish nobleman, the greatest naked-eye observer in history. Lost part of his nose in a **duel with his third cousin Manderup Parsberg, 29 Dec 1566** (a quarrel over mathematics, fought in the dark). Wore a prosthetic nose — **BRASS, not silver**: the 2010 exhumation / 2012 chemical analysis of bone-adjacent traces settled it (he may have owned finer gold-silver ones for occasions, but the everyday prosthesis was brass — flag any "silver nose" line as legend). [Wikipedia: Tycho Brahe]
- At **Uraniborg** (island of Hven) his instruments reached accuracy **approaching 1 arcminute** — about five times better than contemporaries and near the physiological naked-eye limit. Kepler treated ~2′ as the trust threshold for Tycho's Mars data. [Wikipedia; standard in secondary literature]
- 1599: moved to Prague under **Rudolf II** as Imperial Mathematician, working at **Benátky nad Jizerou** castle northeast of Prague.
- **Kepler arrived at Benátky 4 February 1600.** [Wikipedia — exact date verified] Relationship stormy: Tycho treated him as an assistant, not a peer, and **doled out data piecemeal**. Kepler's complaint (letter, Koestler's translation — label as such): Tycho "gave me no opportunity to share in his experiences. He would only in the course of a meal... mention in passing today the figure for the apogee of one planet, tomorrow the nodes of another." Kepler stormed out at least once (April 1600), returned, negotiated a formal contract.
- **The Mars assignment:** senior assistant **Christian Longomontanus** had Mars; when he shifted to the lunar theory, Mars fell to Kepler. **Luckiest possible problem:** Mars has the most eccentric orbit (e ≈ 0.093) of the well-observable planets — the one whose circle-failure is visible at Tycho's precision. (Mercury is more eccentric but too close to the Sun to observe well.) [Wikipedia: Astronomia nova; keplersdiscovery.com]
- **The bet: Kepler boasted he would solve Mars in 8 days.** VERIFIED as a real wager with Longomontanus, made in 1600; it took about **five years**. [Long Now Foundation write-up; the anecdote traces to Kepler's own account — safe as "Kepler wagered," don't invent dialogue beyond the bet itself.] Kepler himself framed the project as his **"war with Mars"** (military metaphors run through *Astronomia Nova*'s dedication to Rudolf II — safe as his own framing).
- **Tycho's death, 24 October 1601:** at a banquet on 13 October he refused to leave the table to relieve himself (etiquette), suffered urinary blockage, died eleven days later. The story comes **from Kepler's own account** in Tycho's observation log. **Mercury-poisoning/murder theories are DEBUNKED**: the 2010 exhumation (2012 results) found mercury levels too low for poisoning — "it is impossible that Tycho Brahe could have been murdered" (investigation conclusion). Cause consistent with bladder/kidney failure. [Wikipedia: Tycho Brahe]
- Tycho's reported deathbed refrain to Kepler — *"Ne frustra vixisse videar"* ("Let me not seem to have lived in vain") — comes from Kepler's account in the log; usable if attributed "as Kepler recorded."
- **Kepler appointed Imperial Mathematician two days later** — Wikipedia states the appointment came two days after Tycho's death; some references give the formal act as early November 1601. Say "within days" or "two days later (per the standard account)."
- **The data:** Tycho's heirs (especially son-in-law **Franz Tengnagel**) fought for control. Kepler's own confession, letter of **1605**: **"I confess that when Tycho died, I quickly took advantage of the absence, or lack of circumspection, of the heirs, by taking the observations under my care, or perhaps usurping them."** [Widely reproduced; standard English rendering] Tengnagel extracted a veto/collaboration right that helped delay *Astronomia Nova*'s publication.

## 5. Astronomia Nova (1600–1609) — THE SPINE

- **The vicarious hypothesis:** Kepler's refined circular model (equant-style, freely placed) matched Tycho's Mars **longitudes at opposition to within ~2′** — better than anyone in history. But it predicted wrong distances/latitudes; when he corrected the geometry to satisfy those (bisecting the eccentricity), longitude errors of up to **8′ appeared at the octants**. Two arcminutes he could accept (Tycho's noise floor); eight he could not. [Wikipedia: Astronomia nova; keplersdiscovery.com]
- **THE QUOTE (Astronomia Nova, ch. 19; Donahue translation, Green Lion Press 1992, p. 286)** — use this version:
  > "Since the divine benevolence has vouchsafed us Tycho Brahe, a most diligent observer, from whose observations the 8′ error in this Ptolemaic computation is shown... it is fitting that we with thankful mind both acknowledge and honour this benefit of God... For if I had thought I could ignore eight minutes of longitude, in bisecting the eccentricity I would already have made enough of a correction... Now, because they could not have been ignored, these eight minutes alone will have led the way to the reformation of all of astronomy, and have constituted the material for a great part of the present work."
  The shorter popular variant ("Divine Providence granted us such a diligent observer in Tycho Brahe that his observations convicted this... calculation of an error of eight minutes...") is a compressed paraphrase found in secondary literature (Koestler lineage) — fine for narration if labeled loosely, but for the page-text bake use Donahue's clauses. CAUTION: the Latin (*"...sola octo minuta viam praeiverunt ad totam Astronomiam reformandam"*) circulates in secondary sources; verify against KGW vol. III before baking Latin into a page — otherwise use English only.
- **Sequence of discovery (order matters for the story):**
  1. Vicarious hypothesis triumph-then-failure (8′) → abandons the whole apparatus of uniform circular motion **and the equant**.
  2. Physics first: the Sun must *drive* the planets (a "magnetic" species/force weakening with distance — wrong mechanism, right instinct; ancestor of gravitation).
  3. **Second law FIRST (1602):** equal areas in equal times, initially as a computational device (distance law).
  4. **Ovals (1603–1605):** years lost to an egg-shaped orbit that almost worked.
  5. **The ellipse (spring 1605):** he derived an equation for the orbit, didn't recognize it, set it aside, and tried a via buccosa/ellipse "as a different thing" — then saw his own equation *was* the ellipse. His exclamation, **Astronomia Nova ch. 58 (Latin verified):** ***"O me ridiculum!"*** — full clause: *"O me ridiculum! perinde quasi libratio in diametro non possit esse via ad ellipsin"* — "O ridiculous me! As if the libration on the diameter could not be the way to the ellipse." [superphysics.org ch. 58 text; Donahue's translation phrasing] The oft-quoted "I awoke as if from sleep and a new light broke on me" is Kepler describing an earlier insight in ch. 58 — usable, but flag as Koestler-lineage translation; the bulletproof verbatim is "O me ridiculum."
  6. **First law:** Mars moves on an ellipse with the Sun at one focus.
- Manuscript complete **September 1607**; **published summer 1609** (Heidelberg) after delays largely due to **Tycho's heirs (Tengnagel)** asserting rights over the data. [Wikipedia]

## 6. Prague — Triumph and Collapse (1601–1612)

- **Supernova of October 1604** ("Kepler's Star," SN 1604, in Ophiuchus): observed and tracked by Kepler; book ***De Stella Nova* published 1606**. Last supernova observed in our galaxy with the naked eye. [Wikipedia; book date 1606 — the star is 1604, don't conflate]
- ***Astronomiae Pars Optica* (1604):** foundation of modern optics — first essentially correct explanation of **vision via the inverted retinal image**, camera obscura/pinhole images, how **eyeglasses** work for near- and far-sightedness. [Wikipedia, SEP]
- ***Dioptrice* (1611):** theory of the telescope (including the Keplerian two-convex-lens design) within two years of Galileo's instrument. [SEP]
- **Galileo:** Kepler publicly backed the telescope discoveries sight-unseen — ***Dissertatio cum Nuncio Sidereo*, April/May 1610** (his reservations concerned priority of the telescope's invention, not the discoveries). Galileo **never reciprocated**: he did not answer Kepler's major works, declined to send him a telescope, and communicated his 1610–11 discoveries as **anagrams**. Kepler mis-solved the Saturn anagram as *"Salve umbistineum geminatum Martia proles"* ("Hail, burning twin, offspring of Mars") — guessing moons of Mars; the real solution was *"Altissimum planetam tergeminum observavi"* ("I have observed the highest planet in triplet form" — Saturn's "handles"). Galileo revealed the answer months later, to the Emperor's circle, not to Kepler. Frame the relationship as **one-sided admiration**. [Wikisource, multiple histories]
- **The disaster year 1611:** son **Friedrich dies of smallpox, age 6** (early 1611); wife **Barbara dies 3 July 1611** of Hungarian spotted fever (typhus); **Rudolf II is forced to cede power** (Prague occupied in the Habsburg brothers' war; Rudolf abdicated the Bohemian crown May 1611, died 20 January 1612). Kepler, kept nominally as Imperial Mathematician by Matthias, moved to **Linz in 1612**. [Wikipedia, Deutsche Biographie]

## 7. Linz (1612–1626)

- Post: **District Mathematician** (Landschaftsmathematiker) of Upper Austria.
- **Excluded from communion:** Lutheran pastor **Daniel Hitzler** refused Kepler communion (from 1612; consistory upheld it) because Kepler would not sign unqualified assent to the **Formula of Concord** — he rejected the **ubiquity doctrine** (Christ's body physically everywhere, hence in the Eucharist). He *also* refused to convert to Catholicism. Caught between churches, loyal to his own conscience — mirror of the 8-arcminute integrity. [Wikipedia, Renaissance Mathematicus]
- **Second marriage:** **30 October 1613**, at Eferding, to **Susanna Reuttinger**, a 24-year-old orphan (daughter of a cabinet-maker, raised in the household of the Baroness Starhemberg). The famous letter — **to Baron Strahlendorf, October 1613** — methodically reviews **ELEVEN candidates** over two years; friends pushed richer matches, but he "returned to the fifth" (Susanna). VERIFIED — the letter is real and often anthologized ("the marriage problem"). [Renaissance Mathematicus; Koestler] The marriage was happy; she bore six children (seven per some counts — hedge before printing), three surviving to adulthood.
- **Wine barrels:** at the wedding, Kepler questioned the Linz merchant's rod-dipped method of gauging barrel volumes → ***Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum* (1615)**, computing volumes of solids of revolution by summing infinitesimal slices — a recognized stepping stone toward **integral calculus**. [Wikipedia, MacTutor]
- ***Harmonices Mundi* (published 1619):** the **THIRD LAW** — T² ∝ a³. Kepler's own dating (Book V, ch. 3, Aiton/Duncan/Field translation): **"conceived mentally on 8th March in this year one thousand six hundred and eighteen, but submitted to calculation in an unlucky way, and therefore rejected as false, and finally returning on the 15th of May and adopting a new line of attack, stormed the darkness of my mind. So strong was the support from the combination of my labour of seventeen years on the observations of Brahe and the present study, which conspired together, that at first I believed I was dreaming..."** [MacTutor quotations — verified] The law sits **buried among harmonic/musical speculation** (planetary "songs") — honest framing: the mystic and the measurer are the same man, and the measurer always got the last word.
  - Note: **23 May 1618** — the Defenestration of Prague, days after the third-law confirmation. The Thirty Years' War begins as Kepler hears the harmony of the world. Use this juxtaposition; the dates are solid.
- **The "golden vessels" passage** (Harmonices Mundi, Book V introduction; translation as on Wikiquote — translations vary, label "from Harmonices Mundi, Book V"): **"I am stealing the golden vessels of the Egyptians to build a tabernacle to my God from them... I am indeed casting the die and writing the book, either for my contemporaries or for posterity to read, it matters not which: let the book await its reader for a hundred years; God himself has waited six thousand years for his work to be seen."** The popular form "...as God has waited six thousand years for an onlooker" is a variant translation — either is defensible; cite "Harmonice Mundi Book V, introduction."

## 8. The Witch Trial of Katharina Kepler (1615–1622)

- **1615:** **Ursula Reinbold** (glazier's wife, after a quarrel/business dispute) accuses Katharina of giving her a "bitter drink" causing illness; local magistrate (Vogt) **Lutherus Einhorn** — who convicted numerous women in the Leonberg witch panic — drives the process. [Wikipedia: Katharina Kepler]
- **December 1616:** Kepler moves his mother to Linz for safety; she later returns to Leonberg.
- **7 August 1620:** arrested (seized at the parsonage in Heumaden), held **14 months** (405 days) in chains, latterly at Güglingen, at her family's expense.
- **Kepler drops everything:** relocates with household to Württemberg to run the defense; he **wrote the concluding legal defense himself** (with counsel), attacking the evidence point by point — the era's most famous scientist doing forensic error analysis for his mother's life. Costs to family: ~900 gulden (over two years of his salary). [Wikipedia: Katharina Kepler]
- **September 1621:** the court orders ***territio verbalis*** — she is **shown the torture instruments** and formally threatened, the final legal step short of torture. She refuses: **"she would still have nothing to confess... she would also want to die on that"** (trial record wording; use as reported speech). [Wikipedia: Katharina Kepler; the confrontation is commonly dated late September 1621]
- **Acquitted/released early October 1621** (Wikipedia gives 4 October 1621 for the acquittal decision; release ~a week after the territio). Duke Johann Friedrich of Württemberg's Tübingen law faculty review (Kepler's old university) underpinned the outcome.
- **Katharina died 13 April 1622**, about six months after release.
- **Somnium connection:** the manuscript (see §13), whose narrator's mother **Fiolxhilde** is a herb-woman who conjures a daemon, circulated from **1611** — i.e., **before** the 1615 accusation — and per Wikipedia "likely fueled the rumour." State as *plausible contributing factor per sources*, not proven cause.

## 9–10. War, Ulm, and the Rudolphine Tables (1618–1627)

- **Thirty Years' War backdrop:** Defenestration of Prague **23 May 1618**; Upper Austria pawned to Catholic Bavaria; Counter-Reformation pressure in Linz (Kepler, as Imperial Mathematician, protected but harassed; his library was temporarily sealed by the Counter-Reformation commission in 1626 — Caspar-derived detail, month-level uncertain, hedge as "his library was sealed").
- **Linz siege, June–August 1626:** the **Upper Austrian Peasants' War**; Linz blockaded ~10 weeks. The house with **the print shop burned**, destroying much of the in-progress printing of the Tables (Kepler's manuscripts survived). Kepler got imperial permission to leave → **Ulm**. [Wikipedia: Upper Austrian peasant war of 1626; Starry Messenger (Cambridge HPS)]
- ***Tabulae Rudolphinae* (Ulm, September 1627):** the payoff of the whole story — **~30 years** from Tycho's first Hven observations to print. Tycho's data + Kepler's ellipse laws + **Napier's logarithms** (with log tables included). Planetary predictions roughly **30× more accurate** than the Alfonsine/Prutenic tradition (star positions good to ~1′; the "30-fold" figure is the standard secondary-literature characterization — safe with "roughly"). Kepler paid printing costs himself, having extracted only 2,000 of 6,299 florins owed for the project. [Wikipedia: Rudolphine Tables]
- **Frontispiece (real, verify against the engraving when drawing):** the **Temple of Urania** — Hipparchus, Ptolemy, Copernicus, and Tycho stand among columns of increasing refinement (rough-hewn logs to elegant brick and marble = progress of astronomy); Tycho points to the ceiling map asking "Quid si sic?" ("What if thus?"). On the **pedestal base, a side panel shows Kepler himself working by candlelight** at a table — the man who built the temple, painted into the basement. A goddess of the Muses holds instruments above; one panel shows the printing press. [Wikipedia: Rudolphine Tables; University of Michigan early-astronomy pages]
- **Vindication after death: 7 November 1631** — **Pierre Gassendi** in Paris observes the **transit of Mercury**, the first transit observation in history, exactly as the Tables predicted (Kepler had alerted astronomers in a 1629 pamphlet). Old tables were off by days; Kepler's by hours. [Wikipedia: Rudolphine Tables — Gassendi 1631 verified; the Nov 7 date is standard]

## 11–12. Wallenstein, Sagan, Death (1628–1630)

- **1608:** Kepler cast an **anonymous horoscope** that turned out to be for **Albrecht von Wallenstein**; **1625** he reluctantly updated it (annotating that the stars incline, not compel). The famous line: for **March 1634** he would predict nothing further, noting the period **"will entail a terrible confusion in the country"** (commonly rendered "dreadful disorders" — hedge exact wording; from the 1625 revision). **Wallenstein was assassinated 25 February 1634.** Frame as eerie coincidence Kepler himself would have discounted — he mocked naive astrology while practicing a reformed one. [Magic Bohemia / Wallenstein horoscope literature; Wikipedia: Wallenstein]
- **20 July 1628:** Kepler arrives at **Sagan** (Silesia) in Wallenstein's service — the general wanted a court mathematician; Kepler wanted his printing subsidized and back pay pursued.
- **Death:** left for **Regensburg** (Diet in session) in **October 1630**, riding in worsening weather, **to press for the 11,817 gulden owed him by the imperial treasury**. Fell ill with fever days after arriving; **died 15 November 1630**, age 58. [Wikipedia, Britannica — debt figure 11,817 gulden verified]
- Buried in the **Protestant cemetery of St. Peter outside Regensburg's walls**; the **grave was obliterated in 1632–33** when Swedish and imperial armies fought over Regensburg. No grave survives — only the epitaph he wrote for himself months before dying:
  > ***"Mensus eram coelos, nunc terrae metior umbras; / Mens coelestis erat, corporis umbra iacet."***
  > "I measured the heavens, now I measure the shadows of Earth. Skybound was the mind, earthbound the body rests." (Common English renderings vary slightly; Wikiquote: "I used to measure the heavens, now I measure the shadows of Earth. Although my mind was heaven-bound, the shadow of my body lies here.") [Wikiquote — Latin verified]

## 13. Somnium

- ***Somnium* ("The Dream"):** origin in a **1593 Tübingen student disputation** (how the heavens would look from the Moon — blocked from presentation); rewritten with the dream-frame **circa 1609**; **manuscript circulated from ~1611** (a copy escaped Prague); Kepler added 223 explanatory notes through the 1620s; **published posthumously 1634** by his son **Ludwig** (with Jacob Bartsch). Plot: Icelandic boy **Duracotus**; his mother **Fiolxhilde**, a herb-selling wise woman, summons a **daemon** who describes the voyage to and astronomy from the Moon (Levania). Recognized by **Carl Sagan and Isaac Asimov** as arguably the **first work of science fiction**. Witch-trial link: see §8 — plausible fuel, label as such. [Wikipedia: Somnium]

## 14. Personality

- **The self-analysis (Graz, 1597, age 25, third person, astrological self-horoscope; Koestler's translation in *The Sleepwalkers* — always credit Koestler):**
  > **"That man has in every way a dog-like nature. His appearance is that of a little lap-dog... He liked gnawing bones and dry crusts of bread, and was so greedy that whatever his eyes chanced on he grasped; yet, like a dog, he drinks little and is content with the simplest food... He has a dog-like horror of baths."**
  [MSU cosmology course pages quoting Koestler; the document is genuine — brutally honest self-portrait, unmatched among scientists]
- His family-horoscope notes are equally unsparing: father "vicious, inflexible, quarrelsome"; mother "small, thin, swarthy, gossiping and quarrelsome, of a bad disposition" (Koestler's translations — label). Use sparingly and with sympathy; he loved and defended this mother for six years.
- Lifelong **bad eyesight** (smallpox damage; multiple-vision/myopia reported): **the greatest theoretical astronomer of his age could barely see the stars** — he needed Tycho's eyes, Tycho needed his mind. This is the partnership in one sentence, and it is well-founded.
- Perpetual money trouble: salaries chronically in arrears from Rudolf onward; died pursuing 11,817 gulden.
- Astrology: practiced it for pay ("the foolish daughter [astrology] feeds the wise mother [astronomy]" — this famous line is a **paraphrase** of Kepler's remarks in *Tertius Interveniens*; flag as paraphrase), but he demolished naive astrology and annotated horoscopes with disclaimers.

## 15. VISUAL / PORTRAIT NOTES

- **Kepler (1610 portrait, unknown artist — the standard likeness):** dark hair, very **high forehead**, long face, trim dark **pointed beard and mustache**, large alert dark eyes, white **ruff collar**, dark doublet. Slight, wiry build (his own "little lap-dog" description). Ages: student ~20 (Tübingen), Graz ~25–28, Prague 29–40, Linz 41–55 (beard greying), final years 56–58 (worn, grey).
- **Tycho:** corpulent, reddish-blond hair receding from a high pate, **enormous handlebar mustache**, the **brass nose-bridge prosthesis** (visible seam across the bridge), rich nobleman's dress — gold chain with elephant medallion, ruff, fur-trimmed robes. Order of the Elephant chain is documented in portraits.
- **Rudolf II:** heavy **Habsburg jaw/underlip** (see the 1590s Hans von Aachen portraits), melancholic hooded eyes, dark Spanish court dress with the Golden Fleece; collector-emperor surrounded by curiosities (Arcimboldo painted him as Vertumnus).
- **Barbara Kepler:** no reliable portrait survives — invent modestly (prosperous Styrian burgher dress, ruff, bonnet); document as "no authenticated likeness." Same for **Susanna Reuttinger** (simpler dress; married in Eferding) and **Katharina** (trial records and Kepler's notes suggest a small, thin, sharp-tongued old woman; no portrait).
- **Mästlin:** surviving portraits show a full dark beard, scholar's gown and ruff, kindly heavy-lidded eyes.
- **Places:** Weil der Stadt — half-timbered Swabian free city, market square (statue there now); Maulbronn — intact Cistercian monastery complex (UNESCO; usable directly); Tübingen — Stift on the Neckar, half-timbered lanes; Graz — Renaissance arcaded courtyards (Landhaus), Stiftsschule; **Benátky castle** — modest Renaissance château above the Jizera, sgraffito facades; Prague — Hradčany skyline, St. Vitus spires, alchemists' Golden Lane era, Charles Bridge tower; Kepler's Prague residence on **Karlova street** (plaque today); Linz — Danube town under the castle; Güglingen/Leonberg — small Württemberg towns, half-timber, the jail a tower cell; Ulm — huge unfinished Minster tower over the Danube; Regensburg — stone bridge, patrician towers; Sagan — Wallenstein's Silesian duchy.
- **Costume era:** 1570s–1620s German/Austrian: ruffs then falling collars (after ~1620), dark doublets, scholars' gowns; soldiers in cuirass/buffcoat during war scenes; Tycho's island observatory interiors = great mural quadrant scene (the famous Uraniborg engraving shows Tycho pointing within the quadrant arc).
- **Iconic image set-pieces:** the nested Platonic-solids model (engraved plate in *Mysterium*, with the goblet-like cutaway); *Astronomia Nova*'s battle-of-ovals diagrams; the Rudolphine frontispiece temple; Kepler by candlelight in that frontispiece panel (a gift: he drew his own portrait-scene for us).

## 16. KEY NUMBERS

| Quantity | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tycho's observational accuracy | ~1′ (best), ~2′ working trust for Mars data | pre-telescopic ceiling; ~5× better than predecessors |
| Vicarious hypothesis success | fit longitudes at opposition to ~2′ | best planetary theory ever built to that date |
| The fatal discrepancy | **8′** (at the octants, after distance-consistent correction) | ≈ 1/4 the Moon's apparent width — invisible to any pre-Tycho astronomer |

## 17. MYTHS & HEDGE LIST

1. **"Kepler poisoned Tycho"** — MYTH, debunked by 2010 exhumation (2012 results): no lethal mercury. Do not even wink at it.
2. **"Tycho's silver nose"** — everyday prosthesis was **brass** (2012 analysis). "Silver-and-gold nose" = legend; flag if referenced.
3. **"Mystic who stumbled onto the laws"** — FALSE framing. The ellipse came from years of rigorous error analysis and the refusal to bury 8′. The mysticism (solids, harmonies) supplied motivation, never evidence he accepted against data. This inversion IS the book's thesis.
4. **"Galileo and Kepler, allies"** — one-sided. Kepler praised publicly; Galileo never engaged Kepler's astronomy, ignored the ellipse to his death, sent anagrams not answers.
5. **"Thinking God's thoughts after Him"** — DISPUTED attribution (Wikiquote: no traced source). Do not use as a quote; at most, echo the sentiment in narration without quotation marks.
6. **"Laws of nature are mathematical thoughts of God"** — earliest attribution 1907; avoid.
7. **"I contemplate the sun captured in my dish"** and similar florid lines — unverifiable; skip.
8. Tycho's deathbed "Let me not seem to have lived in vain" — real but **via Kepler's own log entry**; attribute "as Kepler recorded."
9. Banquet/bladder death story — also from Kepler's account; tell it as "the story Kepler told."
10. **Hedges:** exact day of final Graz expulsion (month-level only); "Imperial Mathematician two days after Tycho's death" (say "within days" if cautious); Wallenstein 1634 wording ("terrible confusion" — translations vary); library-sealed-1626 (month uncertain); Susanna's children count (hedge "six children, three surviving" if needed — verify before printing); Latin of the 8′ passage (verify vs. KGW before baking); MacTutor's "poor first-year grade" (contradicts Caspar — omit); the "foolish daughter/wise mother" astrology line (paraphrase — no quote marks).
11. **Vicarious-hypothesis technicality:** sources compress differently (some say the 8′ appeared "in latitudes," some "at the octants after bisecting the eccentricity"). Safe formulation: *"his best circle matched Tycho's oppositions to 2′, but forcing it to also honor distances produced errors of 8′ — and 8′ Tycho's data could not contain."*

## VERBATIM QUOTES BANK

1. **The eight minutes** — *Astronomia Nova* ch. 19, tr. **Donahue** (1992), p. 286: "Since the divine benevolence has vouchsafed us Tycho Brahe, a most diligent observer, from whose observations the 8′ error in this Ptolemaic computation is shown... it is fitting that we with thankful mind both acknowledge and honour this benefit of God... because they could not have been ignored, these eight minutes alone will have led the way to the reformation of all of astronomy, and have constituted the material for a great part of the present work."
2. **The ellipse realization** — *Astronomia Nova* ch. 58: Latin ***"O me ridiculum! perinde quasi libratio in diametro non possit esse via ad ellipsin"*** — "O ridiculous me! As if the libration on the diameter could not be the way to the ellipse." (tr. after Donahue)
3. **Third law dating** — *Harmonice Mundi* V.3, tr. Aiton/Duncan/Field: "...conceived mentally on 8th March in this year one thousand six hundred and eighteen... finally returning on the 15th of May... stormed the darkness of my mind. So strong was the support... that at first I believed I was dreaming..."
4. **Golden vessels / the die is cast** — *Harmonice Mundi* V, introduction: "I am stealing the golden vessels of the Egyptians to build a tabernacle to my God from them... I am indeed casting the die and writing the book, either for my contemporaries or for posterity to read, it matters not which: let the book await its reader for a hundred years; God himself has waited six thousand years for his work to be seen." (translations vary; this follows Wikiquote's cited rendering)
5. **Taking the data** — letter, 1605: "I confess that when Tycho died, I quickly took advantage of the absence, or lack of circumspection, of the heirs, by taking the observations under my care, or perhaps usurping them."
6. **Self-portrait** — 1597 self-horoscope, tr. **Koestler**: "That man has in every way a dog-like nature. His appearance is that of a little lap-dog... He liked gnawing bones and dry crusts of bread... He has a dog-like horror of baths."
7. **Epitaph** — self-composed, 1630: ***"Mensus eram coelos, nunc terrae metior umbras; / Mens coelestis erat, corporis umbra iacet."*** — "I measured the heavens; now I measure the Earth's shadows. The mind was of heaven; the body's shadow lies here."
8. **Tycho's plea** — as Kepler recorded in the log, Oct 1601: *"Ne frustra vixisse videar"* — "Let me not seem to have lived in vain." (attribute via Kepler)
9. **Tycho at table (data piecemeal)** — Kepler letter, tr. Koestler: Tycho "gave me no opportunity to share in his experiences... would only in the course of a meal mention in passing today the figure for the apogee of one planet, tomorrow the nodes of another." (label: Koestler's translation)
