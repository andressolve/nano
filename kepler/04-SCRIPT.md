- TOP BAND: `Johannes Kepler was born on 27 December 1571 in Weil der Stadt, a small walled town in

- BOX upper-right: `His father was a mercenary — a soldier who fought for whoever paid — and kept leaving

- BOX lower-left: `One night in 1577 she led him up a hill to see the Great Comet burning over the town.

  either night.`
- BOTTOM BAND: `Years later, as a mathematician, he calculated the exact minute of his own conception:
  16 May 1571, at 4:37 in the morning. That was the man this boy became — he counted everything.`

## P3 — The Idea in the Classroom (19 July 1595 → 1596) · T5 · ref_kepler_graz
Scene: S3. Graz classroom; Kepler at the wooden board, chalk raised, frozen mid-stroke before a diagram of
a triangle drawn between two circles; summer light; students exchanging looks. Camera: medium-wide from the
back row. Small inset lower-right: the famous nested-solids model as a chalk sketch on the board's corner.
LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `Graz, 19 July 1595. Drawing a geometry figure for his class, Kepler stopped mid-stroke. The
  spacing of the two circles on his board looked like the spacing of the planets' orbits. What if geometry
  itself decided how the heavens are built?`
- BOX left: `Flat shapes failed. Then he tried solid ones. Exactly five perfect solids exist — shapes like
  the cube, every face identical. Nest them one inside another, a sphere between each, and you get six
  spheres. Six was exactly the number of planets then known.`
- BOX right: `He published the idea in 1596 as the Mysterium Cosmographicum — "The Secret of the Universe" —
  the first book by a professional astronomer to openly defend Copernicus.`
- BOTTOM BAND: `The idea was wrong: there are more planets than six, and no invisible solids hold them. But
  no one had ever asked the question before — WHY this many planets, WHY at these distances? He mailed the
  book to the one man whose measurements could test it.`

LETTERING —

- BOX left: `On his island observatory he had built instruments that could fix a star's place to about one

- BOTTOM BAND: `But Tycho guarded his treasure. He "would only in the course of a meal mention in passing
  today the figure for the apogee of one planet, tomorrow the nodes of another," Kepler complained. The old
  man fed the young one crumbs.`

LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `That summer the blow finally fell in Graz: convert to Catholicism, or leave forever. Kepler

- BOX right: `At Tycho's court, one planet was the problem nobody could crack: MARS, whose motion fit the
  old theories worst of all. When Tycho's senior assistant moved to other work, Mars fell to the newcomer.

  planet easily observed. It is the one place where the ancient assumptions would visibly break.`
- BOTTOM BAND: `Kepler bet a colleague he would solve Mars in eight days. It would take him five years. He
  came to call it "my war with Mars."`

LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `On 13 October 1601, at a nobleman's banquet, Tycho held courtesy above comfort and would not

- BOX left: `Over and over in his fever, Kepler wrote, the old man repeated one plea: "Ne frustra vixisse

- BOTTOM BAND: `Not exactly HIS, though. Tycho's heirs claimed the observations, and Kepler later confessed

## P8 — 🔷 PROTOTYPE · The Best Theory Ever Built — and the Crack · T5 · ref_kepler_prague
Scene: S7. Kepler hunched at the desk in deep night, candle guttering, sheets of arithmetic everywhere —
some fallen to the floor; pinned at center a large ink diagram of a circle with measurement points, two of
them circled hard in iron-gall ink. Through the window, the small red point of Mars. Camera: medium-close,
over-the-shoulder toward the diagram and window.
LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `For two thousand years, since the Greeks, astronomy had rested on one article of faith: the
  heavens move in perfect circles, at perfectly steady speeds. Every model ever built — Ptolemy's, even
  Copernicus's — was a machine of circles bolted onto circles.`
- BOX left: `Kepler attacked Mars with circles too — but with Tycho's numbers as judge. After years of
  brutal arithmetic he had built the best circle-theory ever made: it matched Tycho's sightings of Mars's
  direction to within two arcminutes, inside the error of Tycho's own instruments. Victory.`
- BOX right: `Then he forced the same theory to honor the rest of the data — Mars's distances, not just its
  directions. Now some predictions missed by EIGHT arcminutes. Eight-sixtieths of one degree — a quarter of
  the full Moon's width. An error no astronomer before Tycho could even have seen.`
- BOTTOM BAND: `Every astronomer alive would have shrugged and kept the theory. Kepler stared at those
  eight minutes — and chose Tycho's numbers over two thousand years of beautiful circles.`

## P9 — Five Years in the Dark (1602–1605) · T4 · ref_kepler_prague
Scene: S7 by day, months compressed: Kepler standing over the desk, sleeves rolled, arranging sheets in long
rows across desk AND floor; an oval chalked on a slate propped against the wall; morning light. Camera:
wide interior showing the paper battlefield.
LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `He tore it all down — the circles, the steady speeds, everything — and asked a question
  astronomy had never asked: not just WHERE the planets go, but WHAT MOVES THEM. He guessed a power
  spreading from the spinning Sun, growing weaker with distance. The mechanism was wrong. The question was
  the future — it is the grandfather of gravity.`
- BOX right: `Out of it, in 1602, came a real law: a planet hurries when it is near the Sun and slows when
  far away — so exactly that the line from Sun to planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times.`
- BOTTOM BAND: `But the SHAPE of the orbit would not surrender. For two more years he chased an egg-shaped
  oval through hundreds of pages of arithmetic — failing by fractions, starting again. The war with Mars
  was in its fifth year.`

## P10 — O Me Ridiculum! (spring 1605) · T4 · ref_kepler_prague
Scene: S7 at dawn: Kepler on his feet, one hand to his forehead, half-laughing, the other hand flat on two
sheets laid side by side — an equation-covered page and a drawn ellipse; the candle burned to a stub; first
grey-gold light in the window. Camera: medium, front-on, the joy visible.
LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `In the spring of 1605, deep in the arithmetic, he derived an equation for Mars's path — looked
  at it, did not recognize it, and set it aside. Then he tried one more guess: the ellipse — the gently
  flattened circle you draw around two pinned points instead of one.`
- BOX left: `Working the new curve, he suddenly saw it: his abandoned equation and the ellipse were THE
  SAME THING. He had been holding the answer without knowing its face. He laughed at himself in the pages
  of his book: "O me ridiculum!" — Oh, ridiculous me!`
- BOTTOM BAND: `There it was, after five years: Mars moves on an ELLIPSE, with the Sun at one focus — one
  of the two pinned points. No circles. No excuses. The first true law of the heavens ever written down.`

## P12 — The Other Wonders of Prague (1604–1611) · T5 · ref_kepler_prague
Scene: S7, evening: Kepler demonstrating with a candle and a small pierced screen (camera-obscura style),
projecting a tiny inverted flame image onto paper for two visitors (unnamed); pinned to the wall, a large
ink diagram of the human eye; through the window a brilliant NEW STAR outshining everything near it.
Camera: medium-wide, three figures, warm demonstration light.
LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `Prague made him more than the man of Mars. In October 1604 a new star blazed out, brighter
  than Jupiter, then faded over a year — the last exploding star ever seen in our galaxy with the naked
  eye. It is still called Kepler's Star.`
- BOX left: `That same year he solved vision itself: the eye's lens paints a tiny upside-down picture on
  the back wall of the eye, and that picture is what we see. He explained, at last, why spectacles work.
  The man who could barely see had explained seeing.`
- BOX right: `When Galileo turned the new telescope on the sky in 1610, Kepler backed him in print sight
  unseen — then worked out the theory of the telescope, and a design of his own that astronomers still call
  "Keplerian." Galileo never returned the favor: he sent Kepler riddles instead of answers, and never once
  accepted the ellipse.`
- BOTTOM small box: `The admiration ran one way. Kepler praised. Galileo kept score.`

  thin pieces: a stepping stone toward what the world would one day call calculus.`
- BOTTOM small box: `And from home, darker news: in 1615, back in Württemberg, a neighbor accused his
  mother of witchcraft.`

## P15 — The Harmony and the War (May 1618–1619) · T5 · ref_kepler_older
Scene: S9 night study: Kepler radiant over sheets ruled like music staves mixed with geometric diagrams;
candle high; through the window, far on the horizon, a dull red glow and a thin line of smoke — the page's
only red. Camera: medium, the joy and the horizon in one frame.
LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `Through everything he had chased one more pattern — a single rule binding ALL the planets. On
  15 May 1618 it surrendered, and it was simple and exact: the square of any planet's year, divided by the
  cube of its distance from the Sun, gives the very same number for every planet. One rule for all the
  worlds.`
- QUOTE BOX: `"So strong was the support... that at first I believed I was dreaming," he wrote.`
- BOX right: `Eight days later, rebels in Prague threw the Emperor's governors out of a castle window, and
  the Thirty Years' War began — the most destructive war Europe had yet known. Kepler heard the harmony of
  the world in the very month the world began tearing itself apart.`
- BOTTOM BAND: `He published the law in 1619 in a book named HARMONICE MUNDI — "The Harmony of the World" —
  and dared the future to catch up: "Let the book await its reader for a hundred years; God himself has
  waited six thousand years for his work to be seen."`

## P19 — 🔷 PROTOTYPE · The Ellipse Never Ended (finale, closing-as-invention) · T5 · generate_image
Scene: S13. A vast deep-blue starfield fills the page. A great thin GOLDEN ELLIPSE spans it; the Sun a
small warm blaze at the lower-left focus; Mars a small red-orange point on the line. Stationed along the

LETTERING —
- TOP BAND: `One year after his death came the proof. Kepler had predicted that on 7 November 1631 the
  planet Mercury would cross the face of the Sun. In Paris, the astronomer Pierre Gassendi watched it
  happen — within hours of the Tables' prediction, where the old tables missed by days. From then on, the
  sky was computed Kepler's way.`
- BOX left: `In 1634 his son published his strangest manuscript: SOMNIUM — "The Dream" — the tale of a
  voyage to the Moon, and of how the heavens look from there. Many call it the first work of science

- BOX right: `Fifty-seven years after his death, Isaac Newton showed WHY the planets obey Kepler's three
  laws: a single force — gravity — bends their paths into exactly his ellipses. The stubborn eight minutes
  had led straight to the law that holds the universe together.`
- BOTTOM BAND: `He wanted the heavens to be harmony, and he loved his beautiful ideas. But when the data

  whole discipline of science. He did it first.`

## Quiz seed (build at reader stage — WHY questions, shuffled positions, length-matched distractors)
1. Why did eight arcminutes matter when every earlier astronomer would have ignored them? (Tycho's
   instruments were trusted to ~2′ — the error was real, not noise.)
2. Why was Mars the luckiest possible assignment? (Most stretched-out orbit among observable planets — the
   place circles visibly fail.)
3. Why was Kepler barred from communion in Linz — and what does it have in common with the eight minutes?
   (He would not sign what he doubted; same refusal to say what he did not believe.)
4. Why did Kepler — not a lawyer — win his mother's witch trial? (He dismantled the evidence point by
   point: error analysis applied to testimony.)
5. Why were the Rudolphine Tables the answer to Tycho's dying plea? (Tycho's data + Kepler's laws = the
   proof Tycho had not lived in vain; vindicated by the 1631 Mercury transit.)
