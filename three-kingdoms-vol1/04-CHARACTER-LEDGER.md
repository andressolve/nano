# Character Ledger — 8 locks, hard ceiling

*Page references match the 49-page script; from p 8 on, add one (the owner split page 7 on 2026-10-04; script now 50 pages). Costume switches happen at the forge (p 8), not at the oath.*

Identity hierarchy, in order of priority:
**1.** face shape and apparent age · **2.** hair and facial-hair mass ·
**3.** body silhouette and habitual posture · **4.** costume value pattern ·
**5.** colour accent, prop, repeated gesture.

> Costume and colour may reinforce a character; they may never be the only
> cues. Separation is built through skull, jaw, nose, eye set, hairline,
> apparent age, body mass and posture — the traits that survive grayscale and
> thumbnail reduction. **Every page prompt names both the positive lock and the
> nearest forbidden lookalike.**

The cast divides into silhouettes that must never blur: **three brothers**
who must read as three different species of man before any colour arrives
(slim and long-armed with a light beard; a towering red-faced giant with a
beard to his belt; a stocky barrel of a man with bristling whiskers and
round eyes); **two warriors of the tyrant's** who must never blur with the
brothers (one obese and old, one tall, beardless, and plumed); **two lords**
(a small quick man with a pointed beard; a tall thin aristocrat with a long
three-strand beard and a high hat); and **one old farmer**. The colour
system reinforces it, one owner per colour: Liu Bei white, Guan Yu green,
Zhang Fei black, Cao Cao indigo, Lü Bu vermilion, Dong Zhuo purple and gold,
Yuan Shao ivory and gold, the Yellow Turbans yellow. **No locked character
wears yellow.**

The danger pairs are **Liu Bei / Cao Cao** (two light-bearded men of thirty
who share three scenes), **Guan Yu / Lü Bu** (two giants on horseback with
long-bladed polearms), and **Dong Zhuo / Yuan Shao** (two great lords in
silk).

## 1 · LIU BEI — 28

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** from the great men, to be seen for what his blood says he
is. From his brothers, to deserve that they followed him. From Dong Zhuo,
an acknowledgment he never gets. **Wants (secretly):** the empire. He would
die before saying it. On page 47 Cao Cao names him one of two heroes, and Liu Bei is ashamed of how much he wants it to be true.

**Moral position:** believes he is serving the emperor and the people.
Actually wants to matter more than anything, swallows every insult because
he has learned that a sandal-seller who shows his anger is laughed at, and
finds out at Hulao that the oath matters more to him than being seen. Nobody
says this.

**Arc:** sighs at a wall → ashamed in front of two strangers → swears → rides
out as bait → saves a general who turns his back → swallows it, and holds
Zhang Fei's wrist → reads the letter aloud → called a sandal-seller in front
of eighteen lords → sits at the end of the bench → watches his brothers face
Lü Bu → draws his swords and rides in → says the word "brothers" up at a wall
→ told he is one of two heroes → says he is only a sandal-seller.

**Structural lock:** a broad, compact oval face, smooth and pale, with a
high clear brow, full cheeks, a full lower jaw, and a short, bluntly rounded
chin. The jaw does not taper. His gentle, slightly downturned almond eyes
look sad even when he smiles. **Unusually long, heavy earlobes** hang nearly
to the jaw line, the one feature no one else in the book has. A straight
medium nose, a small mouth, and a thin, neat black moustache; beneath the
lower lip, the chin and jaw are clean-shaven. Black hair in a plain topknot
under a simple dark cloth cap. Tall and slim, long-necked, with narrow,
sloping shoulders that keep his silhouette open and unlike Cao Cao's compact
one. Posture: upright, hands folded low into his sleeves, head very slightly
bowed, until he draws. Habitual gesture: a small bow with folded hands, the
bow of a man used to selling things.

**Costume:** Movement I: undyed hemp robe, faded, patched at the elbows,
straw sandals on his feet, a bundle of straw sandals on a pole. From the
forge (p 8) on: plain dark iron lamellar over a white robe with modest, unbulky
sleeves, a white cloak, white headband, still the straw sandals in the
stirrups; two matching straight swords in black scabbards worn crossed high
on his back, hilts above the shoulders, sheathed until page 44. Accent:
white.

**Never:** a long or tapered lower face; any beard beneath the lower lip or
on the chin or jaw; a pointed goatee, narrow slanted eyes, short stature,
indigo (Cao Cao). A red face, a long beard (Guan Yu). Whiskers on the cheeks
(Zhang Fei). Yellow of any kind (the rebels).

**Voice:** courteous, speaks of others first, asks questions, and lets the
hunger show in what he asks. "Why would anyone follow a man who sells
sandals?"

## 2 · GUAN YU — about 30

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** from Liu Bei, a cause worth the man he killed. From the
lords, the respect his rank denies him, which he takes with a blade.
**Wants (secretly):** to be the man in the stories. He knows how he looks
stroking that beard.

**Moral position:** believes in honor as a thing a man owns, like a weapon.
Actually proud enough that a single sneer from Yuan Shao sends him out alone
against a champion; the wine is a boast, and he makes it good.

**Arc:** bangs the tavern table → "I did not come for your ancestors" →
swears → comes down the left hill → mistrusts Cao Cao's smile → takes down
the blade → "I will bring his helmet to this table" → the cup still warm →
"Cao Cao watched a son kill his father, and he went on bowing" → "Third
Brother, I am here" → "No."

**Structural lock:** the tallest of the brothers by a head. **A deep red,
date-coloured face**, the colour of the skin itself, not a flush, and the
only red face in the book. Long narrow **phoenix eyes** that tilt upward at
the outer corners, usually half-lidded. Heavy, straight, dark brows like
silkworms. A long straight nose. **A long, glossy black beard that falls in
a single sheet to his belt**;
a long moustache joining it; the beard is always loose, never bagged. Hair under a green cloth headscarf. Massive
shoulders, a long torso, a still, upright posture: he never hurries.
Habitual gesture: stroking the beard downward with one hand before he speaks.

**Costume:** a dark green robe over dark lamellar armor, a green headscarf.
The Green Dragon Crescent Blade: a long-handled glaive with a broad
crescent blade and a dragon worked at its base. Accent: green.

**Never:** a pale face, a clean-shaven face, pheasant plumes, vermilion (Lü
Bu). Round bulging eyes, bristling whiskers (Zhang Fei). A short or pointed
beard (Cao Cao, Liu Bei).

**Voice:** few words, formal, final. Says what he will do and then does it.
"Pour it and leave it. I will be back before it is cold."

## 3 · ZHANG FEI — about 25

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** from everyone, a fight, now. From Liu Bei, permission to
hit the people who insult him. **Wants (secretly):** a big brother. He sells
his shop for one in the first movement.

**Moral position:** believes every insult should be answered on the spot.
Actually the only one of the three who says what all three feel; his
temper is the brothers' honesty, and Liu Bei's grip on his wrist is the
book's first test of the oath.

**Arc:** "Why not do something?" → the peach garden is his → sells
everything → "a hundred each" → comes down the right hill → likes Cao Cao at
once → draws on Dong Zhuo, stopped → "the fat pig we saved" → "let's take
the whole gate" → "Lü Bu killed his own father for a horse?" → "Stop, you
slave of three fathers!" → fifty bouts → hammers the gate.

**Structural lock:** a broad, square, heavy head like a leopard's, **round
bulging eyes** with white all round the iris, thick wild eyebrows. A wide
flat nose, a big mouth usually open. **Short, black, bristling whiskers that
stand out all around the jaw and cheeks like a tiger's**, a short spiky
moustache; never long, never smooth. Dark, sun-tanned, ruddy-brown skin.
Black hair pulled up roughly, strands escaping. **Short and enormously
broad**: barrel chest, thick neck, arms like hams, a head shorter than Guan
Yu and twice Liu Bei's width. Posture: fists on hips, chest out, or leaning
forward ready to charge. Habitual gesture: throwing his head back to laugh
or roar.

**Costume:** Movement I: a butcher's leather apron over a dark tunic,
sleeves rolled. From the forge (p 8) on: black lamellar armor, black cloak, a black
horse. The serpent spear: a very long spear with a wavy, snake-shaped blade.
Accent: black.

**Never:** a long beard, a red face (Guan Yu). Fat or age (Dong Zhuo, whose
heaviness is soft and old where Zhang Fei's is hard and young). A smooth or
pointed beard (Cao Cao).

**Voice:** loud, blunt, funny, names things, threatens and jokes in the same
breath, always calls Liu Bei "Big Brother." "Fifty thousand? Good. That's a
hundred each."

## 4 · CAO CAO — about 30

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** from the brothers, men who win, for himself. From Dong
Zhuo, his death. From the lords, an army he can lead without being its
leader. **Wants (secretly):** the empire, and he is the only one in the book
honest enough to say so, to the only man he thinks can stop him.

**Moral position:** believes the empire needs saving and that he is the man
to do it. Actually will do anything to do it, including murder an innocent
old man to keep himself safe. The book never rules on him.

**Arc:** lights the grass → "then you came because you wanted to" → at
Dong Zhuo's court: "Lü Bu" → watches Lü Bu kneel → the dagger, the mirror,
the bluff → the farmhouse → "I would rather betray the whole world" → the
letter → "he came; give him a seat" → the cup of hot wine → "I want you three
with me" → holds his riders still on his own hill → "On my hill, watching" →
"two heroes: you, and me."

**Structural lock:** **short**, a head shorter than Liu Bei, compact and
quick. A narrow, angular face with sharp cheekbones and a pointed chin.
**Narrow, slanted, glittering eyes** under thin arched brows, one often
raised; the eyes are always amused. A slightly hooked nose. **A neatly
trimmed moustache and a short pointed goatee**, both black and precise.
Black hair in a tight topknot under a small black official's cap. Posture:
weight on one hip, head tilted, chin forward. Habitual gesture: a cup in his
hand, or one finger raised as he makes a point; a quick, sudden laugh.

**Costume:** at court, a dark indigo official robe and black cap; in the
field, dark indigo lacquered armor and a black cloak, black-and-indigo
banners. The Seven-Star dagger: a short dagger with seven jewels set in the
hilt and scabbard. Accent: indigo.

**Never:** long earlobes, a soft oval face, height, white (Liu Bei). A long
beard (Guan Yu, Yuan Shao). Vermilion (Lü Bu). Yellow.

**Voice:** quick, witty, flattering, laughs at his own jokes, and goes cold
in a sentence. "Yuan Shao has ancestors. Ancestors don't fight Lü Bu."

## 5 · DONG ZHUO — about 55

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** from the empire, everything: the throne room, the bed,
the dishes, the boy. From Lü Bu, a son who cannot be beaten. From the
brothers, nothing; they are nobody. **Wants (secretly):** to be feared by
the people who once looked down on a general from the west.

**Moral position:** believes he is restoring order to a weak court. Actually
taking it for himself, and the reader watches him do it without a word of
explanation.

**Arc:** surrounded and saved → "What office do you hold?" → his back →
rides up the palace steps → lifts the boy onto the throne → "Do you think my
sword isn't sharp?" → "Then I want him" → leads Red Hare into the stable →
"Father" → faces the wall → the dagger in the mirror → "a red-faced common
soldier" → sends Lü Bu out of the gate.

**Structural lock:** **obese**: a huge round belly, heavy jowls, a thick
neck that merges with his chin, small eyes sunk in fat, a fleshy nose. A
thin, drooping black moustache and a few strands of beard, greying. Heavy
eyelids, a wet mouth. Black-and-grey hair under a tall gold-ornamented
cap. Posture: reclining, leaning back, or carried; when he stands he sways.
Habitual gesture: food in one hand, a drumstick, a cup, a fruit, in nearly
every panel.

**Costume:** in the field, gilded armor straining over the belly. In the
capital, robes of dark purple and gold brocade, heavy rings. Accent: purple
and gold.

**Never:** muscle (Zhang Fei, whose bulk is hard and young). A long thin
beard, a thin body, ivory (Yuan Shao). Indigo (Cao Cao).

**Voice:** mocking, greedy, calls people "little" and "boy," asks questions
he already knows the answer to. "You have a father? Do you?"

## 6 · LÜ BU — about 30

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** to be paid what he is worth, and to be the best. From
Dong Zhuo, the horse, the armor, the gold. From the brothers, nothing until
page 44, when he wants to get away. **Wants (secretly):** a father who is
worth obeying, and he has just killed one and bought another.

**Moral position:** believes loyalty is a price. Actually is the
anti-oath: a man who changes fathers for a horse, against three men who
swore to die together.

**Arc:** stands behind the old man → offered Red Hare → "I have a father" →
the seal on the floor, "Father" → sent to choose a horse for Cao Cao →
"give me the gate" → rides out → champions fall → the halberd over Yuan
Shao → laughs at Zhang Fei, laughs at two → cannot see all three → runs →
"next time, one at a time."

**Structural lock:** tall, as tall as Guan Yu, but long-legged and lean
where Guan Yu is massive. **Handsome and clean-shaven**: the only beardless
grown warrior in the book. A long face, high cheekbones, a strong straight
jaw, a pale complexion, thin lips set in a half-smile. Cold, wide-set eyes
under straight brows. **Two very long pheasant tail-feathers** rising and
curving back from a gold helmet or headdress, so his silhouette is tallest
in any crowd. Posture: easy, loose, the halberd rested across his shoulder,
looking down at people. Habitual gesture: a lazy turn of the halberd, a
laugh.

**Costume:** gold-and-bronze armor with a beast-face belt plate, a vermilion
cloak and vermilion war-robe. The sky-piercer halberd: a long halberd with a
crescent side-blade. Red Hare: a huge blood-red horse with a darker mane.
Accent: vermilion.

**Never:** a beard of any kind, a red face, green (Guan Yu). Black armor
(Zhang Fei). Indigo (Cao Cao).

**Voice:** few words, vain, about himself, his horse, and what he is owed.
"Give me the gate, father, and I will give you all eighteen."

## 7 · YUAN SHAO — about 45

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** from the lords, to lead them. From the brothers, that
they know their place. From Dong Zhuo, his throne room back, for his family.
**Wants (secretly):** to be thanked for it.

**Moral position:** believes rank is worth, and his family has had rank for
four generations. Actually brave once, in a throne room, and since then
mostly concerned with who sits where; saved at Hulao by the men he threw
out, he still calls Liu Bei a sandal-seller.

**Arc:** "Four generations of my family" → "Do you think mine isn't sharp?"
→ chosen leader → "since when does the imperial family sell sandals?" →
"the end of the bench" → "if my generals were here" → "a common soldier?
Throw him out" → "throw out these three nobodies" → "is there no one?" →
his horse stumbles → "stay back, sandal-seller."

**Structural lock:** tall and thin, stiff-backed. **A long, narrow, refined
face with a high forehead**, a long thin nose, a small mouth turned down.
Hooded eyes looking down that nose. **A long, thin, carefully combed black
beard in three strands**, one at the chin and one at each corner of the
mouth, reaching his chest, streaked with first grey. **A tall black
lacquered official's hat** with a flat board on top. Posture: chin up,
shoulders back, hands behind his back or on his sword hilt. Habitual
gesture: smoothing the three strands of his beard between two fingers.

**Costume:** ivory and pale-gold silk with embroidered borders; in the field,
gilded scale armor under an ivory cloak, a great gold command banner behind
him. Accent: ivory and gold.

**Never:** obesity, purple (Dong Zhuo). A pointed short beard, short stature
(Cao Cao). A single sheet of beard to the belt, a red face (Guan Yu). White
hemp (Liu Bei).

**Voice:** pompous, counts ancestors, uses "we" for the lords, never speaks
to the brothers directly when he can speak about them. "Since when does the
imperial family sell sandals?"

## 8 · UNCLE (Lü Boshe) — about 65

**Anchor:** new. **Never named on the page.** Cao Cao calls him "Uncle"; the
narration calls him "the old man." The name Lü must never appear near Lü
Bu's.

**Wants (volume):** from Cao Cao, to give him the best night of his life,
for his sworn brother's sake. **Wants (secretly):** to be remembered as a
good host.

**Moral position:** a good man who keeps an old oath. He is the book's other
sworn brother, and what happens to him is what an oath is worth to Cao Cao.

**Arc:** opens the gate → "your father and I swore brotherhood" → rides for
wine → comes home up the road with a jar and a basket → "Leaving already? I
told them to kill a pig for you!" → "Sorry? Sorry for what?"

**Structural lock:** thin, small, a little bent. A round, deeply lined face
with crow's feet from smiling, cheeks like apples, **nearly bald with a
thin white topknot**, **a sparse white beard** and white eyebrows. Missing a
tooth. Posture: leaning forward, arms open. Habitual gesture: both hands
spread in welcome.

**Costume:** brown and russet farmer's cotton, a straw hat on his back, rides
a grey donkey. Accent: the brown of the earth.

**Never:** black hair, a tall hat, silk (Yuan Shao). Fat (Dong Zhuo).

**Voice:** warm, fussy, talks too much, fond. "Your father's son, at my
gate! Come in, come in, I'll ride for the good wine."

## Reserved design space

No background or unnamed figure may inherit a complete identity stack:

- red face + long black beard → **Guan Yu**
- long pheasant plumes + vermilion → **Lü Bu**
- obese + purple and gold → **Dong Zhuo**
- tall black lacquered hat + three-strand beard → **Yuan Shao**
- long earlobes + white cloak → **Liu Bei**
- bristling cheek-whiskers + black armor → **Zhang Fei**
- short + pointed goatee + indigo → **Cao Cao**
- bald + white beard + donkey → **Uncle**

Scenery guidance: rebels wear yellow headbands and are bareheaded or in
plain caps; coalition lords other than Yuan Shao wear dull bronze and
brown and are never shown in close-up; the boy emperor is a small figure in
imperial yellow robes on a vast throne, the one yellow in the palace (he
belongs to no faction; his yellow is the throne's).
