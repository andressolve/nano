## Blind read
A red-faced, green-robed warrior enters a crowded tavern, demands wine, and announces that he will enlist before the day ends. A broad, exuberant patron recognizes his soldierly spirit and invites him to sit with the two men already drinking. The warrior identifies himself as Guan Yu and explains that he killed a rich man who dispossessed the poor, making him a fugitive for five years. The quieter patron challenges the contradiction between joining the emperor and having fled imperial soldiers. Guan Yu answers that rebels burn villages like his and that he will fight anyone who does the same. Guan Yu unmistakably owns the page; the turn is caused by the quiet patron's challenge, which makes Guan Yu define his allegiance by protection of villages rather than by loyalty to whoever commands the army.
