## Retell

China is starving under a failing Han dynasty, and the Yellow Turbans are marching in rebellion. In Zhuo County, Liu Bei is a poor straw-sandal seller descended from princes. He wants to answer a call for volunteers but has neither money nor weapons. Zhang Fei, a booming, wealthy butcher, notices Liu Bei brooding over the notice and challenges him to act. They meet Guan Yu, a formidable red-faced fugitive who killed a rich man for stealing poor farmers' land. Although Guan Yu distrusts the emperor's soldiers, he agrees that the rebels must be stopped because they burn villages.

The three men talk through the night, swear brotherhood in Zhang Fei's peach garden, and pledge to protect the people and serve the empire. Liu Bei becomes eldest brother, Guan Yu second, and Zhang Fei third. Zhang Fei sells almost everything he owns to equip them. A smith forges Liu Bei two swords, Zhang Fei a long serpent-bladed spear, and Guan Yu a massive curved polearm called the Green Dragon. Five hundred farmers join and train under them.

Their first enemy is a Yellow Turban host a hundred times their size. Liu Bei refuses a direct battle: he draws the rebels between two hills while Guan Yu and Zhang Fei wait in ambush. The trap works, and the impossible victory draws more volunteers to the brothers. They march on the greatest remaining rebel army, only to discover that imperial cavalry commander Cao Cao has already set its dry-grass camp on fire and routed it. Cao Cao respects Liu Bei because his men follow without pay; Zhang Fei likes Cao Cao at once, while Guan Yu distrusts how readily he smiles.

Weeks later the brothers rescue an imperial general, Dong Zhuo, from another battle. Dong Zhuo immediately loses interest when he learns that they are unpaid volunteers with no rank and dismisses them with only a bag of rice. Zhang Fei wants to kill him, but Liu Bei restrains him. By winter the rebellion is broken, the celebrated generals take titles and rewards, and the brothers and their five hundred farmers return home with nothing.

Five years pass. The old emperor dies, and powerful men at court begin killing one another. Dong Zhuo marches into the disorder with an army, seizes the capital, and takes control of the boy emperor. He openly humiliates the court, proposes replacing the young ruler with his nine-year-old brother, and threatens Yuan Shao when he objects. Yuan Shao defies him but leaves. At a banquet another old lord objects, and Dong Zhuo's terrifying young champion Lu Bu silences the room. Cao Cao explains that Lu Bu is the old lord's adopted son and is thought unbeatable.

Dong Zhuo decides to buy Lu Bu's loyalty. He gives him Red Hare, a peerless horse, then persuades him that his adoptive father never valued him. Lu Bu kills the man who raised him, accepts Dong Zhuo as his new father, and gains rich armor and status. Cao Cao sees exactly what this reveals about him.

With Lu Bu protecting him, Dong Zhuo rules as a tyrant: he robs the capital, controls the emperor, and makes every lord submit or flee. Cao Cao hides his opposition behind smiles and deference while carrying a jeweled dagger into court. When Lu Bu leaves to choose him a faster horse, Cao Cao tries to assassinate the sleeping Dong Zhuo. Dong Zhuo wakes and sees the raised blade in a mirror. Cao Cao instantly pretends the dagger is a gift, asks to try the promised horse, and escapes the city. Dong Zhuo soon understands the deception and places a reward on Cao Cao's head.

Cao Cao takes refuge with an old sworn brother of his father. The host rides away to buy wine and arrange a feast. Hearing people in the next room whisper about tying and cutting before someone wakes, Cao Cao assumes he has been betrayed and kills everyone in the house. He then discovers that they had only been preparing a pig for him. When the innocent old host returns, Cao Cao kills him too rather than leave a witness, declaring that he would rather betray the whole world than let the world betray him.

Cao Cao reaches home, sells his father's land, raises an army, and sends letters across the realm calling loyal men to rise against Dong Zhuo. One letter reaches Liu Bei, still selling sandals in Zhuo County. Guan Yu recognizes Cao Cao as dangerously quick to smile, but Liu Bei says the summons is for everybody, not just for Cao Cao's friends. The brothers take up their weapons and set out to correct the mistake they made when they saved Dong Zhuo.

## Cast as read

- **Liu Bei:** Tall and lean, with a narrow mustache, composed features, pale robes, and two matching swords. A poor sandal seller descended from princes; eldest sworn brother, moral center, recruiter, and strategist of the trio.
- **Guan Yu:** Very tall, red-faced, stern, and long-bearded, dressed in green and carrying the enormous curved Green Dragon blade. A fugitive who killed an abusive landowner; second brother and the trio's wary, principled judge of character.
- **Zhang Fei:** Huge, broad, wild-haired, heavily bearded, loud, and expressive, later armored and armed with a long snake-bladed spear. A butcher, farmer, and financier; youngest brother, exuberant bruiser, and the quickest to anger or laugh.
- **Cao Cao:** Lean, sharp-eyed, mustached and goateed, usually in dark armor and a black-blue cloak. First an imperial cavalry commander and clever battlefield opportunist, then a concealed assassin and rebel organizer. Charismatic, perceptive, resourceful, and chillingly ruthless when afraid.
- **Dong Zhuo:** Enormously fat, richly dressed in purple and gold, indulgent, contemptuous, and physically imposing even at rest. The general once rescued by the brothers who later seizes the capital and rules through the child emperor.
- **Lu Bu:** Young, handsome, and severe, with long dark hair, ornate red-and-gold armor, tall feather plumes, and a great halberd. The court's unbeatable warrior; he murders one adoptive father for Red Hare and becomes Dong Zhuo's adopted son and shield.
- **Yuan Shao:** Slender, dignified, bearded, and dressed in pale court robes. A lord from a family that has served four generations of emperors; he openly challenges Dong Zhuo's attempted change of ruler and walks out alive.
- **The old host:** A smiling, white-haired countryman who was the sworn brother of Cao Cao's father. He shelters Cao Cao without hesitation and is murdered for that kindness.
- **The boy ruler and his younger brother:** Children used as pieces in Dong Zhuo's seizure of the court; Dong Zhuo declares the reigning boy weak and moves to put the nine-year-old brother in his place.
- **The Yellow Turbans and the brothers' farmers:** The mass rebel threat and the unpaid village volunteers who allow the brothers' oath to become action.

## Causal chain

Famine and rebellion produce a call for volunteers → Liu Bei's helpless frustration attracts Zhang Fei → Zhang Fei supplies money and introduces the means to act → Guan Yu joins because the rebels harm villagers → the peach-garden oath creates a brotherhood and mission → Zhang Fei's sacrifice equips five hundred volunteers → Liu Bei's ambush turns impossible odds into a reputation → that reputation grows their following and brings them into the imperial campaign → Cao Cao notices Liu Bei's unusual loyalty-based leadership → the brothers save Dong Zhuo but his contempt sends them home unrewarded → five years of court decay let Dong Zhuo seize the capital → Dong Zhuo buys Lu Bu with Red Hare and gains an unbeatable protector → Cao Cao's failed assassination makes him a fugitive → fear makes Cao Cao slaughter an innocent household and its returning host → he nevertheless raises the wider resistance Dong Zhuo has made necessary → his letter reaches the brothers → they march to undo the consequence of having saved Dong Zhuo.

## Reading-pacing check

No page required backtracking or rereading, and I did not misidentify a recurring character. The two large time transitions—winter ending the rebellion on page 16 and Cao Cao's letter returning the story to Liu Bei on page 29—were explicit and easy to follow.

The one event supplied substantially through dialogue rather than being shown is Lu Bu's murder of his first adoptive father before page 21. I understood it immediately from Dong Zhuo's claim that the old man would never refuse again, Lu Bu's acceptance of Dong Zhuo as “Father,” and Cao Cao's statement that Lu Bu killed the man who raised him for a horse. It did not require a guess.

All English narration and dialogue was legible at reading pace. Speech order and attribution held throughout, including the crowded conversations on pages 13, 15, 18–21, and 23–24. I encountered no materially confusing text or attribution.

## What I expect next

Cao Cao's call will gather a coalition of lords and armies against Dong Zhuo. Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei will re-enter the national struggle without official rank and will have to prove themselves again in front of men who value titles. Lu Bu is set up as the immediate military obstacle, while Dong Zhuo is the political target. I expect the brothers to meet Cao Cao again and Guan Yu's distrust to matter: Cao Cao is genuinely opposing the tyrant, but his murder of the innocent household shows that he may become as dangerous to decent people as the enemy he is fighting. Yuan Shao also seems likely to return as a leader or major member of the anti-Dong Zhuo alliance.
