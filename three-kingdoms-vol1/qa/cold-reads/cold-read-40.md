## Retell of the story so far

After four centuries of Han rule, famine and the Yellow Turban rebellion are tearing China apart. In Zhuo County, Liu Bei is a poor straw-sandal seller descended from princes. He wants to answer a call for volunteers but believes he has nothing useful to offer. Zhang Fei, a loud, wealthy butcher, sees him studying the notice, challenges his self-pity, and takes him drinking. At the tavern they meet Guan Yu, a huge red-faced fugitive who killed a rich man for abusing the poor. The three discover that they share the same purpose. At Zhang Fei's farm they swear in the peach garden to become brothers, protect the people, serve the empire, and die together.

They sell what they own, keep only the peach garden, arm themselves, and gather five hundred farmers. Liu Bei turns their weakness into a trap: rather than meet fifty thousand rebels head-on, he draws the enemy into the hills, where Guan Yu and Zhang Fei attack from either side. Their first victory makes their name and draws more men. Later they arrive at a vast rebel camp just after Cao Cao, an imperial cavalry commander, has destroyed it with a midnight grass fire. Cao Cao is impressed that Liu Bei's unpaid farmers came voluntarily; Zhang Fei likes him, but Guan Yu distrusts how quickly he smiles.

The brothers next rescue a surrounded imperial general, Dong Zhuo. Once safe, Dong Zhuo asks their offices and ranks. When Liu Bei admits that they are merely volunteers, Dong Zhuo dismisses them with a bag of rice. Liu Bei prevents Zhang Fei from attacking him because, however insulting he is, Dong Zhuo still wears the emperor's armor.

The rebellion is broken by winter, decorated generals take the rewards, and the brothers return home with nothing. Five years later the old emperor dies and violence breaks out at court. Dong Zhuo marches west with an army, supposedly to restore order, but instead seizes the capital. He deposes the emperor's older son, installs the nine-year-old younger brother, and makes himself the real power beside the child. Yuan Shao openly tells him that his family has served four generations of emperors and refuses to accept his authority. Dong Zhuo threatens him, but Yuan Shao is equally ready to draw steel and walks out.

At a banquet another lord repeats the protest. Dong Zhuo's champion, Lü Bu, makes resistance impossible. Cao Cao identifies Lü Bu as an adopted son whose martial ability is unmatched. Dong Zhuo decides to win him. He gives Lü Bu the incomparable red horse Red Hare, claims that a true father would give a son everything, and supplies gold armor. Lü Bu accepts Dong Zhuo as his new father after killing the man who raised him.

With the boy emperor and Lü Bu under his control, Dong Zhuo rules the capital as a tyrant. Lords either flee or bow. Cao Cao bows lowest, visits daily, and pretends to be pleased while hiding a jeweled dagger. When Dong Zhuo falls asleep, Cao Cao draws it to kill him, but Dong Zhuo sees the raised blade reflected in a mirror. Cao Cao instantly presents the dagger as a gift, asks to try a fast horse, and escapes before Dong Zhuo and Lü Bu work out what happened.

Now wanted across the empire, Cao Cao hides with an old man who calls Cao Cao's father his sworn brother. During the night Cao Cao hears knives being sharpened and whispered instructions to tie legs and make one clean cut. Believing the household is preparing to murder him, he kills everyone. He then finds the trussed pig they were actually preparing for his dinner. When the innocent old man returns with wine and food, Cao Cao kills him too so that the crime cannot be reported, declaring that he would rather betray the whole world than let the world betray him.

Cao Cao returns home, sells his family's land, raises an army, and sends a call throughout the empire for every loyal man to march against Dong Zhuo. One letter reaches Liu Bei, still selling sandals in Zhuo County. Liu Bei, Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, and their five hundred farmers join the eighteen lords gathering before Hulao Gate, the fortress blocking the road to the capital. Yuan Shao leads the coalition. He mocks Liu Bei's humble trade and gives the brothers only a place at the back because they have no rank. Cao Cao speaks for Liu Bei, reminding Yuan Shao that the volunteers came when called and have already defeated Yellow Turbans.

Dong Zhuo's champion rides out and kills the coalition's best fighters one after another. Yuan Shao longs for a great absent general, and Guan Yu volunteers to fight. Yuan Shao initially refuses to let an unranked common soldier represent the eighteen lords, but Cao Cao argues that the enemy will judge Guan Yu by his size, not his title. Cao Cao offers Guan Yu hot wine before battle. Guan Yu leaves it on the table and returns with the champion's helmet before the cup cools.

Instead of honoring the brothers, Yuan Shao throws them out of the commanders' tent. Cao Cao joins them privately and warns that Dong Zhuo will now send Lü Bu, who killed one father for a horse and calls Dong Zhuo Father. Cao Cao asks the brothers to face Lü Bu with him rather than with Yuan Shao; Liu Bei answers that they came to fight Dong Zhuo and will stand beside anyone who does the same. Guan Yu notes that Cao Cao watched a son kill his father and continued bowing to Dong Zhuo.

That night Dong Zhuo orders the gate opened, and Lü Bu promises to bring him all eighteen lords. Mounted on Red Hare, Lü Bu destroys each champion sent against him in no more than three blows. By page 40 he is riding alone into the coalition army, demanding to know whether anyone among eighteen armies can stop one man, while Liu Bei and his brothers watch from the front.

## Cast

- **Liu Bei:** A tall, lean, quiet young man with a narrow face, small mustache, and notably long ears. He begins in patched gray-white robes and later wears pale armor and a white cloak, carrying twin straight swords. He is the impoverished descendant of princes, a sandal seller, the eldest sworn brother, and the strategic and moral center of the trio. He turns farmers into an army but remains reluctant to break faith with the empire even when its officials mistreat him.

- **Zhang Fei:** An enormous, broad, hairy butcher with a wild black beard, booming manner, and frequent grin. He wears rough dark clothes or black armor and fights with a long snake-bladed spear. He has a farm, shop, livestock, and money; he spends them to launch the brothers' cause. He is the youngest brother, the most openly emotional, and always ready to answer insult with violence.

- **Guan Yu:** A towering, red-faced man with stern eyes and an exceptionally long black beard. He wears green robes over armor and carries the massive curved Green Dragon polearm. He is a fugitive because he killed an abusive rich man, the second brother, the trio's most intimidating fighter, and a sharp judge of character. He distrusts Cao Cao before the others do.

- **Cao Cao:** A compact, dark-haired cavalry commander with a mustache and pointed goatee, dressed in black or deep blue armor and cloak. He is observant, politically agile, persuasive, and daring: he admires Liu Bei's volunteers, backs Guan Yu when the lords sneer, and risks killing Dong Zhuo himself. His mistaken massacre and deliberate murder of the old host reveal a ruthless belief in self-preservation that makes him both an ally and a threat.

- **Dong Zhuo:** A massively obese general and court usurper in purple-and-gold robes and an official crown, usually eating or reclining. The brothers save his life during the rebellion, but he scorns them for lacking rank. Five years later he uses his army and the child emperor to seize the state. His power depends on terror, status, appetite, and Lü Bu.

- **Lü Bu:** A very tall, handsome young champion in elaborate gold armor, a red cloak, and long red-and-black plumes. He wields a large crescent halberd and rides the brilliant red horse Red Hare. He is presented as the empire's unmatched individual warrior. He murders the man who raised him, accepts Dong Zhuo as Father in exchange for the horse and gifts, and becomes the physical shield of Dong Zhuo's regime.

- **Yuan Shao:** A lean, proud aristocratic lord with a long narrow beard, pale gold robes and armor, and a formal headpiece. He alone openly defies Dong Zhuo at court, then leads the eighteen-lord coalition. His courage is real, but his obsession with rank makes him belittle Liu Bei and reject the brothers even after Guan Yu wins the coalition's battle.

- **The emperors:** The old emperor dies off-page. His older son is briefly emperor but is declared weak and removed by Dong Zhuo. The younger brother is a nine-year-old boy in yellow imperial robes, installed as a puppet while Dong Zhuo stands beside his throne.

- **Cao Cao's old host:** A poor, white-haired old man who identifies himself as Cao Cao's uncle through a sworn bond with Cao Cao's father. He shelters Cao Cao and goes to buy wine. Cao Cao first murders the host's household by mistake, then kills the returning old man deliberately to hide the crime.

## Causal chain

1. Famine and the Yellow Turban rebellion create the need for volunteers.
2. Liu Bei's desire to serve, Zhang Fei's challenge and resources, and Guan Yu's shared purpose bring the three men together.
3. Their peach-garden oath, sale of their property, and recruitment of farmers create a small volunteer army.
4. Liu Bei's ambush defeats a vastly larger force, building the brothers' reputation and numbers.
5. The brothers meet Cao Cao after his fire attack and later save Dong Zhuo, establishing both future relationships.
6. Dong Zhuo's contempt for their lack of office sends them home unrewarded, while the official generals take the honors.
7. The emperor's death and court disorder give Dong Zhuo the opening to seize the capital and install a child ruler.
8. Yuan Shao's resistance shows that Dong Zhuo needs an unbeatable enforcer; Cao Cao points him toward Lü Bu.
9. Red Hare, armor, and the promise of a richer father induce Lü Bu to murder his adoptive father and join Dong Zhuo.
10. Lü Bu's strength lets Dong Zhuo terrorize the court; that tyranny drives Cao Cao to attempt assassination.
11. Cao Cao's failed attempt makes him a fugitive. His fear causes the household massacre, and his decision to kill the innocent host exposes how far he will go to survive.
12. Cao Cao sells his land and calls the empire to war. The brothers answer because defeating Dong Zhuo matters more to them than their distrust of the caller.
13. Eighteen lords assemble at Hulao Gate, but their status politics marginalize the brothers.
14. Guan Yu's instant victory proves the dismissed volunteers are better than the titled champions, yet Yuan Shao's pride still drives them out.
15. The first champion's death causes Dong Zhuo to release Lü Bu, who crushes every fighter the coalition sends and forces the brothers toward the confrontation promised at page 40.

## What I expect next

The three brothers will enter the fight that the ranked champions cannot win. Guan Yu has just demonstrated that title is a poor measure of ability, but Lü Bu is being built as too formidable for any single fighter, so I expect Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, and Liu Bei to fight him together and make their brotherhood—not merely individual strength—the answer to his superiority. They may drive him back through Hulao Gate without killing him.

Even if the coalition wins this encounter, its internal weakness is already visible. Yuan Shao values rank over merit, while Cao Cao recognizes talent but has shown that he will commit or conceal atrocities to preserve himself. I expect the campaign against Dong Zhuo to continue, but also expect the alliance among the lords to fracture. The brothers' immediate rise in reputation seems likely; their longer-term conflict with Cao Cao feels equally likely, because they share an enemy without sharing a moral code.
