## Retell of the story so far

The Han dynasty has ruled China for four hundred years, but the country is starving and the Yellow Turban rebels are on the march. In Zhuo County, an impoverished sandal seller named Liu Bei repeatedly reads a call for volunteers. He descends from princes but has nothing useful to fight with.

A huge, loud butcher named Zhang Fei notices Liu Bei brooding over the notice and challenges him to act. Zhang Fei has money, land, and an impulsive generosity that Liu Bei lacks. At a wine shop they meet Guan Yu, a formidable red-faced fugitive who killed a rich man for taking poor villagers' land. The rebels have since burned Guan Yu's own village. Liu Bei argues that men should follow a worthy leader rather than an army merely because it belongs to the emperor. Zhang Fei realizes Liu Bei is that leader and proposes that the three become brothers beneath the peach trees behind his house.

They swear to protect the people and serve the empire, and to die on the same day if they can. Liu Bei becomes eldest brother, Guan Yu second, and Zhang Fei youngest. Zhang Fei sells nearly everything he owns to equip them, preserving only his house and peach garden. A smith forges paired swords for Liu Bei, a long spear for Zhang Fei, and the immense Green Dragon blade for Guan Yu. Five hundred farmers join them.

Their first enemy is a Yellow Turban force said to number fifty thousand. Liu Bei sees that the farmers are frightened and makes their weakness useful: Guan Yu and Zhang Fei hide on opposite hills while Liu Bei rides forward with the farmers and draws the rebels into the narrow ground between them. The two hidden brothers charge down from both sides, completing the trap. The victory makes the brothers famous and attracts more men.

They march south toward an even larger rebel encampment in tall, dry grass, intending to attack at dawn. Before they can, the grass and camp are set ablaze at midnight. As the rebels flee the fire, the brothers discover that Cao Cao, commander of the emperor's cavalry, has arrived first and engineered the attack. Cao Cao respects Liu Bei because his soldiers are unpaid villagers defending their own homes. Zhang Fei likes Cao Cao immediately; Guan Yu distrusts how readily he smiles.

Weeks later, the brothers see an imperial force losing a battle and charge in without even knowing the endangered general's name. They rescue him. The general is Dong Zhuo, a richly dressed, corpulent commander. Once he learns that Liu Bei and his brothers have neither office nor rank and are only volunteers, he contemptuously dismisses them. Zhang Fei wants to kill him; Liu Bei restrains him despite trembling with anger.

The rebellion is broken by winter. The great generals receive titles, gold, and land, while the three brothers and their five hundred farmers return to Zhuo County with nothing. Five years pass. The emperor dies, the court descends into murderous disorder, and Dong Zhuo enters the capital with his western army, claiming he will restore order.

Dong Zhuo behaves as a conqueror, riding his horse up the emperor's stairs. He declares the old emperor's elder son weak and foolish and announces that the clever nine-year-old younger son will be emperor instead. Yuan Shao, whose family has served the emperors for four generations, openly tells Dong Zhuo he has no right to choose. They threaten each other with swords, and Yuan Shao leaves the court alive but defeated for the moment.

At a banquet, an old lord repeats the same objection. A towering young warrior in red armor, later identified by Cao Cao as Lü Bu, dominates the room. Cao Cao tells Dong Zhuo that Lü Bu is an almost unbeatable fighter and the adopted son of another man. Dong Zhuo decides he wants him. He offers Lü Bu the extraordinary red horse Red Hare, then tempts him further with gold armor and the promise of command. Lü Bu values the father who raised him, but Dong Zhuo tries to redefine real fatherhood as whoever gives a son everything.

## Cast

- **Liu Bei:** A tall, slender young man with a long-eared profile, narrow mustache, pale gray or white clothes, and a dark headwrap that later becomes a white martial headband and cloak. He begins as a poor sandal seller with princely ancestry. He is observant, restrained, strategically clever, and able to inspire volunteers; the other two call him Big Brother.
- **Zhang Fei:** A massive, broad, hairy man with wild dark hair and beard, powerful bare arms, and initially a bloodstained butcher's apron. In armor he carries an extremely long, snake-bladed spear. He is a prosperous butcher and farmer who finances the brotherhood, its bluntest speaker, its quickest enthusiast, and apparently its most volatile fighter. He is Third Brother.
- **Guan Yu:** An exceptionally tall, red-faced man with a very long black beard, green head covering, and green robes over armor. He carries the huge curved Green Dragon pole blade. A fugitive who killed an abusive rich man, he is grave, principled, and wary of other men's motives. He is Second Brother.
- **Cao Cao:** A trim cavalry commander in dark armor, with swept-back black hair, mustache, and pointed beard. He destroys the large rebel camp by setting the dry grass alight, recognizes the moral force behind Liu Bei's unpaid army, and later serves at court. He is perceptive, socially quick, and politically informed.
- **Dong Zhuo:** An extremely fat general in ornate purple-and-gold clothes and headgear, first encountered commanding imperial troops. He dismisses the brothers because they lack rank, then returns five years later at the head of a western army and takes control of the capital. He is contemptuous, shameless about power, and skilled at buying loyalty.
- **Yuan Shao:** A lean court noble in pale robes, with a narrow mustache and beard. His family has served the dynasty for generations. He openly contests Dong Zhuo's attempt to choose the emperor and leaves rather than submit.
- **Lü Bu:** A striking, very tall young warrior with long black hair, brilliant red-and-gold armor, a red-plumed crown, and a bladed polearm. Cao Cao calls him an unbeatable fighter. He is another man's adopted son, but Dong Zhuo is attempting to purchase his allegiance with Red Hare, armor, gold, and military command.
- **The imperial heirs:** The old emperor's two young sons. Dong Zhuo describes the elder as weak and foolish and the nine-year-old younger brother as clever; he intends to enthrone the younger child.
- **The five hundred farmers:** Poor villagers armed at first with farm tools. They follow Liu Bei without pay because Yellow Turbans have burned their homes. Their trust gives the brotherhood both its army and its claim to moral authority.
- **The Yellow Turbans:** A mass rebel movement identified by yellow headbands and banners. Their advance arises amid famine and imperial decline, but in this portion they function as the immediate military threat burning villages and overwhelming imperial forces.
- **The old lord at the banquet:** An elderly court figure who repeats Yuan Shao's objection to Dong Zhuo's seizure of authority. His identity is not given here, but his function in the scene is clear.

## Causal chain

Famine and collapsing Han authority produce the Yellow Turban uprising. The volunteer notice exposes Liu Bei's desire to act but also his poverty. Zhang Fei's challenge and resources turn that desire into a practical possibility. Meeting Guan Yu supplies the third brother and a shared reason to defend ordinary people. Their oath creates loyalty; Zhang Fei's sacrifice buys weapons; Liu Bei's character attracts five hundred farmers.

Because the farmers cannot survive a direct battle against fifty thousand rebels, Liu Bei uses a feigned retreat and the terrain to draw the enemy between Guan Yu and Zhang Fei. That victory brings reputation and recruits. Their growing army carries them to the larger rebel camp, where Cao Cao's independent fire attack introduces another capable imperial commander. Continuing north, the brothers' instinct to rescue an unknown general saves Dong Zhuo. His contempt for their low status establishes both their lack of reward and his moral character.

Once the rebellion is defeated, the political system rewards rank rather than service, so the brothers go home empty-handed. Five years later the emperor's death creates a power vacuum. Dong Zhuo's army lets him occupy that vacuum and dictate the succession. Yuan Shao's resistance reveals that Dong Zhuo still faces powerful opponents. Cao Cao's identification of Lü Bu gives Dong Zhuo a way to acquire overwhelming personal force, and the gift of Red Hare begins his attempt to detach Lü Bu from his adoptive father.

## What I expect next

I expect Lü Bu to accept Dong Zhuo's definition of loyalty, betray or abandon his adoptive father, and become Dong Zhuo's terrifying champion. With the child emperor under his control and Lü Bu beside him, Dong Zhuo will become the central tyrant. Yuan Shao will seek allies against him; Cao Cao will probably maneuver inside the court before openly opposing him. The story should then return to Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei, whose unrewarded service and earlier encounter with Dong Zhuo place them on a collision course with his regime. Guan Yu's distrust of Cao Cao also feels planted for a later complication even if the two men temporarily fight on the same side.
