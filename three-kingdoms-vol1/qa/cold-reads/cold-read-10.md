# Cold Read — Pages 1–10

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without outside documentation. The three principal characters are distinct, their alliance forms for clear reasons, and the first battle plan is understandable through its execution.

## Retell

China is collapsing under famine and the advance of the Yellow Turbans. In Zhuo County, Liu Bei is a poor sandal seller descended from princes. He wants to fight the rebellion but believes he has nothing useful to offer.

Zhang Fei, a prosperous butcher, notices Liu Bei brooding over the recruitment notice and challenges him to act. He introduces himself, offers Liu Bei a drink, and brings him into conversation. At the tavern they meet Guan Yu, a formidable fugitive who killed a rich man for abusing the poor. Guan Yu says he will fight anyone who harms ordinary people, including the rebels.

At Zhang Fei's farm, Liu Bei argues that the other two could join an imperial army but that no one would follow a sandal seller. Guan Yu answers that he came specifically for the man who cared enough to sigh at the notice, and Zhang Fei reveals that he noticed the same thing. Zhang proposes that they swear brotherhood in his blooming peach garden. The next morning, the three swear to protect the people, serve the empire, and die together.

They establish Liu Bei as eldest brother, Guan Yu as second, and Zhang Fei as third. Each contributes what he has: Liu Bei offers sandals, Guan Yu a horse and cart, and Zhang Fei sells nearly all his property. A smith equips them with Liu Bei's paired swords, Zhang Fei's long spear, and Guan Yu's Green Dragon blade. Five hundred farmers rally to them.

Their first test is an approaching Yellow Turban force said to number fifty thousand. The farmers panic, but Liu Bei refuses a direct fight. He sends Guan Yu and Zhang Fei behind opposite hills while he rides out with the farmers as bait. On the next page, Liu Bei leads the apparent retreat through the pass, the Yellow Turbans pursue, and the two hidden brothers spring the ambush from the heights. Zhang Fei strikes the first enemy shown.

## Cast

- **Liu Bei:** A lean, handsome man with a small mustache and a restrained, thoughtful manner. He first wears a patched pale robe and dark cloth cap, then pale armor and a white headband, carrying two swords. He is the impoverished sandal seller of princely descent, the moral center of the trio, their eldest brother, and their strategist.
- **Zhang Fei:** A huge, broad, muscular man with wild dark hair and beard, expressive eyes, and a stained butcher's apron before he arms himself. He is loud, funny, impulsive, wealthy, and generous. He recruits Liu Bei into action, proposes the oath, finances the group, becomes third brother, and fights with a long, wavy-bladed spear.
- **Guan Yu:** A towering, red-faced man with a very long black beard, green head covering, and green robes over armor. He is grave, controlled, and physically imposing. A fugitive who killed an oppressor, he becomes second brother and carries the massive curved Green Dragon polearm.
- **The Yellow Turbans:** A vast rebel army identified consistently by yellow headbands and banners. They are the immediate threat to the countryside and the brothers' first battlefield enemy.
- **The farmers:** Five hundred plainly dressed local men armed mostly with hoes, pitchforks, and simple spears. They follow the brothers but are understandably frightened by the enemy's size.

## Causal Chain

Famine and Yellow Turban violence create a need for volunteers. Liu Bei's concern draws Zhang Fei's attention. Zhang's challenge and hospitality bring Liu Bei into conversation with Guan Yu. Their shared desire to protect ordinary people leads to the peach-garden oath. Their combined possessions fund weapons and attract five hundred followers. The huge enemy force makes a frontal attack impossible, so Liu Bei uses the farmers as bait and positions his stronger brothers for an ambush. The pursuit into the pass triggers that ambush.

## Backtracking, Rereading, or Guesses

None. The change from civilian dress to armor is substantial, especially for Liu Bei, but the trio's faces, colors, weapons, and positions preserve their identities. I did not misidentify a character.

## Text and Attribution at Reading Pace

All essential English narration and dialogue was readable at normal pace. Speech attribution remained clear from balloon placement, character staging, and voice. The Chinese writing on the posted notice was not readable to me, but the surrounding English narration and recruitment wording supplied everything needed, so it caused no story loss.

## Expected Next

I expect the hilltop ambush to break or scatter the larger Yellow Turban force, proving Liu Bei's tactical ability and the brothers' individual fighting strength. That victory will likely give the three men a reputation, more followers, and entry into the wider war, while also testing whether improvised local fighters can survive sustained campaigning.
