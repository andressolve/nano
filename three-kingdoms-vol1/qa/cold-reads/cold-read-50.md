# Cold read — canonical pages 1–50

## Verdict

**APPROVED**

The story lands without outside documentation. The central relationships, the political situation, the changes in time, and the motives carrying the story into the final page were all understandable on a single normal-paced read.

## Retell of the story so far

The Han empire is collapsing under famine and the Yellow Turban rebellion. In Zhuo County, Liu Bei is an impoverished straw-sandal seller descended from princes. He studies a call for volunteers but despairs that he has no means to fight. Zhang Fei, a huge butcher with money and property, notices him; Guan Yu, a red-faced fugitive who killed a cruel rich man, soon joins them. Liu Bei's seriousness gives the other two a cause worth following. In Zhang Fei's peach garden they swear to protect the people, serve the empire, and die together.

Zhang Fei sells nearly everything he owns, and the three equip themselves and gather five hundred farmers. Faced with a Yellow Turban army a hundred times larger, Liu Bei uses himself and the farmers as bait while his brothers hide in the hills. Their ambush routs the rebels. The three then approach an even larger rebel camp, only to discover that Cao Cao, an imperial cavalry commander, has already destroyed it by setting the dry grass alight. Cao Cao is impressed that Liu Bei's men follow him without pay.

The brothers later save the imperial general Dong Zhuo in battle. Dong Zhuo contemptuously dismisses them as common volunteers and gives them only a bag of rice. Liu Bei stops Zhang Fei from killing him because Dong still wears the emperor's armor and is therefore not yet their enemy. The rebellion is broken, but the brothers receive no office or recognition and return home. Five years pass.

When the old emperor dies, powerful men fight over the succession. Dong Zhuo marches into the capital, deposes the late emperor's weak son, and installs the nine-year-old younger brother as a puppet emperor. Yuan Shao openly defies him and leaves. Dong Zhuo then wins over Lü Bu, the greatest warrior in the empire, by giving him the peerless horse Red Hare and promising wealth and rank. Lü Bu murders the adoptive father who raised him and takes Dong Zhuo as his new father.

With the capital, child emperor, and Lü Bu under his control, Dong Zhuo becomes a tyrant. Cao Cao pretends loyalty while carrying a jeweled dagger meant to kill him. Dong sees Cao raise the dagger in a mirror, but Cao instantly presents it as a gift and asks to test a fast horse. He escapes the capital before the deception is exposed. On the road, an old family friend hides him. Cao mistakes preparations to slaughter a pig for a plot against him and kills the household; when the old man returns, Cao deliberately kills him too so that he cannot report the crime. Cao declares that he would rather betray the world than let the world betray him.

Cao raises an army and calls every loyal man to march against Dong Zhuo. The letter reaches Liu Bei, who has spent the five unrewarded years selling sandals and waiting for a summons. The three brothers bring their original five hundred men to a coalition of eighteen lords at Hulao Gate. Yuan Shao leads the coalition and looks down on Liu Bei's poverty and Guan Yu's lack of rank, while Cao Cao repeatedly recognizes their worth.

After Dong Zhuo's champion kills several coalition fighters, Guan Yu volunteers. Cao gives him hot wine, but Guan Yu leaves it on the table and returns with the champion's helmet before it cools. Yuan Shao still ejects the three low-born brothers from the command tent. Cao visits them afterward and asks them to stand with him when Lü Bu comes; Liu Bei says they came to fight Dong Zhuo and will fight beside anyone doing so, while Guan Yu remains wary of Cao's character.

Lü Bu rides out on Red Hare and defeats the coalition's champions almost instantly. Zhang Fei intervenes and fights him to a standstill, taunting him for betraying his fathers. Guan Yu joins Zhang Fei, yet Lü Bu still holds both brothers off. Yuan Shao orders Liu Bei to remain safely on the hill because he sees him as a sandal seller under his command. Liu Bei rejects that hierarchy: the brothers swore to die together, so he rides down with both swords drawn. Fighting three against one for the first time in his life, Lü Bu loses track of all his opponents and retreats through Hulao Gate under covering arrows.

From the wall, Lü Bu asks who the brothers are, and Liu Bei names all three. When Lü Bu tells them to face him one at a time next time, Guan Yu simply refuses. Cao reveals that he watched the fight rather than joining it, partly to learn what the brothers would do. He tells Liu Bei that only the two of them are heroes in the camp. Liu Bei modestly denies it, but that judgment matters to him.

That night Liu Bei looks at the gate and thinks about the powerful men on both sides: the tyrant who once scorned him, the invincible warrior who sold his father for a horse, the child emperor trapped behind them, the eighteen lords who will do nothing, and Cao Cao, who might do anything. He is ashamed that he wants Cao's praise to be true. At dawn the brothers return to challenge Lü Bu again, with Cao watching from the ridge. Liu Bei does not turn around.

## Cast

- **Liu Bei:** Tall and lean, with a composed face, long ears, a thin mustache, and dark hair first wrapped in black and later bound with a white headband. He wears pale gray or white robes and armor and fights with paired straight swords. He begins as a poor sandal seller with imperial ancestry and becomes the eldest brother and moral center of the trio. He is patient, tactically clever, restrained, and hungry for a worthy role even when he denies that hunger.

- **Guan Yu:** Exceptionally tall and broad, with a deep red face, very long black beard, green hood and robes, and a massive crescent-bladed polearm called the Green Dragon. Formerly a fugitive for killing a rich man who abused the poor, he is the second brother. He is grave, principled, observant, and devastatingly capable; he is the quickest of the brothers to distrust Cao Cao.

- **Zhang Fei:** Huge and powerfully built, with wild dark hair, a thick curly beard, expressive eyes, battered dark armor, and a long serpent-bladed spear. A butcher and landowner before the oath, he finances the brothers' beginning and becomes the youngest brother. He is loud, funny, impulsive, fiercely loyal, and fearless.

- **Cao Cao:** A sharp-featured commander with tied black hair, narrow mustache and beard, dark blue-black armor, and a black cloak. He first appears as an imperial cavalry commander, then becomes the organizer of the anti-Dong coalition. He is perceptive, charismatic, tactically daring, and willing to flatter or deceive, but also frighteningly ruthless: panic causes one massacre, calculation causes another murder. He recognizes Liu Bei as a rival kind of hero.

- **Dong Zhuo:** An enormously fat general in ornate purple-and-gold robes and a court crown, usually eating or reclining. He first dismisses the brothers after they save him, then seizes the capital and the child emperor. He is the principal tyrant: contemptuous, gluttonous, politically cunning, and protected by Lü Bu.

- **Lü Bu:** A tall, handsome young warrior in brilliant red-and-gold armor, a red cape, and a crown with two towering pheasant plumes. He carries an elaborate halberd and rides the red horse Red Hare. He is the empire's unmatched fighter and Dong Zhuo's adopted son and champion, having murdered his previous adoptive father for the horse and promised rewards. His fighting ability is real enough that all three brothers are needed to drive him back.

- **Yuan Shao:** A stately nobleman in cream-and-gold robes and armor, with a long narrow beard and formal headpiece. He defies Dong Zhuo over the succession and is chosen to command the eighteen-lord coalition. He is brave enough to oppose the tyrant but obsessed with pedigree, rank, and credit, which keeps him from recognizing the brothers.

- **The child emperor:** A nine-year-old boy in yellow imperial robes, installed on the throne by Dong Zhuo and visibly surrounded by soldiers and hostile adults. He is the political captive everyone claims to serve.

- **Lü Bu's former adoptive father:** An older court lord who protests Dong Zhuo's seizure of power. He raised Lü Bu, but Lü Bu murders him and delivers his seal to Dong Zhuo.

- **Cao Cao's old family friend:** A poor, white-haired villager who welcomes Cao as family and risks sheltering him. He goes to buy wine while his household prepares a pig, returns unaware of Cao's mistake, and is killed by Cao to protect Cao's escape.

- **The Yellow Turbans, the eighteen lords, and the five hundred farmers:** The rebellion creates the first crisis; the high-born coalition supplies military power without unity; the farmers repeatedly choose Liu Bei despite having neither pay nor status.

## Causal chain

The empire's failure and the Yellow Turban advance create the call for volunteers. Liu Bei's desire to serve attracts Zhang Fei and Guan Yu; Zhang Fei's resources let the three arm five hundred followers; Liu Bei's tactics turn that tiny force into a reputation. Their reputation brings them into contact with Cao Cao and Dong Zhuo, but Dong Zhuo's class contempt denies them advancement and sends them home unrewarded.

The old emperor's death creates a succession struggle. Dong Zhuo uses his army to seize the court, installs a child ruler, and buys Lü Bu's loyalty with Red Hare. Lü Bu's murder of his former father removes a defender of the old order and gives Dong an unbeatable enforcer. That combination lets Dong terrorize the capital.

Cao's failed assassination forces him to flee. His escape, including the murders on the road, establishes both his resolve against Dong and the ruthlessness beneath his charm. Safe at home, he can raise an army and summon the lords. His letter finally gives Liu Bei and his brothers the cause they have awaited for five years.

At Hulao Gate, Yuan Shao's fixation on rank repeatedly wastes the brothers' ability, while Cao's eye for talent keeps making room for them. Guan Yu's victory proves the coalition has misjudged them. Lü Bu's arrival then forces the issue: Zhang Fei's loyalty draws Guan Yu into the fight, Yuan's insult pushes Liu Bei to choose his oath over obedience, and the three brothers together accomplish what the eighteen lords could not. Their success earns Cao's recognition, which awakens Liu Bei's private ambition and leads directly to the dawn return to the gate.

## Backtracking, rereading, guesses, and misidentifications

I did not need to backtrack or reread any page, and I did not misidentify a recurring character.

Three transitions briefly withheld information but resolved naturally in forward reading:

- **Pages 19–21:** The red warrior at the banquet is identified as Lü Bu on page 19, and his relation to the protesting old lord becomes fully explicit through the horse bargain and the delivered seal. I never had to guess which recurring character he was.
- **Pages 27–28:** Page 27 initially makes Cao's slaughter look like the result of a real household threat; the tied pig reveals the mistake, and the old man's return on page 28 makes clear that the host himself had been away buying wine. The intended reversal was immediate and legible.
- **Pages 35–36:** Page 35 leaves Guan Yu's off-page fight suspended on the steaming wine cup. Page 36 supplies the helmet and resolves the action cleanly; the suspense did not require reconstruction.

The small final panel on **page 43** places Liu Bei at a distance while his brothers continue fighting. **Page 44** immediately establishes that he is on Yuan Shao's hill and turns his ride into a clear choice. This was a forward reveal, not a continuity problem.

## What I expect next

The immediate expectation is a second confrontation at Hulao Gate, with Lü Bu now taking the brothers seriously and Dong Zhuo forced to respond to them as more than anonymous commoners. I also expect the coalition's internal weakness to become as dangerous as Dong's army: Yuan Shao will want credit and obedience, while Cao Cao will continue cultivating Liu Bei for motives that are both admiring and competitive.

Longer term, Liu Bei's private response to being called a hero feels like the true hinge. He still defines himself through service, brotherhood, and rescue of the emperor, but he has now seen that official rank and noble coalitions do not produce leadership. I expect him to begin accepting that he may have to become a power in his own right. Cao Cao appears likely to be both ally and eventual rival, while Guan Yu's suspicion of Cao and Zhang Fei's hatred of betrayal are positioned to test Liu Bei's attraction to Cao's recognition.
