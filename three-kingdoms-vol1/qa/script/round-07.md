## Is it alive? (condensed)
- **I (pp 1–9): Yes.** Every man arrives wanting something. **The split p7 was worth a page:** narration turned into drama gave Zhang Fei a comic grievance ("Third! It sounds terrible.") and Liu Bei shame and longing in one beat ("I have twenty pairs of sandals."); the sale is now a choice made in front of the people it is for. Heat: the man just named Big Brother admits he has nothing to lead with, and the youngest answers by giving up everything. "Brothers need weapons, and soldiers, and money" is closest to logistics, and passes — every inventory line is a different voice and mood. The split also improves p9: "Third Brother" is now a name the reader saw given.
- **II (pp 10–16): Yes.** p15 is the heat peak ("Then why are your hands shaking, Big Brother?").
- **III (pp 17–22): Yes.** The Red Hare stable scene is the best-written seduction in the book; the seal reports a death without showing it.
- **IV (pp 23–30): The strongest movement; I would read it twice.** The farmhouse is frightening and fair; "Uncle, I'm sorry." / "Sorry? Sorry for what?" is the most chilling short exchange; p30 is protagonist appetite at its peak.
- **V (pp 31–38): Yes.** The p35 spectacle is correctly withheld, not inverted; p37 has real subtext; Guan Yu's "went on bowing" belongs only to him.
- **VI (pp 39–46): Yes.** The insult is explained in the same exchange; escalation clean; the oath causes the action on p44; the flinch on p46 is paid off on p49.
- **VII (pp 47–50): Yes.** "No." delivers the thesis as a joke and a refusal; p48 is the most subtle page in the book; p50's "He did not turn around" is a real moral fork.
Overall: alive — funny, frightening, morally interesting; I would turn every page.
