## Page intent

## PAGE 25

Illustrated prose. Cao Cao gallops out through the great city gate in a cloud of dust, crouched low over a fast horse, the guards turning too late; that escape is the dominant image and owns the page. Below it, smaller, Dong Zhuo sits up on his couch frowning at the jeweled dagger, Lü Bu beside him. The prose carries the turn: the ride once around the courtyard that never stopped, the slow question of why a man would kneel with the blade already out of its sheath, Lü Bu's flat answer, "he was going to kill you, Father," and by nightfall Cao Cao's face painted on every gate with a price on his head. The prose sits over sky and dust, never over the rider. The reader turns because the whole empire is now hunting Cao Cao.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 25 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every prose paragraph reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no extra text; a paragraph that does not read is blocking, text that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. No balloons appear.

### C2 — The escape owns the page
Cao Cao bursting out through the city gate in dust, guards too late, must be the image the eye finds first. Illustrated-prose mode: one prose field over sky and dust; prose covering the rider, his face, or the horse is blocking only if it hides that he is escaping.

### C3 — The bedchamber beat
Dong Zhuo, sitting up, must be holding and frowning at the dagger, with Lü Bu beside him, so the prose's question and answer have faces. If the dagger is absent, the turn loses its object.

### C4 — Identity
Cao Cao short and goateed in indigo; Dong Zhuo obese in purple and gold; Lü Bu beardless and plumed in vermilion. Cao Cao's horse is not blood-red; a red horse here reads as Lü Bu's and is blocking.
