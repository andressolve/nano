### C1 — Transcription
The three prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no extra text anywhere; a paragraph that does not read, is missing, or is doubled is blocking; a paragraph that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant of the same word are nonblocking; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — Illustrated prose and one image
The page reads as illustrated prose: one great image of the camp-filled valley and the fortress gate filling the pass, with the prose over sky. Prose laid over the gate, the banners, or the brothers, or the page breaking into panels, is blocking.

### C3 — Scale tells the story
The eye finds the vast camp, the great gold banner over the largest tent, and the grey gate with purple banners, and then, small at the edge, three riders and one small tent. If the brothers cannot be found at all, or are drawn as large as the lords' camp, the turn fails. The exact number of banners or tents is nonblocking.

### C4 — The three brothers
The three small riders read as three different men by silhouette and colour: white, green with a red face and long beard, black and broad. Liu Bei's swords, if visible, are sheathed on his back. Faces need not be detailed at this scale.

### C5 — No one else
No figure on the gate or in the camp is singled out in close-up; no blood or bodies; no lettering on banners.
