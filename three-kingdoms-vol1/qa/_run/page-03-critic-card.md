## Page intent

## PAGE 3

Dramatic. Liu Bei sighs at the notice and a stranger looming behind him laughs at it: Zhang Fei, a butcher built like a barrel, asks why a grown man sighs at a wall instead of doing something. The dominant turn is that challenge. Liu Bei, embarrassed, admits he wants to fight but has nothing to fight with except his blood and a stall of sandals. Zhang Fei announces himself, his shop, his farm and his money, throws an arm round him and hauls him off to drink, calling him Prince of Sandals; Liu Bei, quietly, gives his real name. The page belongs to Zhang Fei's energy colliding with Liu Bei's reserve. The reader turns because a stranger with money has just grabbed him and they are going to talk about war. The tavern sign and market crowd stay in the background.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the market of Zhuo County.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** LIU BEI, left, in front of the notice, his
shoulders dropping in a long sigh. Behind him, right, looming over him, ZHANG
FEI: short and enormously broad, a leather butcher's apron, sleeves rolled
over arms like hams, a square head, round bulging eyes, black whiskers
bristling all round his jaw. Arms folded. Grinning.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "A grown man, sighing at a wall! If you want to fight the rebels, why don't you go and do something about it?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Liu Bei turned to face him, embarrassed, his hands
folded into his sleeves.

> **LIU BEI:** "I want to. I have the blood of princes and a stall full of sandals. What would I fight them with?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Zhang Fei thumping his own barrel chest, then throwing
one huge arm round Liu Bei's shoulders and steering him off toward a tavern
sign.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "I'm Zhang Fei. I have a butcher's shop, a farm, and more money than sense. Come and drink with me, Prince of Sandals!"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "My name is Liu Bei."

**Why you turn the page:** a stranger with money just grabbed him, and they're going to talk about war.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 3 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
The four lines must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. The notice may show its own words as object text. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, a regional spelling variant. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The challenge does not land
The first and largest image must show Liu Bei sighing at the notice with a huge grinning stranger looming behind him; the page then moves to his embarrassed answer and to being hauled off. If the challenge does not own the page, it fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the exchange
Zhang Fei owns the opening dare and the self-introduction ending "Prince of Sandals!"; Liu Bei owns the answer about sandals and "My name is Liu Bei." Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his.

### C4 — The two men collide
Liu Bei tall, pale, smooth-cheeked, long-eared, in hemp; Zhang Fei a head shorter, twice as broad, dark, with round bulging eyes and bristling cheek-whiskers, in a butcher's apron. Any confusion of who speaks or who grabs whom is blocking.

### C5 — Focal generation integrity
A duplicated named figure, a third arm on the steering embrace, or a garbled face on either speaker is blocking. Crowd faces and background hands are not.
