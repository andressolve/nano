## Page intent

## PAGE 4

Dramatic. In a low lamplit tavern a giant ducks through the door and bangs the table, calling for wine because he means to enlist before the day ends. The dominant turn is Guan Yu: red-faced, beard to his belt, on the run five years, enlisting. Zhang Fei waves him over; Guan Yu gives his name and his reason: a rich man in his village beat the poor, so he killed him. Liu Bei asks the hard question, whether he will fight for the emperor whose soldiers are hunting him, and Guan Yu answers that he will fight anyone who burns villages like his. Guan Yu owns the page; Liu Bei's question is its edge. The reader turns because three men with nothing in common are at one table and none is leaving. The room and the other drinkers stay subordinate.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the tavern.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** A low-beamed tavern, benches, a wine jar,
lamplight. In the doorway, ducking under the lintel, GUAN YU: a head taller
than anyone in the room, a deep red face, long narrow eyes tilted up at the
corners, a glossy black beard falling in one sheet to his belt, a green
headscarf. He has slammed one fist on the nearest table. Liu Bei and Zhang
Fei at a table in the foreground, turning to look.

> **GUAN YU:** "Wine, and quickly! I'm going to enlist before the day is over, and I won't do it thirsty."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Zhang Fei, left, delighted, waving him over. Guan Yu,
right, sitting down heavily at their table.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Now that is a soldier! Hey, Red Face! Sit with us. We're enlisting too."
>
> **GUAN YU:** "My name is Guan Yu. In my village a rich man beat the poor and took their land."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The three at one table. Guan Yu, right, stroking his
beard downward with one hand. Liu Bei, left, leaning in.

> **GUAN YU:** "I killed him. I've been running for five years."
>
> **LIU BEI:** "And now you'll fight for the emperor whose soldiers have been chasing you?"
>
> **GUAN YU:** "The rebels burn villages like mine. I'll fight anyone who does that."

**Why you turn the page:** three men who have nothing in common are at one table, and none of them is leaving.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 4 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
The six lines must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, a regional spelling variant. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Guan Yu's entrance does not land
The first and largest image must show a towering red-faced man in the doorway, fist on a table, the two others turning to look. If the entrance is not what the eye finds first, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the exchanges
Guan Yu owns the call for wine, his name and village, "I killed him," and the final answer about burned villages; Zhang Fei owns "Hey, Red Face!"; Liu Bei owns the question about the emperor's soldiers. The question must read before its answer and each line must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail.

### C4 — Three brothers collide
Guan Yu: tallest, deep red face, upturned narrow eyes, black beard in one sheet to the belt, green headscarf. Liu Bei: pale, broad rounded face, long-eared, moustache only. Zhang Fei: broad, round-eyed, bristling whiskers, apron. A red face or long beard on anyone else is blocking.

### C5 — Focal generation integrity
A duplicated named figure or gross error on a speaker's face or the beard-stroking hand is blocking. Background drinkers and furniture are not.
