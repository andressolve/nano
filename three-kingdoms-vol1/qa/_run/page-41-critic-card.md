## Page intent

## PAGE 41

Dramatic. Lü Bu cuts through Yuan Shao's guards; Yuan Shao's horse stumbles and his tall black hat tumbles off. In the large panel Lü Bu stands over the fallen leader with the halberd raised high, and from the right, on a black horse, spear levelled and mouth wide open, Zhang Fei charges in roaring that he is the butcher come to carve the slave of three fathers. The dominant turn is that shout. Then the two face each other: Lü Bu cold, asking what he was called; Zhang Fei grinning, counting the three fathers out loud, the one who made him, the old man he killed, the fat pig who bought him with a horse, and daring him to fight. Yuan Shao scrambles away, subordinate. The reader turns because Zhang Fei has just insulted the greatest warrior alive to his face.

### C2 — The shout lands
The panel of Zhang Fei charging in, mouth open, over Lü Bu and the fallen Yuan Shao owns the page. If the eye settles first on the bottom exchange or the top strip, blocking.

### C3 — Attribution of the exchange
"STOP…" is Zhang Fei's; "What did you call me?" is Lü Bu's and reads before Zhang Fei's answer; both "One father…" and "And a fat pig…" hang from Zhang Fei's mouth, the second after the first. Any of these readable as the other man's is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Lü Bu clean-shaven with plumes on a red horse; Zhang Fei short, broad, bristling-whiskered, in black on a black horse; Yuan Shao loses his tall hat. A swapped trait between the two fighters is blocking.

### C5 — No death drawn
