## Page intent

## PAGE 2

Illustrated prose. In a crowded, dusty market, Liu Bei stands behind his straw-sandal stall and does not sell. He is looking at a recruiting notice pasted on the wall beside him, and the prose tells the reader who he is: twenty-eight, quiet, long-armed, long-eared, a prince's descendant three hundred years down who now bows to every customer. The dominant turn is the sandal-seller with a prince's blood reading the notice eleven times. The large image is the man and the notice, the shoppers passing without a glance; the smaller image is his hands gone still on a half-woven sandal. What changes is that we see him wanting something badly and not moving. The reader turns to find out whether he will. The crowd and the stall goods stay subordinate; the notice is the one thing on the wall that matters.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 2 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the market of Zhuo County.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A crowded dusty market street under a grey tile
gate. In the shade of a wall, a small stall hung with straw sandals. Behind
it, LIU BEI: tall and slim, a faded hemp robe patched at the elbows, a plain
cloth cap, long earlobes, a thin moustache. He is not selling. He is standing
very still, looking at a pale paper notice pasted on the wall beside his
stall, with a red seal at its foot. Shoppers pass behind him without looking.

> **NOTICE** *(the paper on the wall, large, plain)*
> "VOLUNTEERS WANTED
> to fight the Yellow Turbans"

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Close on his long hands, a half-woven sandal in them,
gone still.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the top of the page, over the pale sky above the gate)*
> "In Zhuo County, far in the north, a man named Liu Bei sold straw sandals in the market. He was twenty-eight, tall and quiet, with long arms and ears so long that strangers stared.
>
> Three hundred years ago his family had been princes, the sons of emperors. Now Liu Bei wove sandals, and sold them, and bowed to everyone who bought a pair.
>
> On the morning the notice went up, he read it eleven times."

**Why you turn the page:** a prince's grandson is standing in a market wanting something badly, and he hasn't moved.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two notice lines and the three prose paragraphs must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. The notice lines are object text on the paper on the wall. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The wanting does not land
The page must read as illustrated prose: a man behind a sandal stall frozen before the notice, the passing crowd ignoring him, then his hands gone still on a half-woven sandal. If he reads as selling or chatting, or the notice cannot be found, the turn fails.

### C3 — Liu Bei is not Liu Bei
A tall, slim, pale man with a broad, compact oval face and short rounded chin, very long earlobes, a thin moustache only and a clean-shaven chin, in a patched hemp robe. A long or pointed beard, armor, swords, or white cloak is blocking.

### C4 — Focal hands fail
The close panel shows exactly two hands holding one half-woven sandal; a third hand or a fused or missing hand is blocking. Background hands are nonblocking.
