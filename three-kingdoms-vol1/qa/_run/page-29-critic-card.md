## Page intent

## PAGE 29

Illustrated prose. Cao Cao reaches home, raises an army, and writes to every lord in the empire calling on the loyal to march against Dong Zhuo. The dominant image owns the page: a camp under black-and-indigo banners, riders galloping out of it in every direction with letters in their coats, and Cao Cao on a rise watching them go. Below, smaller, the dusty market of Zhuo County, years later and unchanged, where Liu Bei still sits at his sandal stall under the same wall and takes a sealed letter, one of a bundle, from a dust-covered rider. The prose carries the turn and its irony: nobody there knows what Cao Cao did on the road. The prose sits over the sky above the camp. The reader turns because the letter has reached Liu Bei, and he has waited years for it.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 29 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every prose paragraph reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no extra text; a paragraph that does not read is blocking, text that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. No balloons appear and no letter carries readable words.

### C2 — The letters go out
The camp under black-and-indigo banners with riders galloping out in every direction, and Cao Cao watching from a rise, must be the image the eye finds first. Illustrated-prose mode, prose over sky.

### C3 — The letter reaches Liu Bei
Liu Bei at his sandal stall must be plainly taking a sealed letter from a dusty rider who carries more of them. If the reader cannot see the letter arrive, the page's turn fails.

### C4 — Liu Bei and Cao Cao separated
Liu Bei tall and slim, broad rounded face, sad almond eyes, long earlobes, moustache only, in a hemp robe, his two swords sheathed on his back; Cao Cao short, angular, goateed, in indigo armor. If the two could be taken for one man, blocking.
