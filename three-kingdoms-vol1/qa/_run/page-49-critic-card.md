## Page intent

## PAGE 49

An illustrated-prose page at the edge of the camp, at night. Liu Bei stands alone in moonlight with his back to us, looking at the dark mass of Hulao Gate filling the pass, while behind him his brothers sleep by the embers. The prose carries his sleepless thinking: who is behind the gate, who is in front of it, the moment he flinched and let Lü Bu through, the peach garden, and what Cao Cao said. The dominant turn is the last sentence: he is ashamed of how much he wants it to be true. The single image owns the page; the sleeping brothers stay small and quiet, a reminder of the oath behind him. The reader turns because Liu Bei wants the empire, and the gate is still shut.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 49 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the edge of the camp, at night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Moonlight. Liu Bei standing alone at the edge
of the camp, his back to us, looking at the dark shape of Hulao Gate filling
the pass. Behind him, by the embers of a fire, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei asleep,
Zhang Fei with his mouth open.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the top, over the night sky)*
> "Liu Bei could not sleep. He walked out past his sleeping brothers and looked at Hulao Gate in the moonlight.
>
> Behind it were the fat man who had turned his back on him, the warrior who had sold his father for a horse, and a boy of nine on a throne. In front of it were eighteen lords who would not fight, and one who poured wine and might be anything.
>
> He thought about the moment the halberd came at his face, and how he had flinched, and how Lü Bu had gone through the gap he left.
>
> He thought about the peach garden. He thought about what Cao Cao had said, and he was ashamed of how much he wanted it to be true."

**Why you turn the page:** Liu Bei wants the empire, and the gate is still shut.

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 49 — card

### C1 — Prose transcription
All four paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, over plain night sky and never over Liu Bei, the sleepers, or the gate, with no other text on the page. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant of the same word are nonblocking; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The image does not carry the solitude
The eye must find a single figure, Liu Bei, alone with his back to us, facing the dark gate in moonlight, with his brothers small and asleep behind him. If the sleepers or the camp take the page, or Liu Bei faces us, the turn has not landed. Judge by what the eye finds, never by area.

### C3 — Identity from behind
Liu Bei must read as Liu Bei: tall and slim, white cloak, two sword hilts crossed above his shoulders. The sleepers must read as Guan Yu (massive, green, long black beard) and Zhang Fei (broad, black, mouth open). A third sleeper, a missing brother, or an unrecognisable Liu Bei is blocking.

### C4 — Continuity and integrity
The gate is shut and dark; the swords are sheathed. Any of the people named in the prose drawn on the page, a balloon or caption, or duplicated figures is blocking. Ember glow, stars, and tent detail are nonblocking.
