## Page intent

## PAGE 23

Dramatic. Cao Cao comes late to Dong Zhuo's bedchamber, the imperial bedchamber the tyrant has taken, with an excuse about his slow old horse. Dong Zhuo, too fat to sit up for long, sends Lü Bu down to the stable to choose him a fast one, and Lü Bu goes. Then the tyrant rolls his great body over to face the wall and tells Cao Cao to wake him when the horse comes. The page's one dominant turn: Lü Bu sent away, the tyrant faces the wall, and Cao Cao's hand is on the jeweled hilt in his sleeve. The final image owns the page: the turned back, the man standing behind it, alone, and in the corner a round bronze mirror facing the couch that nobody is looking at yet. The excuse and the errand stay brisk and subordinate. The reader turns because the blade is one movement away.

**Location:** Dong Zhuo's bedchamber.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Dong Zhuo lying on a wide couch, too fat to sit up for
long. Lü Bu standing beside him. In the doorway, left, Cao Cao bowing.

> **DONG ZHUO:** "You're late, Cao Cao."
>
> **CAO CAO:** "Forgive me, my lord. My horse is old and slow."

> **DONG ZHUO:** "Lü Bu, go down to my stable and choose a fast horse for Cao Cao."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** Dong Zhuo has rolled his great body over to
face the wall, his back to the room. Cao Cao stands behind him, alone, one
hand on the jeweled hilt inside his sleeve. In the corner, a round bronze
mirror on a stand faces the couch.

> **DONG ZHUO:** "Wake me when the horse comes."

**Why you turn the page:** the tyrant has turned his back, and Cao Cao's hand is on the dagger.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 23 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every scripted string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement or by naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turned back owns the page
The final image must land by eye: the tyrant turned to the wall, Cao Cao alone behind him, his hand at the hilt in his sleeve. If the excuse or the errand dominates, or Cao Cao's hand is not plainly at his sleeve, the page loses its reason to turn. Dramatic mode.

### C3 — Attribution of the opening exchange
"You're late" is Dong Zhuo's and the excuse about the slow horse is Cao Cao's, the reply reading after the line that prompts it; the errand and "Wake me" hang from Dong Zhuo's mouth. Lü Bu and Cao Cao, where silent, carry no balloon.

### C4 — Identity
Dong Zhuo obese and old in purple and gold; Cao Cao short and sharp-faced with a pointed goatee in indigo; Lü Bu tall, beardless, plumed, in vermilion. Lü Bu must visibly leave or be leaving before the final image.

### C5 — Object state
The dagger stays hidden: a jeweled hilt may show, no bare blade. A bronze mirror on a stand is present in the room; its exact placement is nonblocking.
