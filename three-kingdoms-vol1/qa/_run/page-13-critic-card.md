## Page intent

## PAGE 13

Dramatic. Grey dawn over black stubble. Cao Cao rides up under his black-and-indigo banners: short, quick, amused, the man who lit the fire. He names himself, then asks who pays this ragged army, and Liu Bei, bowing slightly, answers that nobody does; the rebels burned their villages, so they came. Cao Cao's answer is the dominant turn: they are the only men in the war who came because they wanted to, and he will remember Liu Bei. Then he is gone with a smile over his shoulder, and the brothers split on him, Zhang Fei delighted, Guan Yu wary. Cao Cao owns the page; Liu Bei is the man he is measuring. The reader turns because the cleverest man on the battlefield has noticed them, and the brothers cannot agree what that means. The farmers and riders stay background.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the edge of the burnt plain, at dawn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Grey dawn, smoke drifting over black stubble.
From the right, riding up under black-and-indigo banners: CAO CAO, short and
quick in dark indigo armor, a narrow face, glittering narrow eyes, a neat
moustache and pointed little beard. He reins in in front of the brothers,
left, amused.

> **CAO CAO:** "You're late. I set the grass on fire at midnight, and the wind did the rest."
>
> **CAO CAO:** "I'm Cao Cao. I command the emperor's cavalry."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Cao Cao looking past them at the farmers with their
spears and hoes. Liu Bei, left, bowing slightly.

> **CAO CAO:** "Whose army is this? Who pays these men?"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "Nobody pays them. The rebels burned their villages, so they came. I am Liu Bei."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Cao Cao turning his horse to go, one eyebrow up, a quick
smile back over his shoulder. Behind Liu Bei, Zhang Fei grinning and Guan
Yu stroking his beard, watching Cao Cao go.

> **CAO CAO:** "Then they're the only men in this war who came because they wanted to. I'll remember you, Liu Bei."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "I like him!"
>
> **GUAN YU:** "He smiles too quickly."

**Why you turn the page:** the cleverest man on the battlefield noticed them, and the brothers can't agree what that means.

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the seven strings is missing, altered, duplicated, reordered, or not comfortably transcribable from the 600 × 900 proof, or extra text appears. A string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Cao Cao's turn does not land
Cao Cao must read as arriving amused, asking who pays these men, hearing nobody does, and judging that they came because they wanted to. If that judgment does not read as the page's decisive moment, it fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the exchanges
Cao Cao owns his four lines and Liu Bei owns "Nobody pays them…", each hanging from its own speaker's mouth by tail, the answer clearly after the question. Zhang Fei owns "I like him!" and Guan Yu owns "He smiles too quickly."; a swap reverses the brothers' split and is blocking.

### C4 — Liu Bei and Cao Cao collide
Cao Cao must be short, narrow-faced, with a pointed goatee, in indigo; Liu Bei tall, broad rounded face, sad almond eyes, long earlobes, moustache only, in white. Mistaking one for the other is blocking. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei must stay recognisable.

### C5 — Continuity
Liu Bei's swords stay sheathed. No bodies on the stubble.
