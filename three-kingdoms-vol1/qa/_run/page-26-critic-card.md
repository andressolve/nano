## Page intent

## PAGE 26

Dramatic. At dusk a dusty, hollow-eyed Cao Cao reaches a modest farm gate, and a thin little old man throws it open with his arms flung wide: his father's sworn brother from their young days, delighted to see "little Cao Cao." Cao Cao warns him there is a price on his head; the old man will not hear of danger in his house. The page's one dominant turn: the old man takes him in and rides off on a grey donkey with an empty wine jar to fetch the good wine from town, promising to be back by morning, telling him to rest while the family looks after him. The final image owns the page: the old man beaming as he goes, and Cao Cao alone in the courtyard watching him, one hand on his sword. The welcome is warm and talkative; the hand on the sword is the quiet weight. The reader turns because a kind man has left a hunted man alone in his house.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 26 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a farmhouse at dusk.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** Dusk. A modest farm gate in yellow fields. Cao Cao,
dusty and hollow-eyed, on a tired horse. The gate opening: UNCLE, a thin
little old man, nearly bald with a white topknot and a sparse white beard,
his arms flung wide.

> **UNCLE:** "Who's there? Cao Cao? Little Cao Cao, at my gate! Your father and I swore to be brothers when we were young men!"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Cao Cao, low, glancing back down the road.

> **CAO CAO:** "Uncle, there is a price on my head. If they find me here—"
>
> **UNCLE:** "Then they won't find you. Not in this house! Come in, come in."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 40%.** The courtyard. The old man climbing onto a
grey donkey with an empty wine jar under his arm, beaming. Cao Cao alone in
the courtyard, watching him go, one hand on his sword.

> **UNCLE:** "The wine in this house isn't good enough for my brother's son."
>
> **UNCLE:** "I'll ride to the town for the good wine, and be back by morning. Rest! My family will look after you."

**Why you turn the page:** a kind old man has left a hunted man alone in his house.

### C1 — Transcription
Every scripted string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement or by naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The old man leaves him alone
The final image must land by eye: the old man going off on a grey donkey with a wine jar, beaming, and Cao Cao alone in the courtyard watching, a hand on his sword. If the departure or Cao Cao's watching is unclear, the page's turn is lost. Dramatic mode.

### C3 — Attribution
The welcome, "Then they won't find you…", and both wine balloons are the old man's; "Uncle, there is a price on my head…" is Cao Cao's and reads before the reassurance. The two wine balloons both hang from the old man's mouth, not Cao Cao's.

### C4 — Identity
The old man is small, bent, nearly bald with a white topknot and sparse white beard, in brown cotton, never black-haired or in silk; Cao Cao is short, angular, goateed, dusty. The family does not appear.

### C5 — Integrity
Speaking faces have visible mouths; the old man's open-armed welcome reads as welcome. Background finish is nonblocking.
