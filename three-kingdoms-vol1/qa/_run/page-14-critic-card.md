## Page intent

## PAGE 14

Dramatic. Weeks later, on the road north, the brothers look down from a rise on a hillside of trampled imperial banners, where a ring of yellow headbands is closing on a knot of horsemen around a huge fat general in gilded armor, helmet crooked, horse turning in panic. Liu Bei sees the emperor's banner; Zhang Fei asks whose general it is; Liu Bei answers that it doesn't matter, and orders the charge. The dominant turn: an imperial general surrounded, and the brothers cut him out. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei smash a road through the ring side by side while Liu Bei, swords still sheathed, waves the farmers on; the ring breaks and the fat general sags, gasping, his armor dented. Liu Bei owns the decision. The reader turns because they have saved someone important, and he is about to find out who saved him. No one falls in view.

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the four strings is missing, altered, duplicated, reordered, or not comfortably transcribable from the 600 × 900 proof, or extra text appears. A string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The rescue does not land
One image must own the page: the brothers above a hillside of trampled banners and a ring of yellow headbands closing on a fat general in gilded armor. Then the charge breaks the ring, and the general sags, saved. If the reader cannot tell the general was surrounded and is now freed, it fails.

### C3 — Attribution of the A–B–A exchange
Liu Bei owns both his lines and Zhang Fei owns "Whose general is he?", each by tail to its own mouth, in that order; "Ride!" must read as Liu Bei's order.

### C4 — Identity
Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei must each be recognisable; the fat general must read as obese and old in gilded armor, never confusable with Zhang Fei's hard young bulk.

### C5 — Continuity and deaths
Liu Bei's swords stay sheathed through the charge. Any blood, wound, body, or severed part is blocking; rebel counts are nonblocking.
