## Page intent

## PAGE 28

Dramatic. Morning on the road between willows. Cao Cao rides out fast and meets the old man coming home on his donkey with a full wine jar and a basket of fruit, waving, calling that he told them to kill a pig. Cao Cao reins in and says he is sorry; the old man asks, puzzled, sorry for what. The page's one dominant turn: Cao Cao above him, sword drawn, face calm, explaining that the old man would go home, see, and tell the world, and saying the line that defines him: he would rather betray the whole world than let the world betray him. The old man's open, puzzled face is under it. The blow is never shown; the last image is an empty road, the donkey wandering off, the wine jar rolling in the dust, and the old man never came home. The confrontation owns the page. The reader turns because only they saw it.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 28 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the road outside the farm.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Morning. Cao Cao riding out fast between willows.
Coming up the road toward him, the old man on his donkey, a full wine jar
and a basket of fruit, waving.

> **UNCLE:** "Little Cao Cao! Leaving already? I told them to kill a pig for you! Turn around, we'll eat in an hour!"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Cao Cao reined in, looking down at the old man.

> **CAO CAO:** "Uncle, I'm sorry."
>
> **UNCLE:** "Sorry? Sorry for what?"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 35%.** Cao Cao above the old man, his sword drawn,
his face calm. The old man's open, puzzled face.

> **CAO CAO:** "When you get home you'll see what I did, and you'll tell the world."
>
> **CAO CAO:** "I would rather betray the whole world than let the world betray me."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** The empty road. The donkey wandering away. A wine jar
rolling in the dust, fruit scattered behind it.

> **CAPTION** *(lower right)*
> "The old man never came home."

**Why you turn the page:** the man who tried to kill the tyrant has just killed an innocent old man, and only you saw.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 28 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every scripted string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement or by naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The betrayal line owns the page
Cao Cao above the old man, sword drawn, face calm, with the old man's puzzled face beneath, must be what the eye finds. If the sword is not drawn or the old man's incomprehension does not read, the turn fails. Dramatic mode.

### C3 — Attribution of the short exchange
"Uncle, I'm sorry" is Cao Cao's; "Sorry? Sorry for what?" is the old man's and reads after it. The opening call is the old man's; both lines under the drawn sword are Cao Cao's.

### C4 — The empty road
The last image shows the road with the wandering donkey, the rolling wine jar and scattered fruit, and no old man. Any blood, body, wound, or blow on the page is blocking.

### C5 — Identity
Cao Cao short, angular, goateed; the old man small, bald with a white topknot and sparse white beard, on a grey donkey.
