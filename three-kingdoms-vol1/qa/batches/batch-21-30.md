# Sequence gate — pages 21–30

## Blind sequence read

Lü Bu kneels before Dong Zhuo, presents the dead old man's seal, and calls Dong Zhuo Father. Dong Zhuo embraces him as his son and rewards him with gold armor while Cao Cao watches and condemns the betrayal. With the capital, emperor, and Lü Bu all under his control, Dong Zhuo lives as a tyrant; Cao Cao hides his opposition behind obedience and carries a jeweled dagger into court.

Cao Cao arrives late at Dong Zhuo's bedchamber and blames his old horse. Dong Zhuo sends Lü Bu away to choose him a faster one, then turns his back and sleeps. Cao Cao raises the dagger, but Dong Zhuo sees the blade in the mirror and challenges him. Cao Cao instantly recasts the assassination attempt as the presentation of a precious gift, then asks to try the new horse. He rides out of the courtyard and straight through the city gate. Dong Zhuo and Lü Bu recognize the deception too late, and a reward goes out for Cao Cao's head.

At dusk Cao Cao reaches the home of his father's sworn brother. The old man welcomes and hides him, then rides away to buy good wine while his family prepares a feast. Cao Cao hears a knife being sharpened and fragments about tying legs and making one quick cut. Believing the family means to kill him, he kills everyone in the house, only to discover the bound pig they were preparing for him. On the road he meets the returning old man, apologizes, explains that the old man will expose him once he sees the house, and declares that he would rather betray the world than let it betray him. The empty road, spilled provisions, and wandering donkey make clear that Cao Cao kills him too.

Cao Cao reaches home, raises an army, and sends calls to resist Dong Zhuo across the empire. One letter reaches Liu Bei at his sandal stall. Liu Bei reads it with Guan Yu and Zhang Fei: they recognize Dong Zhuo as the man they once saved and Cao Cao as the man whose smile they distrusted. Liu Bei has waited five years for a summons and decides that it does not matter who sent this one. The three brothers leave to correct their old mistake and go to war against Dong Zhuo.

## Script comparison and sequence judgment

The visible action, prose, dialogue, speaker attribution, and named identities match the exact batch script across all ten pages. Lü Bu's seal, armor, halberd, and relationship to Dong Zhuo remain legible through the opening transition; Cao Cao's seven-jeweled dagger, the bronze mirror, the newly offered horse, and the escape carry cleanly through pages 22–25. The farmhouse, pig, wine jar, fruit, donkey, and the old man's identity preserve the cause and consequence of pages 26–28. The sealed call then provides a clear bridge from Cao Cao's camp to Liu Bei and the brothers on pages 29–30.

Cao Cao remains the focal character through the assassination attempt, flight, fearful mistake, and deliberate moral break. The shift to Liu Bei on page 29 is explicit and motivated by the letter, so it does not fracture focal integrity. Page turns consistently preserve or sharpen the active question: the hidden dagger, Dong Zhuo's exposed back, the improvised gift, the flight, the refuge, the overheard threat, the returning host, and finally the brothers' answer to Cao Cao's call.

The pacing does not collapse into compressed summary. The prose pages at 22, 25, and 29 efficiently establish political control, pursuit, and the geographic handoff, while the dramatic pages give the assassination and farmhouse tragedy room to unfold. Their spacing prevents a wall of dramatic scenes. The discovery of the pig supplies the immediate aftermath of the household killings; Cao Cao's apology, maxim, and the empty road supply the aftermath of his decision to kill the old man; and page 29's statement that nobody knows what happened preserves the intended moral irony as he becomes the author of the loyalist call.

No material story, text, attribution, identity, continuity, focal, pacing, aftermath, or page-turn failure justifies a complete-page redraw.
