# Sequence gate — canonical pages 31–40

## Blind sequence read

Eighteen lords answer Cao Cao's call and mass their armies in the mountain
valley before Hulao Gate, with Dong Zhuo and the boy emperor beyond it. Liu
Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei arrive with only five hundred mostly farmer
soldiers and pitch their lone tent at the camp's edge.

Inside the coalition's great tent, Yuan Shao is established as leader. Cao Cao
identifies the three men left standing by the door, but Yuan mocks Liu Bei's
claim to imperial descent and lowly trade. Cao wins Liu Bei a seat at the very
end of the table; Zhang Fei wants to retaliate, and Liu restrains him.

Dong Zhuo's champion then challenges the coalition. After the lords' best
fighters fail to return, Yuan laments the absence of his own great generals.
Guan Yu steps forward and promises to bring back the champion's helmet. Yuan
objects that Guan is a rankless common soldier, but Cao persuades him to let
Guan try. Cao offers hot wine; Guan leaves it on the table and promises to
return before it cools.

The lords listen to the unseen clash beyond the tent while the cup still
steams. When the drums stop, Guan returns with the helmet and finds the wine
still warm. Zhang exults and urges an immediate attack on the gate. Yuan,
angered by the brothers' presumption, orders the three nobodies thrown out,
while Cao insists that the victor's station does not diminish the victory.

That night Cao visits the brothers in their small tent. He warns that Lu Bu
will fight next: Lu Bu killed the man who raised him in exchange for Red Hare,
now calls Dong Zhuo Father, and is expected to defeat any fighter within three
blows. Cao asks the brothers to stand with him rather than Yuan. Liu commits
only to fighting Dong Zhuo beside anyone who does the same. After Cao leaves,
Zhang vows to shame Lu Bu publicly for his betrayal, while Guan reflects on
Cao's ability to witness that betrayal and continue bowing.

On Hulao Gate, Dong Zhuo hears that a red-faced common soldier killed his
champion and was then cast out by the lords. Faced with eighteen armies below,
he takes confidence from Lu Bu, who promises to bring him all eighteen. At
daybreak Lu Bu rides out on Red Hare. The lords send champion after champion;
none lasts three blows. With the coalition line failing and Lu Bu turning
toward the leader's gold banner, Yuan asks whether anyone among the eighteen
armies can stop one man. The three brothers wait on Yuan's rise while Cao and
his force remain visibly separate.

## Script comparison

The visible story matches the exact script in material action, text, and
attribution. The page turns carry their promised questions cleanly: social
humiliation becomes Guan Yu's test; the hot wine sustains suspense through the
off-page duel; the helmet and still-warm cup deliver the result; the brothers'
expulsion leads into Cao's private approach; and Cao's warning is answered by
Lu Bu's introduction and battlefield dominance.

Named identities remain legible and stable across the run. The brothers'
white, green, and dark silhouettes, Yuan Shao's ivory-and-gold authority, Cao
Cao's dark armor, Dong Zhuo's purple bulk, and Lu Bu's gold armor, red cloak,
long plumes, halberd, and Red Hare preserve focal integrity through changes of
setting and scale. The consequential cup of wine, captured helmet, banners,
camp-edge tent, great tent, gate, and opposing battlefield positions all
remain continuous across page turns.

Pacing is varied rather than compressed: the establishing illustrated prose
opens into social drama, the duel is held as a suspense spectacle, its victory
receives both public and private aftermath, the quiet night conversation
deepens motive, the gate-top counterpoint shifts focal side, and the final two
pages expand into spectacle before the next confrontation. The short reports
of failed champions on pages 33 and 40 function as escalation, not summary
substituting for a consequential scene. No material beat lacks its aftermath.
