# Sequence gate — canonical pages 41–50

## Verdict

APPROVED

## Uninterrupted sequence

Lü Bu cuts through Yuan Shao's guards and is about to finish him when Zhang
Fei charges in, names himself, and provokes Lü Bu with the insult that he is a
slave of three fathers. Zhang Fei holds Lü Bu through fifty exchanges without
giving ground, but Lü Bu remains strong. Guan Yu joins his exhausted brother,
and the two fight Lü Bu together through thirty more exchanges without moving
him. Liu Bei watches from the hill until Yuan Shao orders him to stay back and
belittles him. Remembering the brothers' peach-garden oath, Liu Bei chooses to
ride down and share their danger.

With Liu Bei attacking from the third side, Lü Bu can no longer watch every
weapon at once. He feints at Liu Bei, makes him flinch, and escapes through the
gap toward Hulao Gate. The defenders cover Lü Bu with arrows and slam the doors
behind him. From outside, the brothers challenge him; from the wall, Lü Bu asks
who they are. Liu Bei names all three and declares them brothers. Lü Bu asks
for single combat next time, and Guan Yu simply refuses.

At dusk Cao Cao sits with Liu Bei and admits that he watched the brothers as a
test. He dismisses Yuan Shao's inherited prestige and calls himself and Liu Bei
the camp's only two heroes. Liu Bei outwardly retreats into his sandal-seller
identity, but later stands awake before the closed gate, dwelling on his fear,
his brothers, the failed lords, and how badly he wants Cao Cao's judgment to be
true. At dawn Liu Bei rides back to the gate between Guan Yu and Zhang Fei. He
knows Cao Cao is watching from behind and does not turn around.

## Script comparison and sequence judgment

The visible story matches the batch script materially and preserves its causal
chain across every turn: Zhang Fei's intervention saves Yuan Shao; his fatigue
brings Guan Yu; the brothers' continued inability to move Lü Bu forces Liu
Bei's choice; Liu Bei's entry creates both the decisive three-sided pressure
and the gap Lü Bu exploits; Lü Bu's retreat produces the naming at the gate;
that public recognition leads into Cao Cao's private recognition and Liu Bei's
night of reflection; the reflection resolves in the dawn return.

All scripted dialogue, captions, prose, and the end card are present with the
intended speakers and subjects. Liu Bei, Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, Lü Bu, Yuan Shao,
and Cao Cao remain distinguishable where the transitions depend on them. The
consequential objects and settings remain continuous: Lü Bu's halberd and red
horse, Zhang Fei's snake spear and black horse, Guan Yu's crescent blade and
green clothing, Liu Bei's paired swords and white mourning clothes, the allied
gold banner, Cao Cao's dark banners, and the exterior, wall, and closed doors
of Hulao Gate all carry cleanly through the relevant page turns.

The focal movement is controlled. The batch begins with Zhang Fei's reckless
challenge, expands through the three brothers, and then settles decisively on
Liu Bei's ambition and choice without losing the brothers as the emotional
anchor. Page turns escalate or reframe rather than repeat: single combat,
reinforcement, Liu Bei's decision, encirclement, escape, naming, Cao Cao's
assessment, private reckoning, and dawn resolve.

The run does not read as compressed summary. The two full-page combat
spectacles are separated by dramatic multi-panel decisions, and the gate
exchange, camp conversation, illustrated-prose night page, and final dawn
tableau provide meaningful variation in mode and tempo. The consequential
beats receive aftermath: Lü Bu's retreat is followed by the gate confrontation;
the brothers' feat is followed by Cao Cao's political reading; Cao Cao's praise
is followed by Liu Bei's discomfort and then his choice. No material reader
harm or complete-page redraw is justified.
