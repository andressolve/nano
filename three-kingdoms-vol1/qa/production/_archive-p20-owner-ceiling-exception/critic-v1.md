VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
An older man in purple and gold leads a huge red horse into the stable for a lean, red-armored warrior. The horse visually owns the page: the central image is the warrior touching its neck and visibly wanting it. The older man owns the dramatic turn, escalating the gift into an attack on the warrior's father and an offer to replace him; the warrior insists that he already has a father but does not reject the horse.

## Visible text
- Older purple-robed man, top-panel balloon with tail pointing to him: "This is Red Hare. He can run from sunrise to sunset and never tire. There is no horse like him in the empire."
- Red-armored warrior, middle-panel balloon with tail pointing to him: "I have never seen a horse like this."
- Red-armored warrior, bottom-panel left balloon with tail pointing to him: "He raised me. I have a father."
- Older purple-robed man, bottom-panel upper-right balloon with tail pointing to him: "He's yours. What has the old man ever given you? A bed, and orders?"
- Older purple-robed man, bottom-panel lower-right balloon placed directly beneath his preceding balloon: "Do you? A real father would give his son everything. Be my son, Lü Bu, and you'll have gold armor to match the horse."

The names "Red Hare" and "Lü Bu" are lettered within dialogue. No speaker name, label, tag, or other extra text is lettered.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: In the bottom panel, Lü Bu's left-hand balloon, "He raised me. I have a father.", is read before Dong Zhuo's upper-right balloon, "He's yours. What has the old man ever given you? A bed, and orders?" The reply therefore appears before the line that prompts it.
- Material reader harm: The causal exchange is reversed, so Lü Bu seems to defend his father before Dong Zhuo attacks that relationship. This breaks the scripted temptation and weakens Dong Zhuo's subsequent "Do you?" as an answer.
- Redraw justification: The page turn depends on Dong Zhuo offering the horse, attacking the old father, and Lü Bu answering in that order. Reversing that central prompt-and-reply sequence materially damages the page's dominant dramatic relationship, enough to justify replacing the complete page.
