The courtyard rests in morning light as seasons and friendships change, and Guan Yu finally walks out through the gate carrying the Green Dragon blade toward a greater destiny. Guan Yu owns the page, with the final departure image dominant; the turn is his movement from preparation and reflection into departure.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: None of the three required prose paragraphs appears. Four different prose captions appear instead, beginning “AT DAWN, GUAN YU PREPARES FOR THE ROAD.” and ending “A GREATER DESTINY AWAITS.”
- Material reader harm: The page omits the brotherhood order, Zhang Fei's sale, the assignment and naming of each brother's weapon, and the arrival and arming of five hundred farmers, so the scripted narrative information and causal order are absent.
- Redraw justification: The entire owner-controlled prose is replaced by unrelated prose; this cannot be released as the scripted page and is substantial enough to require replacing the complete page.

### Finding C2
- Observation: The dominant image is Guan Yu departing a quiet courtyard. There is no smith's forge, no smith, no one-handed lifting of the great curved blade, and no garden gathering of farmers as the consequence.
- Material reader harm: The required turn from newly forged weapons to the brothers becoming an armed company does not occur; the reader instead receives a solitary departure scene.
- Redraw justification: Both required places and the page's dominant causal relationship are absent, so the page event cannot be recovered without replacing the complete page.

### Finding C4
- Observation: Although Guan Yu's red face, long beard, and green headscarf appear, there is no man lifting the blade and the three brothers are absent from the garden because no farmer gathering appears.
- Material reader harm: The page cannot identify the three brothers as the leaders of the newly gathered company, eliminating the focal group whose decision to go to war drives the turn.
- Redraw justification: The required focal identities and their relationship to the farmers are wholly absent, which is substantial enough to require replacing the complete page.
