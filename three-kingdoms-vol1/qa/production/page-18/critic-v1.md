## Blind read
In the dominant top panel, a large crowned man beside the throne declares that the old emperor’s son has been deposed and that a nine-year-old younger brother will replace him, while the court bows and the child sits under guard. A lone white-robed courtier then rises and identifies himself as Yuan Shao, objecting that the crowned man has no right to choose the emperor. Close-ups turn the confrontation into reciprocal sword threats; the final panel shows Yuan Shao walking alone down the central aisle and away from the throne. The page is owned by the top-panel seizure of the throne and by the crowned man’s clash with Yuan Shao. The turn is caused by Yuan Shao standing against the otherwise prostrate court, and it changes from political objection to an armed personal standoff before he leaves.
