## PAGE 31

Illustrated prose. The page's one turn: eighteen lords before Hulao Gate, and the brothers at the very edge of their camp. One great image owns the page: a mountain valley filled to its far end with tents, smoke and banners of many colours, a great gold banner over the largest tent, and at the head of the valley a grey fortress gate filling the pass under purple banners. The prose sits over the sky and tells the reader who came, where the gate stands, and who waits behind it. Small in the near corner, the three brothers ride in with their farmers and pitch one small tent. The scale is the story: the lords are a city, the brothers a speck. The reader turns because eighteen great lords are about to meet three nobodies.

## PAGE 32

Dramatic. The page's one turn: "I was told he sells sandals," and Liu Bei takes the very end of the bench. The opening image owns the page: the long table of the lords in the great tent, Yuan Shao in ivory under the gold banner at its head, Cao Cao halfway down, the three brothers standing by the door. Yuan Shao asks who they are; Cao Cao vouches for Liu Bei; Yuan Shao sneers at the sandals while the lords laugh behind their sleeves; Cao Cao asks for a seat for him. Liu Bei bows and sits on the last bench, Guan Yu like stone behind him, Zhang Fei bent to his ear offering to put the leader's hat in the soup, and Liu Bei refusing. The reader turns because Zhang Fei is about to burst.

## PAGE 33

Dramatic. The page's one turn: Dong Zhuo's champion kills the lords' best, and Guan Yu volunteers to bring back his helmet. A messenger throws himself down at the head of the table and Yuan Shao orders out their best; the messenger returns, kneeling, to a silent tent; Yuan Shao, pale, strikes the table and wishes for his own great generals. Those three beats are the build. The last image owns the page: from the back of the tent, Guan Yu steps forward, huge, stroking his beard, and every head at the table turns to him as he says he will bring the champion's helmet back to this table. The reader turns because a man with no rank has just volunteered in front of eighteen lords.

## PAGE 34

Dramatic. The page's one turn: Guan Yu bets his life against a cup of wine. Yuan Shao demands the volunteer's rank and Liu Bei answers plainly that his brother has none; Yuan Shao, outraged, orders the common soldier thrown out; Cao Cao stands and talks the tent round. The last image owns the page: Cao Cao pours a steaming cup of wine and holds it out, and Guan Yu, already turning for the door with his great crescent blade, tells him to leave it on the table, because he will be back before it is cold. The reader turns because the wine is poured, Guan Yu is going, and there is no rank to save him if he fails.

## PAGE 35

Spectacle. The page's one turn: the war drums, the steaming cup, and the duel swallowed in dust. One image is the whole page, seen from the head of the table looking down the great tent: in the near foreground the cup of wine still steaming; behind it the lords frozen in their seats, listening; Liu Bei and Zhang Fei on their feet at the far end. Through the open flap, far away on the dusty plain under the grey wall of the gate, two tiny riders, one in green and one dark, meet at full gallop, and a wall of dust swallows them both. No result can be seen. The drums carry the sound across the top. The reader turns because somebody is going to come back through that flap, and the wine is still steaming.

## PAGE 36

Dramatic. The page's one turn: the helmet, the wine still warm, and the three nobodies thrown out anyway. The drums stop; Guan Yu ducks in through the flap and sets a dented plumed helmet on the table before Yuan Shao. The central image owns the page, close and quiet: Guan Yu lifting the cup, steam still rising, and Cao Cao beside him, delighted that it is still warm. Then the noise: Zhang Fei roars along the table that his brother killed the champion and they should take the whole gate; Yuan Shao, white with anger, orders the three nobodies thrown out; Cao Cao shrugs that a victory is a victory. The reader turns because Guan Yu won and they are being thrown out regardless.

## PAGE 37

Dramatic. The page's one turn: Cao Cao warns that Lü Bu comes tomorrow and asks for the brothers, and Liu Bei will not say yes. The opening image owns the page: the brothers' small tent at night, one lamp, Cao Cao crouched alone with the three of them over a wine jar and a basket of roast meat, his face serious for once, telling them who Lü Bu is and what he did for a red horse. He leans closer and asks Liu Bei to stand with him, not with Yuan Shao; Liu Bei answers that they will fight beside anyone who fights Dong Zhuo. Cao Cao gone, the flap still swinging, Zhang Fei punches his palm and Guan Yu judges the man who went on bowing. The reader turns because Lü Bu comes tomorrow and Liu Bei gave Cao Cao no answer.

## PAGE 38

Dramatic. The page's one turn: on the wall, Lü Bu asks Dong Zhuo for the gate. The opening image owns the page: the battlements of Hulao Gate at night under purple banners, Dong Zhuo eating on a heap of cushions, gleeful that a red-faced common soldier killed his champion and was then thrown out by the lords; Lü Bu beside him, halberd on his shoulder, looking down at thousands of campfires in the valley. Dong Zhuo stops laughing as he counts the armies against his one gate. Lü Bu turns from the parapet, plumes black against the moon, calls him Father, and promises all eighteen. The reader turns because tomorrow the gate opens, and Lü Bu comes out.
