## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two title lines and the caption must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text on the page; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The empire is not burning
The page must read at a glance as a burning town and an army in yellow headbands filling the road toward the reader, one image owning the page as spectacle. A page where the march is a thin background band, or where the eye lands on a single figure or the cart, fails the turn.

### C3 — Someone is singled out
No face is close and no marcher reads as a character: a red face, a long beard, plumes, or any balloon on a marcher is blocking. Repeated anonymous faces and rough background anatomy are nonblocking.

### C4 — A death is drawn
Any blood, wound, body, or fallen figure is blocking. Fire and smoke are not.

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two notice lines and the three prose paragraphs must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. The notice lines are object text on the paper on the wall. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The wanting does not land
The page must read as illustrated prose: a man behind a sandal stall frozen before the notice, the passing crowd ignoring him, then his hands gone still on a half-woven sandal. If he reads as selling or chatting, or the notice cannot be found, the turn fails.

### C3 — Liu Bei is not Liu Bei
A tall, slim, pale man with a broad, compact oval face and short rounded chin, very long earlobes, a thin moustache only and a clean-shaven chin, in a patched hemp robe. A long or pointed beard, armor, swords, or white cloak is blocking.

### C4 — Focal hands fail
The close panel shows exactly two hands holding one half-woven sandal; a third hand or a fused or missing hand is blocking. Background hands are nonblocking.

## Page 3 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
The four lines must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. The notice may show its own words as object text. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, a regional spelling variant. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The challenge does not land
The first and largest image must show Liu Bei sighing at the notice with a huge grinning stranger looming behind him; the page then moves to his embarrassed answer and to being hauled off. If the challenge does not own the page, it fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the exchange
Zhang Fei owns the opening dare and the self-introduction ending "Prince of Sandals!"; Liu Bei owns the answer about sandals and "My name is Liu Bei." Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely make sense as his.

### C4 — The two men collide
Liu Bei tall, pale, smooth-cheeked, long-eared, in hemp; Zhang Fei a head shorter, twice as broad, dark, with round bulging eyes and bristling cheek-whiskers, in a butcher's apron. Any confusion of who speaks or who grabs whom is blocking.

### C5 — Focal generation integrity
A duplicated named figure, a third arm on the steering embrace, or a garbled face on either speaker is blocking. Crowd faces and background hands are not.
