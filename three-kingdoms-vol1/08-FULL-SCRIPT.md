**Owner-controlled.** Production roles never edit this file. No compensation
number, generation note, or critic suggestion is ever written into it.

**How to read this document.** Each page is written as a scene first. The spec
under it is three lines and no more: mode (in the header), location, and why
the reader turns the page. Panel notes are staging, not metadata. **If the
metadata for a page is longer than the page's dramatized content, the story is
not being written.**

**Text discipline.** Every string between quotation marks in a speaker,
`PROSE FIELD`, `CAPTION`, `SOUND`, or printed-object line renders exactly as
written. Nothing else in this document renders. `tools/script_audit.py` and
`tools/verify.py` parse this format, so keep it exact.

# MOVEMENT I — THE PEACH GARDEN · pp 1–9

---

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the burning empire.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Loess hills under a smoke-brown sky. A walled
town burning on a hill, orange flame and black smoke. Down the road toward
the reader, filling the lower half of the page edge to edge, a river of men
with yellow cloths tied around their heads, spears and hoes and torches held
high, mouths open, singing. Nobody's face is close. In the lower corner, a
farmer's cart overturned at the roadside, its owner gone.

> **TITLE CARD** *(upper third, over the smoke)*
> "THREE KINGDOMS
>
> The Peach Garden Oath"

> **CAPTION** *(lower left, over the dust of the road)*
> "The Han emperors had ruled China for four hundred years. Now the land was starving, and the Yellow Turbans, an army in yellow headbands, were on the march."

**Why you turn the page:** the empire is on fire, and somebody is going to have to stand in front of that road.

---

## PAGE 2 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the market of Zhuo County.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A crowded dusty market street under a grey tile
gate. In the shade of a wall, a small stall hung with straw sandals. Behind
it, LIU BEI: tall and slim, a faded hemp robe patched at the elbows, a plain
cloth cap, long earlobes, a thin moustache. He is not selling. He is standing
very still, looking at a pale paper notice pasted on the wall beside his
stall, with a red seal at its foot. Shoppers pass behind him without looking.

> **NOTICE** *(the paper on the wall, large, plain)*
> "VOLUNTEERS WANTED
> to fight the Yellow Turbans"

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Close on his long hands, a half-woven sandal in them,
gone still.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the top of the page, over the pale sky above the gate)*
> "In Zhuo County, far in the north, a man named Liu Bei sold straw sandals in the market. He was twenty-eight, tall and quiet, with long arms and ears so long that strangers stared.
>
> Three hundred years ago his family had been princes, the sons of emperors. Now Liu Bei wove sandals, and sold them, and bowed to everyone who bought a pair.
>
> On the morning the notice went up, he read it eleven times."

**Why you turn the page:** a prince's grandson is standing in a market wanting something badly, and he hasn't moved.

---

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the market of Zhuo County.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** LIU BEI, left, in front of the notice, his
shoulders dropping in a long sigh. Behind him, right, looming over him, ZHANG
FEI: short and enormously broad, a leather butcher's apron, sleeves rolled
over arms like hams, a square head, round bulging eyes, black whiskers
bristling all round his jaw. Arms folded. Grinning.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "A grown man, sighing at a wall! If you want to fight the rebels, why don't you go and do something about it?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Liu Bei turned to face him, embarrassed, his hands
folded into his sleeves.

> **LIU BEI:** "I want to. I have the blood of princes and a stall full of sandals. What would I fight them with?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Zhang Fei thumping his own barrel chest, then throwing
one huge arm round Liu Bei's shoulders and steering him off toward a tavern
sign.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "I'm Zhang Fei. I have a butcher's shop, a farm, and more money than sense. Come and drink with me, Prince of Sandals!"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "My name is Liu Bei."

**Why you turn the page:** a stranger with money just grabbed him, and they're going to talk about war.

---

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the tavern.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** A low-beamed tavern, benches, a wine jar,
lamplight. In the doorway, ducking under the lintel, GUAN YU: a head taller
than anyone in the room, a deep red face, long narrow eyes tilted up at the
corners, a glossy black beard falling in one sheet to his belt, a green
headscarf. He has slammed one fist on the nearest table. Liu Bei and Zhang
Fei at a table in the foreground, turning to look.

> **GUAN YU:** "Wine, and quickly! I'm going to enlist before the day is over, and I won't do it thirsty."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Zhang Fei, left, delighted, waving him over. Guan Yu,
right, sitting down heavily at their table.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Now that is a soldier! Hey, Red Face! Sit with us. We're enlisting too."
>
> **GUAN YU:** "My name is Guan Yu. In my village a rich man beat the poor and took their land."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The three at one table. Guan Yu, right, stroking his
beard downward with one hand. Liu Bei, left, leaning in.

> **GUAN YU:** "I killed him. I've been running for five years."
>
> **LIU BEI:** "And now you'll fight for the emperor whose soldiers have been chasing you?"
>
> **GUAN YU:** "The rebels burn villages like mine. I'll fight anyone who does that."

**Why you turn the page:** three men who have nothing in common are at one table, and none of them is leaving.

---

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard of Zhang Fei's farm, at night.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** A rammed-earth courtyard, a fire, a wine jug. Zhang Fei
sprawled laughing, right. Guan Yu upright, centre. Liu Bei, left, staring
into the flames.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "They talked all night, at Zhang Fei's farm."
>
> **LIU BEI:** "You two could join any army in the empire. You don't need me. Why would anyone follow a man who sells sandals?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Guan Yu, stroking his beard. Zhang Fei holding up
fingers.

> **GUAN YU:** "I did not come for your ancestors, the princes. I came for the man who sighed at that wall."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "He read it eleven times. I counted!"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 40%.** Zhang Fei on his feet, pointing past the house
into the dark, where pale shapes of blossoming trees show over a wall. Liu
Bei looking up at him, his face lit and hungry.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Behind my house is a peach garden, and every tree is in bloom."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "Tomorrow morning we go out there and swear to be brothers. Real brothers!"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "Then tomorrow we will be brothers."

**Why you turn the page:** tomorrow, in a garden, three strangers are going to swear.

---

## PAGE 6 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the peach garden.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Morning in a walled garden of old peach trees in
full pink-and-white bloom, petals falling through slanting light. Under the
largest tree, a small table with incense smoking and three cups of wine. The
three kneel side by side facing the table, cups raised: GUAN YU left, LIU BEI
centre, ZHANG FEI right. Their faces are solemn; even Zhang Fei's. The only
soft colour in the whole book. Liu Bei speaks the oath for all three.

> **LIU BEI:** "We swear to stand together, to protect the people, and to serve the empire."
>
> **LIU BEI:** "We were not born on the same day. But we swear to die on the same day."

**Why you turn the page:** they have just promised to die together, and now they have to live up to it.

---

## PAGE 7 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the peach garden.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Under the blossom, the three rising from their knees,
the cups empty on the little table. Zhang Fei, right, counting on his thick
fingers. Liu Bei, left, and Guan Yu, centre, watching him.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Now, who's oldest? You are, Liu Bei. So you're Big Brother."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "Red Face is Second Brother. I'm the youngest, so I'm Third Brother. Third! It sounds terrible."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Liu Bei, left, bowing to them both with folded
hands, moved and hungry at once. Guan Yu, right, stroking his beard.

> **LIU BEI:** "Brothers need weapons, and soldiers, and money. I have twenty pairs of sandals."
>
> **GUAN YU:** "And I have a horse and a cart."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Zhang Fei throwing his arms wide toward the farmhouse
behind the garden wall, grinning.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "And I have a shop, fields, and cattle. Tomorrow I sell all of it. Everything but the house and this garden."

**Why you turn the page:** Zhang Fei has just given away everything he owns, and now they can buy weapons.

---

## PAGE 8 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the smith's forge, and the peach garden full of men.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** A smith's forge, red heat, sparks. Laid across
the anvil and a rack: two matching straight swords in black scabbards; a
spear as long as two men with a wavy blade like a snake; and a great curved
blade on a long pole with a dragon worked at its base. Guan Yu lifting the
curved blade in one hand while the smith gapes.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The peach garden, now in leaf, packed with farmers
holding hoes and pitchforks; the three brothers before them.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the top, over the dark rafters, never over the weapons)*
> "With the money, the smith made a weapon for each brother. For Liu Bei, two matching swords. For Zhang Fei, a spear as long as two men, with a wavy blade like a snake. For Guan Yu, a great curved blade on a long pole, so heavy two men could hardly lift it. He called it the Green Dragon.
>
> Five hundred farmers came to the peach garden with hoes and pitchforks. In a month they had spears."

**Why you turn the page:** three brothers and five hundred farmers are going to war.

---

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a ridge above the rebel army.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A bare ridge. The three brothers on horseback in
front, five hundred farmers with spears behind them. Below, a dust-yellow
plain filled to the horizon with yellow headbands. Between the ridge and the
plain, a narrow valley with a scrubby hill on either side.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Fifty thousand of them and five hundred of us. Good! That's only a hundred each."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Guan Yu, glancing back at the farmers, who are pale and
shaking.

> **GUAN YU:** "The farmers are shaking, Third Brother. They can count too."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Liu Bei, centre, pointing down at the two hills. Zhang
Fei, right, staring at him.

> **LIU BEI:** "Then we won't fight fifty thousand. Second Brother, hide behind the left hill. Third Brother, behind the right."
>
> **LIU BEI:** "I'll ride out with the farmers and let them chase us."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "You? You're the worst fighter of the three of us!"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "That's why they'll chase me. When they're between the hills, come down."

**Why you turn the page:** the plan is that Liu Bei runs from fifty thousand men.

# MOVEMENT II — FIVE HUNDRED · pp 10–16

---

## PAGE 10 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the valley between the two hills.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Down the valley toward the reader: LIU BEI in
front on his horse, the farmers running behind him, and behind them the
yellow army pouring into the valley after them. From the left hill, GUAN YU
crashes down in green with the Green Dragon blade raised; from the right,
ZHANG FEI in black with his snake spear, men streaming behind each of them.
The yellow army is caught between two walls.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Here I am! Who wants to be first?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** A rebel captain in a yellow headband on a brown horse,
going over backward out of the bottom of the frame as Zhang Fei's spear
drives past. His men behind him already turning to run.

**Why you turn the page:** five hundred farmers just broke fifty thousand, and people are going to hear about it.

---

## PAGE 11 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the edge of a plain of tall dry grass, at night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Night. The three brothers on horseback on a low
rise, their small army behind them. Before them, a plain of tall dry grass
to the horizon, and a great camp of rebel tents and watchfires in the middle
of it. On the far side of the camp, a thin line of orange has begun to glow
in the grass.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the top, over the night sky)*
> "Five hundred farmers had beaten fifty thousand rebels, and the story ran ahead of them from town to town. More men came to join them. The brothers marched south to where the biggest rebel army of all was camped, in a plain of tall dry grass.
>
> They meant to attack at dawn. They never got the chance.
>
> At midnight the wind rose. And out in the dark, on the far side of the rebel camp, the grass began to glow."

**Why you turn the page:** someone has set the grass on fire, and it isn't them.

---

## PAGE 12 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the burning plain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 80%.** The whole plain on fire, flames twice the
height of a man, black smoke, rebel tents going up. Yellow-headbanded rebels
running out of the fire toward the reader, and sweeping in from the dark on
both sides to cut them off, horsemen under black-and-indigo banners. In the
foreground, the three brothers in silhouette against the orange, reining in.

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Zhang Fei's face lit orange, mouth wide open in outrage.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Hey! Somebody stole our battle!"

**Why you turn the page:** who got here first?

---

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the edge of the burnt plain, at dawn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Grey dawn, smoke drifting over black stubble.
From the right, riding up under black-and-indigo banners: CAO CAO, short and
quick in dark indigo armor, a narrow face, glittering narrow eyes, a neat
moustache and pointed little beard. He reins in in front of the brothers,
left, amused.

> **CAO CAO:** "You're late. I set the grass on fire at midnight, and the wind did the rest."
>
> **CAO CAO:** "I'm Cao Cao. I command the emperor's cavalry."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Cao Cao looking past them at the farmers with their
spears and hoes. Liu Bei, left, bowing slightly.

> **CAO CAO:** "Whose army is this? Who pays these men?"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "Nobody pays them. The rebels burned their villages, so they came. I am Liu Bei."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Cao Cao turning his horse to go, one eyebrow up, a quick
smile back over his shoulder. Behind Liu Bei, Zhang Fei grinning and Guan
Yu stroking his beard, watching Cao Cao go.

> **CAO CAO:** "Then they're the only men in this war who came because they wanted to. I'll remember you, Liu Bei."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "I like him!"
>
> **GUAN YU:** "He smiles too quickly."

**Why you turn the page:** the cleverest man on the battlefield noticed them, and the brothers can't agree what that means.

---

## PAGE 14 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a hillside of broken banners.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** From a rise, the three look down on a hillside
of trampled imperial banners. Below, a circle of yellow headbands closing on
a knot of horsemen, and in the middle of them a huge fat general in gilded
armor, his helmet crooked, his horse turning in panic.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Weeks later, on the road north."
>
> **LIU BEI:** "That's the emperor's banner. They're about to lose a general."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "Whose general is he?"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "Does it matter whose? Ride!"

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Guan Yu and Zhang Fei crashing into the circle side by
side, the Green Dragon blade and the snake spear sweeping a road through the
yellow headbands. Liu Bei close behind them, swords still sheathed, waving
the farmers forward.

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The circle broken, rebels scattering. The fat general
sagging in his saddle, gasping, his gilded armor dented.

**Why you turn the page:** they've saved someone important, and he's about to find out who saved him.

---

## PAGE 15 · *dramatic*

**Location:** before the general's tent.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Guards help the fat general down from his horse: DONG
ZHUO, enormous, heavy-jowled, small eyes sunk in fat, a thin drooping
moustache, gilded armor straining over his belly. He takes a fig from a
servant's tray. Liu Bei, left, bows with folded hands.

> **DONG ZHUO:** "So. Dong Zhuo owes his life to you. What office do you hold? What is your rank?"
>
> **LIU BEI:** "None, my lord. We are volunteers."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 35%.** Dong Zhuo's face closing. He turns his broad
gilded back on them and walks into his tent, eating, without a word of
thanks. Over his shoulder:

> **DONG ZHUO:** "Volunteers. Farmers with sticks. Somebody give them a bag of rice."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Zhang Fei lunging for the tent with his spear.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "We just saved that fat pig's life, and he gives us rice? I'll go in there and take it back!"

**Panel 4 — 20%.** Liu Bei's long hand clamped on Zhang Fei's wrist. Liu
Bei's jaw is tight; his face is white.

> **LIU BEI:** "He wears the emperor's armor, Third Brother. Kill him and we are the rebels."
>
> **ZHANG FEI:** "Then why are your hands shaking, Big Brother?"

**Why you turn the page:** Liu Bei swallowed it, and Zhang Fei saw what it cost him.

---

## PAGE 16 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the road home to Zhuo County, and the road to the capital.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** Three riders very small on a long empty road
under a huge winter sky, going home.

**Panel 2 — 45%.** Far away and years later: a great army on the march
toward a walled city of grey roofs, purple banners, and at its head a fat
general in gold.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the middle, over the open sky between the panels)*
> "The rebellion was broken by winter. The great generals went to the capital and came home with titles, and gold, and land. Nobody asked about three brothers and five hundred farmers. They went home to Zhuo County with nothing.
>
> Five years passed.
>
> Then the old emperor died, and the great men in the palace began to murder each other. A general from the west marched in with his army, to restore order, he said.
>
> It was the fat general they had saved."

**Why you turn the page:** the man who turned his back on them is marching into the capital.

# MOVEMENT III — THE RED HORSE · pp 17–22

---

## PAGE 17 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the capital and the palace stair.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A walled city of grey tile roofs seen from
above, its avenues flooded with soldiers under purple banners. At the centre
of the page, the palace on its high stone platform and its long white stair,
and riding up the stair on horseback, scattering courtiers in silk who throw
themselves aside: DONG ZHUO in gold, enormous, a half-eaten drumstick in one
fist, the reins in the other.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the roofs)*
> "Dong Zhuo did not get off his horse to climb the emperor's stairs."

**Why you turn the page:** a fat man has ridden into the emperor's palace, and nobody stopped him.

---

## PAGE 18 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the throne hall.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** A vast hall of red lacquer columns and black
floor, braziers smoking. DONG ZHUO, right, beside an enormous gold throne,
lifting a small boy of nine in imperial yellow robes up onto it with one
arm; the boy looks terrified. With his other hand Dong Zhuo points across
the hall, left, at a young man in imperial robes being marched out between
soldiers: the emperor, plainly a second figure, looking back. Courtiers on
their faces in rows.

> **DONG ZHUO:** "The old emperor's son is weak and foolish. He is emperor no longer."
>
> **DONG ZHUO:** "This is his little brother. He is nine years old, and much cleverer. He will sit here now, and I will stand beside him."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** From the bowing rows, left, one man stands up: YUAN
SHAO, tall and thin, a long narrow face, a long combed black beard in three
strands, a tall black lacquered hat.

> **YUAN SHAO:** "I am Yuan Shao. Four generations of my family have served the emperors. You have no right to choose one!"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Dong Zhuo's fat hand on his sword hilt. Yuan Shao's
hand already on his.

> **DONG ZHUO:** "Do you think my sword isn't sharp, Yuan Shao?"
>
> **YUAN SHAO:** "Do you think mine isn't?"

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Yuan Shao walking out between the columns, sword half
drawn, chin up. Nobody stops him.

**Why you turn the page:** one lord defied Dong Zhuo and walked out, and everyone else is still on the floor.

---

## PAGE 19 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the banquet hall.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Low tables of food, lamplight. Dong Zhuo at the head,
eating. Halfway down the hall, an old lord in grey on his feet, pointing at
Dong Zhuo. Dong Zhuo's hand going to his sword.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "At the banquet that night, an old lord stood up and said the same thing, louder."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Behind the old lord, standing quite still, a
giant: LÜ BU, tall and lean, handsome and clean-shaven, pale, two very long
pheasant feathers curving up and back from a gold headdress, a long halberd
resting on his shoulder. He is looking straight at Dong Zhuo, and Dong Zhuo's
hand has stopped.

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Dong Zhuo letting go of the hilt, laughing, picking up

> **YUAN SHAO:** "You stand under my banner, sandal-seller, so you take my orders. Stay back, or you will die with them."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Liu Bei drawing his two swords, one in each
hand, crossing out of their sheaths; his face fiercer than we have ever
seen it.

> **LIU BEI:** "My brothers have fought eighty times while I sat here. In a peach garden, we swore to die on the same day."
>
> **LIU BEI:** "If today is that day, I will not spend it standing on a hill."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Liu Bei galloping down the slope toward the fight, both
swords out, his white cloak flying. Yuan Shao staring after him.

**Why you turn the page:** the worst fighter of the three is riding at Lü Bu.

## PAGE 45 · *spectacle*

**Why you turn the page:** Lü Bu is surrounded, and something has to break.

# MOVEMENT VII — THE GATE · pp 47–50

---

## PAGE 47 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the foot of Hulao Gate.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Zhang Fei, out of arrow range, shaking his spear up at
the wall.

> **ZHANG FEI:** "Come back out, you three-father slave! We're not finished!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Looking up the grey wall: Lü Bu leaning on the
battlements high above, breathing hard, his plumes against the sky, looking
down.

> **LÜ BU:** "Who are you? The three of you. Who are you?"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The three brothers below, side by side, looking up.

> **LIU BEI:** "I am Liu Bei. This is Guan Yu. This is Zhang Fei. We are brothers."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Lü Bu on the wall, and Guan Yu below, stroking his
beard.

> **LÜ BU:** "Next time, come at me one at a time."
>
> **GUAN YU:** "No."

**Why you turn the page:** Lü Bu knows their names now, and the day is ending.

## PAGE 48 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the brothers' fire, at dusk.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Dusk at the edge of the camp. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei a
little apart by the fire, Zhang Fei eating. Cao Cao, alone, sitting down
beside Liu Bei and pouring two cups of wine. Liu Bei does not take his.

> **LIU BEI:** "You asked us to stand with you when Lü Bu came, Lord Cao. Where were you?"
>
> **CAO CAO:** "On my hill, watching. I wanted to see what the three of you would do. Now I know."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Cao Cao, amused, nodding toward the great gold banner
across the camp.

> **CAO CAO:** "Yuan Shao is telling the camp that his guards drove Lü Bu off. Yuan Shao has ancestors. Ancestors don't fight Lü Bu."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 35%.** Cao Cao leaning close, smiling, his cup raised,
his narrow eyes glittering in the firelight.

> **CAO CAO:** "There are two heroes in this camp. You, Liu Bei. And me."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Liu Bei, who would not take the cup, is holding it now,
looking down into it. Cao Cao standing to go, laughing.

> **LIU BEI:** "I am only a man who sells sandals, Lord Cao."
>
> **CAO CAO:** "Of course you are."

**Why you turn the page:** Cao Cao watched them nearly die and called it a test, and he has just said out loud the thing Liu Bei wants most.

## PAGE 49 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the edge of the camp, at night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Moonlight. Liu Bei standing alone at the edge
of the camp, his back to us, looking at the dark shape of Hulao Gate filling
the pass. Behind him, by the embers of a fire, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei asleep,
Zhang Fei with his mouth open.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(across the top, over the night sky)*
> "Liu Bei could not sleep. He walked out past his sleeping brothers and looked at Hulao Gate in the moonlight.
>
> Behind it were the fat man who had turned his back on him, the warrior who had sold his father for a horse, and a boy of nine on a throne. In front of it were eighteen lords who would not fight, and one who poured wine and might be anything.
>
> He thought about the moment the halberd came at his face, and how he had flinched, and how Lü Bu had gone through the gap he left.
>
> He thought about the peach garden. He thought about what Cao Cao had said, and he was ashamed of how much he wanted it to be true."

**Why you turn the page:** Liu Bei wants the empire, and the gate is still shut.

## PAGE 50 · *spectacle*

**Location:** before Hulao Gate, at dawn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Cold blue dawn. The three brothers on
horseback side by side in front of the shut gate: Guan Yu with the Green
Dragon blade, Liu Bei in the middle with his two swords at his back, Zhang
Fei with the snake spear. High on the battlements, small against the sky,
Lü Bu's two pheasant plumes. Behind the brothers, on a ridge in the near
corner, large and clear, Cao Cao on his horse under his black-and-indigo
banners, watching them. Liu Bei faces the gate; he does not look back.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the sky)*
> "At dawn the three brothers rode to the gate to call Lü Bu out again. Behind them on the ridge, Cao Cao was watching. Liu Bei knew it. He did not turn around."

> **TITLE CARD** *(lower edge, small)*
> "END OF VOLUME ONE"

**Why you turn the page:** (the book ends) Liu Bei has chosen his brothers over Cao Cao's praise for today, and the man on the ridge is still watching.
