## The cut list — named, unapologetic, famous material included

### Whole sequences cut

- **The eunuchs, He Jin, and the palace coup** (ch. 2–3). Famous, and the
  actual reason Dong Zhuo reaches the capital. Cut. Every page of it is men
  in a palace explaining who is plotting against whom. One line of narration
  replaces it: the palace fell into chaos and a general from the west
  marched in to "restore order."
- **Zhang Fei flogs the inspector** (ch. 2). Beloved, funny, and a perfect
  Zhang Fei scene. Cut. It repeats the point Dong Zhuo's snub makes (the
  empire does not reward heroes), it costs a ninth face with a balloon, and
  it spends two pages on a post the reader does not care about. Zhang Fei's
  temper gets its page at the snub instead.
- **The Yellow Turban sorcerers and the magic battles** (ch. 1–2: Zhang Bao's
  sorcery, the blood of pigs and dogs, the paper soldiers). Cut. The rebels
  are a sea of yellow headbands with a chant; there is no magic in this book.
- **Lu Zhi, Liu Bei's teacher, arrested in a cage cart** (ch. 1). Cut. It is
  a second snub and an injustice the brothers cannot act on.
- **The exile and murder of the deposed emperor and the Empress Dowager**
  (ch. 3–4). Cut. The deposed emperor is led away; that is all the reader
  sees.
- **Wang Yun's banquet and his gift of the dagger** (ch. 4). Cut. Cao Cao
  already has the dagger; the reader sees him hide it.
- **Chen Gong captures and frees Cao Cao at Zhongmou** (ch. 4). Cut. Cao Cao
  rides straight from the city gate to the farmhouse.
- **The coalition's roll call of eighteen lords by name** (ch. 5). Cut. The
  lords are a field of banners and one face.
- **Sun Jian's defeat at Sishui and the hidden red cap** (ch. 5). Cut.
- **The duels before the brothers: Mu Shun, Wu Anguo's hand, Fang Yue, Yu
  She, Pan Feng.** Cut to unnamed champions whose fall is shown once, in
  a single image each, never with a name.
- **The burning of the capital and the move to Chang'an** (ch. 6). Outside
  the arc. The cover's burning city is the fire of the Yellow Turbans in
  pages 1 and 11, not the capital.

Xian (scenery, unnamed: "the emperor" and "his little brother").

### Characters merged

- **Yuan Shao + Yuan Shu + Gongsun Zan → Yuan Shao.** One face does four
  jobs: defies Dong Zhuo in the throne room and walks out, so he has a
  spine; leads the eighteen lords, so he has power; calls Liu Bei a
  sandal-seller and Guan Yu a common soldier, so he is the closed door; and
  is the lord Lü Bu rides down at Hulao, so the brothers save the man who
  scorned them.
- **Huangfu Song → Cao Cao.** The fire in the grass is Cao Cao's plan, so the
  reader meets him being brilliant.
- **Li Su → Dong Zhuo.** The tyrant brings the red horse to Lü Bu himself, so
  the purchase is a scene between the two men it concerns.
- **Chen Gong → the reader.** Nobody in the book sees what Cao Cao does at
  the farmhouse except the reader, and Cao Cao says his line to his victim.
- **Sishui Pass + Hulao Gate → Hulao Gate.** One wall, two defenders.

### Why the biggest cut pays for the volume

Collapsing the eighteen lords into Yuan Shao deletes the largest set of
production problems at the root: seventeen more armored men of similar age
and rank, each with a banner, a name, and a line, all of whom would collide
with each other on every page and none of whom changes what the brothers
want. It also gives the book its shape. With one lord, the brothers' long
humiliation has one face, it starts in the throne room before they ever
meet him, and it ends with Yuan Shao running from Lü Bu and Zhang Fei
standing in the way. The door that keeps shutting on them is one door, and
the reader gets to watch it open.

## Protected claims

From the readers' vote (2026-09-27, `menu/VOTES.md`), carried by the owner:

1. The oath in the peach garden, with its words: not born on the same day,
   but to die on the same day.
2. The three brothers as the pitch drew them: Liu Bei the sandal-seller of
   imperial descent, Guan Yu the red-faced giant with the crescent blade,
   Zhang Fei the butcher with the voice.
3. Dong Zhuo, who seized the capital, and Lü Bu on the blood-red horse, whom
   nobody has beaten.
4. Cao Cao as an open question, sometimes with them and sometimes not. The
   book never rules on him.
5. The ending at Hulao Gate, the three brothers facing Lü Bu together.

## Sensitive material and its treatment

- **Death in war.** Real and never drawn. The rebel captain at the ambush
  goes down out of frame under Zhang Fei's spear. The champion at the gate
  dies off the page, and Guan Yu sets his helmet on the table. No corpse, no
  blood, no head. Accepted at greenlight ① (2026-10-04).
- **Lü Bu kills his adoptive father** (p. 20). Off the page, in the night.
  In the morning Lü Bu lays the old man's seal of command on Dong Zhuo's
  floor and kneels. One line makes it unambiguous; nothing is shown.
- **The farmhouse** (pp. 25–27). The darkest thing in the book and the heart
  of the Cao Cao question. Cao Cao hears knives sharpened and a voice say
  "tie it up and kill it," and goes through the house with his sword in the
  dark; the next panel is the empty kitchen at dawn and a pig tied on the
  table. On the road he meets the old man coming home with wine, says "I
  would rather betray the whole world than let the world betray me," and
  raises his sword; the next panel is a wine jar rolling in the road. The
  reader understands exactly what happened and sees none of it. Accepted by
  the owner at greenlight ①; the session recommends keeping it, because
  without it Cao Cao is simply the brothers' friend and the boys were
  promised a man who might be a villain.
- **The boy emperor.** A nine-year-old held as a puppet by a tyrant. Shown,
  never harmed. He is the reason the lords rise, and he is still behind the
  gate at the end.
- **Wine.** Guan Yu's cup is the famous wager; the brothers drink at the
  tavern and swear with wine. Nobody is drunk on the page except, once,
  Dong Zhuo.
- **Animal sacrifice at the oath.** The source sacrifices a black ox and a
  white horse. Cut: the oath is incense, wine, and the three of them on their
  knees under the blossoms.
