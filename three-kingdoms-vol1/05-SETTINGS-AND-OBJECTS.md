# Settings and Objects

*Page references below predate the p 29 insertion; from p 29 on, add one. `07-PAGE-CONTRACT.md` is authoritative.*
**World palette:** dust, smoke, and lacquer. A dry yellow-brown north China
of loess hills, packed earth, rammed-earth walls, and grey tile, under big
skies that change with the story: hot white noon, smoke-orange fire, cold
blue dawn. Colour in this world belongs to people and their banners, one
owner per colour (see `04-CHARACTER-LEDGER.md`), so that a field of armies
reads as factions at a glance. Each location is a variation on that, except
the three that must look like a different world: **the peach garden** (the
only soft colour in the book, pink and white blossom and green shade), **the
palace** (dark lacquer red, black, and gold, lit by braziers, too big for
the people in it), and **Hulao Gate** (a grey stone and rammed-earth
fortress filling a mountain pass, so big that armies at its foot are
grass).

Each location names one restrained accent that does dramatic work.

---

## Locations

### 1 · The burning empire · p 1
Loess hills, a walled town on fire, a road filled from edge to edge with
men in yellow headbands, smoke across the sky. Orange firelight against a
dun day. The place says: the world is coming apart.
**Accent:** the yellow headbands, a river of them.

### 2 · Zhuo County market and tavern · pp 2–4
A crowded dusty market street under a grey tile gate, stalls, baskets,
Liu Bei's sandal stall in the shade of a wall where a paper notice is
pasted. The tavern: low beams, benches, a wine jar, lamplight. Noon in the
street, lamp-glow in the tavern. The place says: ordinary people, and
nobody here is anybody.
**Accent:** the notice on the wall, a pale rectangle with the governor's red
seal.

### 3 · Zhang Fei's farm and the peach garden · pp 5–7, 28
A farmhouse of rammed earth with a courtyard, a fire, three men talking by
it at night; behind it a walled garden of old peach trees in full bloom.
Night by firelight; morning light through blossom, petals falling. The
place says: this is where they belong to each other.
**Accent:** falling peach petals. They return in page 28 (the letter read
under the trees, now in leaf) and nowhere else, except in memory on page 47.

### 4 · The two hills and the valley · pp 8–9
A bare ridge with five hundred farmers on it; below, a dust-yellow plain
filled with the rebel army; two scrubby hills framing a narrow valley.
Hard noon. The place says: they are very few.
**Accent:** the two hills; the reader sees on page 8 where the brothers will
come from.

### 5 · The grass plain on fire · pp 10–12
A plain of tall dry grass and a rebel camp of tents; at night, the whole
plain burning, black smoke, and dark riders against the fire. The place
says: someone cleverer got here first.
**Accent:** Cao Cao's black-and-indigo banners against orange flame.

### 6 · The broken imperial camp · pp 13–14
A hillside of trampled banners, a circle of yellow headbands around gilded
horsemen; then the general's tent with its flaps and guards. Late afternoon,
long shadows. The place says: the empire's armies are losing.
**Accent:** Dong Zhuo's gilded armor, the only gold on the field.

### 7 · The capital and the palace · pp 16–24
A walled city of grey tile roofs; the palace on a high stone platform with a
long stair; the throne hall of red lacquer columns, black floors, and a
vast gold throne; a banquet hall with low tables; Dong Zhuo's bedchamber with
a couch, silk curtains, and a bronze mirror on a stand; the palace stable.
Brazier light, smoke, deep shadow between columns. The place says: this
room was built for an emperor and a fat man is eating in it.
**Accent:** the empty-looking vastness of the throne around a small boy.

### 8 · The farmhouse and the road · pp 25–27
A modest farm compound at dusk, a gate, a courtyard, a kitchen with a
cleaver and a whetstone; the road outside through yellow fields, a donkey,
willow trees. Dusk, then pitch dark, then grey dawn, then morning on the
road. The place says: a kind house.
**Accent:** the pig on the kitchen table; then the wine jar on the road.

### 9 · The coalition camp before Hulao Gate · pp 29–35, 38–39, 42, 46–47
A valley floor of tents to the horizon, eighteen kinds of banners, cooking
fires. The great tent: a long table, lords in bronze and silk seated by
rank, Yuan Shao at the head, the brothers standing at the back by the
entrance. The brothers' small tent at the camp's edge. Daylight through
canvas; firelight at night. The place says: everybody here is counting who
sits where.
**Accent:** Yuan Shao's great gold command banner; in the tent, the cup of
hot wine.

### 10 · Hulao Gate · pp 33 (far), 36–37, 40–45, 48
A fortress gate in a mountain pass: a massive gate tower, thick grey walls
running up the slopes on either side, heavy studded doors, Dong Zhuo's purple
banners along the battlements. Before it, a dusty plain where the armies
meet. Hard daylight for the duel; dusk and moonlight after; cold dawn for
the last page. The place says: you cannot get past this.
**Accent:** Lü Bu's pheasant plumes on the battlements, visible from below.
