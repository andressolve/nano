## 1. BIRTH & ORIGINS

- Born **10 July 1856** (some older sources say "night of 9–10 July"), in **Smiljan**.
- Smiljan was then in the **Military Frontier of the Austrian Empire**; the village is **now in Croatia**. (Tesla was an ethnic Serb born in what is today Croatia — get this framing exactly right; he is Serbian by ethnicity, not Croatian.)
- **Ethnicity: Serbian** (family of ethnic Serbs). Eastern Orthodox.
- **Father: Milutin Tesla (1819–1879)**, a **priest of the Eastern Orthodox Church**. Wanted Nikola to enter the priesthood.
- **Mother: Georgina "Đuka" Mandić (1822–1892)**. Her own father was also an Orthodox priest.
  - Had **"a talent for making home craft tools and mechanical appliances"** — i.e., an uncredited household-device inventor. VERIFIED in this general form; specific named gadgets are mostly later embellishment, so describe her as "a maker of home tools and appliances" rather than crediting a specific invention.
  - **Had never received a formal education** (functionally illiterate is the common framing; Wikipedia says "never received a formal education" — safest wording: "self-taught, unschooled").
  - **Prodigious memory:** could memorize long Serbian epic poems. Tesla **credited his eidetic memory and creative abilities to his mother**. This is a strong, well-attested spine beat (genius traced to an unschooled mother).
- **Fourth of five children.**
- **Brother Dane (Daniel):** older, regarded as brilliant; **died ~1863 when Nikola was about five (Dane ~12)**, from injuries connected to a **horse** (commonly told as a riding fall / being thrown). The exact mechanism is told inconsistently across sources (spooked/reared horse). Dane's death haunted Tesla and is often cited as a source of his lifelong anxiety and his drive to live up to the lost brilliant brother. **[Treat the precise accident mechanics as semi-legendary; the death, the rough age, and the lasting psychological effect are solid.]**
- **Priesthood expectation → cholera bargain:** Father intended Nikola for the priesthood. Around 1873, after finishing school, Tesla contracted **cholera, was bedridden ~9 months, near death several times**. His father — to give him will to live — **promised to send him to the best engineering school if he recovered**. This is the pivotal "deal" beat. Well-attested (appears in Tesla's autobiography), though as Tesla's own retelling it carries some self-dramatization; present as Tesla's account.

SOURCE: Wikipedia "Nikola Tesla"; Tesla Science Center (teslasciencecenter.org/pivotalmoments/birth); Tesla Universe timeline 1863; Britannica.

## 2. EDUCATION (Graz Polytechnic)

- Enrolled at the **Imperial-Royal Technical College (Polytechnic), Graz, Austria, in 1875** (electrical/technical studies).
- **First year:** exceptional — never missed a lecture, **passed nine exams** (nearly twice the required number), highest grades. Solid spine beat for "the prodigy."
- **The Poeschl / Gramme dynamo argument:** In his second year, Tesla clashed with **Professor [Jacob] Poeschl** during a demonstration of a **Gramme dynamo** (a DC machine that could run as a motor). Tesla argued the **commutator and its sparking brushes were unnecessary** and that the machine could be run on **alternating current** without a commutator. Poeschl publicly ridiculed the idea, reportedly devoting the rest of the lecture to mocking it / declaring it a perpetual-motion-type impossibility. **VERIFIED in outline** across Tesla biography sources, BUT the scene comes largely from **Tesla's own later recollection**, so treat the exact dialogue as Tesla's memory, not documented transcript. The *substance* (he intuited commutator-free AC at Graz) is widely accepted.
- **He did NOT graduate.** He **left Graz in December 1878** without a degree. In the second year he **lost his scholarship and fell into gambling** (cards/billiards); did not sit/receive grades for the final period. One biographer suggests he may even have been **expelled for gambling and womanizing** — **[DISPUTED/EXAGGERATED:** the "expelled" version is one biographer's claim; the safe, verified statement is "he lost his scholarship, gambled heavily, and left without graduating."]**
- After Graz he drifted, later worked in **Budapest (1881, telephone exchange)** and **Paris (Continental Edison)** before America.

SOURCE: Wikipedia "Nikola Tesla"; Tesla Universe timeline 1875; Tesla Science Center "College Years"; Historydraft (Poeschl episode).

## 3. THE ROTATING MAGNETIC FIELD EPIPHANY (Budapest, 1882)

- **Year: February 1882** (late Feb), **Budapest city park**, at **sunset**, walking with his friend **Antal Szigeti (Anthony Szigety)**.
- Tesla, exhausted from months of obsessing over the motor problem (had suffered a near-breakdown), was **reciting Goethe's _Faust_** when the **complete vision of the rotating magnetic field / polyphase AC induction motor** came to him.
- **The insight:** use **multiple alternating currents out of phase** (polyphase) to create a **rotating magnetic field** that drags the rotor around — **no commutator, no brushes, no sparking** (the very thing he'd argued at Graz). He reportedly **drew the design in the dirt/sand with a stick** to show Szigeti. **[The stick-in-the-sand detail is widely repeated and plausibly from Tesla's autobiography, but treat as Tesla's own dramatic retelling — semi-legendary in its vividness.]**
- **The Faust lines** he recalled reciting (from the closing of Part I, Scene 2, "Before the Gate" / Bayard Taylor translation):
  > "The glow retreats, done is the day of toil;
  > It yonder hastes, new fields of life exploring;
  > Ah, that no wing can lift me from the soil,
  > Upon its track to follow, follow soaring!"
  Use the first two lines as the on-page quote; they are the canonical pair tied to the epiphany.
- **NOTE on timeline:** the idea (1882) preceded the working patents by **six years** (US patents 1888). The motor was *conceived* in Budapest but *built/patented* later in America. Don't let the page imply he built it on the spot.

SOURCE: Tesla Universe "The Man Who Invented Tomorrow"; Explora (rotating magnetic field); Tesla Science Center "Rotating Field Revelation"; Tesla Society. (Faust lines: Goethe _Faust_ Pt I, Bayard Taylor trans.)

## 4. COMING TO AMERICA & EDISON (1884)

- **Arrived New York June 1884**, nearly penniless — popularly **"four cents in his pocket and a book of poetry"** (charming detail, treat as the standard anecdote).
- **Batchelor letter of introduction to Edison** — the famous line: **"I know two great men and you are one of them; the other is this young man."** **[DISPUTED / LIKELY APOCRYPHAL.]** It traces to Tesla's first biographer **John J. O'Neill**, not a surviving document; **its actual existence is disputed.** Charles Batchelor (Edison's associate, Tesla's Paris boss) plausibly *did* send a recommendation, but the quoted wording should be presented as legend, not fact. **Do not state it as documented.**
- Worked at the **Edison Machine Works** improving DC dynamos.
- **The $50,000 promise:** **[DISPUTED — careful here.]** In **Tesla's own autobiography**, the **manager of the Machine Works (Batchelor)** offered a **$50,000 bonus** to redesign ~24 standard machines; when Tesla finished, he was told **"it turned out to be a practical joke."** The version where **Edison himself** says **"Tesla, you don't understand our American humor"** is a **later embellishment** — the line is **not documented from Edison** and the "Edison said it" framing is popular myth grafted onto Tesla's manager-told story. Historians also note the $50,000 figure is implausible (the cash-strapped firm/stingy Batchelor wouldn't have had it). **Recommended on-page handling: tell it as Tesla's account of a broken bonus promise; if you use the "American humor" quip, label it as legend or attribute it to Tesla's telling, not to a verified Edison statement.**
- **Resignation: 1885.** Tesla resigned (over the bonus dispute and/or shelved arc-lighting work — the precise trigger is unclear) and briefly dug ditches before finding backers.

SOURCE: Wikipedia "Nikola Tesla" ($50k bonus = autobiography, manager not Edison); Tesla Universe "Man Who Invented Tomorrow"; David J. Kent (Batchelor letter dispute); O'Neill biography (origin of the "two great men" quote).

## 5. THE WAR OF THE CURRENTS (AC vs DC)

- The fight: **Edison's DC** vs. **Westinghouse/Tesla AC**, ~late 1880s–early 1890s. AC won (it transmits efficiently over long distances; DC doesn't).
- **1888:** Tesla's **polyphase AC patents licensed to George Westinghouse / Westinghouse Electric.** (See cluster 6 for the deal terms — sources conflict.)
- **Edison's smear campaign — VERIFIED in part:** Edison's camp promoted AC as deadly, staged **public electrocutions of stray animals (dogs, a horse)** to dramatize AC danger, and Edison-aligned figures (notably **Harold P. Brown**, secretly backed by Edison) pushed AC for the **first electric chair**. **William Kemmler** (note: commonly misspelled "Kimmler") was **the first person executed by electric chair, 6 Aug 1890**, using a **Westinghouse AC generator** — a deliberate attempt to brand AC as "the killing current." Westinghouse opposed and funded Kemmler's appeal.
- **TOPSY THE ELEPHANT — COMMON ERROR, FLAG HARD.** **[MYTH / WRONG ERA.]** Topsy was electrocuted at Luna Park, Coney Island, **4 January 1903 — about a DECADE AFTER the War of the Currents ended.** Per the **Rutgers Edison Papers**, **Edison had nothing to do with the decision and took no part**; he was no longer in the electricity business (his company had merged into General Electric in 1892). Edison's film company merely *filmed* the event as a newsreel. **Do NOT depict Topsy as Edison's anti-Tesla/anti-AC stunt — that is the single most common Tesla-myth error.** Tesla and Westinghouse had no connection to Topsy at all.

SOURCE: Wikipedia "War of the currents"; Rutgers Edison Papers "Myth Buster — Topsy the Elephant"; Topsy (elephant) Wikipedia.

## 6. THE TORN-UP CONTRACT (moral hinge of the book)

- **The deal (1888) — SOURCES CONFLICT on exact terms; flag this:**
  - Most-cited version: Westinghouse paid roughly **$60,000** (cash + ~150 shares of stock) **plus a royalty of $2.50 per horsepower** of AC capacity sold. The **$2.50/hp royalty** is the famous figure and the one that, compounding, could have made Tesla extraordinarily rich.
  - A competing popular version (e.g., the Tesla Universe article) says **"$1,000,000 cash for the patent rights"** — this is **less reliable / likely conflates later payments**. **Use the $60,000 + $2.50/hp-royalty framing; note the $1M figure as a circulating discrepancy.**
- **The torn-up contract (~1890–1891):** During the **financial panic / War-of-Currents cash drain**, Westinghouse Electric was near collapse; **bankers demanded the Tesla royalty clause be scrapped** as a condition of refinancing. Westinghouse personally appealed to Tesla. **Tesla tore up the royalty contract**, reportedly saying words to the effect of **"you will save your company"** (and that Westinghouse had believed in him when no one else would). He **forgave/relinquished the royalties** to save the company. This is the GENEROSITY beat — and it is **broadly accepted as true in outline**, though:
  - **[FLAG: the exact dialogue and the dramatic "tore it to pieces in the office" staging come from biographical retellings, not a verified transcript.]** The *act* (he gave up his royalties to save Westinghouse) is well-supported; the theatrical details are biographer color.
- **"Richest man alive / world's first billionaire" framing — [EXAGGERATED].** This is a popular extrapolation: *if* the $2.50/hp royalty had run on all the AC capacity later installed, it would have totaled an enormous (some say billions, in later dollars) sum. It's a **hypothetical**, not a fortune he actually held and walked away from. **Honest framing: "he gave up a royalty that could have made him one of the richest men in the world,"** NOT "he was the richest man alive and tore it up." Keep the conditional.

SOURCE: Celebrity Net Worth / Arden League (popular torn-contract retellings); History of Applied Science & Technology (Rebus) ch.12; Tesla Universe timeline 1888. **Note the $60k+royalty vs $1M discrepancy across sources.**

## 7. NIAGARA FALLS & THE VINDICATION OF AC

- **1893 World's Columbian Exposition (Chicago World's Fair):** Westinghouse (using Tesla's polyphase AC) **won the contract to light the fair** — a massive public showcase of AC. (Widely cited; the fair ran 1893.)
- **Niagara Falls hydroelectric plant (Adams Power Plant):**
  - **1893:** Westinghouse/Tesla awarded the Niagara contract (to build generators using Tesla's polyphase system).
  - **First large generator tested ~April 1895.**
  - **16 November 1896:** power transmitted **~22 miles to Buffalo, NY** — the system went live. (First large-scale AC hydroelectric power transmission.)
  - Generated on the order of **15,000 horsepower** initially.
- This is the **VINDICATION** beat: the rotating-field idea from a Budapest park (1882) now powering a city from a waterfall. Strong arc closure.

SOURCE: Tesla Society "Niagara Falls Power Plant (1895)"; Legal Legacy (16 Nov 1896 transmission to Buffalo); David J. Kent (Niagara); EBSCO research-starter.

## 8. COLORADO SPRINGS (1899–1900)

- **Experimental station, Colorado Springs**, occupied roughly **mid-May/June 1899 to ~7 January 1900** (chose Colorado for its dry air, altitude, and frequent natural lightning).
- **Magnifying transmitter:** a giant resonant Tesla coil; the secondary coil was **~52 feet in diameter**, with a **~142-foot mast/aerial** topped by a copper-clad ball.
- **Artificial lightning:** produced discharges of **millions of volts**; the **largest bolt reported ~130–135 feet long**, thunder allegedly heard ~15 miles away. **[Use "over 100 feet" / "about 130 feet" — the exact figure varies by source; ~135 ft is the high end. Some of the most spectacular numbers are Tesla's own promotional claims.]**
- **The famous "lightning" photograph — IMPORTANT, FLAG.** The iconic image of Tesla **sitting calmly in a chair reading while giant bolts crackle around him is a STAGED DOUBLE EXPOSURE**, made by photographer **Dickenson Alley for _The Century Magazine_**. **Tesla himself acknowledged the trickery** (the coil was sparked separately and the seated Tesla composited in). **Do not present it as a real simultaneous moment — it's a deliberate publicity composite.** (Great for the book *if* labeled as a famous staged photo, which fits the "showman" thread.)
- **Wireless power dream:** Colorado Springs is where Tesla convinced himself he could transmit **power wirelessly through the earth** — the dream he carried into Wardenclyffe. He also recorded signals he believed might be extraterrestrial (almost certainly natural/terrestrial noise — **[don't lean into the "aliens" angle]**).

SOURCE: Tesla Experimental Station (Wikipedia); Open Tesla Research "Colorado Springs Lab 1899-1900"; Public Domain Review (the photo); History Colorado "The Wizard in the Mountains."

## 9. WARDENCLYFFE (1901–1917) — THE GREAT FAILURE

- **Wardenclyffe Tower (a.k.a. Tesla Tower)**, **Shoreham, Long Island, New York**, built **1901–1902**.
- **Funded by J.P. Morgan: ~$150,000** (verified figure; Morgan got a majority stake in Tesla's patents in return). **Far below what the project actually needed** — chronic underfunding doomed it.
- **The dream:** the "**World Wireless System**" — global wireless transmission of **BOTH information AND power**.
- **Marconi beat him on radio:** **Guglielmo Marconi achieved the first transatlantic radio transmission in December 1901**, using simpler, cheaper equipment — undercutting Tesla's rationale and Morgan's confidence. (Note: Tesla had pitched Wardenclyffe partly as transatlantic *messaging*; Marconi's success deflated that selling point.)
- **Morgan withdrew further funding** (~1903–1906); Morgan grew skeptical of commercial viability and the slow, escalating cost. The work effectively **ended by 1906**; tower never became fully operational.
- **"If anyone can draw on the power, where do we put the meter?"** — attributed to **J.P. Morgan** as his reason for killing wireless *power*. **[DISPUTED / LIKELY APOCRYPHAL.]** Widely repeated (even by PBS American Experience) but **not documented as Morgan's actual words**, and historians note Tesla hadn't even clearly told Morgan the tower would broadcast free power. **Use only as legend, or as a thematic line attributed to "the logic of his backers," not a verified Morgan quote.** (It does capture the real business tension — un-meterable free power has no profit model.)
- **Demolition: 1917** — the tower was **demolished/dynamited for scrap** (to pay Tesla's debts, partly under wartime security concerns about its possible use). This is the FAILURE low-point of the arc.

SOURCE: Wardenclyffe Tower (Wikipedia); Tesla Science Center "The Tower" / "Tesla's Wireless Power"; PBS American Experience (Morgan-meter quote, as folklore).

## 10. LATER LIFE & DEATH

- Declining finances; lived for decades in **New York hotels** (Waldorf-Astoria, St. Regis, Governor Clinton, and finally the **Hotel New Yorker, 1934–1943**).
- **Died there in Room 3327 of the Hotel New Yorker.** **Death: 7 January 1943, age 86, alone**, cause **coronary thrombosis**. Effectively **broke / in debt** (Westinghouse had quietly paid some of his hotel bills in later years).
- **Pigeons:** Tesla famously **fed and loved pigeons** in NYC parks (Bryant Park, St. Patrick's steps), kept some in his rooms, nursed injured ones.
  - **The white pigeon:** he said of a particular white female pigeon, **"I loved that pigeon as a man loves a woman, and she loved me."** He described her death (in a vision, ~1922) with light beaming from her eyes and felt his life's work was finished. **VERIFIED as Tesla's own words** (from interviews/biographies; this is one of the most poignant authentic beats). Strong, real, usable.
- **"Death ray" / "Teleforce":** **[LATER-LIFE, CONTESTED — flag clearly.]** In **1934** Tesla announced a **particle-beam "teleforce" weapon** ("death ray") he claimed could destroy fleets of aircraft. **No working device was ever built or demonstrated**; it lived in press releases and his birthday announcements. Present as an unbuilt claim of his eccentric final years, NOT an invention.
- **Birthday press conferences:** in his final decade he held annual birthday press events making grand, often unsubstantiated announcements — part eccentric genius, part attention-seeking. (Useful for the "lonely showman" texture.)
- **FBI / government seizure of papers:** After his death, the **U.S. Office of Alien Property Custodian seized his papers and effects** (fear of "death ray" secrets reaching enemies). **John G. Trump (MIT engineer, uncle of Donald Trump)** examined them over ~3 days and concluded they were **largely speculative/promotional, no workable secret weapon**. **VERIFIED.** (The FBI's own files were later released; the "FBI seized everything and hid the secrets" version is partly mythologized — the review found nothing actionable.)

SOURCE: Tesla Society "Remembering Tesla's Death"; Tesla Universe "Hotel New Yorker"; All That's Interesting; Wikipedia (teleforce, Trump examination). White-pigeon quote: Tesla via O'Neill / contemporaneous interviews.

## 11. POSTHUMOUS VINDICATION

- **SI unit "tesla" (symbol T)** for **magnetic flux density** — adopted at the **General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) in 1960**, named in his honor (proposed by Slovenian engineer France Avčin). 1 tesla = 1 weber/m². **VERIFIED.**
- **1943 Supreme Court radio-patent ruling** — **[COMMONLY OVERSTATED — flag.]** *Marconi Wireless Telegraph Co. of America v. United States*, **320 U.S. 1 (1943)**, decided **June 1943** (months after Tesla's death). The Court **invalidated key broad claims of Marconi's radio patent**, finding them **anticipated by prior patents — primarily Oliver Lodge and John Stone Stone, and ALSO Tesla.** **It did NOT rule "Tesla invented radio."** The case was about **patent priority/government compensation for WWI-era use**, and it left Marconi's reputation as a practical radio pioneer intact. **Honest framing: "a 1943 Supreme Court ruling restored priority for some of Tesla's early wireless patents," NOT "the Supreme Court declared Tesla invented radio."** The "Tesla invented radio, the court proved it" line is the popular misreading.
- **Modern namesakes:** the **Tesla automobile company** (named in his honor, not founded by him), **Tesla Science Center at Wardenclyffe** (museum on the original site), Belgrade airport, the **Nikola Tesla Museum (Belgrade)** holding his ashes & papers, his face on Serbian currency, etc.

SOURCE: Tesla (unit) Wikipedia / NIST "What's in a Name? The Tesla"; *Marconi v. United States* 320 U.S. 1 (Justia / Wikisource); "Misreading the Supreme Court" (Mercurians).

## 12. QUOTES

### Likely-GENUINE (usable, with sources)

1. **"Let the future tell the truth, and evaluate each one according to his work and accomplishments. The present is theirs; the future, for which I really worked, is mine."**
   — Sourced to the **Dragislav Petković interview, _Politika_, April 1927**; reproduced in Cheney's biographies. The two famous lines ("Let the future tell the truth…" and "The present is theirs; the future… is mine") are **the same quote** — Tesla said them together about the radio-invention controversy. **STRONGEST genuine spine quote** (resilience/vindication). VERIFIED.

2. **"I love that pigeon as a man loves a woman, and she loved me."** (about the white pigeon) — Tesla's own words via biographers (O'Neill, _Prodigal Genius_). Authentic, poignant. (Cluster 10.)

3. **"The scientists of today think deeply instead of clearly. One must be sane to think clearly, but one can think deeply and be quite insane."** — attributed to a 1934 _Modern Mechanix_ interview. Reasonably attested.

4. **"Our virtues and our failings are inseparable, like force and matter. When they separate, man is no more."** — from Tesla's writings/_My Inventions_ era. Attested in quote collections sourced to Tesla.

5. **"I do not think there is any thrill that can go through the human heart like that felt by the inventor as he sees some creation of the brain unfolding to success…"** — from Tesla's autobiographical writing. Attested.

6. **"If [Edison] had a needle to find in a haystack… I was a sorry witness of such doings, knowing that a little theory and calculation would have saved him ninety percent of his labor."** — Tesla on Edison, from his own published reminiscence (New York Times, 1931, on Edison's death). VERIFIED in substance — useful for the Edison-contrast pages.

7. **Goethe _Faust_ lines** (recited at the 1882 epiphany — see cluster 3): *"The glow retreats, done is the day of toil; / It yonder hastes, new fields of life exploring…"* (Goethe, not Tesla, but central to the scene.)

> For #3–#6, prefer pulling exact wording from **Wikiquote "Nikola Tesla" (sourced section)** or Cheney's _Tesla: Man Out of Time_ before lettering them in-image.

### MISATTRIBUTED / DUBIOUS — DO NOT USE as Tesla

- **"If your hate could be turned into electricity, it would light up the whole world."** — **DUBIOUS.** Hugely circulated online; **no primary source.** Tone/diction unlike his documented writing. The brief specifically flagged this — **avoid.**
- **"If you want to find the secrets of the universe, think in terms of energy, frequency and vibration."** — **MISATTRIBUTED.** No Tesla source; a modern internet invention. Avoid.
- **"The day science begins to study non-physical phenomena, it will make more progress in one decade than in all the previous centuries…"** — **MISATTRIBUTED / unsourced.** Avoid.
- **"Everything is energy and that's all there is to it…"** and the **"Earth is a realm, it is not a planet…"** passage — **FABRICATED**, not Tesla. Avoid.
- The general rule: **online Tesla quote-images are a swamp.** Only letter a quote into a page if it traces to Wikiquote's *sourced* section, the 1927 Politika interview, _My Inventions_, or a cited Cheney/O'Neill passage.

SOURCE: Wikiquote "Nikola Tesla" (sourced vs. misattributed sections); _Politika_ 1927 interview via Cheney; flatearth.ws & metaphysicalhub (debunking the "realm"/"frequency-vibration" fakes).

## 13. PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION (for the illustrator)

- **Height: tall — 6 ft 2 in (1.88 m)** is the verified/most-cited figure. **[The "6 ft 6" you sometimes see is EXAGGERATED; use ~6'2"–6'3".]** Effect: he towered over most contemporaries.
- **Build: very thin / slender / erect.** ~**142 lb (64 kg)**. Long limbs, long thin hands and fingers (he was self-conscious about/proud of his hands).
- **Hair: dark (black, later graying/white), parted precisely in the MIDDLE, combed back flat** — every hair in place; fastidious grooming.
- **Mustache:** neat dark mustache through his prime (war-of-currents/fame era). In old age, clean-shaven or very sparse.
- **Eyes:** described variously as **pale/light — deep-set grey or pale blue**, often noted as intense/piercing. **[Accounts conflict grey vs. blue; "pale, deep-set, piercing light eyes" is the safe rendering.]**
- **Attire:** **immaculate dark formal dress** — frock coats/dark suits, high collars, sometimes **grey silk gloves and a walking stick/cane**; dandyish, meticulous, conservative. Always formal even in the lab.
- **Demeanor:** ramrod-straight posture; precise, formal, fastidious (later: germophobic, counted in threes, avoided handshakes). Useful body-language notes for the artist.

### Age phases for the book:
- **~26 (1882, Budapest / 1884 arrival):** young, lean, **dark hair + dark mustache**, intense eyes, threadbare-but-neat clothing on arrival; the hungry-immigrant-genius look.
- **~35–40 (1888–1896, War of Currents / fame / Niagara):** peak "wizard" image — impeccable dark formal suit, center-parted dark hair, trim mustache, upright and magnetic; the celebrated showman-inventor.
- **~43 (1899–1900, Colorado Springs):** still dark-haired, mustached, tall and gaunt; lab setting, electrical apparatus; the staged-lightning-photo era.
- **~80s (1930s–1943, Hotel New Yorker / pigeons):** **old, very gaunt, hollow-cheeked, white/thin hair, clean-shaven or wispy**, deeply lined, often in a worn-but-still-formal dark suit; frail, feeding pigeons; the lonely-twilight register.

SOURCE: Wikipedia "Nikola Tesla" (height/weight); Tesla Universe "Youth and Strength at 78"; contemporary press descriptions; Cheney biographies. (Cross-check exact look against period photographs — many are public domain.)

## DISPUTED / MYTHS TO AVOID (master checklist)

1. **TOPSY THE ELEPHANT (1903) was NOT part of the War of the Currents and NOT Edison's anti-Tesla/anti-AC stunt.** Wrong decade, Edison uninvolved, Tesla/Westinghouse unconnected. **The #1 error in pop-Tesla retellings — do not depict it in the AC/DC fight.**
2. **"Tesla invented radio / the Supreme Court proved it" — OVERSTATED.** The 1943 ruling (320 U.S. 1) was a narrow patent-priority decision invalidating broad Marconi claims, crediting Lodge, Stone, *and* Tesla — not a coronation of Tesla as radio's inventor.
3. **"He tore up the contract that made him the richest man ALIVE" — EXAGGERATED conditional.** He relinquished a **royalty that *could have* made him among the world's richest** — a hypothetical, not a held fortune. Keep it conditional. (The generous act itself is real.)
4. **Batchelor's "I know two great men…" letter — LIKELY APOCRYPHAL** (traces to O'Neill, no surviving document). Don't state as fact.
5. **Edison's "you don't understand our American humor" / the $50,000 — from Tesla's autobiography, and the joke was told by his MANAGER, not documented from Edison.** Don't put the quip in Edison's mouth as verified history.
6. **"Where do we put the meter?" (J.P. Morgan) — undocumented / likely apocryphal.** Use only as thematic folklore.
7. **The Colorado Springs lightning photo is a STAGED DOUBLE EXPOSURE** Tesla admitted to — not a real captured instant.
8. **Lightning-bolt length ~130–135 ft is the high end and partly Tesla's own promotion** — say "over 100 feet," don't over-claim.
9. **Height "6'6\"" — EXAGGERATED; use ~6'2".**
10. **"Death ray"/teleforce (1934) was never built or demonstrated** — an announced claim of his eccentric final years, not an invention.
11. **The fake inspirational quotes** ("energy/frequency/vibration," "if your hate could be turned into electricity," "Earth is a realm," "non-physical phenomena") — all **NOT Tesla.** Avoid entirely.
12. **Mother as illiterate "inventor":** real in spirit (unschooled maker of home devices, prodigious memory) but **don't credit her with specific named patented inventions** — that's embellishment.
13. **"Expelled from Graz":** one biographer's claim; the verified statement is "lost scholarship, gambled, left without a degree."
14. **Dane's death mechanics** (the exact horse accident) are told inconsistently — keep it general ("a fall involving a horse"); the death + lasting effect are solid.
