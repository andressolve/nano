# 00 — PROJECT BRIEF

## Title
**Tesla**
*Subtitle:* **The Man Who Gave the World Its Power**

(Double meaning is intentional: he gave the world electrical *power*, and he gave away the *power*/fortune that was rightfully his.)

## One-sentence window
This bio covers Nikola Tesla from his birth in a mountain village in 1856 to his lonely death in a New York hotel room in 1943 — and the 1960 moment the world wrote his name into the language of science.

## Format
Biographical graphic novel, **biographical mode**: 3:2 landscape (1536×1024), oil-painting realism, `gpt-image-2` standard. Dark-theme page-flipping reader + 5-question WHY-quiz. Matches the register of Newton / Gauss / da Vinci / Honda.

## Page count
**18 pages + cover = 19 images.** Stop when the arc lands; expand a beat into two pages rather than crowd captions.

## The human spine (NOT the intellectual spine)
*The immigrant genius who gave the world its electricity, tore up the contract that could have made him one of the richest men alive, chased a bigger dream and lost everything, and died broke in a hotel room feeding pigeons — but the future he worked for came true.*

The arc is **GENEROSITY → over-reach → FAILURE → VINDICATION**, not a parade of inventions. The single technical idea (alternating current / the rotating magnetic field) is the one thing the reader must understand, and it is **concentrated in one two-page block (P5–P6)**, not threaded through every page. Everything else is human: the cholera bargain, the man who believed in him (Westinghouse), the torn contract, the great tower that failed, the white pigeon, the empty room.

The moral hinge is **P10 — the torn-up royalty contract.** Design the book around it.

## Structure & deliberate ordering (documented per playbook §11)
**Frame device:** the book opens (P1) and closes (P17) in **Room 3327 of the Hotel New Yorker, 1943**, with old Tesla and the white pigeon. The life is told as the story he is remembering.

**Chronology note — deliberate:** Tesla's great *triumph* (Niagara, 1896, P11) chronologically precedes his great *failure* (Wardenclyffe, 1901–1917, P13–P14). This is the true shape of his life — he won the war that mattered, then over-reached and lost. We do NOT reorder to force a tidy rise-then-fall; instead the **montage finale (P18)** carries the final, lasting vindication (the world his AC built + the SI unit "tesla," 1960). The reader feels: the failure was real, but the victory was permanent.

## Page map
- **P1** — Frame open. 1943, Room 3327, old gaunt Tesla feeds a white pigeon at the window. The paradox stated. *(cinematic, T4)*
- **P2** — Smiljan, 1856. Mother Đuka, the unschooled maker of home tools with a prodigious memory ("his genius, he always said, came from her"); brother Dane's death shadows the family. *(cinematic, T4)*
- **P3** — The cholera bargain (~1873). The priest-father who wanted him for the Church promises the best engineering school if the dying boy lives. *(cinematic, T4)*
- **P4** — Graz, 1875. The Gramme-dynamo argument with Professor Poeschl: the student who said the sparking commutator was *unnecessary* — that a machine could run on alternating current. The class laughs. *(cinematic, T4)*
- **P5** — **HERO/BREAKTHROUGH.** Budapest park, sunset, February 1882. Reciting Goethe's *Faust*, the rotating-magnetic-field vision arrives; he draws it in the dirt with a stick. *(cinematic + quote artifact, T5)*
- **P6** — **HERO/ANNOTATED.** What the idea *is*: AC vs DC and the rotating field that spins a motor with no brushes — the one technical page. *(annotated-breakthrough, T5)*
- **P7** — New York, 1884. Arrives with almost nothing; works for Edison on DC; the broken bonus promise (told as Tesla's account); resigns and digs ditches. *(2-char, T4)*
- **P8** — 1888. George Westinghouse believes in him and licenses the AC patents. The man who bet on him. *(partnership, 2-char, T4)*
- **P9** — The War of the Currents. Edison's camp brands AC "the killing current"; AC wins anyway because it travels far. *(cinematic, T4)*
- **P10** — **HERO/MORAL HINGE.** ~1890–91. Westinghouse near collapse; bankers demand the royalty be scrapped. Tesla tears up the contract that could have made him one of the richest men alive — because Westinghouse believed in him. *(2-char, T5)*
- **P11** — **VINDICATION 1.** Niagara Falls, 1896. The rotating field from a Budapest park now powers a city from a waterfall. *(cinematic, T5)*
- **P12** — **HERO/SPECTACLE.** Colorado Springs, 1899. The magnifying transmitter, artificial lightning over 100 feet, the famous staged photograph; the wireless-power dream begins. *(cinematic, T5)*
- **P13** — Wardenclyffe, 1901. The tower; J.P. Morgan's money; the World Wireless System — free power *and* information for the whole earth. The biggest dream. *(2-char, T4)*
- **P14** — **THE FAILURE.** Marconi beats him across the Atlantic; the money stops; the tower is dynamited in 1917. *(cinematic, T5)*
- **P15** — The lonely years. Hotels, debts, birthday press conferences, the unbuilt "death ray" claim. *(cinematic, T4)*
- **P16** — The white pigeon. "I loved that pigeon as a man loves a woman, and she loved me." *(cinematic + quote, T5)*
- **P17** — **FRAME CLOSE.** January 7, 1943. Room 3327, alone, broke. The 1927 quote: the future was his. *(cinematic + quote, T4)*
- **P18** — **CLOSING-AS-INVENTION / MONTAGE FINALE.** The rotating field as the structure of the page: the whole electrified world spinning on his idea; the unit *tesla* (1960); old Tesla and the white pigeon small at the center. *(montage, T5)*

## Key reminders (from research — do NOT violate)
- **NO Topsy the elephant** in the War of the Currents (wrong decade, Edison uninvolved). #1 pop-Tesla error.
- **Do NOT say the Supreme Court "declared Tesla invented radio."** Narrow patent-priority ruling only — and it's optional; the cleaner vindication is the *tesla* unit + the world AC built.
- **Keep the torn-contract framing conditional:** "a royalty that *could have* made him one of the richest men alive," NOT "he was the richest man alive."
- **Do NOT put "you don't understand our American humor" in Edison's mouth as fact** — tell the broken bonus as Tesla's account.
- **Morgan's "where do we put the meter?"** — use only as the *logic of his backers*, not a verified quote.
- **Colorado Springs photo is a staged double exposure** Tesla admitted to — lean into the "showman" reading, don't present it as a real instant.
- **Lightning:** "over 100 feet," don't over-claim 135 ft.
- **Height ~6'2", very thin** — towering and gaunt, NOT 6'6".
- **Only letter VERIFIED quotes** (see 04-SCRIPT lettering + research cluster 12). Avoid all the internet fake quotes.
- **Mother:** unschooled maker of home tools + prodigious memory — do NOT credit named inventions.
