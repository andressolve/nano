# 04 — SCRIPT

Verbatim captions and dialogue. The prompt copies these word-for-word; never paraphrase at generation time. Speech bubbles carry NO quotation marks (the bubble shape is the quote). Every page lists its density tier and which ref to attach as the `edit_image` `imagePath` (+ which characters are prose-locked, Strategy 1).

Lettering treatment: off-white caption boxes/bands, dark serif text. Quote artifacts in an italic period serif. Open every lettering block with `LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:` and close every prompt with the restrictions block from 01-STYLE-GUIDE.

## P1 — FRAME OPEN (1943, Room 3327) · Tier T4
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_old.png`. Prose-lock: the white pigeon (from `ref_pigeon_white`).
**Scene:** A small, plain Art-Deco hotel room high above Manhattan, winter dusk. A very old, skeletally thin man in a worn dark suit sits at the window, gently feeding a single pure-white pigeon on the sill. Cold grey city light through the glass, one warm lamp behind. Lonely, tender, hushed. NO electric blue here.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `In the winter of 1943, in a small room on the thirty-third floor of a New York hotel, an old man fed a white pigeon at the window. His name was Nikola Tesla.`
- Bottom band: `He had given the world the electricity that lights its cities and turns its machines. He had once held a contract that could have made him one of the richest men alive. He died here — alone, owing money for the room. This is the story of what he gave away, and what he was right about.`

## P2 — SMILJAN, 1856 · Tier T4

A bearded priest-father dim in the background. Wooded Austrian-frontier hills through a small window.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**

- Lower-left box: `His mother, Đuka, had never spent a day in school. Yet she built her own tools for the house from memory, and could recite long poems without missing a word. All his life, Nikola said his gift came from her.`
- Lower-right box: `When Nikola was small, his clever older brother Dane died after a fall from a horse. The shadow of the lost brother never quite left him.`

## P3 — THE CHOLERA BARGAIN (~1873) · Tier T4

**Scene:** A dim sickroom. A gaunt pale young man of about seventeen lies weak in an iron bed, Orthodox icons on the wall above. His bearded priest-father leans close at the bedside, gripping his hand, urgent and loving. A single candle's warm amber against the cold grey window.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `When Nikola was seventeen, he nearly died of cholera. For months he lay in bed, too weak to stand.`
- Speech bubble, tail to the bearded father: `If you live, you will go to the finest engineering school in the world. I promise it.`
- Bottom band: `His father had always wanted him to become a priest. But to give his dying son a reason to live, he set him free toward machines instead. Nikola lived — and he never forgot the bargain.`

## P4 — GRAZ, 1875 (the AC seed) · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_young.png`. Prose-lock: the professor (older, bearded, severe).
**Scene:** A tiered 19th-century university lecture hall, dark wood and gaslight. On the demonstration bench, a brass-and-iron dynamo machine throws small sparks from its spinning brushes. The tall lean young student stands among the seated rows, pointing at the machine; a severe older professor at the bench gestures dismissively. Tension.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `At nineteen, Nikola went to study engineering at the Polytechnic in Graz, Austria. He was the best student in the hall — and the most stubborn.`
- Speech bubble, tail to the tall young student: `Those sparks are wasted power. The machine could run without them.`
- Speech bubble, tail to the older professor: `Mr. Tesla may do many great things. This he will never do.`
- Bottom band: `The machine ran on direct current — electricity that flows one way, like water down a pipe — and it needed sparking brushes to turn. Nikola was sure the sparks could be thrown away if the current rushed back and forth instead. No one believed him. The answer would take seven more years.`

## P5 — BUDAPEST EPIPHANY (Feb 1882) · HERO · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_young.png`. Prose-lock: a friend walking beside him (generic young man, Antal Szigeti — do not name in image).
**Scene:** A formal European city park at golden sunset, February, bare trees, gravel path. The tall young man, lit orange by the low sun, has crouched to draw a circular diagram in the dirt with a stick, face alight with revelation; his friend leans in, astonished. Warm sunset gold, long shadows — the most lyrical page in the book.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `Seven years later, in February 1882, Nikola was walking through a park in Budapest at sunset, reciting a poem he loved — Goethe's Faust — when the answer struck him all at once.`
- Italic quote artifact box, mid-right: `The glow retreats, done is the day of toil; it yonder hastes, new fields of life exploring...` then smaller beneath: `— Goethe, Faust`
- Speech bubble, tail to the crouching young man: `The field itself turns! The motor will spin with no sparks at all.`
- Bottom band: `He saw it whole: not one current but several, rising and falling out of step, making a magnetic field that spins on its own and drags the motor around with it. No brushes. No sparks. He drew it in the dirt with a stick. It was the idea that would one day power the world.`

## P6 — THE IDEA, IN PLAIN SIGHT · HERO / ANNOTATED · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_coil.png` (use for the painterly apparatus register); the page is a diagram-painting, not a portrait.
**Scene:** An annotated oil-painting diagram on warm aged-paper tone. Center: a glowing painted illustration of three alternating currents (three wavy lines stepping out of phase) feeding a ring of coils that produces a SPINNING magnetic field (shown as a luminous blue whirl) turning a simple motor rotor — no brushes. Four callout caption boxes around it with thin painted leader-lines. Clean, museum-plate clarity.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Title band, top: `THE IDEA, IN PLAIN SIGHT`
- Callout 1 (upper left): `DIRECT CURRENT flows one way, like water down a pipe. A motor that uses it needs sparking brushes — and the current fades over distance.`
- Callout 2 (upper right): `ALTERNATING CURRENT rushes back and forth many times a second. It can travel hundreds of miles on thin wire without fading away.`
- Callout 3 (lower left): `Tesla sent SEVERAL alternating currents, each a step out of time with the others. Together they make a magnetic field that SPINS.`
- Callout 4 (lower right): `The spinning field drags the motor around — no brushes, no sparks. This one idea still runs the motors of the world.`
- Small footer label, bottom center: `POLYPHASE ALTERNATING CURRENT`

## P7 — AMERICA & EDISON (1884) · Tier T4
**Ref:** edit on `ref_edison.png`. Prose-lock: young Tesla (tall, lean, dark center-parted hair + mustache, immaculate threadbare dark coat).
**Scene:** A crowded gaslit machine shop full of dynamos and belts. The stocky, rumpled, clean-shaven American inventor stands among his machines; the tall elegant gaunt young Tesla beside him — a study in opposites. Warm lamp glow, cold dawn light through high windows.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `In 1884, Nikola arrived in America with almost nothing — by his own telling, four cents and a book of poetry. He went to work for the most famous inventor in the world: Thomas Edison, the champion of direct current.`
- Bottom band: `By Tesla's account, he was promised fifty thousand dollars to improve Edison's machines. He did the work — then was told the promise had only been a joke. He quit. For a time, the man who would electrify the world dug ditches for two dollars a day. The two inventors never agreed: Edison trusted direct current; Tesla knew the future was alternating.`

## P8 — WESTINGHOUSE BELIEVES (1888) · PARTNERSHIP · Tier T4
**Ref:** edit on `ref_westinghouse.png`. Prose-lock: prime Tesla (tall, gaunt-elegant, jet-black center-parted hair, trim mustache, impeccable dark three-piece suit).
**Scene:** A solid Gilded-Age industrial office, rolled drawings on the desk. The heavyset, genial, walrus-mustached industrialist clasps the tall elegant inventor's hand across the desk over the AC patent papers. Warm, decent, a deal of trust.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `But one powerful man believed him. George Westinghouse, an inventor and factory-owner, had made his fortune on the railways. In 1888 he bought the rights to Tesla's alternating-current designs.`
- Speech bubble, tail to the walrus-mustached industrialist: `Your system is the future, Mr. Tesla. We will light America with it.`
- Bottom band: `Westinghouse promised Tesla a royalty — a small fee for every unit of power his system would ever produce. If alternating current won, Tesla would grow immensely rich. First, it had to survive a war.`

## P9 — THE WAR OF THE CURRENTS · Tier T4
**Ref:** edit on `ref_edison.png` (Edison's camp). Prose-lock: scene-driven, no second locked face.
**Scene:** A dramatic split-mood street: lurid newspaper headlines and warning posters about "the killing current," a tense crowd, the cold blue of an arc-lamp overhead versus the warm yellow of old gas. Ominous, journalistic — NO animals, NO execution imagery. The clash of two technologies fought in the newspapers.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `It was called the War of the Currents. Edison fought to keep the world on his direct current. To frighten people away from alternating current, his side branded it the killing current.`
- Bottom band: `They staged grim demonstrations and warned that alternating current was too dangerous for homes. But one fact settled it: direct current faded after a mile or two, while alternating current could be carried across whole states on thin wires. The future needed power that could travel — and so the future was alternating. Tesla's current won.`

## P10 — THE TORN-UP CONTRACT (~1890–91) · HERO / MORAL HINGE · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_westinghouse.png`. Prose-lock: prime Tesla (tall, gaunt-elegant, jet-black center-parted hair, trim mustache, dark three-piece suit).
**Scene:** The same office, now in crisis — ledgers, anxious lamp-light, a window onto smokestacks. The heavyset industrialist sits heavy with worry; the tall elegant inventor stands and tears a legal contract document clean in two with his long hands, calm and certain. The torn paper is the focus.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `Then victory nearly destroyed the man who had bet on it. The long war had drained Westinghouse's company of money, and his bankers refused to save it unless one thing was cut: the royalty owed to Tesla — the fee that would have made him one of the richest men alive.`
- Speech bubble, tail to the worried industrialist: `If I pay what I owe you, the company will fail. I cannot ask this of you.`
- Speech bubble, tail to the tall inventor tearing the paper: `You believed in me when no one else would. Here is your contract. Now save your company.`
- Bottom band: `Tesla tore the contract up with his own hands, and gave away a fortune almost beyond counting — to protect the one man who had trusted him. He would never be rich again. He said he did not mind.`

## P11 — NIAGARA FALLS (1896) · VINDICATION · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_prime.png` (small confident figure on the powerhouse floor).
**Scene:** The vast Niagara powerhouse: enormous iron-and-copper Tesla polyphase generators in a row, the great falls thundering beyond tall windows, cold blue current-glow, power lines marching toward the distant lit city of Buffalo on the horizon. The tall elegant inventor stands small and proud among his giant machines. Awe and scale.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `In 1896, the idea Nikola had drawn in the dirt fourteen years earlier came roaring to life. At Niagara Falls, giant machines built on Tesla's design turned the force of the falling water into alternating current.`
- Bottom band: `It was sent twenty-two miles to light the city of Buffalo. For the first time, a great waterfall was powering a distant city. The rotating field from a Budapest park was now turning the wheels of the world — and alternating current has powered our cities ever since.`

## P12 — COLORADO SPRINGS (1899) · HERO / SPECTACLE · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_coil.png`. Prose-lock: prime Tesla as a small silhouette.
**Scene:** A great barn-like wooden laboratory on the high plains at night. The enormous magnifying transmitter hurls colossal cold electric-blue artificial lightning bolts, over a hundred feet long, arcing across the dark space and out the open roof; the tall inventor stands a tiny calm silhouette beneath them. Overwhelming, sublime, blue-white power.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `Now famous, Nikola dreamed bigger. In 1899 he built a laboratory high in the mountains at Colorado Springs to chase an astonishing idea: sending electricity through the air itself, with no wires at all.`
- Small caption box, lower left: `His machine threw man-made lightning bolts over a hundred feet long. The thunder was heard fifteen miles away.`
- Bottom band: `His most famous photograph shows him sitting calmly, reading, while the lightning crackles around him. It was a trick — a clever double exposure, taken in two parts. Nikola was a showman as well as an inventor. But the dream behind it was real: power for everyone, everywhere, free through the air.`

## P13 — WARDENCLYFFE, THE GREAT DREAM (1901) · Tier T4
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_prime.png`. Prose-lock: the tower (from `ref_wardenclyffe` — Re-Read it first), the banker J. P. Morgan (heavyset, balding, grey mustache, large bulbous nose, severe black banker's dress).

**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `To make the dream real, Nikola began his greatest project. On a field on Long Island, near New York, he built a strange tower one hundred and eighty-seven feet tall, topped with a great copper dome. He called his plan the World Wireless System.`
- Speech bubble, tail to the tall inventor: `From this tower I will send messages — and power — to the whole earth.`
- Bottom band: `The richest banker in the world, J. P. Morgan, paid one hundred and fifty thousand dollars toward it. It was Nikola's boldest dream: a single tower that could carry voices, pictures, and electricity itself — free — to every corner of the planet.`

## P14 — THE FAILURE (1901 → 1917) · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_wardenclyffe.png` (the tower is the subject). Prose-lock: small figure of the banker turning away.
**Scene:** Two moods in one cold grey painting: the dark, never-finished tower under a heavy overcast sky while the stern old banker turns his back and walks away; and, suggested at the edge, the tower wrapped in dynamite and collapsing into dust and rubble. Bleak, grey, the blue light gone.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `But the dream was too big, and the money too small. While Nikola built, a rival named Guglielmo Marconi sent a simple radio signal clear across the Atlantic Ocean in 1901 — using cheaper equipment, and beating Tesla to it.`
- Bottom band: `And Nikola's backers saw the trouble with free power: if it flowed through the air to everyone, no one could ever sell it — there would be nowhere to put a meter. The money stopped. The great tower never worked. In 1917 it was wrapped in dynamite and blown to the ground for scrap. Nikola's boldest dream ended in a heap of rubble.`

**Scene:** A dim hotel room, an aging gaunt Tesla alone among scattered papers and unpaid bills, a grey window onto the city. On the desk, a newspaper with a sensational headline about his latest announcement. Faded grandeur, quiet.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `The last years were lonely ones. The money was gone. Nikola moved from hotel to hotel, often unable to pay — his bills sometimes quietly covered by old friends.`
- Bottom band: `Once a year, on his birthday, reporters came to hear him announce grand new inventions — even a "death ray" that could stop armies from the sky. He never built it. The world that ran on his electricity had begun to forget the man who gave it to them.`

## P16 — THE WHITE PIGEON · HERO · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_old.png`. Prose-lock: the white pigeon (from `ref_pigeon_white`).
**Scene:** The hotel room, intimate and tender. The very old, gaunt, white-haired man cradles a luminous pure-white pigeon in his long bony hands at the window, his lined face soft with love. Warm lamp on him, cold grey Manhattan beyond the glass. The most tender page in the book.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band: `In those years, Nikola loved the pigeons of New York. He fed them in the parks, nursed the hurt ones in his room, and came to love one white pigeon above all.`
- Italic quote artifact box, mid-frame: `I loved that pigeon as a man loves a woman, and she loved me.`
- Bottom band: `The man who had wanted to give power to the whole world, and kept almost none of it for himself, poured his great tenderness into a small white bird. When she died, he said, he felt that his life's work was finished.`

## P18 — THE FUTURE WAS HIS · MONTAGE FINALE / CLOSING-AS-INVENTION · Tier T5
**Ref:** edit on `ref_tesla_coil.png` (the rotating-field motif anchors the center). Prose-lock: small old Tesla + white pigeon at center.
**Scene:** A single unified painterly montage radiating outward from a glowing rotating-magnetic-field motif at the center, all washed in the book's electric blue: a city lit at night, marching power lines, spinning electric motors, a great hydro dam, a simple wall socket, a factory floor — the whole electrified world turning on his one idea. At the very center, small, the gaunt old white-haired man stands with the white pigeon. Timeless, not modern-branded. Awe and benediction.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Title band, top: `THE FUTURE WAS HIS`
- Small caption, left: `Every wall socket and every electric motor still runs on his alternating current.`
- Small caption, right: `In 1960 the world named a unit of magnetism after him — the tesla. His name became a word in the language of science.`
- Small caption, lower center: `He died with nothing. He had given all of it away.`
- Bottom band: `Nikola Tesla traded a fortune for a future, and a future for a dream that failed — yet the world he believed in arrived exactly as he first saw it: in a park, at sunset, with a stick in his hand. The lights of the world are his monument.`
