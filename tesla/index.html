The Lights of the World Are His Monument

Tesla — The Man Who Gave the World Its Power

A priest's son who nearly died of fever saw a whole machine in a flash of sunset light — a current that rushes back and forth and spins a motor with no sparks at all. It became the electricity that still runs every wall socket and every motor on earth. He could have been one of the richest men alive; instead he tore up the contract to save a friend, chased an even bigger dream, and lost everything. He died alone in a hotel room, feeding a white pigeon, certain the future would prove him right. It did. One quiz stands between you and the end — it tests the WHY, not the what.

Test Your Understanding

1. Why was Tesla sure the sparking brushes on the machine could be thrown away?
Right — that was his whole insight. Instead of one current flowing one way (which needs sparking brushes), several currents rushing back and forth out of step make a field that spins on its own and drags the motor around. No brushes, no sparks.
Not quite. It wasn't magnets, polish, or oil. Tesla saw that if the current rushed back and forth — alternating — several such currents could make a magnetic field that spins by itself and turns the motor, so the sparking brushes were no longer needed at all.
2. Why did alternating current win the War of the Currents?
Right — that one fact settled it. Direct current faded after a mile or two; alternating current could be carried across whole states on thin wires. The future needed power that could travel, so the future was alternating.
Not quite. Edison never conceded, and no law decided it. It came down to distance: direct current faded after a mile or two, while alternating current could travel for miles on thin wire — so it won.
3. Why did Tesla tear up his contract with Westinghouse?
Right — this is the heart of the book. The royalty would have made Tesla one of the richest men alive, but paying it would have destroyed Westinghouse's company. To protect the man who had trusted him, Tesla gave the fortune away.
Not quite. There was no richer backer, and it wasn't anger. Westinghouse's company would fail if it paid Tesla what it owed — so to save the one man who had believed in him, Tesla tore up the contract and gave away the fortune.
4. Why did Tesla's backers stop paying for the great Wardenclyffe tower?
Right — that was the trouble with free power. If electricity flowed through the air to everyone, there was nowhere to put a meter, so no one could ever sell it. The money stopped, and the tower was never finished.
Not quite. It wasn't the dome's weight or a misspent fund. The backers saw that free wireless power could never be metered or sold — there was no way to make money from it — so they cut off the funds.
5. Why does the book say Tesla was right that the future belonged to him?
Right — he gave away the money and died owing for his room, but every wall socket and every electric motor still runs on his alternating current, and in 1960 a unit of magnetism was named the tesla. The future he worked for arrived.
Not quite. The tower was never rebuilt, and the fortune was never returned. He was right in a deeper way: he died with nothing, yet the electric world he first imagined in a park at sunset is the one we live in now.
