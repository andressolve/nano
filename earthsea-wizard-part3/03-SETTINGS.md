# Settings - Part Three

## The Ninety Isles

A tidal maze between larger lands: countless low green-brown islets, reed beds, mudbanks, narrow saltwater lanes, rope ferries, fish stakes, footbridges, and little working boats. The horizon is broken everywhere. Some rocks vanish when the tide turns. The visual pleasure is horizontal, intimate, and inhabited—the opposite of Gont's vertical solitude and Roke's monumental stone.

## Low Torning township

Ten or twenty small islets functioning as one community. Tarred timber houses on short pilings, thatch or weighted plank roofs, narrow docks, drying nets, boat sheds, smoke from low chimneys. No grand harbor, castle, stone quay, paved street, or generic medieval-European village square.

Route value: `low-torning`.

## Ged's house

A small windowless one-room house with packed earth floor, good dry thatch, simple hearth, narrow bed, worktable, peg for cloak, and space for Hoeg beneath the bench. Poor but carefully made. It begins warm and sufficient; after the Dry Land the same room becomes bound with protective signs and surrounded by cold fear.

## Pechvarry's boat shed

Open-sided timber shelter over tidal mud, half-built narrow boats, ribs like bones, adzes, pegs, tar pot, ropes, curls of pale wood. Water rises under the floor at high tide. This is the friendship setting: hands working in parallel, speech without ceremony.

## Pechvarry's house and sickroom

Low warm room with woven mats, plain bed pallet, oil lamp, bowl of cooling water, herb steam, damp cloaks, and a small carved boat near the child. The room should hold four worried adults without feeling theatrical. The child is bundled and partly obscured.

Route value: `sickroom`.

## The Dry Land

A vast shallow slope under a starless black sky, rendered in ash-grey stone and dead distance. No wind, water, plants, moon, clouds, fire, architecture, or colored light. The dead appear only as distant quiet figures. Sound feels swallowed.

The low wall is ordinary rough stones, knee- to waist-high, running beyond the frame in both directions. It is visually modest because its meaning is absolute. The living side lies beyond it; the shadow waits there.

Route value: `dry-land`.

## Pendor approach

Open western sea with no shelter. The ruined island grows slowly from haze: broken towers, flooded streets, drowned quays, collapsed masts, black rock, and seabirds giving it distance. The boat remains tiny in every wide shot.

## Pendor harbor and city

Once a rich island city, now half drowned and empty. Jagged tower shells rise from cold water; gold appears only as dull scattered glints deep among rubble and as light in dragon eyes. Do not build a clean fantasy citadel or a treasure cavern. Yevaud has coiled so long around the tallest ruin that stone and scale initially read as one silhouette.

Route value: `pendor`.

## The oath space

Not a separate chamber: the bargain happens across water between Ged's small boat and Yevaud's ruined tower. Use alternating scale—Ged close and human, Yevaud immense and still—so dialogue remains attributable without long balloon tails. The last oath panel widens until both figures fit, then the final departure returns Ged to a small silhouette on the sea.

Route value: `oath`.
