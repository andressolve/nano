# Style Guide - Expanded Earthsea Part Three

## Style block

> Bold ink-line graphic-novel art with clean, confident black linework and flat muted color. Fine etched crosshatching in copper and black skin, rough wool, tarred rope, wet wood, salt grass, broken masonry, dragon scale, sea spray, and weather, matching the finished `../earthsea-wizard-part2/pages/` artwork exactly. Serious mythic realism, cinematic staging, realistic heroic proportions. Cold maritime palette: sea-grey, slate blue, fog-white, salt-marsh green, tar-black, weathered brown wood, dull iron, and muted ochre. Low saturation throughout. Warm gold appears only in hearths, lamps, the yew staff's defensive blaze, and dragon eyes or breath. Wind, tide, rain, spray, and low island sky remain active parts of every exterior.

## Part Three palette arc

- **Ninety Isles:** softened blue-grey water, pale sky, green-brown salt grass, warm working interiors.
- **Sickroom:** close umber lamplight surrounded by encroaching cool dark.
- **Dry Land:** near-monochrome ash-grey and matte black; no stars, moon, blue magic, mist glow, or dramatic colored sky.
- **Return to life:** one hard white-gold staff flare, then exhausted dawn-grey.
- **Pendor:** iron-grey ruins, soot-black dragons, cold sea, restrained furnace-gold eyes and breath.
- **Oath:** the gold recedes; final sea panel returns to cold grey-blue, showing that Ged has won no personal safety.

## Locked character rules

- Scarred Ged has deep red-brown copper skin, straight rough black hair, sharp hawk-like features, and four pale healed claw marks on the left cheek. He is eighteen, lean, and still young.
- Ged's yew staff is plain dark wood. It does not have an orb, gemstone, forked fantasy head, decorative metal cage, or glowing runes.
- Pechvarry is a dark-brown-skinned working boatwright with rope-strong hands, short curled hair, and a weathered open face.
- Pechvarry's wife and Ioeth share the same dark-brown Archipelagan complexion. Ioeth remains bundled during the illness.
- The township witch is an older black woman in practical island clothing, without pointed hat, occult jewelry, or villain coding.
- Hoeg is a tiny brown-black otak, large-rat/tiny-cat scale.
- Yevaud is the locked iron-grey dragon reference from `../earthsea/refs/ref_yevaud.png`.
- Juvenile dragons share Yevaud's lean, long-necked, ragged-winged anatomy at a much smaller scale. They are still enormous relative to Ged's boat.

## Magic and metaphysics

- Working magic is physical and restrained: a tightened rope, briefly silvered seams, a shift in wind, a hand laid on wood.
- Ged's spirit journey is shown by leaving his body visible in the sickroom before the Dry Land panels begin. Do not duplicate speaking Geds in one physical scene.
- The Dry Land's child figure is distant and simplified, not a ghost with transparent blue glow.
- The low wall divides the Dry Land from life. Ged returns by leaping toward the living side through the waiting shadow's reach.
- The staff flare is white-gold and defensive, never a projectile beam.
- The dragon change is one dangerous continuous action: human silhouette, stretched winged transition, dragon-form Ged. Avoid a separate magical aura or costume-like transformation sequence.
- The shadow is absolute flat matte black with no face, eyes, texture, inner smoke detail, reflected light, or colored rim.

## Page construction

- 3:2 landscape, exactly 1536x1024.
- Usually three panels; four for intimate exchanges or controlled montage.
- Give the Dry Land threshold, Yevaud reveal, and final oath wide or full-width panels.
- Solid black borders and clean off-white gutters.
- Reading order must be unambiguous left-to-right, top-to-bottom.
- Caption boxes use weathered parchment and dark serif text.
- Speech bubbles use off-white fill and dark serif text.
- All lettering is baked into the generated image.
- No page should require zoom at a 1200-pixel display width.

## Lettering restrictions

Render every supplied line exactly. Spell all words correctly. Do not duplicate, omit, paraphrase, or invent text. No quotation marks inside speech bubbles. No labels, signs, page numbers, watermarks, decorative pseudo-writing, fake runes, modern objects, visible sound-effect lettering, or extra balloons unless specified.

## Dialogue blocking

- Treat attribution as scene blocking. A page must remain readable even if a tail stops slightly short.
- For A-B dialogue, stage A left and B right whenever possible.
- Define every balloon by ordinal, speaker, exact text, balloon position, speaker position, and tail endpoint.
- State exact balloon counts and explicitly identify silent characters.
- Use one visible speaker and one balloon per panel for the high-risk Yevaud exchange.
- In dragon dialogue, keep Ged and Yevaud in separate panels whenever a long tail would have to cross sea, ruins, or scale differences.
- Inspect full resolution for wrong tails, torso-pointing tails, orphan fragments, duplicate balloons, silent-character violations, and canvas drift.
- If attribution fails, regenerate the full page with revised staging. Never crop-patch a tail.
