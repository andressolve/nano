## Ged / Sparrowhawk

- Eighteen years old, lean and wiry.
- Deep red-brown copper skin; never pale, tan-white, or olive.
- Straight rough black hair, sharp brows, hawk-like nose and gaze.
- Four pale healed scars on the left cheek from beside the eye toward the jaw.
- Plain grey-beige tunic, dark trousers and boots, weathered charcoal cloak, plain yew staff.
- Carries authority uneasily. On Low Torning his posture should soften around working people; fear returns after the Dry Land.
- In the Dry Land he appears as the same person stripped of warm color, not translucent or aged.

## Hoeg

- Locked Part Two otak identity.
- Brown-black fur, rounded ears, narrow curious face, long low body.
- Large-rat/tiny-cat size. Never dog-, fox-, or cat-sized.
- Hoeg does not speak, cast magic, glow, or perform a supernatural cure. Its importance is ordinary living presence.

## Pechvarry

New solo reference required.

- Dark-brown-skinned man in his early forties.
- Stocky working build, short tightly curled black hair, close beard, broad weathered face.
- Tar- and salt-marked hands, practical brown wool and canvas, rope belt, no armor.
- Warm, direct, patient. He teaches Ged sailing without awe and asks for help as a friend, not as a worshipper.
- His grief is contained and physical: folded shoulders, hands with nothing to repair.

## Pechvarry's family and the township witch

New three-panel group reference required: `ref_low_torning_family.png`.

### Pechvarry's wife

- Dark-brown-skinned woman in her late thirties.
- Strong, practical face; dark hair wrapped in a muted blue-grey cloth.
- Plain layered island dress and wool shawl.
- Never reduced to background grief; she watches Ged clearly and understands the cost of his attempt.

### Ioeth

- Pechvarry's young son, about six.
- Dark-brown skin, short black curls, slight build.
- Healthy identity panel only: curious, serious, holding a tiny carved boat.
- In illness scenes he is bundled to the shoulders. No exposed torso, sores, medical devices, or dead-face close-up.

### Township witch

- Older black woman, late sixties.
- Cropped grey curls, deep-set observant eyes, lined face.
- Practical salt-grey clothing, herb satchel, plain wooden walking stick.
- No pointed hat, occult symbols, exotic jewelry, skulls, or green magic.

Each panel contains a portrait and a simple full-body or three-quarter view. Consistent dark Archipelagan skin tones, neutral warm-grey background, no text or labels. Use the Part Three style block.

## Head Isle-Man and Low Torning people

New group reference required: `ref_low_torning_people.png`.

- Head Isle-Man: black man in his late fifties, spare build, short grey beard, weathered dark face, dull ochre sea-cloak, carved driftwood staff that is civic rather than magical.
- Two working islanders: one dark-brown-skinned fisherwoman and one black-skinned thatcher, both middle-aged, practical clothes, dignified and unadorned.
- The crowd identity law is brown and black Archipelagans in low-saturation wool, canvas, and oilskin. No white-majority crowd drift.

Portrait inset plus full bodies, salt-weathered clothing, neutral warm-grey background, no text, labels, weapons, crowns, or wizard props. Use the Part Three style block.

## Juvenile dragons

New creature reference required: `ref_young_dragons.png`.

- Children of Yevaud, much smaller than the old dragon but each roughly longship-scale.
- Iron-black to soot-grey scale, long narrow bodies and necks, whip tails, ragged batlike wings, hooked narrow heads.
- They are lean airborne predators, not horned dinosaurs, bulky wyverns, or four-legged fire-breathing tanks.
- Individual variation should be minor: different torn wing edges, horn nubs, and grey values. They still read as one brood.
- Restrained dull-gold eyes. Fire is brief dirty orange-gold, never neon.

Neutral storm-grey background with one tiny boat silhouette for scale. No text, labels, humans, blood, or combat. Use the Part Three style block.

## Yevaud

- Reuse `ref_yevaud.png`.
- Ancient iron-grey dragon, long and lean, ragged vast wings, narrow armored head, slow furnace-gold eyes.
- Large enough to be mistaken for part of Pendor's highest tower when coiled around it.
- Speech is measured and amused. Do not give him a human grin, expressive eyebrows, or cartoon mouth shapes.
- His threat is intelligence and scale. Keep motion economical.

## The shadow

- No new reference. Use the approved shadow treatment in Part Two pages 26 and the cover.
- Absolute flat matte black, faceless, textureless, lightless.
- In the Dry Land it is scarcely shaped, but still not smoke, a demon, or a person with eyes.
- It has no balloons.
