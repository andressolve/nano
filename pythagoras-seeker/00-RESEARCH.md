# Pythagoras Vol 1 — Research Archive (early life, pre-Croton)

**Governing caveat (Walter Burkert):** "There is not a single detail in the life of Pythagoras that stands uncontradicted." No writings of Pythagoras survive; no source within ~200 years of his death quotes him; the detailed biographies (Diogenes Laërtius, Porphyry, Iamblichus) are 700–900 years later and packed with myth. **Editorial consequence: the myth-vs-reality contrast is the narrative engine of this book, not a problem to hide.**

## Source-reliability ladder
- **Near-contemporary (most reliable):** Xenophanes (~570–475 BC), Heraclitus (fl. ~500 BC), Herodotus (mid-5th c.), Empedocles. Fragments and mentions, not biographies.
- **4th c. BC, Aristotle's circle (reliable-ish):** Aristotle (lost treatise on the Pythagoreans), Aristoxenus (source for the age-40 departure), Dicaearchus.
- **Late biographies (legend unless corroborated):** Diogenes Laërtius, Porphyry, Iamblichus.

## Confidence-tagged facts

### Birth & Samos
- **ATTESTED:** Born on Samos (eastern Aegean, off the Ionian coast of Asia Minor). Father **Mnesarchus** (Herodotus, Isocrates; corroborated inside Heraclitus's hostile fragment "Pythagoras, son of Mnesarchus").
- **PROBABLE:** Mnesarchus a **gem-engraver or merchant**. Birth **c. 570 BC** — back-calculated from the age-40 departure, NOT a recorded date.
- **LEGENDARY:** Mother **Pythais**, "descended from Ancaeus founder of Samos." Late. Use as flavor only.

### Early teachers
- **PROBABLE:** **Pherecydes of Syros** — the most-named teacher; "may well be some historical foundation" (Riedweg). Associated with early soul-doctrine — consistent with Pythagorean metempsychosis.
- **LATE/DISPUTED:** **Thales** and **Anaximander** of Miletus (geographically plausible — just across the water — but only in late biographers).

### Harmony of the hammers (forge story)
- **LEGENDARY and physically false.** Earliest source Nicomachus, 1st–2nd c. AD (~700 yrs later). Legend's numbers: hammers 12, 9, 8, 6 → 2:1 octave, 3:2 fifth, 4:3 fourth, 9:8 tone.
- **Why false:** pitch of a struck solid does not scale with hammer weight (f = v/2l). String-tension version also wrong (frequency ∝ √tension).
- **What's real:** those ratios genuinely govern **string length** on a **monochord**. The durable Pythagorean idea — consonant intervals = simple whole-number ratios — is real.
- **Book framing:** present the forge as *the story people tell*; the real string experiment is a Vol 2 payoff. In Vol 1 the forge is the boyhood spark — caption it honestly as the famous tale.

### Polycrates of Samos
- **ATTESTED:** Tyrant of Samos (Herodotus Bk 3). Seized power **c. 540/538 BC**; ruled to **c. 522 BC**; crucified by the Persian satrap **Oroetes** c. 522. Made Samos a naval power; patronized the **Eupalinos tunnel** (1,036 m aqueduct bored from both ends) and the great **Heraion**.
- **TRADITION (motive is spin):** Pythagoras left to escape Polycrates' tyranny (~530 BC, age 40). Departure well-anchored; *motive* is later admirers casting him as freedom-lover. Alternative: left because overburdened by public duties.

### Egypt & the rope-stretchers
- **LEGENDARY:** Detailed Egyptian-priest itinerary (Amasis II's letters, Heliopolis→Memphis→Thebes, initiation trials). Late and "extremely dubious." An Egypt trip is plausible-and-early-rumored; the guided temple tour is late legend.
- **ATTESTED:** Egyptian surveyors used **knotted cords** pulled taut to lay out boundaries and temple foundations; the sacred **"stretching of the cord" (pedjeshes)** ceremony is attested across ~3,000 years. The math existed (Rhind Papyrus, c. 1650 BC).
- **THE 3-4-5 ROPE (the technique is geometrically valid):** a loop marked into **12 equal segments by 13 knots**, pegged into sides of **3, 4, 5** segments. Because 3²+4²=9+16=25=5², the corner between the 3-side and 4-side is a perfect 90°. A repeatable square corner from a knotted cord.
- **DEBATED — be honest:** NO surviving Egyptian text states 3-4-5, and NO direct evidence Egyptians used the 3-4-5 rope for right angles. The 3-4-5 rope-stretcher story is a 19th-c. scholarly conjecture (Moritz Cantor); skeptics (Boyer line) call it unproven. **Book framing:** stage the rope vividly; caption it as the technique surveyors *could have used* / that tradition connects to Pythagoras — NOT "the Egyptians proved the theorem." The honest "they used it but couldn't say WHY" is exactly the hook the volume needs.
- 3-4-5 is the simplest **Pythagorean triple** — one instance, used practically; not a general proof.

### Persia / Babylon
- **LEGENDARY:** Taken to Babylon after Cambyses II conquered Egypt (**525 BC**), studied with Magi/Chaldeans (astronomy, number-mysticism). Late sources; modern scholarship skeptical-to-dismissive. The 525 conquest is real and gives the legend its hook.
- **ATTESTED & crucial:** Babylonians knew "Pythagorean" triples ~1,000+ yrs before Pythagoras — **Plimpton 322** (Old Babylonian clay tablet, c. 1800 BC, base-60). **Use this for honesty:** the math attributed to Pythagoras was circulating in the ancient Near East before him; his role is synthesizer/teacher, not sole discoverer.

### Return & departure to Italy
- **PROBABLE/ATTESTED (best-anchored event after his birth):** Emigrated to **Croton (Kroton), Magna Graecia (southern Italy), c. 530 BC** (Aristoxenus: left Samos at 40). Quickly gained a large following.
- **LEGENDARY but usable:** return-to-Samos dissatisfaction; the **"semicircle" (hemikyklion)** where he taught publicly; a **cave outside the city** where he withdrew. Flag as tradition.

### Quotes — what is safe
**No verbatim quotation of Pythagoras the man is safe to attribute.** He wrote nothing. Do NOT attribute the Golden Verses or generic web quotes ("Number rules the universe," etc.).
- **Pythagorean tradition (first recorded by Aristotle) — present as *Pythagorean sayings*, not first-person:** "Abstain from beans" (κυάμων ἀπέχεσθαι); the **tetraktys** riddle ("the harmony in which the Sirens sing").
- **Real early texts ABOUT him (good caption epigraphs):**
  - **Heraclitus fr. 129:** Pythagoras "practiced inquiry (*historíē*) most of all men… a polymathy, a fraudulent art (*kakotechníē*)." (Proof he *read others' books*.)
  - **Xenophanes fr. 7 (the puppy / soul anecdote — best authentic-early window):** seeing a puppy beaten, *"Stop, don't keep hitting it — it is the soul of a man, a friend of mine; I knew it when I heard it cry out."*
- **Rule:** if Pythagoras speaks in dialogue, write original lines; reserve real quotation marks + Greek for the testimonia above, properly attributed.

### Visual details for the artist
- **Pythagoras:** no reliable ancient portrait. Default: dignified Archaic-Ionian male, **full beard, long-ish hair**, chiton + himation. Late tradition adds white robes / veiled head — distinctive but flag as non-historical.
- **6th-c. Ionian male dress:** **chiton** (light **linen** tunic, pinned at shoulders, belted; the Ionian chiton was long and "trailing" — fuller, slightly luxurious vs. plain Doric); **himation** (large **wool** mantle over the left shoulder). Sandals or barefoot.
- **Samos:** mountainous Aegean island; peak Mount Kerkis. Polycrates monuments: the **Heraion** (vast temple of Hera), the **Eupalinos tunnel**, a busy harbor with triremes/merchant ships, a gem-cutter's workshop (father). 
- **Egypt:** flat Nile floodplain after inundation; surveyors in linen kilts pulling a **taut knotted cord** with wooden pegs; the "stretching of the cord" temple-foundation ritual; pylons, columns, obelisks; papyrus math scrolls; sun-baked palette.
- **Babylon:** mud-brick **ziggurat**; flat alluvial plain; **clay tablets with cuneiform** (Plimpton-322-style number tables); Chaldean astronomer-priests on temple platforms watching a base-60 sky; glazed-brick blue for grandeur (slightly later but evocative).
