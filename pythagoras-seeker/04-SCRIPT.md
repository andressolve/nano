# Ratio: The Seeker — Script (Vol 1)

18 images: cover + 17 story pages. Captions are VERBATIM — copy word-for-word into the lettering block, never paraphrase at generation time. Each page lists: format, density tier, the ref to anchor, the composition, and the exact lettering.

**Narration voice:** a calm third-person narrator, present-aware that this is a life reconstructed from fragments. Pythagoras's own lines are in-scene narrative, never framed as historical quotation. Real quote marks + Greek reserved for attested testimonia.

**Caption-clarity check applied:** every period word glossed on first use; no name dropped without a role; no cryptic teaser captions; each line stands alone for a first-time reader.

**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band, cream parchment, dark serif: `No one wrote down the life of Pythagoras while he lived. What we have are stories, told long after — some true, some half-true, some surely invented. This is the most likely shape of them.`
- Lower-left small cream box: `He was born on the Greek island of Samos, about 2,500 years ago. His father, Mnesarchus, cut tiny pictures into gemstones for a living.`
- Lower-right small cream box: `The boy could do the work. But his mind kept slipping out the door, toward every sound the island made.`

## P2 · The forge · `pages/page-02.png`
**Format:** cinematic single-image. **Tier:** T4. **Ref:** ref_pyth_boy.png.
**Composition:** S2, the blacksmith's forge at evening. The boy stands at the threshold, lit orange by the hearth, head tilted, listening intently as three smiths strike iron on the anvil with hammers of different sizes. Sparks, smoke, hot light against the cool blue doorway. His whole attention is on the SOUND.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band, cream serif: `A story people still tell goes like this. One evening, passing a forge, the boy noticed that some pairs of hammers rang together sweetly — and some clashed.`
- Lower-left small box: `He wondered if the sweetness had a reason. Could a sound you LIKE be hiding a number inside it?`
- Lower-right small box, smaller italic: `(It is only a story — and the hammer part is not really true. But the question in it was real, and it never left him.)`

## P7 · The rope-stretchers (HERO PAGE) · `pages/page-07.png`
**Format:** annotated-breakthrough. **Tier:** T5. **Ref:** anchor ref_egyptian.png; describe youth in prose at the edge of frame, watching.
**Composition:** S6, the flooded survey field, clear bright morning. CENTER and large: a long rope laid out flat on the dark silt, knotted at even intervals, pegged into a triangle with sides of 3, 4, and 5 knot-segments. The corner where the short sides meet is a clean, obvious RIGHT ANGLE (square corner). An Egyptian priest-surveyor kneels, pulling the rope taut at a peg; a second surveyor holds another corner. The young Greek traveler stands at the edge, riveted. Keep the composition clean and legible so the triangle and the right-angle corner read instantly. 3–4 small painted callout boxes point at the rope.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Top band, cream serif: `The surveyors were called harpedonaptai — the rope-stretchers. After each flood erased the fields, they re-drew the boundaries with a single knotted rope.`
- Callout box near the short side: `3 spaces`
- Callout box near the other short side: `4 spaces`
- Callout box near the long side: `5 spaces`
- Callout box with an arrow to the corner: `A perfect square corner — every time.`
- Bottom band, cream serif: `Three, four, five. Pull it tight, and the corner is always exactly square — a right angle. They had used this rope for a thousand years. It never failed.`

## P8 · The unanswered why · `pages/page-08.png`
**Format:** two-figure close. **Tier:** T4. **Ref:** anchor ref_egyptian.png; describe youth in prose.
**Composition:** S6 continued, closer in. The young Greek crouches by the pegged rope, looking up and asking; the Egyptian priest stands, coiling the rope, calm and a little puzzled by the question. The right-angle corner still visible between them on the ground.
**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
- Speech bubble from the young man (tail to him): `But WHY three, four, five? Why is the corner always square?`

- Bottom band, cream serif: `It was not enough for him. A thing could be true and useful — and still no one could say why. That gap became the question he carried everywhere.`

**LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:**
