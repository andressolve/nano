## The Style Block (paste verbatim, first, into every page prompt)
> Oil-painting realism, painted brushwork, cinematic lighting, muted period palette of the 6th century BC Aegean and Near East — warm stone, sea blue-grey, bronze, sun-baked ochre, lamp-lit gold. ## Anti-drift directive (paste verbatim, third)

## Palette and light
- **Samos:** warm Aegean daylight, blue-grey sea, pale limestone, dark cypress, terracotta roofs, bronze. Forge scenes: hot orange firelight against blue evening.
- **Egypt:** high sun, bleached sandstone and ochre, deep shadow under pylons, Nile green-brown, white linen. The rope-stretcher hero page: clear bright surveying light so the rope and the right angle read cleanly.
- **Babylon:** cooler, dusk and night; mud-brick brown by day, glazed-brick blue and lamp-gold by night; the star-sky deep indigo with visible constellations.
- **Sea crossings:** dawn/dusk, low sun on water, sails.
- Hold a consistent painterly grain across the volume. No photographic crispness, no digital-painting gloss, no concept-art rim-light.

## Camera language
- Default to the **cinematic single-image page**: one composed scene, eye-level or slightly low, with depth (foreground figure, midground action, background landscape/architecture).
- The **rope hero page** is an **annotated-breakthrough** composition: the knotted rope and its 3-4-5 triangle large and central, the corner's right angle clearly readable, 3–4 small painted callout captions around it.
- The **Babylon number-sky page** can split day/night or foreground tablet + background star-platform.
- The **Croton arrival / assembly** pages are wide and aspirational — figure small against a new city, then a low-angle step-to-the-rostrum.

## Caption / lettering treatment (the Honda formula)
- **Caption boxes:** "weathered cream parchment box, dark serif ink, readable." One register, held across the volume. Top band for setup, bottom band for resolution on hero pages; small corner boxes (upper-left setup, lower-right resolution) on lighter pages.
- **Speech:** round off-white bubble, dark serif, tail explicitly aimed at the speaker. Under 15 words. NO quotation marks inside bubbles — the bubble shape is the quote.
- **Greek with English helper (project rule):** any in-image Greek gets an English line in the same panel. The tetraktys dots, the word ΡΥΘΜΟΣ/ARITHMOS if used, an attested epigraph — all paired with English. Ornamental Greek too small to read needs no helper.
- **Restrictions block (close every prompt):**
> All words spelled correctly. Do not duplicate text. Do not invent extra captions. NO modern logos, NO watermarks, NO spurious signage. NO quotation marks inside speech bubbles.

## Period vocabulary — gloss on first use (caption-clarity rule)
Gloss inline, em-dash or parentheses, on first appearance: *tyrant* (a ruler who seized power by force), *himation* (a woolen cloak), *harpedonaptai / rope-stretchers* (Egyptian surveyors who measured land with knotted cords), *Magi* (Babylonian priest-astronomers), *right angle* (a perfect square corner, like the corner of a tile), *ratio* (how many times one number fits into another), *Magna Graecia* (the Greek cities of southern Italy). Introduce *right angle* and *ratio* in plain words early; after first use the bare word may stand.

## Anti-drift specifics for this volume
- Pythagoras must read **Ionian Greek**, not generic-toga Roman and not fantasy-wizard. Trailing linen chiton + wool himation, full beard at the mature phase.
- Egyptian and Babylonian figures must be period-distinct (Egyptian linen kilts/shaved heads; Babylonian fringed wool robes, long curled beards) so the three civilizations never blur.
- Watch the recurring child-book pull on boy-Pythagoras: realistic 10-year-old, not a chibi.
- The math must be **correct on the page**: the 3-4-5 rope must show a genuine right angle and the knot-counts must be plausibly 3 / 4 / 5 segments. If geometry fumbles, that single page may use `thinking: true`.
