# Ratio: The Seeker — Settings (Vol 1)

Recurring locations, so prompts stay consistent across pages. Each is a paste-able scene anchor.

## S1 — Samos: the gem-cutter's workshop (Mnesarchus's bench)
6th-c. BC Ionian craft workshop. A stone bench by a shuttered window with Aegean light, small bronze tools, a bow-drill, half-cut seal-stones and gem blanks, a bowl of abrasive sand, oil lamp. Plastered walls, terracotta floor, a doorway opening to a sunlit harbor street. Warm, intimate, dust in the light. Lived-in, not a museum.

## S2 — Samos: the blacksmith's forge
A 6th-c. BC smithy. A stone hearth glowing orange, a heavy anvil, hanging tongs and hammers of different sizes, sparks, smoke-darkened beams. Hot orange firelight against the cool blue of an evening doorway. Two or three smiths at work. The boy stands at the threshold, listening.

## S3 — Samos: the harbor and the island
A busy Aegean port under the tyrant Polycrates: stone quays, beached and moored triremes and merchant ships, fishing nets, crates of goods, the blue-grey sea, the mountainous green island rising behind (Mount Kerkis in the distance). On a far headland the vast columns of the Heraion (temple of Hera) and, hinted, the mouth of the Eupalinos tunnel cut into the hillside. Wealth, ambition, ships from many lands.

## S4 — Samos: Pherecydes' quiet place / a hillside above the sea
An olive grove or rocky terrace above the sea at evening, a few cypress, the old teacher and the boy seated on stone. Calm, golden, contemplative. The sea and sky meeting in the distance.

## S5 — Egypt: the temple precinct
A great Egyptian temple complex in bright high sun: massive sandstone pylons, a colonnade of lotus-and-papyrus columns, an obelisk, hieroglyph-carved walls, deep hard-edged shadow, ochre and bleached white, a strip of green Nile floodplain and palms beyond. Shaved-headed priests in white linen. Awe and scale; the protagonist small among the columns.

## S6 — Egypt: the surveying field (THE ROPE-STRETCHER hero setting)
A flat, freshly-flooded Nile field at clear bright morning, dark wet silt drying, boundary markers. Surveyors in white linen kilts stretch a long knotted cord taut between wooden pegs to lay out a perfect square corner. Open sky, low horizon, clean light so the rope and the right angle read clearly. A temple foundation or boundary stones in the midground. This is the volume's most important non-portrait setting — keep it legible and uncluttered.

## S7 — Babylon: the city and ziggurat by day
The flat alluvial plain of Mesopotamia; a massive stepped mud-brick ziggurat (temple-tower) rising in tiers; mud-brick city walls, palm groves, a slow brown river, glazed-brick gateways with hints of blue. Dusty gold daylight. Vast and foreign.

## S8 — Babylon: the astronomers' platform by night
The top terrace of the ziggurat at night under a deep indigo sky thick with stars and visible constellations. Chaldean priest-astronomers observe and mark clay tablets; an oil cresset burns; the city lights small below. Cuneiform tablets and a star-chart. Cool blue-black and lamp-gold. Numbers in the sky.

## S9 — The sea crossing
An open boat or small merchant ship on the Aegean / Ionian sea at dawn or dusk, low sun on the water, a single sail, the lone traveler at the rail looking ahead, coastlines as thin distant lines. Used for Samos→Egypt and Samos→Italy; vary the light (hopeful dawn outbound, contemplative dusk for the final crossing west).

## S10 — Croton (Kroton), southern Italy
A Greek colonial city of Magna Graecia on the southern Italian coast: a stone agora (public square), Doric columns, sunlit limestone, the sea behind, citizens gathering. Cleaner, brighter, newer-feeling than weathered Samos — a place of beginnings. The final movement's destination: first seen from the sea, then the public assembly space where the protagonist steps up to speak.

## Continuity notes
- Keep the three civilizations visually distinct: Samos = Aegean blue-grey + limestone + bronze; Egypt = ochre + bleached white + hard sun; Babylon = mud-brick brown by day, indigo + glazed-blue + lamp-gold by night.
- Mount Kerkis and the Heraion columns are Samos's signature silhouette — reuse them so "home" is recognizable when he returns in Movement C.
- The knotted surveying rope (S6) is a motif object: it should visually echo forward — the reader should remember the rope when Vol 2 pays off the theorem.
