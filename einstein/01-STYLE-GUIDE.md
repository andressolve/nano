# Relativity — Style Guide

## Style Block (COPY VERBATIM TO EVERY PROMPT)

```
Art Style: Painterly realism with an early-20th-century modernist edge — cinematic lighting, grounded historical graphic novel aesthetic. Think Ashcan School meets mid-century illustration. Brush and ink textures, visible painterly marks.
Color palette: Muted sepia browns, patent-office amber, chalk-dust white, Bern mountain blue, Berlin ash gray, Princeton autumn gold, starlight indigo
Effects: Cinematic side-lighting, warm lamp glow, chalk-dust diffusion, textured brushwork, softly blurred backgrounds with sharp character focus
```

## Visual Identity

This story spans 1879–1955 across Germany, Italy, Switzerland, and the United States. Unlike the Dutch Golden Age register of *Cogito*, Einstein's world is the late-industrial and early-modern era — gas lamps giving way to electric bulbs, horse-drawn carts giving way to trains and automobiles, a world on the edge of its modern shape.

The dominant register is **painterly early-20th-century realism** — think Edward Hopper's lonely interiors, John Singer Sargent's portrait lighting, and the rich sepia-and-amber palette of period photography. Not photoreal. Painterly but grounded, with visible brushwork.

### Atmosphere Notes

- **Desks dominate.** Einstein thought at desks: the patent office desk, the Berlin study, the Princeton office. Many pages feature a desk with papers, chalkboard, or notebook.
- **Hair is a clock.** Early pages: short, dark, combed. Middle: curly, fuller, mustache. Late: the iconic white halo. The hair literally shows time passing.
- **Light as motif.** The compass (page 2) is about an invisible force. The beam of light (page 5) is about riding it. The photoelectric effect (page 10) is light as grains. The eclipse (page 16) is bent light. The curved-spacetime grid (page 20) is light following the shape of space. Let light itself be the through-line.
- **Chalkboards and equations.** On physics pages (10–15), equations appear naturally — chalk on slate, ink on paper, marks on a patent clerk's blotter. Not floating abstractions.
- **Aging is visible.** Albert goes from age 5 to age 76. His face should change dramatically: soft child → thin teenager → sharp-eyed clerk → steady middle-aged scientist → craggy elder sage.

## Panel Style

- **Borders:** Thin dark lines, slightly rough — pen-and-ink feel
- **Gutters:** Dark brown or near-black, 4–6px
- **Caption boxes:** Warm cream with dark serif text, placed at top or bottom of panels. Used for narration and Einstein's own words.
- **Speech bubbles:** Traditional white with black outlines. Tail points clearly to speaker. Keep dialogue under 15 words.
- **Thought text:** Italicized in caption boxes, attributed to Einstein. Not in thought bubbles.
- **Mathematical notation:** Chalk-style or ink notation. E=mc², the Lorentz factor, curved-grid spacetime diagrams rendered cleanly against textured surfaces.

## Typography Guidance

- **Narration (caption boxes):** Third person, present tense, concise. "He is five years old. His father hands him a small brass compass."
- **Einstein's own words (caption boxes, italic):** Direct quotes from letters, papers, or reliably attributed statements. Always in italics.
- **Dialogue (speech bubbles):** Natural, era-appropriate, accessible. German-accented English mood without fake accent marks.
- **Mathematical text:** Equations rendered cleanly. E = mc² should always appear exactly that way.

## What This Should NOT Look Like

- Children's book illustration (soft, rounded, bright, cute)
- Manga or anime styling
- Modern digital art / concept art sheen
- Flat colors or cel-shading
- Steampunk or retrofuturist elements
- Oversimplified "educational" graphics
- Generic "old-timey" sepia — this is a specific early-20th-century painterly register, not a vintage filter

## CRITICAL: Anti-Drift Directive

If any generated page shows signs of children's book aesthetic (oversized eyes, rounded features, bright saturated palette, soft focus, cartoon proportions), add this reinforcement line to the prompt:

```
STYLE REINFORCEMENT: NOT a children's book. Serious mature graphic novel. Painterly early-20th-century realism. Realistic human proportions. Muted sepia and amber palette. Cinematic side-lighting, visible brushwork.
```

Monitor especially for drift on:
- Pages with young Albert (child or teenager, pages 2–5) — model will want to make him cute
- The happiest thought (page 14) — keep surreal but painterly, not cartoony
- Physics diagram pages (10–13, 15, 20) — keep the diagrams precise but the world around them painterly, not flat-vector

## Continuity Markers for Albert Across Ages

1. **The deep-set dark eyes** — same from age 5 to 76
2. **The slightly mournful, slightly amused expression** — never fully happy, never fully sad
3. **The dark hair that lightens to white** — short/combed → curly/fuller → wild/white
4. **The mustache appears at ~17 and never leaves**
5. **The distinctive broad, heavy nose**
6. **Ears that stick out slightly** — present at every age
7. **A certain softness in the mouth** — even the old sage retains boyish traces
