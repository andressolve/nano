# Relativity — Settings

## 1. Munich, Germany (1884–1894) — Pages 2–4

The Einstein family home and Hermann's electrical workshop. Munich is a bustling industrial city — gaslit streets, horse-drawn carts, the first electric streetcars. The family is comfortably middle-class Jewish.

### Visual Direction

- **Architecture:** Late-19th-century Bavarian townhouse — plastered walls, heavy wooden doors, tall windows with lace curtains. Inside: a parlor with a piano, framed prints, a dining table with a white cloth. Outside: cobblestone street, gaslight, bourgeois bustle.
- **Interior — Albert's room / family parlor:** Dark wooden furniture, patterned wallpaper, a large tile stove (Bavarian *Kachelofen*) in the corner. Morning light through lace curtains.
- **Interior — Hermann's workshop:** Sparks, wires, dynamos, copper coils. Gas lamps overhead. Industrial but not yet modern — the cusp of electrification.
- **Interior — dining room (page 3):** A Thursday-night dinner. Heavy oak table, warm oil lamp, plates. Max Talmud eating with the family. Science books stacked to the side.
- **Interior — Luitpold Gymnasium (page 4):** Cold classroom, rows of wooden desks, a teacher in dark uniform at the front, stiff-collared students reciting in unison. Tall windows, gray light.
- **Light:** Warm gaslight indoors. Gray overcast outdoors. Church bells, a sense of order and conformity.
- **Season:** Mild summer for the compass scene (page 2). Winter/autumn for Thursday dinner (page 3) and school (page 4).
- **Key details:** The brass compass. The algebra chalkboard. The stack of Bernstein's *Popular Books on Physical Science*. The wooden desks of Luitpold. Uniform and rigidity as contrast to Albert's mind.

## 2. Aarau, Switzerland (1895–1896) — Page 5

The small Swiss town where Albert finished high school at the liberal cantonal school. He lived with the Winteler family. He rode a bicycle in the hills. He had his first real thought experiment.

### Visual Direction

- **Architecture:** Small Swiss town — half-timbered buildings, cobblestone streets, steep red-tiled roofs, church spires. The Jura mountains in the distance.
- **Interior — Winteler home:** Warm, informal, full of books and music. Not stiff like Munich.
- **Exterior — hills above Aarau:** Green rolling hills, a bicycle leaning against a tree, a boy sitting in grass imagining. Wildflowers, clouds.
- **Light:** Bright, clear, high-altitude Alpine sunlight. A sky that feels open. First real freedom.
- **Season:** Summer 1895. Everything is green and warm.
- **Key details:** The bicycle. The open sky. The thought-bubble vision of a boy riding alongside a beam of light — stretched out, the beam seemingly standing still. A first glimpse of relativity.

## 3. ETH Zurich (1898–1900) — Page 6

The Swiss Federal Polytechnic. Albert studies physics and mathematics. Mileva is the only woman in the physics section.

### Visual Direction

- **Architecture:** Grand 19th-century academic building — high ceilings, tall arched windows, dark wood paneling, chalkboards everywhere, long tables, rows of chairs. A lecture hall with tiered seating.
- **Interior — lecture hall / seminar room:** Chalk dust in light beams. Equations on the blackboard. Mileva and Albert in a corner, sharing a textbook. The other students don't quite include them.
- **Interior — café or walk outside:** A Zurich café or a walk along the Limmat river — cobblestones, trams, bridges, a view of Lake Zurich.
- **Light:** Cool academic interior light. Chalk-dust motes. Outside: clear Swiss light.
- **Season:** Autumn 1899.
- **Key details:** The blackboard with Maxwell's equations. The shared notebook. Two people who found each other in a room of men.

## 4. Bern, Switzerland — Patent Office (1902–1909) — Pages 7–14

The "cobbler's job" that gave Einstein the quiet hours he needed. Third-class patent examiner. Later second-class. The office on Speichergasse, near Bern's medieval clock tower.

### Visual Direction

- **Architecture:** A late-19th-century Swiss government building — plastered walls, heavy wooden doors with frosted glass panes reading "Eidgenössisches Amt für Geistiges Eigentum" (Federal Patent Office). Inside: a room of wooden desks, each with a patent examiner hunched over technical drawings.
- **Interior — patent office:** Rows of identical desks. Green-shaded banker's lamps. Blueprints and technical drawings piled up. Einstein's desk in a corner — his work done, his own papers surreptitiously on top. Dust motes in the light.
- **Interior — Einstein's apartment, Kramgasse 49:** A small Bern flat. Wooden floors, modest furniture, a single desk by the window. A view of the Kramgasse street with its arcaded sidewalks and the Zytglogge (medieval clock tower) visible in the distance.
- **Exterior — Bern:** Medieval arcaded streets, the Aare river, the Zytglogge clock tower, the Alps in the distance on clear days.
- **Light:** Warm lamp glow on polished wood desks. Cool Swiss daylight from tall windows. Evening: warm oil-lamp or gas-lamp glow in the apartment. Candle on the desk when thinking late.
- **Season:** Varied — the 1905 papers span March to September, so spring through early autumn.
- **Key details:** The technical drawings. The banker's lamp. The stack of Einstein's own manuscripts growing beside the assigned work. The ink and pen. The clock tower framing the patent-office window — a visual pun: the man thinking about time, working next to the most famous clock in Switzerland.

## 5. The Olympia Academy — Bern Apartment, Evenings (Page 8)

Einstein's tiny reading group: himself, Maurice Solovine (Romanian philosophy student), Conrad Habicht (Swiss mathematician). Named sarcastically — "the Academy." They read, argued, ate sausages and cheese, drank cheap Turkish coffee, laughed until dawn.

### Visual Direction

- **Architecture:** A small book-cluttered Bern apartment living room. Three chairs pulled close around a small table. Books piled everywhere — Hume, Spinoza, Poincaré, Mach.
- **Interior:** Single oil lamp glow. Cigar or pipe smoke. Remains of a simple meal. Three young men leaning forward, arguing.
- **Light:** Warm lamp halo at the center, everything else falling into shadow. A very specific kind of intellectual warmth — the warmth of your twenties, your friends, and the ideas that are changing you.
- **Key details:** The book spines (legible: HUME, SPINOZA, POINCARÉ). The coffee cups. The sausage and cheese on a board. The sense that this is the most important thing in the world, even though no one outside this room knows it's happening.

## 6. Thought-Experiment / Physics Pages — Pages 10–14, 15

For the pages on specific physics ideas, settings are partly concrete (his desk, a classroom, a Berlin office) and partly diagrammatic (the idea itself, rendered visually).

### Visual Direction

- **Page 10 — photoelectric effect:** A metal plate, a beam of light hitting it, tiny golden "grains" of light knocking electrons loose. Einstein at his patent desk in the foreground, a chalk diagram on a slate behind him.
- **Page 11 — Brownian motion:** A microscope view — dust specks dancing in water, tiny atoms bumping into them. Rendered like a scientific plate from 1905.
- **Page 12 — special relativity (train and platform):** Two panels, one of a train racing past a platform, a person on the train and a person on the platform, each with their own clock. The clocks show different times. A single lightning strike hitting both ends of the train — simultaneous for one, not for the other.
- **Page 13 — E = mc²:** The equation itself, chalk on slate, glowing. A tiny stone on a scale with an energy beam exploding from it on the other side of a conceptual scale.
- **Page 14 — happiest thought:** A man falling from a roof, inside an elevator cutaway, weightless, in the same moment. Gravity and acceleration rendered as two sides of one coin.
- **Page 15 — general relativity:** A blackboard covered in tensor equations. Einstein in his Berlin office, gaunt, eight years of work visible on his face. The curved-grid spacetime beginning to appear over a star.
- **Light:** Concept pages have a cleaner, brighter, more diagrammatic light than the world pages — but still painterly, not flat-vector.

## 7. Berlin, Germany (1914–1932) — Pages 15, 17

Einstein at the Prussian Academy of Sciences, Kaiser Wilhelm Institute. The years of general relativity and rising fame. Also the years of growing antisemitism and the coming catastrophe.

### Visual Direction

- **Architecture:** Grand imperial Berlin — stone facades, tall windows, heavy doors. The academy building, Einstein's office: dark wood, leather chairs, a large chalkboard, a desk piled with papers.
- **Interior — Einstein's Berlin office:** The blackboard with general relativity equations. The window looks over a grey European capital. Coat on a hook.
- **Exterior (page 17):** Brownshirts in the street. Swastika flags on buildings. The menacing new Berlin of 1933.
- **Light:** Cool northern European grey. Gas and early-electric light indoors. By 1933, ominous.
- **Season:** Winter 1915 for page 15 (the breakthrough year of general relativity). Early 1933 for page 17 (Nazi takeover).
- **Key details:** The chalkboard equations. The overcoat on the hook. The Berlin window framing a darkening Europe.

## 8. Príncipe / Sobral — The 1919 Eclipse — Page 16

Arthur Eddington's expedition photographed the solar eclipse from the island of Príncipe off West Africa, confirming that starlight bent around the sun exactly as Einstein predicted.

### Visual Direction

- **Setting:** A tropical island observation site. Telescope on a wooden platform. Eddington at the eyepiece. The eclipse in progress — the sun blackened, the solar corona blazing, stars visible in day.
- **Split/secondary scene:** Einstein in Berlin, reading the telegram. Or a newspaper headline — "LIGHTS ALL ASKEW IN THE HEAVENS" (The New York Times, November 10, 1919).
- **Light:** The deep indigo of eclipse day. The golden corona. The white shock of a star that should not be visible, now visible and in the wrong place.
- **Key details:** The telescope. The photographic plate showing a star displaced from its expected position. The telegram. The sense of a theoretical prediction meeting physical reality.

---

## 9. Princeton, New Jersey (1933–1955) — Pages 17, 18, 19, 20

Einstein's final home. The Institute for Advanced Study. A quiet American college town, autumn leaves, a modest clapboard house at 112 Mercer Street.

### Visual Direction

- **Architecture:** American collegiate gothic at the Institute. A simple wood-frame American house at 112 Mercer Street — white clapboard, green shutters, a porch, tall trees. An ordinary, almost unremarkable house.
- **Interior — Einstein's Princeton study:** Books everywhere, an upright piano, a violin in a corner, a pipe, a desk by a window, autumn leaves outside. A photograph of himself sailing. An American flag or two on the shelf. Quiet.
- **Exterior — Princeton streets:** Tree-lined suburban streets. Elms and oaks turning gold and red. Einstein walking to work in his famous wool sweater, white hair wild, the neighbors waving.
- **Light:** Warm autumn American light. Afternoon gold. A sense of exile turned into home.
- **Season:** American autumn. Always autumn at Princeton.
- **Key details:** The violin. The sweater. The pipe. The autumn leaves. The ordinary American suburban street that contains the most famous mind in the world.

## 10. Legacy — Page 20

The closing image uses curved spacetime as its visual structure — moments from Einstein's life plotted on a gently warped grid, the way Descartes's legacy page plotted his life on the Cartesian plane.

### Visual Direction

- **The structure:** A softly curved grid of light, like a trampoline stretched by a heavy object. The grid bends around invisible masses. Against this cosmic background, small painterly vignettes from Einstein's life appear as if suspended in pools of warm light on the bent grid.
- **The vignettes (reading left to right, low to high):**
  - The brass compass (childhood)
  - The bicycle against an Alpine tree (Aarau)
  - The patent office desk with a single lamp (Bern)
  - The chalked equation E = mc² (1905)
  - The telescope and eclipse (1919)
  - The violin and the wool sweater (Princeton)
  - A star at the top, its light visibly bent by the grid below
- **Light:** Cosmic indigo background, warm amber pools for each vignette, starlight gold accents.
- **Key details:** The grid is the subject as much as the vignettes. It is the shape of spacetime — the idea he gave the world. Everything human happens on it.
