# Cogito — Settings

## 1. La Haye en Touraine, France (1596–1606) — Pages 2–3

A small town in the Loire Valley. René's maternal grandmother's house, where he was raised after his mother's death.

### Visual Direction
- **Architecture:** Modest French provincial stone house. Thick walls, small windows, heavy wooden beams. Not a grand estate — comfortable rural gentry.
- **Interior:** René's bedroom — a simple bed with heavy linen covers, a wooden crucifix on the wall, morning light filtering through a small window with uneven glass. Plaster walls, stone floor.
- **Light:** Warm, soft, morning. Dust motes. The gentleness of a grandmother's house.
- **Season/weather:** Late morning, spring or summer — the light is kind.
- **Key details:** The ceiling above the bed (he stares at it). Cracks in plaster. Shadows that move.

## 2. Collège Royal Henry-le-Grand, La Flèche, France (1607–1615) — Pages 3–5

One of the finest Jesuit colleges in Europe. Founded 1604. Stone buildings, cloisters, a well-stocked library.

### Visual Direction
- **Architecture:** Grand stone Jesuit college. Arched corridors, tall windows, heavy wooden doors. Gothic and Renaissance elements. A large library with floor-to-ceiling shelves.
- **Interior — dormitory:** Simple bed in a row of beds, but René is alone in his (the others have gone to class). Morning light from high windows. His bed is a cocoon of thinking.
- **Interior — library:** Candlelit, warm amber light on leather spines. A heavy wooden table where René sits with Euclid's *Elements* open before him. Stone walls, arched ceiling.
- **Interior — corridor/classroom:** Tall stone arches, a Jesuit teacher in black robes at a lectern. Students in rows. René apart, thinking.
- **Light:** Candlelight in library scenes. High window light in corridors. Always some shadow — it's a stone building.
- **Key details:** Books everywhere. Euclid's geometric diagrams visible on open pages. The contrast between the rigid order of the school and the freedom of René's mind.

## 3. Breda, Netherlands (1618) — Page 6

A Dutch military garrison town. René has joined the army of Maurice of Nassau. The marketplace where he meets Beeckman.

### Visual Direction
- **Architecture:** Dutch 17th-century town — brick buildings, stepped gables, a busy marketplace with stalls. Cobblestone streets. A wooden placard board where notices and challenges are posted.
- **Exterior — marketplace:** Bustling with soldiers, merchants, townspeople. Overcast Dutch sky, cool light. The placard board is the focal point — a mathematical problem written in Dutch, which René cannot read.
- **Light:** Outdoor, bright overcast — the flat, cool, even light of the Low Countries.
- **Season:** Late autumn 1618. Cool, damp.
- **Key details:** The placard with Dutch text and mathematical notation. The chance encounter. Two men from different worlds meeting over a problem.

## 4. Neuburg an der Donau, Germany (November 1619) — Pages 7–8

A small German town on the Danube. René is alone in a "poêle" — a small room heated by a large ceramic tile stove, standard in German houses of the period.

### Visual Direction
- **Architecture:** Simple German townhouse interior. Wooden floor, whitewashed walls, low ceiling with exposed beams. The dominant feature is the large ceramic *Kachelofen* (tile stove) — a tall, rectangular structure of glazed ceramic tiles, radiating heat.
- **Interior — the stove room:** Small, enclosed, warm. A wooden chair, a small table, a candle. No books — this is pure thinking. The stove glows with heat. The single small window shows darkness and snow outside.
- **Light:** Firelight from the stove's grate, a single candle. Warm orange against the cold darkness pressing in from outside. For the dream sequence, the light becomes unstable — flickering, shifting, splitting into unnatural colors.
- **Season:** November 10, 1619. Deep winter. Snow. The contrast between the heated room and the freezing world outside mirrors the contrast between René's inner fire and the cold uncertainty around him.
- **Key details for the dream page (8):** This page should be visually distinct from all others — surreal, fractured panels. A whirlwind tearing at him. Sparks and light filling a dark room. A book of poetry open to: "Quod vitae sectabor iter?" (What path shall I follow in life?). The surrealism should feel painterly (Goya, late Rembrandt), not cartoonish.

## 5. Europe — Montage Locations (1620s) — Page 9

René's wandering decade. He travels through Germany, Italy, and France before settling in Holland.

### Visual Direction
- **Multiple locations in montage format:**
  - **Venice/Italy:** Warm Mediterranean light, arched bridges, water reflections
  - **Paris:** Gray stone, salon interiors, a Minim friar's cell (Mersenne's)
  - **French countryside:** The estate he sells — fields, a stone house
- **Light:** Varied — each panel has its own light quality to convey a different place
- **Key details:** The sense of movement, searching, restlessness. Notebooks filling with sketches and equations. No single place is home yet.

## 6. The Netherlands — René's Rooms (1628–1649) — Pages 10–11

Descartes lived at 18+ addresses in the Netherlands over 22 years, always moving to preserve his solitude. The visual should be a composite "Descartes room" — the essential space of a thinker.

### Visual Direction
- **Architecture:** Dutch Golden Age interior. Tall windows with rectangular panes, white plastered walls, dark wooden floor and beams. A canal visible through the window. Brick buildings across the way.
- **The desk:** A heavy wooden table covered with papers, geometric diagrams, an open book, a compass, a ruler, quill pens, an inkwell. A single candle in a brass holder. This is the center of his world.
- **The bed:** Visible in the background — rumpled linens. He has just risen at 11 a.m. The morning's thinking is done in bed; the afternoon's work is at the desk.
- **Light:** Vermeer light — cool white-blue from the tall windows, falling across the desk and floor in geometric planes. Warm candle glow for evening scenes.
- **Season/time:** Late morning. The house is quiet. Amsterdam's bustle is outside, muffled.
- **Key details:** The solitude. Papers with geometric curves and algebraic notation. A cat sometimes. The absolute stillness of a room where someone is thinking.

## 7. Egmond / Santpoort — Francine's World (1635–1640) — Page 11

The brief, warm period when Descartes had a family. Helena and Francine lived with him near Haarlem and later at Egmond.

### Visual Direction
- **Architecture:** A Dutch country house, simpler than Amsterdam lodgings. A garden visible. Warmer, more domestic than René's solitary rooms.
- **Interior:** A living space with a child's presence — small chair, toys. But also René's papers on a desk in the corner. Two worlds sharing a room.
- **Light:** Warmer than the solitary rooms. Golden. Home light.
- **For the death scene:** The warmth drains. The room becomes the cold, muted palette of grief. A small empty bed. René alone.

---

## 8. Stockholm, Sweden (1649–1650) — Page 15

Queen Christina's court. The cold that killed him.

### Visual Direction
- **Architecture:** Swedish royal palace interior — grand but cold. High ceilings, dark wood paneling, tall windows showing pre-dawn darkness and snow. Christina's library: bookshelves, a globe, heavy chairs.
- **Exterior (glimpse):** Stockholm in winter — frozen harbor, dark sky, snow on rooftops. 5 a.m. darkness.
- **Light:** Cold blue pre-dawn light. No warmth. The candles in the library give light but not heat. This is the opposite of the warm Dutch rooms — everything is cold, grand, and hostile to the body.
- **Season:** January–February 1650. The coldest winter. Snow and ice everywhere.
- **Key details:** The contrast with everything before. René in black against the cold blue. A man out of his element, forced out of bed at 5 a.m. after a lifetime of mornings spent thinking in warmth. The cold is visible — breath in the air, frost on windows, the physical weight of it.
