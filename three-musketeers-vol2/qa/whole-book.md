# Whole-book release gate: *The Three Musketeers*, Volume II: *Milady* (pp 1–58)

## 1. Blind account (written before opening the script or the contract)

**Reading-pace record.** I read all 58 proofs at 600 × 900, in order, once, with story material closed. I opened the 768 × 1152 proofs of pp 16, 22 and 37 only after the blind pass, to confirm three lettering observations. The book reads at a steady pace. The fast stretches are the action runs: pp 5, 13–14, 29–34 and 46–51. The slow, quiet stretches are pp 15–17, 37–38, 43–44 and 52–57. Nothing made me stop to work out who was who or what had happened. The p22 and p11 balloon order, covered below, made me pause briefly. Neither one cost me the story.

**Retell.** It is summer in Paris, after the Queen's ball. Athos, Porthos and Aramis wear the Musketeers' blue cloak. d'Artagnan, a Gascon boy on a yellow horse, does not, and he wants one. Across the river, the Cardinal has failed to stop the boy four times through his man Rochefort, so he sends Milady instead. She is to find out where Constance, the girl downstairs and the Queen's go-between, is being sent, and get the boy out of the way. Milady stages a runaway carriage. The boy stops it, and she invites him in "by the front door." Constance leaves for a hidden convent "in the north." She warns him about "ladies who are kind for no reason" and makes him promise not to come until the Queen says she is safe. Flattered, the boy tells Milady about the convent in the north on his first visit. He stops showing up for his friends.

At the tavern, Athos asks the boy to describe her and goes still at the description. The next day the boy overhears Milady reporting to Rochefort. In the struggle her sleeve tears and he sees a lily branded on her shoulder. She swears he will die, and he escapes through the window into a hay cart. Athos asks which shoulder, says "Then she isn't dead," and tells the story of a count who hanged his branded young wife. The count was Athos. The four are now in danger, and war calls them to La Rochelle. Milady's carriage follows the Cardinal's train.

In the camp, a poisoned bottle arrives "from" the three. Athos stops the boy from drinking it, and the wine turns the grass black. Escorting the Cardinal to the Red Dovecote inn, Athos listens through a broken stovepipe. He hears Milady's price, the Gascon and the girl he loves, and hears the Cardinal name the convent: Béthune. The Cardinal gives her a paper that covers anything she does. Athos goes upstairs, takes the paper and lets her go, saying he will not judge her alone a second time. The boy rages at him, but deserters are shot. Athos's plan is a bet with the whole army that the four will eat breakfast in the bastion of Saint-Gervais for an hour. That gives them a place where nobody can listen and a feat that will win the King's leave. They read the paper, fight off two attacks, and Porthos pushes a wall down on the second one. They win the leave. The Cardinal works out where they are going and sends Rochefort north ahead of them.

At Béthune, Milady arrives disguised as a hunted widow and wins Constance's trust. Rochefort warns her that the four are coming. Constance sees the yellow horse and refuses to leave, so Milady poisons her drink from her ring and slips away. Constance dies in the boy's arms: "You promised, and you came." Athos takes the blame, the boy refuses to put it on him, and they ride. In a storm they find her abandoned carriage, then Milady on a ferry raft by a weir with a knife at the rope. Athos stops the boy's sword. The four name her crimes, and she cuts the rope. Athos wades in, held by a chain of his friends, and offers her a trial. She refuses the hand, the raft goes over the weir, and the lantern goes out. They bury Constance under the roses.

Back in Paris, Rochefort takes the boy to the Cardinal. The boy accuses him and then hands back the protecting paper without being asked. The Cardinal burns it and gives him a blank officer's commission. Athos writes the boy's name in it. The boy, now in blue, lights the lamp at Constance's dark door. Four blue cloaks walk off down the street while the man in red watches from a window.

**Cast as I identified them, all correct.** d'Artagnan: dark hair, ochre doublet, grey hat. Athos: tall, long dark hair, white plume, still. Porthos: huge, red hair, red plumes, eating. Aramis: fair, white plume, religious jokes. Constance: dark curls, white cap. Milady (Lady de Winter): gold ringlets, plum silk, dark ring. Rochefort: black clothes, red feather, scar. The Cardinal: red robes, grey cat. I misidentified nobody and gave no line to the wrong speaker.

**Causal chain.** The Cardinal wants Constance's location → Milady's staged carriage → the boy's vanity ("front door") → he tells her "north" → she reports it → the brand is exposed → Athos's past → the poison → the stovepipe gives Béthune → Athos takes the paper and spares her → they need leave → the bastion wins it → the Cardinal sends Rochefort ahead → Milady poisons Constance → the chase → the ferry, where she chooses the weir over a trial → the boy returns the paper unasked → the Cardinal answers with a commission → Athos writes the name.

**Ending: what it cost and why the last image matters.** The boy gets the blue cloak he wanted on p3. He gets it by not hiding behind the Cardinal's paper, and the price was Constance, lost through his own talking. P57 says it plainly: he would give the cloak back to hear someone moving about downstairs. The final image puts the opening four-abreast walk next to its answer. Now there are four blue cloaks, and the man in red who signed one is watching. It is earned and bittersweet, and it leaves the Cardinal unfinished.

## 5. Nonblocking observations

- **p22, panel 2 (balloon order).** The Cardinal's inset balloon ("The girl is shut away… Take her.") is level with Milady's ("And my price, Eminence?…") and sits to its left, so some readers will read the answer before the question. The facts that matter survive either order: Béthune, the boy and the girl as her price. It does not change the reading. A balloon nudge is optional.
- **p11, panel 3 (balloon order).** Athos's "Her name." sits nearly level with the boy's description. It reads correctly as a zigzag, but only just.
- **pp 5→6 (time of day).** The carriage is stopped "at noon exactly" on p5, but p6 shows wet evening light with the street lamps lit. The scene is still clearly continuous.

## 6. Nonblocking — work list (in-generator edits before the reader is built)

1. **p16, prose field (punctuation).** The opening quote mark before "I had a friend," is missing, so a stray closing ' after "friend" stands alone. The paragraph-opening ' marks before "One day…", "He was the lord…" and "When he went back…" are missing. The closing ' after "became a Musketeer." is missing. The script has 'I had a friend,' … each paragraph opening with ' … and the final paragraph closing with Musketeer.'
2. **p49, panel 1 (identity drift).** Milady on the raft is drawn with dark brown hair. She is gold-ringleted everywhere else, including on pp 48 and 50.
3. **p32, panel 2 (identity drift).** Aramis, second from left, wears a red-plumed hat and looks like a second Porthos. His hat should carry his white plume.
4. **p50, panel 1 (stray figures).** Three standing figures in hats appear on the landing steps at the top right. The script has no onlookers here, and all four friends are in the water chain.
5. **p44 (costume and prop drift).**
   - Panel 1: Constance wears a white apron that is not on her convent dress in pp 37–43.
   - Panel 2: the glass on the grass is stemmed. Pages 41 and 43 show a plain tumbler, and the script calls for "two plain glasses."
6. **pp 5→6 (setting drift).** P6 is lit as a rainy dusk with lamps burning, right after p5's noon. Daylight on p6 would match the script.
7. **p58 (small drift).** d'Artagnan walks second from the left instead of in the middle, and his hat shows no broken feather. It reads fine.
8. **p37, prose field, line 1 (lettering).** There is a slight letter-spacing gap inside "Constance" ("Con stance"). Cosmetic.
