## Retell of the story so far

Paris, the summer after the Queen's ball: d'Artagnan is still the youngest of four sworn friends and the only one not yet wearing a Musketeer's blue cloak. He came from Gascony with almost nothing, became the friend of Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, helped save the Queen from the Cardinal, received a ring from her, and fell in love with Constance. The Cardinal has noticed him and remembers his name.

The Cardinal wants d'Artagnan removed and Constance found because she carries the Queen's secrets. His agent Rochefort has already failed repeatedly to stop the boy, so the Cardinal turns to Milady, Lady de Winter. She arranges for her carriage horses to bolt in d'Artagnan's path. He stops them, believes he has rescued her, and accepts her invitation to visit.

That same night Constance leaves on a secret mission for the Queen. She says the Cardinal's people keep asking who carries the Queen's letters, so the Queen will hide her in an unnamed convent somewhere in the north. Constance warns d'Artagnan that women who are kind without reason may be dangerous, specifically after learning that Lady de Winter has approached him. D'Artagnan promises to wait until the Queen declares Constance safe before going to her.

Milady flatters d'Artagnan and lets him talk. He tells her about the Queen's diamonds and the adventure in London and, trying to impress a beautiful grand lady who seems fascinated by him, reveals that Constance is hidden in a northern convent. He then neglects his friends while returning to Milady day after day. When he finally returns to the tavern, Athos becomes alarmed by his description of her extreme pallor, icy eyes, and smile. The name Lady de Winter means nothing to Athos, but the description does.

The next day d'Artagnan arrives early for another visit and overhears Rochefort reporting to Milady. This proves that she works for the Cardinal and that she deliberately used him to learn about Constance. D'Artagnan confronts her. In the struggle he exposes a fleur-de-lis burned into her left shoulder, the mark of a convicted thief. Milady tells him that nobody who sees it remains alive, but Rochefort cannot stop d'Artagnan from escaping through the window.

D'Artagnan tells Athos about the brand. Athos then tells the story of a supposed friend: a count who married a beautiful, mysterious girl, discovered the fleur-de-lis on her shoulder, and hanged her from a tree as her legal judge. The rope was later cut and she vanished. D'Artagnan realizes that the count was Athos. Athos confirms that identity and understands that Milady is the wife he believed dead. He also understands that she means to kill d'Artagnan and will pursue Constance. Before the four can act, war calls the King's Musketeers and d'Artagnan, serving as a volunteer, to the siege of La Rochelle. The Cardinal travels with the army, with Milady's closed carriage behind his train.

At the siege, a bottle of wine arrives for d'Artagnan supposedly from his three friends. Athos stops him from drinking because he never sent wine. The four pour it out and watch the grass die, confirming another attempt by Milady. They cannot safely send a warning to the Queen because every letter leaving camp crosses the Cardinal's desk.

That night the Cardinal orders Athos, Porthos, and Aramis to escort him to an inn called the Red Dovecote while d'Artagnan, not being a Musketeer, must remain behind. The three listen through a broken stovepipe as the Cardinal sends Milady to England to carry out an unnamed political killing or crime. Her price is vengeance on d'Artagnan and possession of Constance. The Cardinal reveals that Constance is at the convent in Béthune and gives Milady a signed paper that excuses whatever the bearer does in the good of France.

Athos pretends his horse has thrown a shoe so that he can remain after the others leave. He goes upstairs and confronts Milady as her supposedly dead husband, the Count de la Fère. He takes the Cardinal's authorization from her and warns that if she goes near d'Artagnan or Constance, he will judge her again, this time publicly and with witnesses. Milady still leaves for the north before dawn. Athos brings the paper back to camp and tells d'Artagnan that Milady is heading toward Béthune, but deserting the siege would get all four shot.

To secure leave and speak where nobody can overhear, the friends stage a spectacular public wager: they will eat breakfast for an hour in the exposed bastion of Saint-Gervais beneath the enemy's guns. While the whole army watches, they discuss the stolen authorization, fight off an attack, push down a wall onto the attackers, raise a flag so the King can identify the bastion, and remain for the full hour. Their courage wins the King's admiration and immediate leave.

Rochefort, however, has worked out that Athos overheard Béthune at the Red Dovecote. The Cardinal knows the King will reward the four and sends Rochefort north to warn Milady that they are coming. The friends ride hard through six days of rain, changing every horse except d'Artagnan's remarkable yellow one. D'Artagnan breaks his promise to wait because he knows Milady is about half a day ahead and every delay brings her closer to Constance. Unknown to them, Rochefort is riding still faster.

At the convent in Béthune, Constance has been safe but lonely. Milady arrives disguised as a widow and claims that the Cardinal's men are hunting her. Her kindness wins Constance's trust. Constance innocently tells her about d'Artagnan, even saying that Lady de Winter had been kind to him and that she herself warned him about such women. Milady quietly confirms that she has found the right victim.

Rochefort reaches the convent at night. He tells Milady that all four friends are coming with the King's leave and that d'Artagnan is in front on his yellow horse. Milady orders her carriage brought at first light and plans to take Constance toward the coast, where d'Artagnan would have to follow them onto the boat to England. If Constance refuses, Milady says she will remain at the convent "one way or another."

In the morning Constance sees d'Artagnan's distinctive yellow horse and recognizes him approaching with the other three. Milady lies that the riders are the Cardinal's men and urges Constance into her carriage. Constance refuses to flee because she knows it is d'Artagnan. Milady changes tactics and proposes that they drink to him first, ending page 40 with the clear implication that she means to poison Constance before he reaches her.

## Cast

- **d'Artagnan:** A very young, slim Gascon with tousled black hair, a battered gray hat, patched mustard-brown clothes, a sword, and a conspicuous yellow horse. He is brave, impulsive, proud, romantic, and not yet officially a Musketeer. He is the central target of the Cardinal and Milady and is racing to save Constance.

- **Athos / the Count de la Fère:** Tall and lean, with long dark hair, a dark beard and mustache, black clothing, a black hat with a white feather, and a blue Musketeer's cloak. He is the grave, controlled leader of the friends, Milady's husband, and the man most capable of recognizing and countering her. His earlier private judgment of Milady is the source of both his guilt and his insistence on witnesses now.

- **Porthos:** Huge, broad, red-haired, and extravagantly mustached, usually in red beneath his blue Musketeer's cloak and a hat with large red feathers. He is jovial, theatrical, physically powerful, perpetually hungry, and the source of much of the group's humor. He also supplies the public spectacle that helps win the four their leave.

- **Aramis:** Slender and fair-haired, with a neat mustache, gray clothes, a blue Musketeer's cloak, and a black hat with a white feather. He is elegant, dryly witty, observant, and inclined toward religion; his alertness helps distinguish danger from bravado.

- **Constance:** A young brunette woman with curls under a white servant's cap, first in a white apron and later a plain gray convent dress. She serves the Queen, carries her secrets, and loves d'Artagnan. She is brave and loyal but isolated enough at Béthune to be vulnerable to Milady's manufactured kindness.

- **Milady / Lady de Winter / Athos's wife:** A strikingly pale blonde woman with long curls, icy blue eyes, and a poised, aristocratic manner. She wears rich purple in Paris and later a black widow's dress and veil. A fleur-de-lis is branded on her left shoulder. She is the Cardinal's seductive and lethal agent, Athos's supposedly dead wife, d'Artagnan's would-be killer, and the immediate threat to Constance.

- **Cardinal Richelieu:** An older, gaunt man in red clerical robes and skullcap, usually wearing a cross. He is the political power directing the villains: he wants the Queen's secrets controlled, commissions Milady's work in England, places Constance in Milady's reach, and grants Milady advance immunity for her crimes.

- **Rochefort:** A lean, dark-haired and mustached man in black, marked by a facial scar and often a red feather. He is the Cardinal's persistent field agent and Milady's accomplice. He reports information, helps spring traps, recognizes the friends' plan, and beats them to Béthune with a warning.

- **The Queen:** Not seen directly in these pages, but the absent center of the earlier diamond affair and of Constance's loyalty. She hides Constance and is supposed to summon d'Artagnan when it is safe.

- **The King:** Also not seen directly. His authority matters at the siege: the friends turn their bastion exploit into a performance he cannot ignore, and his reward gives them the leave needed to pursue Milady.

## Causal chain

1. D'Artagnan's earlier rescue of the Queen and bond with Constance put him on the Cardinal's list of threats.
2. Rochefort's repeated failures lead the Cardinal to deploy Milady against both d'Artagnan and Constance.
3. Milady stages the runaway-carriage encounter, gains d'Artagnan's trust, and extracts the fact that Constance is in a northern convent.
4. D'Artagnan overhears Milady and Rochefort, exposes Milady's brand during the confrontation, and escapes to Athos.
5. The brand lets Athos identify Milady as his supposedly dead wife, turning the danger from a vague spy plot into a personal vendetta.
6. War moves all of them to La Rochelle, where Milady attempts to poison d'Artagnan and the Cardinal gives her Constance's exact location plus written immunity.
7. Athos overhears the bargain and steals the immunity paper, but Milady leaves for Béthune while the friends remain trapped by military law.
8. The friends' one-hour breakfast under fire both gives them privacy to plan and publicly earns the King's leave.
9. Rochefort deduces that Athos overheard Béthune, so the Cardinal sends him ahead to warn Milady.
10. Milady reaches Constance first, gains her trust under a false identity, and prepares to remove or kill her.
11. The friends arrive within sight of the convent, but Milady has a final interval in which to use a poisoned drink.

## What I expect next

Milady will try to poison Constance in the few moments before d'Artagnan reaches the convent. Either Constance will drink and collapse just as he arrives, or her trust will finally break in time for her to refuse. The four friends will confront Milady, and Athos's insistence on witnesses suggests that she will be captured and judged publicly rather than attacked in private. The Cardinal's authorization paper will probably protect the friends or expose his responsibility when that judgment occurs. Rochefort may attempt to delay or ambush them, while the still-unnamed English mission remains available as Milady's escape route if she gets Constance into the carriage.
