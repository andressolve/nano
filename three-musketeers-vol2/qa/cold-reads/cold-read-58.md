# Cold read — pages 1–58

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without outside documentation. I could follow the characters, motives, reversals, travel, and final moral outcome at normal reading pace. I did not encounter a page whose meaning depended on knowing the source.

## Retell of the story so far

Paris, in the summer after the Queen's ball: d'Artagnan is still the only one of four inseparable friends who is not a King's Musketeer. He is a young Gascon who has already helped Athos, Porthos, and Aramis save the Queen from the Cardinal, won Constance's love, and made himself memorable to the Cardinal and the Cardinal's agent Rochefort.

The Cardinal wants d'Artagnan removed and wants the Queen's hidden correspondence found. Rochefort's repeated attacks have failed, so the Cardinal gives the task to Milady, a beautiful Englishwoman. Rochefort arranges a runaway carriage on the river road, but d'Artagnan stops it and saves the passenger—Milady herself. She invites him to visit her. At almost the same moment, Constance is being sent away because the Cardinal's people are asking about the Queen's letters. She cannot tell d'Artagnan where she will hide, but says it is a northern convent and makes him promise to wait until the Queen declares her safe.

D'Artagnan visits Milady and is flattered by her attention. Wanting someone grand to listen to him, he tells her about the Queen's diamonds and eventually lets slip that Constance is in a convent in the north. He neglects his three friends while returning to Milady day after day. Athos becomes alarmed when d'Artagnan describes her. On a later visit, d'Artagnan overhears Milady and Rochefort discussing the hidden girl and realizes he has been used. He confronts Milady and sees a fleur-de-lis brand on her left shoulder. She tries to kill him for seeing it, but Rochefort blocks the door and d'Artagnan escapes through a window.

Athos then admits that he once had another identity: he was a count who married a beautiful, mysterious young woman. After discovering the same brand, which marked her as a convicted criminal, he hanged her as judge on his own estate. Her body disappeared. Milady is the wife he believed dead. Athos warns that she keeps her threats and that they must now guard everything they eat and drink. The friends know the Cardinal can identify the correct northern convent and will send Milady there.

War interrupts them. The King and his army march to besiege La Rochelle, and the four friends serve there, with d'Artagnan volunteering so he can remain beside the Musketeers. A bottle of wine supposedly sent by Athos arrives for d'Artagnan; Athos stops him from drinking it, and the poisoned wine kills the grass. D'Artagnan realizes Milady is travelling in the Cardinal's black carriage and wants to warn the Queen, but Aramis explains that every letter leaving camp can be read by the Cardinal.

That night the Cardinal orders Athos, Porthos, and Aramis to escort him to the Red Dovecote. Athos listens through a broken stovepipe while the Cardinal meets Milady upstairs. He hears the Cardinal order her to sail to England and carry out an earlier assignment. In payment, he tells her that Constance is at the convent in Béthune. He also gives Milady a signed paper excusing whatever she does in the service of France. When the Cardinal leaves, Athos confronts Milady as her supposedly dead husband and takes the paper. He refuses to kill her privately a second time, but warns her not to go near d'Artagnan or Constance.

Milady leaves before dawn. Athos urges d'Artagnan to get leave from the army immediately, but leaving the siege without the King's permission would get all four men shot. They devise a spectacular way to win leave and speak where the Cardinal cannot overhear: they bet the whole army that they can eat breakfast for an hour in the exposed bastion of Saint-Gervais, under enemy guns. During the meal Athos shows the others the Cardinal's paper. When enemy soldiers attack, the four fight them off and shove a damaged wall onto the advancing force. They finish the hour, raise a flag, and return as heroes. The King grants them leave. The Cardinal has watched the exploit and orders Rochefort to ride ahead and arrange for Milady to intercept them.

The four friends ride north for six days, but Milady is half a day ahead. At Béthune, Constance has grown lonely in the convent. Milady arrives disguised as a widow fleeing the Cardinal's men, wins her trust, and learns both who she is and that she expects d'Artagnan. Rochefort arrives at night and reports that the four friends will reach the convent by noon. Milady plans to take Constance toward the coast and force d'Artagnan to follow her to the England-bound boat.

When the four riders appear in the morning, Constance recognizes d'Artagnan's unique yellow horse. Milady lies that they are the Cardinal's men and urges her into the carriage, but Constance refuses to leave. Milady proposes a toast to d'Artagnan, secretly poisons the wine from her ring, and leaves Constance to drink it while she goes to meet the supposedly arriving friends. D'Artagnan finds Constance alive but dying. She understands that the kind pale-haired woman deceived her, takes comfort that d'Artagnan kept his promise, and dies in his arms. He recognizes that his careless disclosure to Milady led her there.

The friends pursue Milady through a violent storm, find her abandoned carriage near a ferry, and corner her alone on a raft above a weir. D'Artagnan wants immediate revenge, but Athos insists that she first hear every charge aloud: poisoning Constance, sending poisoned wine to d'Artagnan under the friends' names, buying Constance's life as her price from the Cardinal, and deceiving Athos when she married him. Milady cuts the raft's rope and dares them to judge her from the bank. Athos enters the flood and offers his hand, promising a public judge and daylight trial rather than another private killing. Milady refuses, tries to make d'Artagnan release Athos's safety line, and lets the current carry her raft over the weir. Her body is not found. The friends bury Constance beneath the convent roses, and Athos privately mourns Milady by the river despite all she did.

Back in Paris, Rochefort makes a sixth attempt to arrest or kill d'Artagnan, now under direct orders. D'Artagnan chooses to meet the Cardinal alone and presents the Cardinal's own signed immunity paper, explaining that Milady poisoned Constance but ultimately cut herself loose and died without being touched by the four friends. The Cardinal accepts the returned paper, burns it, and rewards d'Artagnan's courage and incorruptibility with a blank commission as an officer in the King's Musketeers. D'Artagnan refuses to put Rochefort's name on it. He offers the commission in turn to Athos, Porthos, and Aramis; all decline for reasons true to themselves, and Athos writes d'Artagnan's name. D'Artagnan finally receives the blue cloak he wanted, but it feels inseparable from the loss of Constance. The volume ends with all four friends walking together in blue while the Cardinal watches from above, alongside the gray cat that had been with him throughout his plotting.

## Cast as read from the pages

- **d'Artagnan:** A lean, dark-haired young man, roughly eighteen, usually dressed in worn mustard-brown clothes and riding a conspicuous pale yellow horse. He is the Gascon newcomer, Constance's lover, and the impulsive fourth friend who longs to become a Musketeer. He is brave, vain, emotionally open, and dangerously eager to be admired, but he accepts responsibility for his mistakes and refuses the final invitation to murder.
- **Athos:** A tall, dark-haired and bearded Musketeer in a black hat and blue cloak, grave and controlled. He is the moral and strategic center of the group and Milady's estranged husband, formerly a count. His old private judgment of Milady haunts him, so he insists on witnesses and lawful judgment when they meet again.
- **Porthos:** A huge, red-haired, red-mustached Musketeer in a flamboyant red-feathered hat, often eating or laughing. He is the loud, comic, physically powerful friend whose appetite never interferes with his loyalty or courage.
- **Aramis:** A slim, elegant blond Musketeer with a fine mustache and pale-feathered hat. He is clever, observant, and perpetually talking about entering the Church; his religious aspiration and courtly air distinguish him from the others.
- **Constance:** A small, young, brown-curled woman with freckles, usually shown in a gray dress and white cap. She is d'Artagnan's beloved and a trusted servant or messenger of the Queen. Her loyalty requires hiding in the convent; her loneliness and instinctive kindness make Milady's deception possible.
- **Milady / Lady de Winter:** A striking, pale-skinned woman with extremely long, pale-blond curls, blue eyes, elegant purple dresses, and later a black widow's dress. A fleur-de-lis is branded on her left shoulder. She is the Cardinal's operative, Athos's presumed-dead wife, a practiced manipulator, and the principal threat to d'Artagnan and Constance.
- **The Cardinal:** An older, thin man with a pointed gray beard, red robes and skullcap, a cross, and a gray cat often in his lap. He is the political power directing Rochefort and Milady against the Queen's circle. He is ruthless but also pragmatic enough to recognize courage and recruit rather than destroy d'Artagnan.
- **Rochefort:** A lean, dark-haired man with a narrow mustache, scarred cheek, black clothes, and a black hat with a red plume. He is the Cardinal's persistent field agent, responsible for surveillance, traps, pursuit, and repeated attempts against d'Artagnan.
- **The Queen:** Not present in person in these pages, but the political and personal loyalty around which the opening danger turns. Constance protects her secrets, and d'Artagnan and the Musketeers previously saved her.
- **The King:** Mostly an off-page authority during the siege. His leave is the obstacle that forces the friends into the Saint-Gervais exploit, and his Musketeer commission becomes d'Artagnan's final reward.

## Causal chain

The Cardinal remembers d'Artagnan as an enemy and wants the Queen's secrets, so he assigns Milady after Rochefort repeatedly fails. Rochefort's carriage trap accidentally lets d'Artagnan meet and impress Milady. Constance's sudden departure gives him secret information, and his hunger for Milady's attention makes him reveal enough for her to identify the hiding place. His later suspicion exposes Milady's brand, which lets Athos identify her as his supposedly dead wife and establishes how lethal she is.

War traps the friends at La Rochelle while Milady travels freely. The poisoned wine proves she is actively hunting d'Artagnan. Athos's eavesdropping reveals that the Cardinal has located Constance and licensed Milady's crimes; taking the immunity paper gives the friends future leverage, but it does not close Milady's half-day lead. Because only the King can release them, the friends deliberately perform a public feat at the bastion, win leave, and race north.

Constance's isolation makes her welcome a kind stranger. Milady's disguise wins her trust, and Constance innocently confirms her own identity. Rochefort's warning that the friends are close forces Milady to act. Constance recognizes d'Artagnan's horse and refuses abduction, so Milady poisons her instead. Constance's death drives the pursuit to the river. There, Athos refuses to repeat his original secret execution and offers Milady a trial; Milady refuses rescue and chooses the river rather than judgment.

Finally, Rochefort's ordered sixth attack brings d'Artagnan before the Cardinal. The seized immunity paper protects him and turns the Cardinal's own authority back on him. D'Artagnan's refusal to use it for revenge convinces the Cardinal that he is worth recruiting, producing the blue cloak d'Artagnan wanted from the beginning—but only after the choices made in pursuit of that ambition helped cost Constance her life.

## Backtracking, rereading, and guesses

No page required backtracking or rereading to understand the action, speaker, or consequence.

On pages 22–23, the exact act Milady is meant to perform after sailing to England is deliberately unnamed: the Cardinal calls it “the work we spoke of.” The signed paper's reference to murder makes the assignment plainly lethal, but the intended English target is not identified in these pages. I treated that as withheld future information, not a comprehension failure, because the immediate causal line—the Cardinal sends Milady away and pays her with Constance's location—is complete.

On pages 51–52, I inferred that Milady dies over the weir because the raft vanishes, the river is empty the next morning, and the surviving characters speak and behave as if she is dead. The missing body leaves a conventional possibility of survival, but the emotional and narrative meaning of the sequence is clear.

I did not misidentify any recurring character. Their silhouettes, clothing, hair, and repeated roles remained distinct even in crowded scenes and the storm sequence.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text failed at reading pace. Narration, speech order, and balloon attribution were consistently clear. The multi-person exchanges on pages 30–34 and 47–50 remained readable despite four similar uniforms because faces, positions, and balloon tails distinguished the speakers. The poison action on page 41 was legible without explanation: Milady opens the ring, drops powder into the red wine while Constance watches the gate, and then leaves Constance the dosed glass.

## What I expect next

This reads as a completed volume rather than a cliffhanger. If the story continues, I expect d'Artagnan to grow into his officer's commission while discovering that gaining the blue cloak cannot restore the simpler ambition he had before Constance's death. I expect the four friends to remain loyal but begin moving toward different futures: Aramis toward the Church, Porthos toward comfort and status, Athos toward some reckoning with his former title and grief, and d'Artagnan toward greater responsibility.

The Cardinal now appears less like a defeated villain than a dangerous patron who values d'Artagnan and may try to use him. Rochefort's rivalry also feels capable of becoming respect after six failed encounters. Milady's missing body leaves open a narrow possibility that she survived, but the volume presents her fall as a moral and emotional ending, so I would not require her return. The next major conflict could instead come from the uneasy fact that the friends now serve a system whose most powerful man authorized the crimes they just survived.
