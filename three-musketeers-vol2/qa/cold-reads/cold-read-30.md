# Cold read — canonical pages 1–30

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without documentation. I followed the cast, the trap around d’Artagnan, Milady’s connection to Athos, the move to La Rochelle, and the friends’ emerging counterplan at normal reading pace.

## Retell of the story so far

Paris, the summer after the Queen’s ball: d’Artagnan is an eighteen-year-old Gascon who has become inseparable from Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, three King’s Musketeers. He is the only one without their blue cloak. The four previously saved the Queen from the Cardinal, and d’Artagnan’s name has remained in the Cardinal’s memory. D’Artagnan is in love with Constance, the Queen’s maid, but she has disappeared into hiding with the Queen’s secrets.

The Cardinal decides ordinary force has failed against the four friends. His agent Rochefort proposes more men, but the Cardinal instead calls in Milady, a beautiful Englishwoman also known as Lady de Winter. He orders her to find Constance through d’Artagnan and then remove the boy. Milady arranges a carriage accident in d’Artagnan’s path. He stops the runaway horses, believes he has rescued her, and accepts an invitation to her home.

Constance leaves Paris for a northern convent because the Cardinal’s men keep questioning her about the Queen’s letters. Before she goes, she warns d’Artagnan that Milady’s kindness is a trap, asks him to keep her ring on, and makes him promise to wait until the Queen says Constance is safe before coming after her.

D’Artagnan nevertheless visits Milady. She draws from him the story of the Queen’s diamonds and then, by flattering his loneliness and fascination with her, learns that Constance is hidden in a convent somewhere in the north. D’Artagnan becomes so absorbed in Milady that he neglects his friends. When he finally returns to the tavern and describes her, Athos recognizes something in the description, though he initially says he does not know the name Lady de Winter.

On d’Artagnan’s next visit, he discovers Rochefort with Milady and realizes she engineered the carriage incident and has been reporting to the Cardinal. He confronts her with a knife. During the struggle he sees a fleur-de-lis brand on her left shoulder. Milady tells him that nobody who has seen it remains alive, but Rochefort blocks the door rather than catching him, so d’Artagnan escapes through the window.

D’Artagnan tells Athos everything. Athos reveals that he was once a count who married a mysterious, extraordinarily beautiful woman. When an accident exposed the same criminal brand on her shoulder, he judged and hanged her himself. She survived, and Athos abandoned his title and became a Musketeer. He admits that this woman was Milady and warns that she will keep her threat against d’Artagnan. Because d’Artagnan already told her about Constance’s northern convent, the Cardinal can now locate it.

War begins at La Rochelle. The King and the Musketeers march to the siege, with the Cardinal accompanying them despite having said he would remain in Paris. D’Artagnan volunteers with the King’s Guards so that he can stay near his three friends. He notices Milady’s black carriage at the rear of the Cardinal’s train.

At camp, a boy brings d’Artagnan a bottle of wine supposedly sent by Athos, Porthos, and Aramis. Athos stops him from drinking it because he never sent it. The four pour the wine on the grass, which blackens, proving it was poisoned. They understand that Milady is at the siege and that she is already trying to kill d’Artagnan. They also know ordinary letters are unsafe because the Cardinal reads the camp’s mail.

That night the Cardinal orders Athos, Porthos, and Aramis to escort him to the Red Dovecote inn, excluding d’Artagnan because he belongs to the Guards. At the inn, the three overhear the Cardinal meet Milady upstairs. He sends her by ship to England within the week to carry out unspecified deadly work. As her price, Milady demands both the Gascon and the woman he loves. The Cardinal tells her Constance is in the convent at Béthune and orders her to take Constance. He then gives Milady a signed paper protecting its bearer from punishment for acts done in France.

Athos pretends his horse has thrown a shoe so that he can remain behind when the others leave with the Cardinal. He confronts Milady privately, naming himself as the Count de la Fère, the husband who believed he had killed her. He takes the Cardinal’s paper and warns that he will not judge her alone a second time: if she approaches d’Artagnan or Constance, there will be witnesses.

Back at camp, Athos tells d’Artagnan that Milady will pass Béthune on her way to the northern coast and will be half a day ahead even if the four obtain leave immediately. D’Artagnan wants to pursue her at once, furious that Athos let her leave, but Athos refuses to repeat his earlier solitary judgment and points out that deserters will be shot on the road. The four need both privacy to plan and official leave from the King.

Porthos supplies the answer. He publicly wagers that the four friends can eat breakfast for an hour in the exposed bastion of Saint-Gervais, directly under the enemy city’s guns. The whole army bets against them and watches as they walk out with food and swords. In the ruined bastion, where nobody can overhear, Athos shows the others the Cardinal’s paper. Aramis grasps how much impunity it grants, d’Artagnan wants to burn it and race to Constance, and Athos insists they keep it as leverage that may prevent the Cardinal from protecting Milady once they catch her.

## Cast

- **d’Artagnan:** A slim, young, black-haired Gascon in worn mustard-brown clothes and a battered gray hat, usually carrying a rapier and initially riding a pale yellow horse. He is the fourth friend but not yet a blue-cloaked Musketeer. Brave, quick, romantic, and dangerously impulsive, he loves Constance and becomes Milady’s target.

- **Athos / the Count de la Fère:** A tall, lean, dark-haired and dark-bearded Musketeer, usually dressed in black beneath his blue cloak and wearing a black hat with a white plume. He is the grave, perceptive leader of the friends. He is also Milady’s former husband, burdened by having once tried to execute her alone.

- **Porthos:** A very large, broad Musketeer with curly red hair, a great red mustache, red clothing, and a red-plumed hat. He is hearty, theatrical, fond of food, and capable of turning those qualities into the public wager that gives the friends privacy.

- **Aramis:** A slender, fair-haired Musketeer with a small mustache and a black hat with a white plume. He is thoughtful, dryly humorous, religiously inclined, and says he intends to enter the Church.

- **Constance:** A young, brown-curled woman first seen in a maid’s cap and apron, later leaving in a plain brown traveling cloak. She is the Queen’s trusted maid, the keeper or carrier of the Queen’s secret correspondence, and d’Artagnan’s beloved. She is hiding in a northern convent, later identified as the convent at Béthune.

- **Milady / Lady de Winter:** A striking, pale-blonde woman with long curls, blue eyes, rich purple gowns, and later a dark traveling cloak. A fleur-de-lis is branded on her left shoulder. She is an Englishwoman, the Cardinal’s spy and operative, Athos’s surviving wife, and a skilled manipulator and would-be murderer. She is being sent toward England while also hunting d’Artagnan and Constance.

- **The Cardinal:** An older, gaunt man in red robes and skullcap, often shown with a gray cat. He directs Rochefort and Milady, commands a private red-cloaked force, accompanies the army to La Rochelle, and uses secrecy, surveillance, abduction, poison, and political protection to destroy his enemies.

- **Rochefort:** A lean, dark-haired man in black with a mustache, a facial scar, and a black hat with a red feather. He is the Cardinal’s established field agent, has already confronted d’Artagnan repeatedly, helps set Milady’s trap, and reports between her and the Cardinal.

- **The Queen:** Not seen in these pages. She is the royal figure the four friends previously saved, Constance’s mistress, and the owner of the secrets the Cardinal wants.

- **The King:** Not seen directly. His war against La Rochelle moves the four friends to the siege, and his authority is the lawful leave they need before pursuing Milady.

## Causal chain

1. The four friends previously thwarted the Cardinal while saving the Queen, so the Cardinal remembers d’Artagnan and seeks a method subtler than more soldiers.
2. The Cardinal assigns Milady to use d’Artagnan to locate Constance and then eliminate him.
3. Milady stages a carriage emergency; d’Artagnan rescues her and accepts her invitation.
4. Constance flees to a northern convent and warns d’Artagnan against Milady, but d’Artagnan visits Milady anyway.
5. Milady wins d’Artagnan’s confidence and learns that Constance is in a convent in the north.
6. D’Artagnan discovers Rochefort in Milady’s house, exposes her allegiance, and sees the brand on her shoulder, making himself an immediate murder target.
7. The brand lets Athos identify Milady as the wife he believed he had executed; the friends now understand both her identity and the seriousness of her threat.
8. War carries the four friends, the Cardinal, and Milady to La Rochelle, where Milady tries to poison d’Artagnan.
9. Because the Cardinal monitors correspondence, the friends cannot safely warn Constance or the Queen by ordinary letter.
10. The Cardinal’s forced escort places Athos, Porthos, and Aramis beneath his secret meeting with Milady, allowing them to overhear her mission, her price, and Constance’s exact location.
11. Athos delays his departure, confronts Milady, and takes the Cardinal’s paper, gaining both leverage and proof of the Cardinal’s protection.
12. The need to pursue Milady without being shot as deserters forces the friends to seek leave from the King; the need to plan beyond the Cardinal’s ears forces them to find an exposed but private meeting place.
13. Porthos’s wager gets all four into the Saint-Gervais bastion under the eyes of the army, where they can plan and where the King may hear of their conspicuous courage.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

None. I did not need to return to an earlier page or guess which character was speaking or acting. Athos’s third-person account of “a friend” on page 16 is revealed plainly as his own history on page 17, so the brief delay reads as intentional disclosure rather than confusion.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text or attribution failed at reading pace. The prose on page 16 is the densest passage, but it remained readable and its transition from Athos’s narrated past to the present conversation was clear. The overheard conversation on pages 22–23 was also easy to attribute: Athos, Porthos, and Aramis are downstairs listening through the broken stovepipe while the Cardinal and Milady speak upstairs.

## What I expect next

The four will have to survive or repel enemy fire long enough to complete their hour in the Saint-Gervais bastion, turning the reckless breakfast into the conspicuous feat that helps them obtain leave from the King. In that temporary privacy they will settle a plan to intercept Milady on the northern road, protect Constance at Béthune, and stop Milady’s passage to England. The Cardinal’s signed paper will become their shield or bargaining weapon, while Athos’s refusal to kill Milady alone and d’Artagnan’s impatience will remain the main tension inside the group.
