# Cold read — pages 1–50

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without documentation. I could follow the characters, the Cardinal's scheme, Milady's manipulation of d'Artagnan and Constance, the danger at Bethune, and the pursuit to the river on one normal-paced read. I did not misidentify any recurring character.

## Retell of the story so far

Paris, in the summer after the Queen's ball: d'Artagnan is still the young Gascon outsider among Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, the three King's Musketeers who have become his closest friends. He has the Queen's ring and the love of Constance, while Cardinal Richelieu remembers his name and wants him removed.

The Cardinal's agent Rochefort has failed four times to stop d'Artagnan. The Cardinal turns instead to Milady, Lady de Winter, ordering her to learn where Constance has gone and get d'Artagnan out of the way. Milady arranges a carriage accident on the river road. D'Artagnan stops her runaway horses and, not knowing who she is, accepts her invitation to visit.

That night Constance leaves on a secret mission for the Queen. She explains that the Cardinal's people keep asking who carries the Queen's letters and that the Queen is being hidden in an unnamed convent somewhere in the north. Constance warns d'Artagnan about Milady, then asks him not to seek her until the Queen sends word that she is safe. D'Artagnan promises.

Milady charms d'Artagnan into describing the Queen's ball and admitting that the woman he loves is hidden in a northern convent. He keeps returning to Milady and neglects both his friends and his promise to Constance. Athos becomes alarmed when d'Artagnan describes Milady and names her Lady de Winter.

D'Artagnan arrives early for another visit and overhears Milady reporting to Rochefort: she has learned that the girl is in a northern convent, and when she has finished with d'Artagnan, Rochefort may have him. D'Artagnan confronts her. During the struggle he sees a fleur-de-lis brand on her left shoulder. Milady says that nobody who sees it remains alive, but d'Artagnan escapes through a window and tells Athos.

Athos reveals that he was once the Comte de la Fère. He married a beautiful, mysterious woman and later discovered the same criminal brand on her shoulder. Believing he had the right to judge on his own land, he hanged her from a tree. When the cut rope and missing body showed she had survived, he abandoned his title and became Athos. Milady is his wife, and she has now threatened d'Artagnan and learned enough to endanger Constance.

War takes the four friends to the siege of La Rochelle, where the King is fighting a coastal city waiting for English help. D'Artagnan serves as a volunteer so he can remain beside the Musketeers. The Cardinal joins the campaign, and Milady follows in a black carriage. She sends d'Artagnan a bottle of wine falsely labeled as a gift from Athos, Porthos, and Aramis. Athos notices that his name is on a gift he never sent; the wine has killed the grass where it spilled. The friends realize Milady is near and cannot safely send a warning letter because the Cardinal reads mail leaving the camp.

The Cardinal orders Athos, Porthos, and Aramis to escort him secretly to the Red Dovecote, leaving d'Artagnan behind because he is not a Musketeer. At the inn, the three overhear the Cardinal instruct Milady to sail for England and carry out an unspecified mission. In exchange, he identifies Constance's hiding place: the convent at Bethune. Milady demands both Constance and d'Artagnan as her price. The Cardinal gives her a signed paper absolving its bearer of anything done for the good of France.

Athos follows Milady upstairs, confronts her, and takes the Cardinal's paper. He refuses to repeat his earlier private execution, telling her that the next judgment will have witnesses. Milady leaves immediately for the north. Athos returns to camp and warns d'Artagnan, but none of the four can leave the siege without royal permission.

To gain that permission and discuss the secret where nobody can listen, the four publicly wager that they will eat breakfast for an hour in the exposed bastion of Saint-Gervais under the enemy guns. While dining, they read the Cardinal's paper and decide to keep it as leverage. Enemy soldiers attack. The friends shoot back, push a damaged wall down onto the attackers, raise a flag so the King can identify the bastion, and remain for the full hour. They return to the army as heroes. The Cardinal understands that they overheard Bethune and expects the King to reward them with leave, so he sends Rochefort ahead to tell Milady that the four are coming.

The King grants leave that afternoon. D'Artagnan, Athos, Porthos, and Aramis ride north as hard as they can, changing horses and barely stopping. Rochefort remains ahead of them.

At Bethune, Constance is safe but lonely until Milady arrives disguised as a widow seeking protection from the Cardinal's men. Milady treats her with calculated kindness. Constance, unaware that this is Lady de Winter, reveals that the Queen will write to d'Artagnan when it is safe and that he will then come for her. She even repeats her warning to him about Milady. Rochefort reaches the convent at night and tells Milady that the four have royal leave and will arrive by noon. Milady orders her carriage for first light, intending to abduct Constance to the coast and England if d'Artagnan follows, or abandon her if he does not.

The next morning Constance sees d'Artagnan's distinctive yellow horse approaching and refuses to leave. Milady claims the riders are the Cardinal's men and proposes a toast to d'Artagnan before going out to meet them. While Constance watches the gate, Milady poisons her wine with powder from her ring. Constance drinks. Milady departs in her carriage just before the four arrive.

D'Artagnan finds Constance weak in the convent garden. She tells him that a pale-haired woman hid there and was kind to her, and she dies in his arms as Athos, Porthos, and Aramis gather around them. D'Artagnan understands that he gave Milady the clue that led her there. Athos refuses to let him bear the blame alone and points out that Milady poured the wine. The four pursue her carriage through a storm.

Near midnight they find the abandoned carriage and follow Milady to a ferry beside a dangerous weir. She has sent the ferryman away and threatens to cut the raft's rope if they approach. D'Artagnan wants to attack immediately, but Athos insists that she first hear every charge aloud. D'Artagnan accuses her of murdering Constance; Porthos accuses her of sending poisoned wine; Aramis accuses her of bargaining with the Cardinal for Constance's life; and Athos accuses her of deceiving him when they married, while saying that personal wrong is his alone to forgive.

Milady cuts the rope and places the violent river between herself and the four men. Athos enters the water, and d'Artagnan, Porthos, and Aramis form a human chain back to a post so nobody is swept away. Athos offers to bring Milady across for a trial by daylight, explicitly refusing another secret hanging. Milady tries to persuade d'Artagnan to release Athos and let him drown, claiming Athos would do the same to him. D'Artagnan refuses. The sequence ends with the chain holding in the flood and the confrontation unresolved.

## Cast

- **d'Artagnan:** An eighteen-year-old Gascon with shaggy black hair, a patched mustard-brown doublet, a broad battered hat, and a yellow horse. He is brave, impulsive, romantic, and not yet a Musketeer. He loves Constance, is accepted as an equal by the three Musketeers, and carries the guilt of having trusted Milady.
- **Athos / the Comte de la Fère:** A tall, lean, dark-haired, bearded Musketeer, usually in black beneath his blue cloak and white-plumed hat. He is controlled, perceptive, and burdened by the earlier private judgment of his wife. Milady is that wife. He now insists on witnesses and a daylight trial.
- **Porthos:** A very large, red-haired, extravagantly mustached Musketeer in red beneath his blue cloak and a red-plumed hat. He is comic, hungry, theatrical, physically powerful, and dependable in danger.
- **Aramis:** A slim, fair-haired, carefully dressed Musketeer with a pale feather. He talks about entering the Church, prays over Constance, and is dryly observant. He is the most clerical and polished of the group.
- **Constance:** A young, freckled brunette with curly hair under a white cap, usually dressed plainly in gray. She is d'Artagnan's beloved and the Queen's trusted messenger. Her secrecy and loyalty make her a target. Milady poisons her at Bethune just before d'Artagnan reaches her.
- **Milady / Lady de Winter:** A beautiful, pale-blonde woman with long curls, icy eyes, elegant purple gowns, and later a black widow's dress. A fleur-de-lis is branded on her left shoulder. She is the Cardinal's operative, Athos's presumed-dead wife, and a highly skilled manipulator who uses charm, lies, poison, and murder.
- **Cardinal Richelieu:** An elderly, gaunt man in red robes and skullcap, usually composed and often accompanied by a gray cat. He directs the campaign against the heroes, commissions Milady, reveals Constance's location, and gives Milady a signed protection for crimes committed in France's name.
- **Rochefort:** A lean, dark-haired man in black with a mustache and a scar, often wearing a black hat with a red feather. He is the Cardinal's field agent and Milady's collaborator. He scouts, reports, and rides ahead to warn her that the four friends are coming.
- **The Queen:** Not shown in these pages, but central to the danger. Constance carries her letters and is hidden while serving her. The Cardinal is trying to discover and stop her secret communications.
- **The King:** Not shown closely, but his authority matters. He commands the siege force and rewards the four men's feat at the bastion with the leave they need to pursue Milady.

## Causal chain

1. The Cardinal remembers d'Artagnan's role in the Queen's earlier victory and wants him neutralized.
2. Rochefort's repeated failures lead the Cardinal to send Milady after d'Artagnan and Constance.
3. Milady stages the runaway-carriage encounter, earning d'Artagnan's trust and an invitation into his confidence.
4. D'Artagnan tells Milady that Constance is hidden in a northern convent.
5. He overhears Milady's report, discovers her brand, and tells Athos, who identifies her as his surviving wife.
6. At La Rochelle, Milady's forged gift exposes her continuing attempt to murder d'Artagnan.
7. Athos, Porthos, and Aramis overhear the Cardinal name Bethune and authorize Milady's mission.
8. Athos takes the Cardinal's immunity paper, but Milady leaves before the friends can lawfully quit the siege.
9. The four stage the bastion breakfast to speak privately and win conspicuous royal favor.
10. The King grants them leave, while the Cardinal sends Rochefort ahead to warn Milady.
11. Milady reaches Bethune first, disguises herself, and wins Constance's trust.
12. Rochefort's warning tells Milady the four are close; Constance's refusal to flee forces Milady to poison her and escape alone.
13. Constance's death turns the rescue into a pursuit and makes each hero's earlier encounter with Milady part of a public charge against her.
14. Milady tries to escape across the river and separate the men, but they literally hold together while Athos offers her a witnessed trial rather than a private execution.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

None. I did not need to return to an earlier page or consult anything outside the proofs. The exact physical method of crossing the flood on pages 49–50 is supplied by both narration and image: Athos leads, the other three form a chain to the fixed post, and d'Artagnan refuses to release him.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text failed at reading pace, and no speech attribution was unclear. The recurring characters remained visually distinct through changes of weather, location, and dress. The pale-haired woman in widow's black at Bethune was immediately recognizable as Milady even though Constance did not recognize her.

## What I expect next

I expect the four men to get Milady safely across the flood and subject her to the witnessed judgment Athos has promised, with the Cardinal's signed paper serving as evidence or leverage. Milady will continue trying to split the men—especially by turning d'Artagnan's grief and Athos's past against them—and Rochefort or the Cardinal's soldiers may still intervene. After the immediate confrontation, I expect the story to reckon with Constance's death, d'Artagnan's guilt, and whether the Cardinal can evade responsibility for enabling Milady.
