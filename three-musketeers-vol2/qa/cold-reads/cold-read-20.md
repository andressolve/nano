APPROVED

The story lands without documentation. I could follow the identities, loyalties, trap, revelation, and move to La Rochelle at normal reading pace. The sequence ends on a clear new threat: Milady is with the Cardinal's train and has already tried to poison d'Artagnan.

## Retell of the story so far

Paris, the summer after the Queen's ball. D'Artagnan is an eighteen-year-old Gascon who has come to Paris, befriended Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, fought beside them, helped save the Queen from the Cardinal, and fallen in love with Constance. The Queen has given him a ring, and Constance has given him her smile. The Cardinal has learned his name and remembered it.

D'Artagnan trains with his three friends. He jokes that nobody in Paris has tried to kill him for a month and wants a blue Musketeer cloak of his own. Across the river, the Cardinal tells Rochefort that direct attacks on the boy have failed. He gives Milady a subtler assignment: use Constance, who carries the Queen's secrets, to remove d'Artagnan.

Milady and Rochefort stage a runaway-carriage accident. D'Artagnan rides after the carriage and stops its horses, believing he has rescued a beautiful stranger. She introduces herself as Lady de Winter, called Milady in Paris, and invites him to visit. That night, Constance tells d'Artagnan that the Queen is sending her away to hide in an unknown northern convent because the Cardinal's people keep questioning those who carry the Queen's letters. D'Artagnan tells Constance about Milady despite Constance's warning that palace society knows Milady's marriages and who pays for them. Constance leaves after making him promise to come only when the Queen says it is safe.

D'Artagnan visits Milady and tells her about the diamonds and the Queen's ball. Flattered by her attention and increasingly infatuated, he also lets slip that the girl he loves is hiding in a convent in the north. He returns to Milady day after day, neglecting sword practice and his friends. When Athos asks for a description, d'Artagnan describes Milady but says Athos does not know her by that name.

On the next visit, d'Artagnan discovers Rochefort with Milady and understands that the carriage accident and invitation were a Cardinalist trap. He confronts Milady and exposes the deception. During the struggle, her dress slips and reveals a lily brand on her left shoulder. Milady says no one who sees it remains alive, while Rochefort blocks the door. D'Artagnan escapes through the window by swinging down on a curtain into a hay cart.

He runs to Athos and admits that he told Milady about Constance's northern convent. He also reports the lily brand and Milady's death threat. Athos recognizes the mark. He tells d'Artagnan about a count who married a beautiful sixteen-year-old with pale-gold hair, discovered the same brand, hanged her as a convicted thief, and abandoned his title when her body later disappeared. D'Artagnan realizes the count was Athos. Athos confirms it: the count is dead, but the woman survived and has become Milady.

Athos warns that Milady keeps promises of vengeance, and the friends resolve to eat and drink only what they share. They also understand that the Cardinal can narrow the search for Constance to northern convents. Before they can act in Paris, the King orders the Musketeers to war at La Rochelle, and the Cardinal travels with the army.

At the siege, a boy hands d'Artagnan a bottle supposedly sent by Athos, Porthos, and Aramis. Athos stops him from drinking because he never sent it. They pour the wine onto grass, which blackens, confirming poison. Athos then points out the black carriage at the rear of the Cardinal's train: Milady has followed them. D'Artagnan wants to warn the Queen so Constance can be moved, but Aramis warns that every letter leaving camp passes the Cardinal's desk and would reveal any convent named in it.

## Cast

- **D'Artagnan:** An eighteen-year-old Gascon with shaggy black hair, a battered feathered hat, worn mustard-brown clothes, and a yellow horse. He is brave, impulsive, hungry for recognition, in love with Constance, and eager to join the Musketeers. His weakness here is that Milady's attention makes him boast and betray a secret.
- **Athos:** A tall, dark-haired, bearded Musketeer in a blue cloak, usually dressed in black beneath it. He is grave, observant, and the most guarded of the friends. He was once a count and Milady's husband; he believed he had executed her after discovering her brand.
- **Porthos:** A broad, heavyset Musketeer with red-brown curls, a large moustache, an extravagant red-plumed hat, and red clothes beneath his blue cloak. He is hearty, amused, and proud of his quick promotion.
- **Aramis:** A slim, fair-haired Musketeer with a neat moustache and white-plumed hat. He is witty, thoughtful, religiously inclined, and alert to the practical danger of sending a letter through the Cardinal's network.
- **Constance:** A young brunette woman with curls, usually in a white cap and modest servant's dress. She carries the Queen's letters and secrets, loves d'Artagnan, and is sent into hiding in an unknown northern convent.
- **Milady / Lady de Winter:** A striking pale blonde with very long curls, blue eyes, rich purple dresses, and a lily brand burned into her left shoulder. She works for the Cardinal, uses charm and intimacy as weapons, has a concealed criminal past, and is the woman Athos married and thought dead.
- **The Cardinal:** An elderly, sharp-faced man in red robes and skullcap, often shown with a gray cat. He directs the campaign against the Queen's agents and chooses manipulation after force fails.
- **Rochefort:** A lean, dark-haired man with a moustache, facial scar, black clothes, and a red feather. He is the Cardinal's operative, has already tried repeatedly to stop d'Artagnan, engineers the runaway carriage, helps spring Milady's trap, and travels with her.
- **The Queen:** Not seen in these pages. She is the powerful woman whom the four friends previously saved and whose secrets Constance carries.
- **The King:** Not seen directly. He orders the army and Musketeers to besiege La Rochelle.

## Causal chain

The four friends save the Queen, so the Cardinal learns d'Artagnan's name and regards him as an obstacle. Rochefort's repeated attacks fail, so the Cardinal sends Milady to exploit d'Artagnan's youth and attachment to Constance. Rochefort creates a false rescue, giving Milady a convincing reason to invite d'Artagnan into her confidence. Constance's sudden departure leaves him lonely and gives him a secret worth extracting. Milady's attention makes him return, neglect his friends, and reveal the northern-convent clue. He eventually sees Rochefort in her rooms, recognizes the trap, and in the confrontation discovers her brand. The brand connects her to Athos's supposedly dead wife and establishes why her threat is credible. D'Artagnan's disclosure puts Constance in danger, but the King's march removes the friends from Paris before they can search. Milady follows the Cardinal's army, attempts to poison d'Artagnan under his friends' names, and makes direct communication with the Queen dangerous because the Cardinal controls outgoing letters.

## What I expect next

The friends will need a way to warn the Queen or locate Constance without naming the convent in a letter the Cardinal can intercept. At the same time, they will have to survive the siege and guard d'Artagnan from further attacks. I expect Milady to use the Cardinal's train, Rochefort, or another forged message to isolate d'Artagnan, while Athos's recognition of her gives the four friends their best chance to anticipate how she works.
