## PAGE 1 — Four friends on the bridge

### Reader turn
Four friends walk across a Paris bridge straight at the reader, and only three of them wear the blue cloak.

### Ordered moments
1. One image, the whole page, about 100%. A hazy summer morning on a stone bridge lined with tall houses, grey-blue slate roofs climbing to the sky, the brown river shining below. Walking toward the viewer, four abreast, large in the lower half, from LEFT to RIGHT: Athos, tall and still; Porthos, enormous, biting an apple; Aramis, slim and neat; and on the far RIGHT, a head shorter than all of them, the boy in faded ochre, talking with one hand up, leading the old buttercup-yellow horse by the bridle just behind him. Three blue cassocks with white crosses in a row, then one ochre doublet: the difference must read at a glance. The three Musketeers smile and are plainly not listening. Nobody has a balloon.
2. The title card sits in the open sky of the upper third, two lines, large and calm, no box. The caption is a tail-free matte field along the lower edge over the cobbles, never over a face or a hand.

TITLE CARD: `THE THREE MUSKETEERS`
