## PAGE 1 — Four friends on the bridge

### Reader turn
Four friends walk across a Paris bridge straight at the reader, and only three of them wear the blue cloak.

### Ordered moments
1. One image, the whole page, about 100%. A hazy summer morning on a stone bridge lined with tall houses, grey-blue slate roofs climbing to the sky, the brown river shining below. Walking toward the viewer, four abreast, large in the lower half, from LEFT to RIGHT: Athos, tall and still; Porthos, enormous, biting an apple; Aramis, slim and neat; and on the far RIGHT, a head shorter than all of them, the boy in faded ochre, talking with one hand up, leading the old buttercup-yellow horse by the bridle just behind him. Three blue cassocks with white crosses in a row, then one ochre doublet: the difference must read at a glance. The three Musketeers smile and are plainly not listening. Nobody has a balloon.
2. The title card sits in the open sky of the upper third, two lines, large and calm, no box. The caption is a tail-free matte field along the lower edge over the cobbles, never over a face or a hand.

### Exact strings
TITLE CARD: `THE THREE MUSKETEERS`
TITLE CARD: `Milady`
CAPTION: `Paris, the summer after the Queen's ball. Four friends — and only three of them wore the blue cloak.`

Render exactly these 3 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

Athos, not attached, drawn from words: the tallest man, long pale face, short dark pointed beard and neat moustache, straight dark hair to the shoulder, grey hooded eyes; blue cassock open over black, black hat with one white plume, steel hilt, no sling; hands still. Aramis, not attached, drawn from words: slim, fine oval face, blond curls to the collar, small blond moustache with a tiny tuft, white lace collar over dove grey under the blue cassock, black hat with one white plume. PROSE FIELD 1: `In the spring he had ridden to Paris on a yellow horse that everyone laughed at. In one month he had made three friends, Athos, Porthos and Aramis, the King's Musketeers, and the four of them had sworn: all for one, and one for all. Together they had saved the Queen from the Cardinal, the most powerful man in France. The Queen had given the boy a ring. Constance, the girl downstairs, had given him her smile.`
