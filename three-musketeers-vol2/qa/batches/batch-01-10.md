# Sequence gate — pages 1–10

## Blind-pass sequence

Paris is quiet in the summer after the Queen’s ball. D’Artagnan remains the one friend without a blue Musketeer cloak: the young Gascon has won Athos, Porthos, and Aramis as friends, helped save the Queen, received her ring and Constance’s affection, and become a name the Cardinal remembers. A peaceful month leaves him restless. He spars and jokes with the three Musketeers, presses Porthos about earning a cloak, and tells Athos he is tired of waiting; Athos shares the unease.

Across the river, Rochefort admits that four attempts to stop the boy have failed. The Cardinal changes tactics and gives Milady a week to learn where Constance goes and remove d’Artagnan from the way. Milady arranges a trap: an anonymous note lures d’Artagnan to the river road, where Rochefort releases a carriage’s horses. D’Artagnan rides after the runaway carriage and stops it while Rochefort watches. The passenger is Milady, presenting herself as Lady de Winter. She rewards the rescue with intimate attention and a Thursday invitation to enter her house by the front door.

D’Artagnan excitedly tells Constance. She is leaving that night on the Queen’s business because the Cardinal’s people are searching for the Queen’s letters; she will hide in an unnamed northern convent. D’Artagnan offers to follow, but she refuses to reveal the place and explains that secrecy is the point. When he identifies his new admirer as Lady de Winter—Milady—Constance warns that everyone at the palace knows her marriage and who pays for it, and that her kindness has a motive. At their farewell, Constance tells him to keep the attic, keep the Queen’s ring on, keep his sword sheathed until she returns, and come for her only when the Queen says she is safe. He promises to obey the first day the Queen gives word, then watches Constance’s carriage disappear.

On Thursday, a footman receives d’Artagnan at Milady’s house. Inside, Milady draws him out with wine and admiration. He tells her about the diamonds and the London ball, then—after only token resistance—reveals that Constance is in a convent somewhere in the north and believes the Cardinal cannot reach her. Milady recognizes the value of the disclosure. D’Artagnan returns Friday, Saturday, and every day afterward, enthralled by her attention and telling her everything. He neglects his practice with the Musketeers twice; the final empty chair and the three friends no longer looking toward the door show the immediate emotional cost of his fixation.

## Comparison with the exact script

The rendered batch preserves the intended causal chain without requiring inference: d’Artagnan’s restlessness makes Milady’s staged opportunity irresistible; the carriage rescue earns the invitation; Constance’s warning establishes the danger before he walks into it; Milady extracts the northern-convent clue; and his repeated returns produce a visible cost in the abandoned practice and empty tavern chair.

The material story, exact text, and speaker attribution match the script across all ten pages. Named characters remain distinct and stable. Consequential visual threads—the three blue cloaks against d’Artagnan’s ochre, the yellow horse, Rochefort’s black dress and red feather, Milady’s plum dress and dark ring, Constance’s bundle and departing coach, and the Queen’s ring carried in text and dialogue—remain intelligible across page turns. The river road continues cleanly from the bolting carriage to the stopped horses, and Constance’s night warning advances clearly into her dawn departure.

Focal intent and page turns land throughout: the missing cloak, the Cardinal’s memory, the change of tactics, the foreknown trap, Milady’s invitation, Constance’s warning and departure, the disclosure, and the empty chair each provide a distinct forward pull. The batch varies spectacle, illustrated prose, and dramatic pages. Its dramatic stretch changes location and function often enough not to become a wall, while the illustrated-prose pages frame the movement and give both context and aftermath. Page 10 compresses repeated visits intentionally, but the concrete missed meetings and friends who have stopped waiting keep it from reading as mere summary. No consequential beat in this batch is left without a readable emotional or causal consequence.
