# Sequence gate — canonical pages 11–20

## Blind sequence read

At the tavern, d'Artagnan's delighted account of Lady de Winter makes Athos quietly suspicious. The next day d'Artagnan arrives early with flowers, overhears Milady and Rochefort confirm that she serves the Cardinal and has used him to locate Constance, confronts her, sees the branded lily on her left shoulder, and escapes their trap through the window into a hay cart.

He goes directly to Athos and reports Milady's allegiance, threat, and brand. The left-shoulder detail forces Athos to reveal that he once married this woman, judged and hanged her after discovering the lily, then abandoned his title when he found that she had survived. D'Artagnan recognizes that the unnamed count was Athos. Athos accepts the identification only by separating his dead former identity from the man he is now, then turns the revelation into action: the friends will control everything d'Artagnan eats and drinks, and they must find Constance before the Cardinal does.

Porthos and Aramis bring news that the King, the Musketeers, and the Cardinal are all leaving for war at La Rochelle. The march carries the friends away from Paris while a black carriage with drawn plum curtains follows the Cardinal's train; Athos sees it, but d'Artagnan does not. At the siege camp, a bottle falsely labeled as a gift from Athos, Porthos, and Aramis nearly reaches d'Artagnan's lips. Athos stops him, and the wine blackens the grass. The poison confirms that the carriage is Milady's and that she has followed them. D'Artagnan's impulse to warn the Queen and move Constance is blocked when Aramis explains that the Cardinal reads every letter leaving camp.

The focal line stays with d'Artagnan's discovery and its widening consequences, while Athos's confession deepens rather than displaces that line. Page turns consistently convert setup into consequence: suspicion into betrayal, entrapment into escape, the brand into Athos's history, history into a survival pact, the march into Milady's concealed arrival, and the false gift into proof of immediate danger.

Pacing varies effectively among dramatic pages, a full-page spectacle, and two illustrated-prose pages. The historical and travel compression is purposeful and legible rather than a stretch of compressed summary. Consequential beats receive aftermath within the batch, especially the escape, Athos's revelation, the forced departure for war, and the attempted poisoning.
