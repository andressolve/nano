# Sequence gate — pages 51–58

## Uninterrupted sequence

In the storm, the four men drag one another back toward the landing while the raft and its lantern recede toward the weir. By morning the river is quiet and empty. Constance is buried at Béthune, d'Artagnan remains cold and exhausted rather than transformed by the ordeal, and Athos can acknowledge the loss only with his habitual “Good.”

Back in Paris, Rochefort summons d'Artagnan to the Cardinal alone. D'Artagnan accepts but takes the Cardinal's paper from Athos. Before the Cardinal, he answers the accusation surrounding the death at the ferry, names the Cardinal's responsibility for Constance's poisoning, denies that the four men killed the Cardinal's agent, and returns the signed authority that could shield him. The Cardinal burns it, then offers an unnamed commission in the King's Musketeers because the four cannot be bought.

At the tavern, d'Artagnan first offers the commission to Athos. Athos, Porthos, and Aramis each refuse it, and Athos writes d'Artagnan's name on it. Alone afterward, d'Artagnan wears the new blue cloak but measures his admission to the Musketeers against the loss of Constance. He lights the neglected lamp at her door and goes to find his friends. The four blue cloaks then walk abreast through Paris while the Cardinal watches from above.

## Gate judgment

The visible sequence matches the script in material story, text and attribution, named identity, focal ownership, and consequential continuity. The raft, severed ferry rope, yellow horse, Cardinal's signed paper, blank commission, new blue cloak, lamp, four companions, and watching Cardinal all carry cleanly across their relevant page turns.

The death receives a full quiet aftermath before the return to Paris, and d'Artagnan's public advancement receives a private emotional reckoning before the final group image. Pages 53–56 form a sustained dramatic run, but the gate confrontation, moral confrontation, Cardinal's counter-move, and tavern decision each perform a distinct beat; the surrounding spectacle and illustrated-prose pages provide effective variation. No stretch reads as compressed summary, and no consequential beat is left without aftermath.
