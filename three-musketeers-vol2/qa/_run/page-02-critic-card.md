**Location:** the attic.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Night. The attic under the roof: a bed, a
table, one candle, a sword on a nail. The boy at the open window in his shirt

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dark wall, never over the boy)*
> "His name was d'Artagnan. He was eighteen, and a Gascon — which only means he came from Gascony, in the far south of France, where boys are brought up on garlic and pride and told never to back down from anyone.
>
> In the spring he had ridden to Paris on a yellow horse that everyone laughed at. In one month he had made three friends, Athos, Porthos and Aramis, the King's Musketeers, and the four of them had sworn: all for one, and one for all. Together they had saved the Queen from the Cardinal, the most powerful man in France. The Queen had given the boy a ring. Constance, the girl downstairs, had given him her smile.
>
> And the Cardinal had read the boy's name off a letter, and remembered it."

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Prose transcription fails
All three prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, in one prose field, with no extra text; a paragraph or sentence that does not read, is altered, dropped, doubled or reordered is blocking, and a paragraph that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. Any balloon or caption is blocking.

### C2 — The mode does not land
The page must read as illustrated prose: the field owns the reading, set over plain dark wall, never over the boy's face or hands, beside one quiet candlelit picture of the boy at the attic window looking down the stair to a lit door, with a smaller memory of four swords meeting. Prose over the face, or a page that reads as a dialogue page, is blocking.

### C3 — Identity
The boy reads as the beardless, black-haired youth, alone, in shirt sleeves. A beard, blue clothing, or a second person in the attic or on the stair is blocking. Whether the ring is visible is nonblocking.

### C4 — Generation integrity
The memory must show four blades and four hands with no faces and no blood; a different number of swords there is blocking because the oath is four. Background furniture detail is not.
