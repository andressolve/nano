## Page intent

## PAGE 53

A dramatic page at a gate of Paris, in the evening. The four friends ride home under the arch and find Rochefort waiting for them with the Cardinal's guards and an order. Porthos offers all four of them; the boy refuses the help. The dominant turn is the arrest: Rochefort's hand on d'Artagnan's shoulder while the boy waves his friends back, and Rochefort, who has lost to him five times, finally gets to win. The page belongs to the boy's choice, not to Rochefort's triumph: he goes on his own, and before he goes he turns to Athos, holds out his hand, and asks for the Cardinal's paper, because this part is his to do. Athos puts it into his open hand without argument. Nobody draws a sword and nobody is chained. The reader turns because the boy is walking into the Cardinal's study carrying the Cardinal's own paper in his coat.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 53 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a gate of Paris.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Evening. The four riding in under a Paris gate; ROCHEFORT
waiting in the arch with four red-cloaked guards.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Monsieur d'Artagnan. His Eminence wants you. Alone."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Then His Eminence can have all four of us."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Rochefort's hand on the boy's shoulder; the boy
waving his friends back.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "No. I'll go. Wait for me at the tavern."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Meung, the Carmelites, the alley, Calais, a window. Five times, Gascon. This is the sixth, and this time I have an order."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The boy turning back to Athos, his hand out.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Athos. Give me the Cardinal's paper. This part is mine to do."
>
> **ATHOS:** "This was always yours to finish."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is walking to the Cardinal with the Cardinal's own paper in his coat.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 53 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All six lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order: Rochefort's two from the man in black, Porthos's one from the huge rider with red plumes, the boy's two from the beardless boy in ochre, Athos's one from the tall bearded man in blue. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, as "Athos." does) is owned even if its tail wanders. The folded paper carries no scripted words.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read: the four ride in and are stopped, Rochefort's hand is on the boy's shoulder as the boy waves his friends back, the boy takes the paper from Athos. If the hand on the shoulder is not the image the eye finds first and longest, or the page reads as a fight, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Attribution across the gate exchange
Rochefort's first line must precede Porthos's reply; the boy's "No. I'll go" must precede Rochefort's "Five times" speech. Each hangs from its own speaker's mouth by tail, not merely making sense as his.

### C4 — Athos and Rochefort fail separation
Athos tall with a dark pointed beard in blue with a white plume; Rochefort shorter, thin moustache only, all black with one red feather, scar on the left cheek only. Two bearded dark men in the same costume, or a scar on the right cheek, is blocking.

### C5 — d'Artagnan fails identity or state
Beardless, black hair in his eyes, ochre with no cloak and no blue. A moustache or a blue cassock is blocking.

### C6 — Staging that breaks the story
No chains, no sword drawn on the boy, no blood. Exactly one yellow horse. The number of guards is nonblocking.
