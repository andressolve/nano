## Page intent

## PAGE 40

A dramatic page. The dominant turn: Constance sees the yellow horse and refuses to run. In the morning she leans out of her window with both hands on the sill and, far down the long straight road, sees four riders, the first on a horse no one else in France would ride. Behind her the widow, cloak already on, urges her down to the carriage, calling them the Cardinal's men; Constance plainly refuses and stays. The widow goes very still, and then smiles again and offers a toast. The reader turns because that smile has meant harm before. Subordinate: the room's furniture and the riders' faces, which are far too small to matter.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 40 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Constance's room at the convent, with its window on the road.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Morning. CONSTANCE at her window, both hands on
the sill, leaning out; far down the long straight road, four riders, the one
in front on a horse of unmistakable bright yellow.

> **CONSTANCE:** "It's him! Look, that's his horse! Nobody else in France would ride a horse that colour!"

**Panel 2 — 30%.** MILADY behind her in the room, travelling cloak on, urgent.

> **MILADY:** "Those are the Cardinal's men, child. Come down to my carriage, quickly, before they see you."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "No. It's d'Artagnan. I'm staying right here."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Milady, very still for a moment; then smiling again.

> **MILADY:** "Then let's drink to him first."

**Why you turn the page:** Milady has smiled again, and the reader knows what that means.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or

focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 40 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All four strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
Constance at the window must plainly see a bright yellow horse leading riders far down the road, and then refuse to go with the widow. If the yellow horse cannot be found, or the window panel does not own the page by eye, it is blocking. The number of riders is nonblocking if the yellow horse leads.

### C3 — Attribution
The widow's urging and Constance's refusal must each hang from the right mouth; the refusal read as the widow's is blocking. The widow's toast must be hers alone in the last beat.

### C4 — The two women collide
Tall, pale gold hair bound under a black veil, grey cloak against small, freckled, dark curls under a white cap, light grey with a white collar. Confusion between them, an apron on Constance, or plum on the widow is blocking.

### C5 — Generation integrity
A second yellow horse, a duplicated named figure, or gross anatomy on a focal face or Constance's hands on the sill is blocking. Rider detail at distance is not.
