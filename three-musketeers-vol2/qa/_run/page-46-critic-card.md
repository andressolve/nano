## Page intent

## PAGE 46

An illustrated-prose page. The storm owns it: four riders flat out along a flooded road at night under lashing rain and lightning, hatless and streaming, the yellow horse in front. The prose field rides in the storm sky above them and tells the chase: the storm, the wheel marks turning to rivers, the carriage found tipped and empty, the coachman fled, and ahead a lantern swinging on a ferry while a weir roars downstream. A smaller panel below shows that empty black carriage in a ditch by the river bank with its door hanging open and, far off through the rain, the ferry lantern. The dominant turn is the storm, the abandoned carriage, the ferry by the weir. The reader turns because she is at the river and there is only one ferry. Nobody speaks; the carriage stays subordinate to the ride.

>
> Ahead, through the rain, a lantern was swinging on a ferry by the river, and downstream, in the dark, a weir was roaring, where the whole river fell over a wall of stones."

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 46 — card

### C1 — Transcription

Every scripted prose paragraph reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no extra text anywhere; a paragraph that does not read is blocking, a paragraph that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant of the same word are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. The run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue is nonblocking.

### C2 — The ride owns the page

The storm ride must be the image the eye finds first, in the illustrated-prose mode, with the prose field sitting in the sky and never across a rider or a horse. Blocking if the carriage panel takes over the page or the prose covers the riders; finish of the rain and lightning is nonblocking.

### C3 — Four riders, the yellow horse first

The reader must see four riders, hatless, with the yellow horse leading. Blocking if the yellow horse is missing or a second yellow animal appears, or if a rider count other than four is plain at a glance. Individual faces at this distance are nonblocking.

### C4 — The empty carriage and the far lantern

The carriage must read as tipped, door open, empty, with one lantern far off at the river. Blocking if a figure is in or by the carriage or if the ferry lantern is absent; the exact look of the carriage is nonblocking.
