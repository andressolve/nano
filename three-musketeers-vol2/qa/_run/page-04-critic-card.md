## Page intent

## PAGE 4

Dramatic. Across the river, in the Cardinal's red study with no window and one candle, the Cardinal strokes his grey cat while Rochefort, in black, hat in hand, begs for ten men after four failures. The Cardinal refuses: he will try something else. Then the dominant moment: a tall woman steps out of the shadow of the hangings into the candlelight, pale gold ringlets and deep plum silk, turning a dark-stoned ring with her thumb, and Rochefort stiffens. She is Milady; she promises the boy in a week, and with a smile tells Rochefort her horses will bolt tomorrow at noon. The one dominant turn: the Cardinal sets Milady on the boy, and she plans the runaway horses. The reader turns knowing what will happen at noon, while the boy does not.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Cardinal's study.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A red room with no window, one candle. THE CARDINAL in
his chair, a grey cat on his lap, his hand stroking it. Before the desk,
ROCHEFORT, all in black, hat in hand, the pale scar down his left cheek.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the red hangings)*
> "Across the river, in his red room, the Cardinal sat with Rochefort, his man, who had tried to stop the boy four times."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Meung, the Carmelites, the alley, Calais. Four times, Eminence. Give me ten men—"
>
> **CARDINAL:** "You have had four times. I am going to try something else."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** From the shadow by the hangings, a tall woman
steps into the candlelight: pale ash-gold ringlets past her shoulders, a
gown of deep plum silk with silver lace, turning a dark-stoned ring on her
finger with her thumb. Rochefort stiffens.

> **CARDINAL:** "Milady. The girl downstairs from that boy carries the Queen's secrets. Find out where the girl goes, and get him out of my way."
>
> **MILADY:** "A boy of eighteen? Give me a week, Eminence. Boys tell me everything."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Milady, turning to Rochefort with a smile.

> **MILADY:** "Tomorrow at noon my horses will bolt on the river road. See that he's there to stop them."

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what is going to happen at noon, and the boy doesn't.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 4 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All six strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order: the caption tail-free first, then each line hanging from its own speaker's mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn lands by eye
The dominant moment must be Milady stepping out of the shadow into the candlelight, with the Cardinal seated and Rochefort stiff behind; the reader must see that the Cardinal is sending her after the boy. If she is lost in the room or the page reads as a conversation between two men, it is blocking.

### C3 — Attribution
Rochefort's "Four times" and the Cardinal's refusal; the Cardinal's order to Milady, then her "Give me a week"; then her plan for noon, said to Rochefort. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth; any line given to the wrong mouth, or a balloon on silent Rochefort in the second or third moment, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
The Cardinal seated in red with a grey pointed beard and the grey cat; Rochefort standing in black with a thin black moustache and a scar on his left cheek only; Milady tall, very pale, pale gold ringlets past the shoulder, plum silk, a dark-stoned ring. A scar on Rochefort's right cheek, the Cardinal standing or out of red, or Milady in grey widow's dress is blocking.

### C5 — Continuity and integrity
A windowless room, one candle, exactly one cat. A window or daylight, a second cat, or gross anatomy on a speaker's face or Milady's ring hand is blocking. Desk clutter is not.
