## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the field behind the Carmelite convent.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Late afternoon in the long grass by a high
yellow wall. D'ARTAGNAN, left, lunging at ARAMIS with far too much energy;
Aramis, right, parrying with one hand, bored. PORTHOS sits on the stone
trough eating a chicken leg. ATHOS stands apart, both hands folded on his
sword pommel, not moving. The yellow horse drinks from the trough.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Aramis, a whole month, and nobody in Paris has tried to kill me. Not once!"
>
> **ARAMIS:** "Lunge less, Gascon. I'm trying to think about God."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The boy stopping, sword point dropping, pointing it at
Porthos's blue cassock with the white cross.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Porthos! When do I get a blue cloak like yours? I've earned it twice over."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "The King gives it, when you do something nobody in France can forget. I did it in a week. Of course, I'm me."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The boy turning to the tall still one by the wall.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Athos, tell them I'm not patient enough to wait for it."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Good. Nor was I."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy wants something nobody can forget, and somebody across the river is about to give him something.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 3 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All six strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, each hanging from its own speaker's mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Attribution across the three exchanges
The boy's complaint, then Aramis's "Lunge less"; the boy's question, then Porthos's reply about the King; the boy's appeal, then Athos's "Good. Nor was I." Each line must hang from its own speaker's mouth, each reply after its prompt; "Good. Nor was I." given to the boy or Porthos's answer given to anyone else is blocking.

### C3 — The turn lands
The boy, lunging and then pointing at a blue cassock, must own the page; the reader must grasp that he wants the blue cloak. If the bout reads as a real fight (someone hit, blood) it is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
The boy beardless, black-haired, in ochre with no blue; Aramis slim and blond with a small moustache; Porthos enormous with a red-brown waxed moustache and red plumes; Athos tall, still, dark pointed beard. The boy and Aramis drawn alike, or the boy in blue, is blocking.

### C5 — Generation integrity
A duplicated named figure, extra hands on a sword, or gross anatomy on a speaker's face is blocking. The horse's exact position and grass texture are not.
