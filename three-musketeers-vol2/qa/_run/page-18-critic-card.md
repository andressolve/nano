## Page intent

## PAGE 18

Illustrated prose. The army goes to war. The dominant image is the march on a dusty road: columns of soldiers and blue cassocks, the boy in ochre on the yellow horse among them, and far behind, after a column of red-cloaked guards, a black carriage with its plum curtains drawn. Athos, riding, has turned in his saddle to look back at it. Below, the destination: La Rochelle, a walled city on its harbour with the grey sea beyond, ringed by the King's trenches. The prose explains the siege, why the boy marches as a volunteer, and why the Cardinal goes — and ends on the carriage the boy never saw and Athos did. The dominant turn is that Milady's carriage goes with the army. The reader turns because she is in the camp, and the boy doesn't know.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 18 — card

### C1 — Prose transcription fails
All three paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no extra text (nothing on flags or carts). A paragraph that does not read is blocking; text that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order are what must match. No object carries scripted words.

### C2 — The carriage does not land
In the march, the eye must find the black carriage with drawn plum curtains far back behind red-cloaked guards, and Athos turned in his saddle looking back at it. If the carriage cannot be found or Athos faces forward, the turn fails. The mode is illustrated prose, the field over sky, never over riders.

### C3 — Identity
The boy in ochre on the only yellow horse; Athos with his dark pointed beard and white plume. A second yellow horse, or the boy in blue, is blocking.

### C4 — La Rochelle
The lower panel reads as a walled city by the grey sea with trenches round it; the count of tents, cannon and soldiers is nonblocking.
