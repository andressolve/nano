## Page intent

## PAGE 56

A dramatic page at the tavern table, lamplit. The boy throws the sealed commission down in front of Athos and tries to give it away: he will not wear the Cardinal's gift, and Athos has earned it ten times over. Athos refuses in three short sentences that carry his whole history. Porthos pushes it on to Aramis because officers have to read things; Aramis is leaving for the Church, next year, probably. The comedy is warm and quick and stays subordinate. The dominant turn is the last panel: Athos takes up the pen and writes on the empty line while the boy watches, and tells him that the blue is the King's, earned at the bastion in front of the whole army, and that he should wear it and make the Cardinal look at him in it. The reader turns because the boy's name is on the commission now.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 56 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order: two from the boy, three from Athos, one from Porthos, one from Aramis. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text is blocking, except the boy's name legible on the commission under Athos's pen, which repeats the spoken word and is nonblocking; any other word on the sheet is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, as "Aramis?" does) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read: the boy throws the commission down, Athos refuses, Porthos and Aramis refuse, Athos writes the name while the boy watches. If Athos writing with the boy watching is not the image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Attribution across the refusals
Athos's "Too much for Athos" hangs from Athos, not the boy; Porthos's line from the huge red-plumed man, Aramis's reply from the slim blond man; Athos's last two lines from Athos. A refusal owned by the wrong friend is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Athos: tallest, dark pointed beard, blue. Porthos: enormous, red-brown waxed moustache, red plumes. Aramis: slim, blond curls, small moustache, lace collar. The boy: beardless, black hair, ochre, no blue on this page. Two friends merged into one look, or the boy in blue, is blocking.

### C5 — Focal generation integrity
One sealed sheet, one quill in Athos's hand; his writing hand and holding hand well formed. A duplicated sheet or a malformed writing hand is blocking.
