## Page intent

## PAGE 8

Dramatic. Dawn, the street blue and empty, a plain coach at the door. The dominant moment is Constance in her brown travelling cloak with her hand on the boy's cheek while he tries not to look as if he minds; she asks for three promises and he jokes that he can keep two. From the coach window as it starts to move she makes him promise to come for her only when the Queen says she is safe, not one day before, and he promises. Then the coach goes down the empty street and the boy stands alone in the doorway watching. The one dominant turn: Constance leaves at dawn, and he promises to come only when the Queen says she is safe. The reader turns because she is gone, and on Thursday there is a front door.

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All five strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order: four lines each hanging from its own speaker's mouth by tail and placement, then the caption tail-free, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Attribution
Constance's three things, then the boy's "two of those"; Constance's "Promise me" from the coach window, then the boy's "I promise". The promise given to Constance, or her request to him, is blocking: the reader must know who promised.

### C3 — The turn lands by eye
Constance's hand on the boy's cheek at dawn must own the page, and the last moment must show the coach going and the boy alone in the doorway. If the farewell is not the dominant image or the boy is not left alone at the end, it is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and state
Constance small, freckled, dark curls under the white cap, in a brown travelling cloak; the boy beardless in ochre without blue. A black coach with plum curtains (Milady's) at the door, or Constance in her later costume, is blocking.

### C5 — Generation integrity
Her hand on his cheek reads as one clean hand; gross anatomy there or on either face is blocking. Street detail is not.
