## Page intent

## PAGE 38

A dramatic page. The dominant turn: Constance warns Milady against Lady de Winter, to her face. Walking arm in arm at evening, the widow asks about the boy, and Constance answers gladly that he will come. In the large middle panel Constance, worried for him, names Lady de Winter and repeats her warning, while the widow listens smiling and agrees she was right. The last close beat is the widow's face over Constance's shoulder with the smile gone, her thumb turning her dark ring, as Constance tells her she is not like that. The reader turns knowing exactly how much danger Constance has just talked herself into. Subordinate: the cloister and the evening light.

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All six strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. ### C2 — The turn does not land
The reader must see Constance name Lady de Winter to the widow's face and the widow agree, smiling, and then the smile gone over Constance's shoulder with her thumb on the ring. If the last beat's cold face is missing or the warning panel does not own the page by eye, it is blocking.

### C3 — Attribution of the exchanges
The widow's question and Constance's answer; Constance's two warning lines and the widow's reply; Constance's final line. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail or placement; the widow's reply read as Constance's, or "You're not like that" read as the widow's, is blocking.

### C4 — The two women collide

### C5 — Generation integrity
