## Page intent

## PAGE 54

A dramatic page in the Cardinal's study, red and dark, one candle. The Cardinal sits stroking the grey cat and tells the boy, quietly, that a lady in his service is dead and that a yellow horse was seen at the river. The boy, muddy and white with anger, answers with the truth: she poisoned Constance, the Cardinal gave her Constance as her price, and the four never touched her. The Cardinal's one defence, that he did not pour the wine, is left standing in the air. The dominant turn is the last panel: the boy lays the folded paper on the desk, opens it flat under the candle, and pushes it across, giving back the one thing that could have protected him for life, because he will not hide behind anything this man has signed. Rochefort stays silent by the door. The reader turns to see what the Cardinal does with his own words.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 54 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Cardinal's study.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** THE CARDINAL in his chair, the grey cat on his lap, his
hand stroking it. D'ARTAGNAN before the desk, muddy, hat in hand. Rochefort
by the door.

> **CARDINAL:** "A lady in my service is dead, at a ferry in the north."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "Four men were seen on the bank. One rode a yellow horse."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The boy, white with anger, leaning on the desk.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "She was in your service when she poisoned Constance. You gave her Constance as her price. Athos heard you."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "I named a convent. I did not pour the wine."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "And we never touched her. She cut her own rope."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** The boy laying a folded paper on the desk,
opening it flat under the candle, and pushing it across to the Cardinal.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "This is yours. It says whoever carries it did what he did on your order, for France."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I could hide behind it for the rest of my life. I won't hide behind anything you've signed."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy has just given back a paper that could have protected him from anything.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 54 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order: three from the seated man in red, four from the muddy boy; Rochefort by the door carries none. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text, including legible words on the paper, is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read: the Cardinal names the death, the boy accuses him, the boy pushes the paper across the desk to him. If the boy's hands sending the paper across the desk are not the image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Attribution in the accusation exchange
The boy's accusation, then the Cardinal's "I named a convent. I did not pour the wine.", then the boy's "And we never touched her." Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth; a Cardinal line owned by the boy, or the reverse, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity fails
The Cardinal: thin, grey pointed beard, red robe and skullcap, seated, grey cat on his lap. The boy: beardless, black hair, muddy ochre, no cloak. Rochefort, if visible: all black, red feather, silent. A standing or dark-haired Cardinal, or a second figure in red robes, is blocking.

### C5 — The study and the paper
One candle, no window, no daylight; the paper is a single cream sheet, unsealed, distinct from any map. A daylit room is blocking; the paper's exact fold is nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation integrity
Exactly two hands on the paper, both the boy's; the Cardinal's hand stays on the cat. A third hand on the paper or a malformed focal hand is blocking.
