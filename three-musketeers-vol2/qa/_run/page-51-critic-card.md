## Page intent

## PAGE 51

A spectacle page, one great image. The raft, small now, spins away into the dark and the rain toward the long white line of the weir, its lantern a single point of light, Milady kneeling on it with her back to the bank. In the near foreground the chain of four men hauls itself back toward the landing, each holding the next. One caption over the black water says she turned her back on the hand and the lantern went out. The dominant turn is the raft going into the dark toward the weir as the lantern goes out. Nothing is shown beyond the light vanishing; the four are safe and spent. The reader turns because it is over and none of them knows yet what that feels like.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 51 — card

### C1 — Transcription

The caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no extra text and no balloon; a caption that does not read is blocking, a caption that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant of the same word are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. The run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue is nonblocking.

### C2 — The raft into the dark

A single spectacle image where the eye finds the small raft, its one lantern, heading for the white line of the weir. Blocking if the raft or the weir cannot be found, or the page is cut into panels.

### C3 — The four come back

The four men in the foreground haul each other toward the landing, all four safe. Blocking if any of them reads as lost or hurt; their individual faces in the dark are nonblocking.

### C4 — Storybook danger

Milady stays a small figure on the raft, never in the water and never close; nothing goes over the weir in the picture; no blood. Blocking on any of these.
