## Page intent

## PAGE 58

A spectacle page, one full image, nearly silent: the last page of the volume. Seen from above and behind, the four friends walk away down a narrow Paris street, four abreast, all four now in the Musketeers' blue cassock with the white cross: the smallest in the middle with the grey hat and the broken feather and his hand on his too-long sword, the huge one with the red plumes, the slim fair one, the tall one on the outside. The old yellow horse follows on a loose rein. At the top of the frame, in the foreground, the Cardinal stands at a window above the street with one hand on his grey cat, watching the man whose commission he signed. The dominant turn is the picture itself: four blue cloaks, and the man in red still at his window. A single caption and the closing title card are the only words.

### C1 — Text transcription fails
The caption and the title card read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, the caption above the title card. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any balloon or extra text is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
One image: four men in blue walking away together, and a man in red at a window above them. If the four do not read as the image, or the man in red is missing, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Four, and who they are
Exactly four men in blue cassocks with white crosses, walking abreast; the count carries the page. Among them the smallest in a grey hat with a broken feather, one huge with red plumes, one slim and fair, one tallest. A fifth Musketeer, or the four not separable from behind, is blocking.

### C4 — d'Artagnan and Aramis confused
Black hair and a grey hat with a broken feather against blond curls and a black hat with a white plume. Two fair or two identical hats is blocking.

### C5 — The yellow horse and the man in red
The old buttercup-yellow horse follows riderless; the figure at the window is thin, in red, with a grey cat. A second yellow horse or a ridden one is blocking; the cat's exact pose is nonblocking.
