# Exact script — pages 11–20

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 11 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the tavern.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The boy bursting in late and flushed, dropping into the
empty chair. PORTHOS, left, booming.

> **PORTHOS:** "Look who remembers us! The great lady let him out!"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "She isn't a great lady. She's — all right, she's a great lady. You'd like her."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** ARAMIS, two fingers at his moustache.

> **ARAMIS:** "A great lady once let me in by her front door. It's why I'm going into the Church."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "That was you. This is me. She listens to me."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** ATHOS, who has not spoken, setting his glass
down on the table very carefully, and not picking it up again.

> **ATHOS:** "Describe her."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Tall. Hair so pale it's almost silver. Eyes like ice water. And she smiles before she means it — you can see it coming."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Her name."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Lady de Winter."
>
> **ATHOS:** "I don't know that name."

**Why you turn the page:** Athos says he doesn't know the name, and he has not touched his wine.

## PAGE 12 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Milady's house.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Afternoon. The boy inside the empty gold salon with
flowers in his hand, early. From behind a half-drawn plum curtain, voices.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the gold wall)*
> "The next day he came early. She was not alone."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Through the gap in the curtain: MILADY, right,
at a small writing table, and ROCHEFORT, left, standing, the scar toward
us. In the foreground the boy, half hidden, frozen, the flowers lowering.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Well? His Eminence is waiting."
>
> **MILADY:** "The girl is in a convent in the north. The boy told me the first day; I waited to be sure he wasn't lying."
>
> **MILADY:** "His Eminence can find out which one."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Rochefort, laughing through his nose.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "And the boy?"
>
> **MILADY:** "When I've finished with him, he's yours. Try not to lose him a fifth time."

**Why you turn the page:** he has heard all of it, and he is about to be seen.

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Milady's house.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The flowers falling; the boy stepping through the
curtain, his sword half out.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You're the Cardinal's. You were the Cardinal's from the river road. The horses, the front door, all of it."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 55%.** Milady flying at him with a small knife snatched
from the writing table; he catches her wrist; her left sleeve tears open at
the shoulder seam, and on the back of her bare left shoulder is a small dark
lily, burned into the skin. He sees it. She sees him see it.

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Milady clutching the torn sleeve, white with fury.
Rochefort behind her, at the salon door, sword out.

> **MILADY:** "You've seen it. Nobody who has seen that is still alive. You won't be either."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "I have the door, Milady. He's not getting past me."

**Why you turn the page:** the door is taken, and there is only the window.

## PAGE 14 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the square outside Milady's house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The boy coming out of the tall first-floor
window in a tangle of plum curtain, in mid-air above the square, hat flying,
sword in his fist, toward a hay cart passing underneath; the carter looking
up open-mouthed. At the window above, Rochefort leaning out, and behind him
a pale gold head.

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the cobbles)*
> "Gascon boys are taught never to run from anyone. Nobody had said a word about windows."

**Why you turn the page:** he got out, and now he knows what she is, and so does she.

## PAGE 15 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Athos's room.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Night. The boy in the doorway with hay in his hair,
gasping. ATHOS at the bare table with one candle and a glass.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "She's the Cardinal's! Milady. She asked me about Constance the first day, and I told her!"
>
> **ATHOS:** "Sit down."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The boy pacing instead, his hand on his own left
shoulder.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "And she has a mark burned into her shoulder. The kind they brand on a thief. A lily."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "She said nobody who's seen it stays alive. She meant me."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Athos, for once not quite still: his hand flat
on the table beside the glass, his face gone grey in the candle.

> **ATHOS:** "Which shoulder?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "The left. Athos, why does that—"
>
> **ATHOS:** "Then she isn't dead."

**Why you turn the page:** Athos knows this woman, and he is going to say how.

## PAGE 16 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — Athos's room, and a wood in his memory.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A memory, in faded dusk colours: a young lord in
black and silver on a grey horse, small and seen from behind, riding away
from the edge of a dark wood. In the foreground, high, one bare branch
against the sky.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Athos at the table, his face in the candlelight, looking
at nothing. The boy sitting on the floor against the wall, listening.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dusk sky, never over the rider)*
> "'I had a friend,' Athos said. 'A count, with land and a name. At twenty-five he married the most beautiful girl in the province. Sixteen, with hair like pale gold. Nobody knew where she came from, and he never asked.
>
> 'One day out hunting she fell from her horse, and her sleeve tore, and on her shoulder was the lily. The mark the law burns on a thief.
>
> 'He was the lord there, so he could be the judge. He was the judge that same hour, alone, without asking her one question. He hanged her from a tree in his own wood, and rode away.
>
> 'When he went back, the rope was cut and she was gone. So he gave up his name, and became a Musketeer.'"

**Why you turn the page:** the boy has to say out loud who the friend was.

## PAGE 17 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Athos's room.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The boy getting slowly to his feet.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Your friend. The count. It was you."
>
> **ATHOS:** "It was a count. The count is dead. I'm Athos."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Athos standing, taking the sword down from the
whitewashed wall.

> **ATHOS:** "She swore you wouldn't live, because you've seen the lily. She keeps that kind of promise."
>
> **ATHOS:** "From tonight you eat only what we eat, and drink only what we drink."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "And Constance? I told Milady she's in a convent in the north."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Then the Cardinal will find out which one. So must we."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The door banging open: PORTHOS and ARAMIS, cloaked,
excited; behind them in the street, a drummer.

> **PORTHOS:** "War! The King marches on La Rochelle tomorrow, and the Musketeers with him!"
>
> **ARAMIS:** "And the Cardinal goes with him. I had prayed he would stay in Paris."

**Why you turn the page:** everyone is going to war, including her.

## PAGE 18 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the road west, and the camp before La Rochelle.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The army on a dusty road: columns of soldiers,
blue cassocks, the boy in ochre on the yellow horse among them; far behind,
after a column of red-cloaked guards, a black carriage with plum curtains
drawn. Athos, riding, has turned in his saddle to look back at it.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** La Rochelle: a walled city on its harbour, the grey sea
beyond, and the King's trenches dug round it in a ring.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the sky, never over the riders)*
> "La Rochelle was a city by the sea that had shut its gates against the King, and was waiting for English ships to come and help it. So the King marched his army across France, dug a ring of trenches round its walls, and waited for it to get hungry.
>
> The Musketeers went. d'Artagnan went too, as a volunteer with the King's Guards, because it was the only way to march beside his friends; and a volunteer who walks away from a siege is a deserter like anybody else.
>
> The Cardinal went, because the war was his idea. And at the back of the Cardinal's train, behind his red-cloaked guards, came a black carriage with its curtains drawn. d'Artagnan never saw it. Athos did."

**Why you turn the page:** she is in the camp, and the boy doesn't know.

# MOVEMENT III — THE RED DOVECOTE · pp 19–25

---

## PAGE 19 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the camp before La Rochelle.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Morning among the tents, the city walls on the skyline. A
camp boy handing D'ARTAGNAN a dark green bottle with a paper tied round its
neck. The boy reading the paper aloud, delighted.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "'To d'Artagnan, from Athos, Porthos and Aramis. Drink to us!' They do love me."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The boy, cork out, lifting the bottle to his
lips. Behind him ATHOS striding up fast between the tents, PORTHOS and
ARAMIS behind; Athos's hand coming down over the mouth of the bottle.

> **ATHOS:** "Stop."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "But it's from you!"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Athos holding the bottle away from the boy at arm's length.

> **ATHOS:** "That's my name on a bottle I never sent. I have never in my life sent anyone wine. I drink it."

**Why you turn the page:** if the wine isn't from his friends, it is from someone who wants him dead.

---

## PAGE 20 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the camp before La Rochelle.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Athos tipping the bottle out onto the grass;
where the wine falls, the grass goes black and curls. The four looking
down at it.

> **PORTHOS:** "That was good grass."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** ATHOS, left; the boy, right, pale, rounding on him.

> **ATHOS:** "There's a black carriage at the back of the Cardinal's train, with plum curtains. She's here."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You saw her carriage, and you didn't tell me?"
>
> **ATHOS:** "I wasn't sure it was hers. I am now."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** D'ARTAGNAN, left; ARAMIS, right, two fingers at his
moustache, speaking low.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then I'll write to the Queen tonight and beg her to move Constance."
>
> **ARAMIS:** "Don't. Every letter out of this camp crosses the Cardinal's desk. I've had my own read back to me."

**Why you turn the page:** Milady is trying to kill him, and they can't even send a letter to save Constance.
