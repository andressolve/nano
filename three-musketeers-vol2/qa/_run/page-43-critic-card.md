## Page intent

## PAGE 43

A dramatic page. The dominant turn: Constance goes quietly in his arms, and her last words are "You promised, and you came." The boy runs into the cloister; she is on the bench, very pale, smiling, an empty glass in her lap, glad she knew his horse. In the large middle panel he kneels and holds her, her head on his shoulder, the glass slipping from her fingers onto the grass unbroken, while she tells him about the kind lady with pale hair and that he kept his promise. Then her eyes close, peaceful, and he says her name; behind them Athos takes off his hat. The reader turns because she is gone and the boy does not understand yet. Subordinate: the glass and the table.

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All five strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
One close panel must own the page: the boy kneeling and holding her, her head on his shoulder, the glass slipping from her fingers; then her eyes closed and his one word. If the holding panel does not own the page by eye, or the ending does not read as quiet and final, it is blocking.

### C3 — Attribution
Her two lines in the close panel must be hers, and "Told who?" and "Constance?" his. Any of her lines read as his is blocking.

### C4 — Tone and object state
Storybook stillness: a mark on her, visible pain, a struggle, or a broken or spilled glass is blocking; a single plain glass falling or lying whole is what the page needs. Athos in the arch is silent; a balloon on him is blocking.

### C5 — Identity and integrity
The boy beardless with black hair, in ochre; Constance small, freckled, dark curls under a white cap, in grey. A duplicated figure or gross anatomy on a focal face or hand is blocking.
