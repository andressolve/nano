# Exact script — pages 31–40

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 31 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bastion of Saint-Gervais.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Over the broken parapet: a dozen townsmen-soldiers in dun
coats and steel caps climbing the slope toward the bastion with a ladder.
ARAMIS, left, pointing at them with a chicken leg; PORTHOS, right, chewing.

> **ARAMIS:** "Gentlemen, we have company. In theory, this is where we leave."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "I haven't finished my chicken."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The four at the parapet firing muskets into a
wall of white smoke; beyond the smoke, the attackers dropping the ladder and
running back down the slope.

> **SOUND** *(across the smoke)*
> "BLAM! BLAM!"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Back at the tablecloth: Porthos picking up his chicken
again; the boy at the parapet, staring north, not eating; Athos sitting down.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Fifteen minutes. Forty-five more, and all that time she's riding."
>
> **ATHOS:** "They'll come back with more. Good. It makes the hour go faster."

**Why you turn the page:** Athos is right, and they are coming back.

## PAGE 32 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bastion of Saint-Gervais.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Down the slope: a whole company of attackers coming up with
three ladders, forty men, steel caps glinting. The boy at the parapet.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "There are forty of them this time!"
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Then that wall is in their way. Help me give it to them."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 75%.** Porthos with his huge shoulder set against the
tall cracked wall that leans out over the slope, the boy and Aramis pushing
beside him, Athos with his back braced against it too; the top stones
beginning to tilt outward, dust falling.

> **PORTHOS:** "All for one — and push!"

**Why you turn the page:** the wall is going over.

---

## PAGE 33 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the bastion of Saint-Gervais.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The great wall coming down outward across the
slope in a roaring avalanche of stone and dust, the ladders vanishing into
it, the attackers far below all running back toward the city, nobody under
it. On the broken top of the ruin, small against the sky, Porthos with both
arms in the air.

> **SOUND** *(across the dust)*
> "RRRUMBLE"

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the slope)*
> "Nobody was under it. Everybody was running."

**Why you turn the page:** the hour isn't over yet.

## PAGE 34 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bastion of Saint-Gervais.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** On top of the rubble, Aramis tying his white
lace handkerchief to a pike as a flag, the wind snapping it out; Athos
looking at the sun; the boy rounding on him, desperate.

> **ARAMIS:** "A flag, so the King can see whose bastion this is. I shall want it back."
>
> **ATHOS:** "That's the hour. And one minute more, for good manners."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "One more minute? She's half a day ahead of us, and closer to Constance every minute!"
>
> **ATHOS:** "Then it's a minute this army will never forget. Now we go."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The four walking back across the open ground, Porthos
with the basket; along the trenches, the army on its feet, hats in the air.

> **SOUND** *(over the trenches)*
> "HURRAH!"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** PORTHOS, left, with the basket, looking back at the
flag; D'ARTAGNAN, right, already looking north.

> **PORTHOS:** "They'll talk about my wall for a hundred years. I left half a chicken behind it."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Let them talk. Béthune is six days north."

**Why you turn the page:** the whole army saw it — and so did a man in red on the hill.

## PAGE 35 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a hill behind the lines.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** THE CARDINAL on a grey horse on a hill, his red
cape showing under a dark cloak; ROCHEFORT beside him on a black horse. Far
below, four small figures walking into a cheering camp; on the bastion, a
tiny white flag.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Three Musketeers and the Gascon, Eminence. Again."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The Cardinal, quiet.

> **CARDINAL:** "The tall one rode with me to the Red Dovecote, and his horse threw a shoe. I think he heard me name Béthune."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "The King will give them leave for this, and they'll ride there."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "I would rather have that boy in my red than in the ground."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "He would sooner die, Eminence."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The Cardinal turning his horse away.

> **CARDINAL:** "Then let Milady arrange it. Ride north tonight, Rochefort."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "Tell her four men are coming."

**Why you turn the page:** the four have their leave, and a rider is going north ahead of them.

## PAGE 36 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the north road.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Four riders on a long straight road through
flat country in driving rain, the yellow horse in front; poplars bending;
a post inn behind them.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Far ahead in the night, small under a moon: a single rider
in black with a red feather, galloping.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the grey sky, never over the riders)*
> "The King gave the four of them leave that same afternoon. He had watched the whole breakfast from the trenches. The army paid its bets, grumbling. The four were on the north road before supper.
>
> It was six days to Béthune. They changed horses where they could and slept where they fell. The yellow horse would not be changed, and kept up, and nobody could explain it.
>
> d'Artagnan did not talk. He had promised Constance not to come before the Queen said she was safe. He was coming anyway. At every milestone he did the sum again: she was half a day ahead, and a carriage is slower than a horseman. Half a day was nothing. Half a day was everything.
>
> Somewhere ahead of them, a man in black was riding faster."

**Why you turn the page:** the race is on, and somebody is ahead of them both.

## PAGE 37 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the convent at Béthune.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** A small cloister of pale stone with roses and a
well. CONSTANCE in her grey dress and white cap, a straw basket on her arm,
looking up at the gate; at the gate, a nun letting in a tall lady in a plain
dark widow's dress, a black veil pushed back from pale gold hair.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The two women on a stone bench in the cloister, the lady
holding both of Constance's hands.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the pale cloister wall, never over the women)*
> "At the convent at Béthune, Constance was safe, and very bored, and very lonely. The nuns were kind and did not talk. She weeded the roses and watched the road.
>
> Then one afternoon a lady came to the gate in a plain widow's dress and asked the nuns to hide her. She said the Cardinal's men were hunting her. She said she had nobody in the world.
>
> She was the kindest person Constance had met since Paris. Constance, who had told a boy to beware of ladies who are kind for no reason, never once asked herself why. They sat in the cloister until the bell rang. Constance thought they were both hiding from the same man."

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows who the kind widow is, and Constance doesn't.

## PAGE 38 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the cloister at Béthune.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Evening in the cloister. CONSTANCE, left, and MILADY in
her widow's dress, right, walking arm in arm; the dark ring on Milady's hand.

> **MILADY:** "And this boy of yours. Does he know where you are?"
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "Not yet. The Queen promised to write to him when it's safe, and then he'll come. He promised me."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Constance, worried for him, telling it; Milady
listening with her smile.

> **CONSTANCE:** "I worry about him. In Paris a great lady was being kind to him. Lady de Winter."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "I told him to be careful of ladies who are kind for no reason."
>
> **MILADY:** "Lady de Winter. I've heard of her. You were right to warn him."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close: Milady's face over Constance's shoulder, the smile
gone; her thumb turning the dark ring.

> **CONSTANCE:** "You're not like that. You're kind because you're hiding too."

**Why you turn the page:** Constance warned the boy against Milady by name, and has just told Milady so.

## PAGE 39 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the back gate of the convent at Béthune.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Moonlight at a small back gate. ROCHEFORT on a
lathered black horse, leaning down; MILADY at the gate in her slate-grey
cloak.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Four of them, on the north road. They had leave from the King. They'll be here by noon tomorrow."
>
> **MILADY:** "The Gascon?"
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "In front, on that yellow horse."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Milady, cold.

> **MILADY:** "Then bring my carriage to this gate at first light. I'll take the girl to the coast."
>
> **MILADY:** "If he wants her back, he can follow me onto the boat to England."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "And if she won't come?"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Milady turning back toward the dark convent, turning the
ring.

> **MILADY:** "Then she'll stay here. One way or another."

**Why you turn the page:** the carriage comes at first light, and so do the four.

## PAGE 40 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Constance's room at the convent, with its window on the road.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Morning. CONSTANCE at her window, both hands on
the sill, leaning out; far down the long straight road, four riders, the one
in front on a horse of unmistakable bright yellow.

> **CONSTANCE:** "It's him! Look, that's his horse! Nobody else in France would ride a horse that colour!"

**Panel 2 — 30%.** MILADY behind her in the room, travelling cloak on, urgent.

> **MILADY:** "Those are the Cardinal's men, child. Come down to my carriage, quickly, before they see you."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "No. It's d'Artagnan. I'm staying right here."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Milady, very still for a moment; then smiling again.

> **MILADY:** "Then let's drink to him first."

**Why you turn the page:** Milady has smiled again, and the reader knows what that means.
