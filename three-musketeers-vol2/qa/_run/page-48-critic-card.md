## Page intent

## PAGE 48

A dramatic page at the ferry landing in the rain. Three of the friends stand in a row at the edge of the timbers, the boy, Porthos and Aramis, and each names one of Milady's crimes to her face. Then the page belongs to Athos alone, a step in front of the others, quiet, rain running off his face, saying the charge that is his own: she lied to him the day he married her; that one he can forgive, the others he cannot. Behind him Porthos and Aramis glance at each other. Milady answers with a smile and cuts the rope, and the raft lurches out into the flood with her kneeling on it. The dominant turn is the four naming her crimes; she cuts the rope. The reader turns because the raft is loose and the weir is roaring.

landing, left to right: the boy, Porthos, Aramis.

>

the raft lurching out into the flood with her on it.

> **MILADY:** "Then judge me from the bank, gentlemen."

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 48 — card

### C1 — Transcription

Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant of the same word are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. The run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue is nonblocking. A line owned by placement, directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Athos's charge owns the page

Athos alone, quiet, must be the image the eye finds, in the dramatic mode, with the cut rope after it as the release. Blocking if the three-man row or the raft overpowers him.

### C3 — Attribution of the three charges

The wine line belongs to the boy, the poison line to Porthos, the Cardinal line to Aramis, the marriage line to Athos, the last line to Milady. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail and read in that order. Blocking if any charge reads as another man's.

### C4 — Identity and Milady against Aramis

The boy beardless in ochre; Porthos enormous with the waxed red-brown moustache; Aramis slim, blond, moustached in blue; Athos tallest with the pointed beard; Milady with long pale-gold ringlets and a grey cloak over plum. Blocking if Milady and Aramis could be confused or any two friends read as the same man.

### C5 — The rope cut, Milady on the raft

The rope must read as cut and the raft loose with Milady kneeling on it. Blocking if she is in the water, if anyone is cut or hit, or if blood appears.
