# Exact script — pages 51–58

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 51 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the river.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The raft, small now, spinning away into the dark
and the rain toward the long white line of the weir, its lantern one point
of light; in the near foreground the chain of four men hauling each other
back toward the landing.

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the black water)*
> "She turned her back on the hand. The river took the raft toward the weir, and the lantern went out."

**Why you turn the page:** it is over, and none of them knows yet what that feels like.

## PAGE 52 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the river at dawn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Grey dawn. The river brown and calm, the cut
ferry rope hanging slack into the water. The four sitting on the bank in
their wet clothes, the horses behind them, the yellow horse among them.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Athos alone at the water's edge, looking downstream.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the grey sky, never over the four)*
> "In the morning the river was brown and quiet and empty. The ferry rope hung down into the water, and there was nothing on the end of it.
>
> d'Artagnan had thought that when it was over he would feel something enormous. He felt cold, and very tired, and he wanted Constance to be alive, and none of that had changed.
>
> They buried her at Béthune, in the convent garden, under the roses.
>
> Athos stood a long time by the river. When he came back he said only, 'Good.' He always said it when things were bad; and for the first time the boy understood that it was the only way Athos could bear them."

**Why you turn the page:** they have to go back to Paris, where the Cardinal is waiting.

# MOVEMENT VII — THE PAPER · pp 53–58

## PAGE 53 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a gate of Paris.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Evening. The four riding in under a Paris gate; ROCHEFORT
waiting in the arch with four red-cloaked guards.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Monsieur d'Artagnan. His Eminence wants you. Alone."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Then His Eminence can have all four of us."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Rochefort's hand on the boy's shoulder; the boy
waving his friends back.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "No. I'll go. Wait for me at the tavern."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Meung, the Carmelites, the alley, Calais, a window. Five times, Gascon. This is the sixth, and this time I have an order."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The boy turning back to Athos, his hand out.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Athos. Give me the Cardinal's paper. This part is mine to do."
>
> **ATHOS:** "This was always yours to finish."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is walking to the Cardinal with the Cardinal's own paper in his coat.

## PAGE 54 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Cardinal's study.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** THE CARDINAL in his chair, the grey cat on his lap, his
hand stroking it. D'ARTAGNAN before the desk, muddy, hat in hand. Rochefort
by the door.

> **CARDINAL:** "A lady in my service is dead, at a ferry in the north."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "Four men were seen on the bank. One rode a yellow horse."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The boy, white with anger, leaning on the desk.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "She was in your service when she poisoned Constance. You gave her Constance as her price. Athos heard you."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "I named a convent. I did not pour the wine."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "And we never touched her. She cut her own rope."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** The boy laying a folded paper on the desk,
opening it flat under the candle, and pushing it across to the Cardinal.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "This is yours. It says whoever carries it did what he did on your order, for France."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I could hide behind it for the rest of my life. I won't hide behind anything you've signed."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy has just given back a paper that could have protected him from anything.

## PAGE 55 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Cardinal's study.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The Cardinal reading the paper; his hand on the
cat has stopped, for the first time.

> **CARDINAL:** "My own words, in my own hand."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "And you give them back to me. Unasked."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The Cardinal tearing the paper slowly across, and again,
and dropping the pieces into the candle.

> **CARDINAL:** "Rochefort tells me you would sooner die than wear my red. I begin to believe him."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The Cardinal writing on a fresh sheet, sealing it in red
wax, and holding it out across the desk.

> **CARDINAL:** "A commission as an officer of the King's Musketeers. I've left the name empty. Write in it whoever you like."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Why would you give me that?"
>
> **CARDINAL:** "Men who can't be bought are rare. I would rather they were in France than under it."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is holding a blue cloak with no name in it, from the one man he hates.

## PAGE 56 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the tavern.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The tavern table, the three; the boy throwing the sealed
sheet down in front of ATHOS.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I gave him back his paper, and he gave me this. An officer's commission in the Musketeers, with no name in it."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I won't wear his name on me. Athos, you've earned it ten times over. You take it."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Too much for Athos. Too little for the man I was. No."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** PORTHOS, left, pushing it on; ARAMIS, right.

> **PORTHOS:** "Me, an officer? I'd have to read things. Aramis?"
>
> **ARAMIS:** "I'm leaving for the Church. Next year. Probably."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Athos taking up the pen and writing on the empty
line, the boy watching him.

> **ATHOS:** "It isn't his. It's the King's blue, and you earned it at the bastion with the whole army watching."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Wear it, and make him look at you in it. There. D'Artagnan."

**Why you turn the page:** his name is on it.

## PAGE 57 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the attic.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Evening in the attic: the boy standing at the
window in a new blue cassock with the white cross, over his ochre doublet,
the ring on his hand, looking down through the open door at the stair.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The foot of the stair: Constance's door, dark, the lamp
bracket empty, and the boy's hand lighting the lamp.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dark beams, never over the boy)*
> "The blue cloak was new, and too big in the shoulders, and the blue was exactly the blue of the other three.
>
> He had come to Paris wanting to be let in. A grand lady had let him in by her front door, and it had cost him Constance. Now the King's Musketeers had let him in, and he would have given the cloak back to hear someone moving about downstairs.
>
> Nobody lit the lamp at Constance's door any more. He went down the stair and lit it himself, and then he went out to find his friends."

**Why you turn the page:** his friends are waiting in the street.

## PAGE 58 · *spectacle*

**Location:** a Paris street.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** From above and behind: four men in blue cassocks
with white crosses walking away from the reader down a narrow Paris street,
four abreast. The smallest one in the middle in a grey hat with a broken
feather, a hand on his too-long sword; the huge one with the red plumes; the
slim fair one; the tall one on the outside. The old yellow horse follows on
a loose rein. In the foreground at the top of the frame, a window, a thin
figure in red in it, one hand on a grey cat on the sill.

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the cobbles)*
> "Four blue cloaks, four abreast. And at a window above the street, the man in red who had signed one of them."

> **TITLE CARD** *(bottom, small)*
> "End of Volume II"

**Why you turn the page:** there is no next page; the man in red will be at his window when there is.
