## Page intent

## PAGE 39

A dramatic page. The dominant turn: Rochefort brings word that four riders will arrive by noon, and Milady decides she will take the girl or leave her. At a small back gate in moonlight, Rochefort leans down from a tired black horse to tell her the four are on the north road, the Gascon in front on the yellow horse. Milady, cold, orders her carriage for first light and plans the boat to England; when he asks what if the girl refuses, she turns back toward the dark convent, turning her ring, and answers in four quiet words. The reader turns because the carriage and the four are both coming at first light. Subordinate: the horse and the gate.

**Location:** the back gate of the convent at Béthune.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Moonlight at a small back gate. ROCHEFORT on a

>

>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "And if she won't come?"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Milady turning back toward the dark convent, turning the
ring.

> **MILADY:** "Then she'll stay here. One way or another."

**Why you turn the page:** the carriage comes at first light, and so do the four.

## Page 39 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The reader must see a rider bring news at a back gate by night, and Milady decide: the carriage at first light, the girl taken or left. If the news panel does not own the page by eye, or her final line reads as anyone's but hers, it is blocking.

### C3 — Attribution across the three-way exchange
Rochefort's news, her "The Gascon?", his answer; her two orders and his question; her last line. A reply read before the line that prompts it, or her question owned by him, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Rochefort reads as the dark man in black with one red feather, thin moustache, no beard, chin clean; any scar only on his left cheek. Milady reads as the tall pale woman with pale gold hair bound up and a black veil, a grey cloak over dark clothes. A scar on his right cheek, a beard, or Milady in plum is blocking.

### C5 — Generation integrity
A duplicated named figure, a yellow horse at the gate, or gross anatomy on a focal face is blocking. The horse's exact markings are not.
