## Page intent

## PAGE 24

A dramatic page in the small room above the inn. Athos stands in the open doorway with his hat in his hand and asks Milady whether she knows him. The dominant turn: Athos and Milady face to face; he takes the paper and lets her go. The largest image is Milady rising with the candle between them and her face changing; it is the only time in the book she is afraid. She names him as a count she heard was dead, and he answers that he thought he had hanged her. Then his sword point rests on the table, never on her; he asks for the Cardinal's paper and she pushes it across, mocking him; he tells her he will not judge her alone twice, and warns her off the boy and the girl. The reader turns because Athos has the paper, and he has let Milady go.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 24 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the room above the common room of the Red Dovecote.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The door open. ATHOS in the doorway, hat in his hand;
MILADY at the table, turning.

> **ATHOS:** "Do you know me?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Milady rising, the candle between them, her
face changing. It is the only time in the book she is afraid.

> **MILADY:** "The Count de la Fère. I heard you went into the army and were killed."
>
> **ATHOS:** "And I thought I'd hanged you. We were both misinformed."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Athos's sword point resting on the table between them;
her hand pushing the folded paper across to him.

> **ATHOS:** "Give me the Cardinal's paper."
>
> **MILADY:** "Take it. You couldn't finish it then. You won't now."
>
> **ATHOS:** "I judged you once, alone, in an hour. I won't do it twice."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Go near the boy or the girl, and next time there will be witnesses."

**Why you turn the page:** Athos has the paper, and he has let Milady go.
