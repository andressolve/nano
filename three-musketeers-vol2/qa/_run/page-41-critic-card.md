## Page intent

## PAGE 41

A dramatic page. The dominant turn: Milady opens her ring over Constance's glass. At a little table in the cloister the widow pours wine into two plain glasses and toasts the lucky boy while Constance watches the gate. In the large close panel, as the widow hands a glass across, her thumb flicks open the dark stone of her ring and a few grey grains fall into the wine; the caption tells us Constance did not see. Then Constance drinks to d'Artagnan, and the widow is already walking away, saying she will meet them at the gate. The reader turns because the glass is empty and Milady is not going to the gate. Subordinate: the jug, the table, the roses.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 41 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All four strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, the caption tail-free, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
One close panel must own the page: the widow's hand passing a glass, the ring's stone flipped open, grey grains falling into the wine, Constance looking away. If the open ring or the falling grains cannot be seen, the page fails its turn and is blocking.

### C3 — Object state
One jug and exactly two plain glasses on the table; the ring open on her left hand in the close panel. A bottle, a third glass, a skull or label on the ring, or smoke or glow from the wine is blocking. Ring hardware and glass shape are nonblocking.

### C4 — Attribution and identity
The toast "To him" is the widow's, "To d'Artagnan" is Constance's as she drinks, and the last line is the widow's as she walks away; any misowned line is blocking. The two women must not be confused, and Constance must not wear an apron nor the widow plum.

### C5 — Generation integrity
A duplicated named figure, an extra hand in the close panel, or gross anatomy on the focal hand is blocking. Background roses are not.
