## Page intent

## PAGE 55

A dramatic page in the Cardinal's study. The dominant turn is the Cardinal reading his own paper, and his hand on the cat stopping for the first time in two books: he has met a man who cannot be bought, and it is the wrong one. He says so in a quiet sentence, then tears the paper slowly across, twice, and lets the pieces burn in the candle flame. Then he writes on a fresh sheet, seals it in red wax, and holds it out across the desk: a commission as an officer of the King's Musketeers, with the name left empty. The boy asks why, and the Cardinal's answer is the most honest thing he says in the volume. The fire stays small, inside the candle. The boy stays subordinate until his single question. The reader turns because the boy is holding a blue cloak with no name in it, from the man he hates most.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 55 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All six lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order: five from the Cardinal, one ("Why would you give me that?") from the boy. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text, including legible words on either paper, is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read: the Cardinal reads his own paper with his hand stopped on the cat, he tears and burns it, he offers a sealed commission. If the Cardinal's reading face with the still hand is not the image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Attribution in the commission exchange
The Cardinal's offer, the boy's question, the Cardinal's answer, in that order; the question must hang from the boy and the answer from the Cardinal. A reply readable before its question is blocking.

### C4 — Identity fails
The Cardinal thin, grey-bearded, red robe and skullcap, seated, the grey cat. The boy beardless, black-haired, muddy ochre, no blue. A standing Cardinal or a moustached boy is blocking.

### C5 — Object state
The paper is torn and its pieces burn in the candle; the fire stays in the flame, no blaze. The commission is one sheet with one red seal. A burning desk or room is blocking; the number of torn pieces is nonblocking.
