## Page intent

## PAGE 7

Dramatic. Night at Constance's street door under the lantern. The boy bounds up grinning with his news of a great lady; Constance, white-faced in the doorway with a lamp, a packed bundle behind her, tells him she leaves at first light. The dominant moment is at the foot of the steep stair, Constance holding both his hands: the Queen is hiding her in a convent somewhere in the north, and she will not tell him which, so nobody can make him tell. The reader turns because she is leaving at first light, and she has warned him.

> **CONSTANCE:** "The Cardinal's people keep asking who carried the Queen's letters. She's hiding me in a convent, somewhere in the north."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Which convent? Tell me where, and I'll come."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "If you don't know, nobody can make you tell. That's the whole point."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Constance, sharp now, her hands still in his.

> **CONSTANCE:** "What lady?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Lady de Winter. Milady."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "Everyone at the palace knows her carriage, and nobody knows who pays for it. Be careful of ladies who are kind for no reason."

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All eight strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, each hanging from its own speaker's mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Attribution across the two A–B–A exchanges
At the stair: Constance's convent line, the boy's "Which convent?", Constance's "If you don't know". Then Constance's "What lady?", the boy's "Lady de Winter. Milady.", Constance's warning. Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth, each reply readable only after its prompt; the warning or "What lady?" given to the boy, or a reply read before its question, is blocking.

### C3 — The turn lands by eye
Constance holding both his hands at the foot of the stair must own the page; the reader must grasp that she is going into hiding in the north and has warned him. A page whose dominant image is the doorway greeting is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and state
Constance small, round freckled face, dark curls under a white cap, dove grey with the white apron; the boy beardless, black-haired, in ochre with no blue. Constance without her apron (her later costume), taller than the boy, or the boy in blue is blocking. The keys and the crown on the apron are nonblocking if unclear.

### C5 — Generation integrity
Both of her hands holding both of his read as four hands, cleanly; fused or extra hands in that grip are blocking. Stair detail is not.
