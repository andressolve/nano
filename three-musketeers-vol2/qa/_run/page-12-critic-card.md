## Page intent

## PAGE 12

Dramatic. Milady's gold salon, afternoon. The boy comes early with flowers and finds the room empty, and voices behind a half-drawn plum curtain. Through the gap he sees Milady at a small writing table and Rochefort, the man with the scar, standing over her. She reports: the girl is in a convent in the north, the boy told her the first day, and His Eminence can find out which one. Rochefort laughs through his nose and asks about the boy; she says that when she has finished with him he is Rochefort's. In the foreground the boy, half hidden, frozen, lets the flowers sink. The dominant turn is the boy overhearing that he told her the first day: every kindness was work. Milady owns the page; the boy's frozen back is its witness. The reader turns because he has heard all of it and is about to be seen.

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
The caption and five lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A line that does not read is blocking; a line that reads is not to be measured. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order are what must match. No object carries scripted words.

### C2 — The overhearing does not land
The eye must find, in one panel, Milady and the scarred man in the cabinet and the boy hidden in the foreground, frozen, flowers sinking. If the boy is not plainly present and unseen, or reads as part of their conversation, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution
"Well? His Eminence is waiting." and "And the boy?" hang from Rochefort; the convent report, "His Eminence can find out which one." and "When I've finished with him…" hang from Milady. A Milady line read from Rochefort's mouth, or from the hidden boy, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Milady: pale ash-gold ringlets far past the shoulder, ice-blue eyes, deep plum silk. Rochefort: all black, one red feather, thin moustache, no beard, chin clean, a scar on the LEFT cheek only; a scar on the right cheek or a beard is blocking. The boy: beardless, black hair, ochre.

### C5 — Register
The salon is brush-and-ink and matte gouache in gold candlelight; a glossy, photographic room that no longer reads as the book's drawn world is blocking.
