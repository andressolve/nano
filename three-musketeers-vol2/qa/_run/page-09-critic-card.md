## Page intent

## PAGE 9

Dramatic. On Thursday a footman bows the patched ochre boy in at Milady's tall front door. The dominant moment is her salon of gold candlelight and plum hangings: Milady on the sofa pouring wine, the boy perched on the edge of a gilt chair with his hat on his knee, dazzled, boasting that it was him, well, four of them, who saved the Queen. Then, close, her thumb turns the dark ring and her eyes wait over the glass; she asks whether there is a girl, and he hesitates and then tells her: a convent in the north. She smiles. The one dominant turn: in the gold salon the boy tells Milady the girl is in a convent in the north. The reader turns because he has just told her half of what the Cardinal wants, and she is smiling.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 9 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order: the caption tail-free first, then each line hanging from its own speaker's mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, and a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Attribution of the confession
Milady's "Tell me everything", then the boy's boast; her question about a girl; the boy's two lines, the second ending "A convent in the north."; her "How very clever of her." The convent line must hang from the boy's mouth and the last line from hers; either given to the wrong mouth is blocking, because the page's turn is that he told her.

### C3 — The turn lands by eye
The gold salon must own the page: Milady pouring wine on the sofa, the boy perched on a gilt chair, dazzled. The close moment must show her thumb on the dark ring and her waiting eyes. If the salon does not read as a world of gold candlelight and plum, or the footman's doorway outweighs it, it is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Milady tall, very pale, pale gold ringlets past the shoulder, plum silk, ring on her left hand; the boy beardless, black-haired, in ochre without blue. The footman bareheaded and silent. A balloon on the footman, or the boy in blue, is blocking.

### C5 — Generation integrity
Her ring hand and the glass read cleanly; a duplicated Milady in the mirror reading as a second woman, or gross anatomy on a speaker's face, is blocking. A plain reflection and furniture detail are not.
