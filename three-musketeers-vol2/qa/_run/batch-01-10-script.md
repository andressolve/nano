# Exact script — pages 1–10

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** a bridge over the river in Paris.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A summer morning on a stone bridge lined with
tall houses, the river shining below, slate roofs to the sky. Coming toward
the reader across the bridge, four abreast: a tall still man in a blue
cassock with a white cross; a huge man in blue with red plumes, eating an
apple; a slim fair man in blue; and, a head shorter than all of them, a boy
in a faded ochre doublet and a grey hat with a broken feather, leading an
old buttercup-yellow horse by the bridle. Three blue cassocks and one ochre
doublet. The boy is talking; the others are not listening, and are smiling.

> **TITLE CARD** *(upper third, over the sky)*
> "THE THREE MUSKETEERS
>
> Milady"

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the cobbles)*
> "Paris, the summer after the Queen's ball. Four friends — and only three of them wore the blue cloak."

**Why you turn the page:** one of the four has no blue cloak, and you want to know why.

## PAGE 2 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the attic.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Night. The attic under the roof: a bed, a
table, one candle, a sword on a nail. The boy at the open window in his shirt
sleeves, a small ring on his right hand, looking down through the open attic
door at the steep stair, where at the bottom a warm lamp burns at a door.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** A memory, framed in the candlelight: four sword points
meeting above a table, four hands on four hilts.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dark wall, never over the boy)*
> "His name was d'Artagnan. He was eighteen, and a Gascon — which only means he came from Gascony, in the far south of France, where boys are brought up on garlic and pride and told never to back down from anyone.
>
> In the spring he had ridden to Paris on a yellow horse that everyone laughed at. In one month he had made three friends, Athos, Porthos and Aramis, the King's Musketeers, and the four of them had sworn: all for one, and one for all. Together they had saved the Queen from the Cardinal, the most powerful man in France. The Queen had given the boy a ring. Constance, the girl downstairs, had given him her smile.
>
> And the Cardinal had read the boy's name off a letter, and remembered it."

**Why you turn the page:** the most powerful man in France remembers his name.

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the field behind the Carmelite convent.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Late afternoon in the long grass by a high
yellow wall. D'ARTAGNAN, left, lunging at ARAMIS with far too much energy;
Aramis, right, parrying with one hand, bored. PORTHOS sits on the stone
trough eating a chicken leg. ATHOS stands apart, both hands folded on his
sword pommel, not moving. The yellow horse drinks from the trough.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Aramis, a whole month, and nobody in Paris has tried to kill me. Not once!"
>
> **ARAMIS:** "Lunge less, Gascon. I'm trying to think about God."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The boy stopping, sword point dropping, pointing it at
Porthos's blue cassock with the white cross.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Porthos! When do I get a blue cloak like yours? I've earned it twice over."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "The King gives it, when you do something nobody in France can forget. I did it in a week. Of course, I'm me."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The boy turning to the tall still one by the wall.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Athos, tell them I'm not patient enough to wait for it."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Good. Nor was I."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy wants something nobody can forget, and somebody across the river is about to give him something.

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Cardinal's study.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A red room with no window, one candle. THE CARDINAL in
his chair, a grey cat on his lap, his hand stroking it. Before the desk,
ROCHEFORT, all in black, hat in hand, the pale scar down his left cheek.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the red hangings)*
> "Across the river, in his red room, the Cardinal sat with Rochefort, his man, who had tried to stop the boy four times."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Meung, the Carmelites, the alley, Calais. Four times, Eminence. Give me ten men—"
>
> **CARDINAL:** "You have had four times. I am going to try something else."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** From the shadow by the hangings, a tall woman
steps into the candlelight: pale ash-gold ringlets past her shoulders, a
gown of deep plum silk with silver lace, turning a dark-stoned ring on her
finger with her thumb. Rochefort stiffens.

> **CARDINAL:** "Milady. The girl downstairs from that boy carries the Queen's secrets. Find out where the girl goes, and get him out of my way."
>
> **MILADY:** "A boy of eighteen? Give me a week, Eminence. Boys tell me everything."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Milady, turning to Rochefort with a smile.

> **MILADY:** "Tomorrow at noon my horses will bolt on the river road. See that he's there to stop them."

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what is going to happen at noon, and the boy doesn't.

## PAGE 5 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the river road in Paris.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Noon. A black carriage with plum curtains,
two black horses bolting along the river road, the crowd scattering, a
fruit stall going over. Coming up flat out alongside, the old yellow horse,
and the boy leaning right out of the saddle to grab the lead horse's bridle,
hat flying off behind him.

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Small, on a balcony above the road: Rochefort in black
with a red feather in his hat, the scar on his left cheek, watching,
laughing through his nose.

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the road)*
> "At noon the next day a note from 'his friends' sent the boy down the river road. At noon exactly, Rochefort let the horses go."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy has done exactly what Milady planned.

---

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the river road in Paris.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The carriage stopped, the horses blowing; D'ARTAGNAN,
left, still holding the bridle, hair in his eyes, breathing hard. The
carriage door, right, opening; a gloved hand on it.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Are you hurt, Madame? Your horses were trying to swim to the sea."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** MILADY stepping down, pale gold ringlets, plum
silk, looking at him as if he were the only person on the road. He has
forgotten to let go of the bridle.

> **MILADY:** "You stopped them. A hundred men watched, and you were the only one who moved."
>
> **MILADY:** "I'm Lady de Winter. In Paris they call me Milady — it's English, for 'my lady'. And you are?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "d'Artagnan. From Gascony."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** She takes his hand off the bridle and holds it a moment.

> **MILADY:** "Come and see me on Thursday, Monsieur d'Artagnan. Not by the kitchen door. By the front door."

**Why you turn the page:** nobody in Paris has ever asked him in by the front door.

---

## PAGE 7 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Constance's door and the stair.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Night. The boy bounding up to the street door under the
lantern, grinning. CONSTANCE in the doorway with a lamp, white-faced, a
packed bundle on the step behind her.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Constance! A great lady asked me to her house. By the front door!"
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "I'm going away at first light. The Queen is sending me."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Inside, at the foot of the steep stair,
Constance holding both his hands.

> **CONSTANCE:** "The Cardinal's people keep asking who carried the Queen's letters. She's hiding me in a convent, somewhere in the north."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Which convent? Tell me where, and I'll come."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "If you don't know, nobody can make you tell. That's the whole point."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Constance, sharp now, her hands still in his.

> **CONSTANCE:** "What lady?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Lady de Winter. Milady."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "Everyone at the palace knows her carriage, and nobody knows who pays for it. Be careful of ladies who are kind for no reason."

**Why you turn the page:** she is leaving at first light, and she has warned him.

---

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the street outside Constance's door.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** Dawn, the street blue and empty. A plain coach
at the door. Constance in a brown travelling cloak, her hand on the boy's
cheek; he is trying not to look as if he minds.

> **CONSTANCE:** "Keep the attic. Keep the ring on. And keep your sword in its scabbard until I'm back."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I can promise you two of those."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Constance at the coach window, the coach beginning to move.

> **CONSTANCE:** "When the Queen says I'm safe, you can come for me. Not one day before. Promise me."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I promise. The first day she says."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The coach going away down the empty street; the boy alone
in the doorway, watching it go.

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the street)*
> "He watched the street until there was nothing in it."

**Why you turn the page:** she is gone, and on Thursday there is a front door.

---

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Milady's house.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** A tall front door on a square; a footman in livery
holding it open and bowing low to the boy in his patched ochre.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the stone)*
> "On Thursday a footman bowed to him, and he nearly turned round to see who was behind him."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** A salon of gold candlelight and plum hangings,
a gilt mirror, a harpsichord. MILADY, right, on a sofa, pouring wine; the
boy, left, perched on the edge of a gilt chair, hat on his knee, dazzled.

> **MILADY:** "Tell me everything. The diamonds, London, the ball. All Paris says it was you."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "It was. Well — four of us. But I was the one who got there."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close: Milady's thumb turning the dark ring; her eyes on
him over the glass, warm and waiting; the boy hesitating, then smiling back.

> **MILADY:** "And is there a girl, Monsieur Gascon? There's always a girl."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "There is. I'm not supposed to say where she's gone."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Well — somewhere the Cardinal can't get her. A convent in the north."
>
> **MILADY:** "How very clever of her."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just told her half of what the Cardinal wants, and she is smiling.

## PAGE 10 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the square outside Milady's house, and a tavern.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Evening on the square. The boy running up the
steps of Milady's house with a bunch of flowers, the footman already
opening the door.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** A tavern table: three Musketeers and an empty fourth
chair.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the evening sky, never over the boy)*
> "He went back on Friday, and on Saturday, and every day after that. Milady laughed at all his stories, even the ones that weren't funny. She asked about Gascony and his father, his sword and his horse, and his three friends, and he told her everything, because nobody grand had ever wanted to hear it before.
>
> He forgot to go to the field behind the Carmelites. Twice he forgot that he had promised to eat with his friends.
>
> At the tavern there was an empty chair, and three Musketeers who had stopped looking at the door."

**Why you turn the page:** there is an empty chair at the tavern, and his friends have stopped waiting for him to fill it.
