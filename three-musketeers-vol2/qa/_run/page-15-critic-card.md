## Page intent

## PAGE 15

Dramatic. Athos's bare room at night, one candle and a glass. The boy, hay still in his hair, bursts in gasping: she is the Cardinal's, and he told her about Constance. Athos only says to sit. The boy paces instead, his own hand on his own left shoulder, and tells him the rest: a mark burned into her shoulder, the kind they brand on a thief, a lily, and her vow. Then the page belongs to Athos, for once not quite still: his hand flat on the table beside the glass, his face gone grey in the candlelight. He asks which shoulder. The left. Then she isn't dead. The dominant turn is that question and that answer. The reader turns because Athos knows this woman and is about to say how. The boy's alarm is subordinate to Athos's stillness breaking.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or

focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A line that does not read is blocking; a line that reads is not to be measured. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking; the words and their order are what must match. No object carries scripted words.

### C2 — Athos's stillness breaking does not own the page
The eye must land on Athos close, hand flat beside the glass, face grey in the candle — larger than the boy's arrival. If the boy's pacing dominates, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution of the closing exchange
"Which shoulder?" and "Then she isn't dead." hang from Athos's mouth; "The left. Athos, why does that—" from the boy's. "Sit down." is Athos's. A swapped or ambiguous owner in the closing three lines is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Athos: tallest, long pale face, dark pointed beard, blue cassock over black. The boy: beardless, black hair with hay, ochre. Any vision of Milady or a drawn lily on this page is blocking.

### C5 — Register
Night, one candle, drawn and matte; a daylit room is blocking because the scene is the night of the escape.
