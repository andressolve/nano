## PAGE 21 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the heath at night, and the yard of the Red Dovecote.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Night on a dark heath. Three Musketeers on
horseback, stopped by a cloaked rider with two red-cloaked guards; under the
rider's cloak, a flash of red robe.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** A lonely inn with one lit window and a sign painted with
a red dove; a lady's carriage in the yard with its curtains drawn.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the night sky, never over the riders)*
> "That night Athos, Porthos and Aramis rode the camp's outer patrol. d'Artagnan, who marched with the Guards and not the Musketeers, was not allowed to come.
>
> On the dark heath a cloaked rider stopped them. Under the cloak there was a flash of red, and the voice was very quiet. 'You will escort me to the inn they call the Red Dovecote,' said the Cardinal. 'You will wait downstairs. And afterwards you will forget it.'
>
> In the yard of the inn stood a lady's carriage, with the curtains drawn."

**Why you turn the page:** the Cardinal has come to meet someone, and the three are waiting right underneath.

## PAGE 22 · *dramatic*

**Location:** two — the common room of the Red Dovecote, and the room above it.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Downstairs by the hearth: Porthos and Aramis at dice;
Athos standing by the wall where a broken iron stovepipe runs up through the
ceiling, his ear near it.

> **ATHOS:** "Quiet. This stovepipe is broken. You can hear every word in the room upstairs."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Upstairs, by one candle: THE CARDINAL at a
small table, a plain cloak over his red, writing; MILADY across from him in
a slate-grey hooded travelling cloak, hood back.

> **CARDINAL:** "You'll take ship for England from the north coast within the week, and do the work we spoke of."
>
> **MILADY:** "And my price, Eminence? The Gascon. And the girl he loves."
>
> **CARDINAL:** "The girl is shut away from the Queen, and no use to me now. She is in the convent at Béthune. Take her."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Downstairs: Athos's face beside the stovepipe; Porthos
with the dice stopped in his fist.

**Why you turn the page:** Athos now knows where Constance is, and so does Milady.

## PAGE 23 · *dramatic*

**Location:** two — the room above, and the common room of the Red Dovecote.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Upstairs: the Cardinal folding a paper and
handing it across the candle to Milady.

> **CARDINAL:** "Take this. With it, whatever you do, you did by my order, for France. No court in the kingdom can touch you."
>
> **MILADY:** "Then I shall do a great deal."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Downstairs: the Cardinal coming down the stair with his
hood up; Porthos and Aramis rising. Athos has not moved from the wall.

> **CARDINAL:** "We ride."
>
> **ATHOS:** "My horse has thrown a shoe, Eminence. I'll follow you."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Athos alone at the foot of the dark stair, looking up at
the line of candlelight under the door at the top, his hand on his hilt.

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the stair)*
> "He waited until the hoofbeats were gone. Then he went up."

**Why you turn the page:** Athos is going up the stair to a woman he once hanged.

## PAGE 24 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the room above the common room of the Red Dovecote.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The door open. ATHOS in the doorway, hat in his hand;
MILADY at the table, turning.

> **ATHOS:** "Do you know me?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Milady rising, the candle between them, her
face changing. It is the only time in the book she is afraid.

> **MILADY:** "The Count de la Fère. I heard you went into the army and were killed."
>
> **ATHOS:** "And I thought I'd hanged you. We were both misinformed."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Athos's sword point resting on the table between them;
her hand pushing the folded paper across to him.

> **ATHOS:** "Give me the Cardinal's paper."
>
> **MILADY:** "Take it. You couldn't finish it then. You won't now."
>
> **ATHOS:** "I judged you once, alone, in an hour. I won't do it twice."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Go near the boy or the girl, and next time there will be witnesses."

**Why you turn the page:** Athos has the paper, and he has let Milady go.

## PAGE 25 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the heath at dawn, and a tent in the camp.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Dawn on the heath: a black carriage racing away
up a long straight road to the north, small; Athos on his horse on a rise,
watching it go, the folded paper in his hand.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** A tent in the camp at first light: Athos telling the boy,
the two of them alone; the boy already on his feet.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dawn sky, never over the carriage)*
> "Before the sun was up, the black carriage left the Red Dovecote and turned north. Athos watched it go from a rise, with the Cardinal's paper in his hand, and then he rode back to the camp.
>
> 'Take ship for England from the north coast,' the Cardinal had said. The road to the north coast runs through the flat country, and Béthune lies on that road.
>
> 'She isn't going to wait,' Athos told d'Artagnan. 'She'll stop there on her way to the boat. By the time we get leave to follow her, she'll be half a day ahead.'
>
> d'Artagnan was buckling on his sword before he had finished."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is ready to ride, and nobody has said he can.

## PAGE 26 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Athos's tent in the camp.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** First light through canvas. D'ARTAGNAN, left, at the tent
flap, sword on, hat on, shaking. ATHOS, right, seated on a camp stool, not
moving.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You had her at the end of your sword, and you let her ride away toward Constance?"
>
> **ATHOS:** "The last time I judged her, I did it alone, in an hour, without one question. I won't do that twice."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The boy and Athos face to face, the boy's hand
on his hilt, Athos still seated, looking up at him.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then I'll do it for you. I'm going now."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Walk away from the siege without the King's leave, and they shoot you on the road."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Then they shoot the three of us for following."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** D'ARTAGNAN, left, furious, slamming his sword home in its
scabbard and dropping his hand from the hilt; ATHOS, right, quiet, his hand
on the boy's arm.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then get us leave today, Athos. And I'm not forgiving you for this."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Good. Hate me on the road, if you like. But get leave first, and go alive."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is furious with Athos, and they still need a way out of the camp.

## PAGE 27 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Athos's tent in the camp.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** PORTHOS and ARAMIS ducking into the tent, just arrived.
ARAMIS, low, glancing at the canvas walls.

> **ARAMIS:** "We heard you shouting from three tents away. So did everyone else."
>
> **ARAMIS:** "Don't talk about leaving in here. Every tent in this camp has ears. Last week I heard my own prayers repeated at supper."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Athos standing at last, looking out through the
flap at the city walls and, halfway to them, a ruined fort on the bare
ground. The boy, still angry, beside him.

> **ATHOS:** "Then we need two things. A place where nobody can listen, and leave from the King. I know where to get both."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** ATHOS, left, turning to PORTHOS, right.

> **ATHOS:** "Porthos. Are you hungry?"
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Always. Why?"

**Why you turn the page:** Athos has a plan, and it begins with breakfast.

## PAGE 28 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the camp before La Rochelle.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** Morning. PORTHOS standing on a barrel in the
middle of the camp, enormous, arms wide; soldiers crowding round. Athos,
Aramis and the boy below him.

> **PORTHOS:** "Soldiers! We four will eat our breakfast in the bastion of Saint-Gervais, right under the city's guns, and stay one whole hour!"
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Who'll bet against us?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Soldiers laughing, coins raining into an upturned helmet.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the crowd)*
> "The whole army bet against them."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** D'ARTAGNAN, left, and ATHOS, right, low.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Out there, nobody can listen to us."
>
> **ATHOS:** "And everybody will watch. The King rewards what the whole army saw."

**Why you turn the page:** the bet is made, and four men are walking out to the enemy's walls with a basket.

## PAGE 29 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the open ground between the trenches and the city.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** From behind and above the trenches: the four
walking out across broken, cratered ground toward a ruined fort in the
bright morning, Porthos carrying a huge basket, Aramis a bottle under each
arm, Athos with a folded white tablecloth over his arm, the boy in front.
The whole army standing up along the trenches behind to watch. Ahead, the
grey walls of the city.

> **CAPTION** *(upper edge, over the sky)*
> "They walked out at seven in the morning, with a basket, a tablecloth and four swords. Behind them, the whole army climbed up on the trenches to watch."

**Why you turn the page:** they are about to sit down to breakfast in a fort the enemy wants back.

## PAGE 30 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the bastion of Saint-Gervais.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Inside the ruined bastion: a white tablecloth
over a fallen block of stone, bread, a roast chicken, wine; the four sitting
down to breakfast, Porthos already eating. Behind them a tall cracked wall
leans outward. Athos unfolding the Cardinal's paper.

> **ATHOS:** "Now nobody can hear us. Listen. This is what the Cardinal gave her."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close: the paper in Athos's hands, its words large.

> **PRINTED PAPER** *(in Athos's hands)*
> "It is by my order and for the good of France that the bearer of this has done what has been done. — The Cardinal"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** ARAMIS, left; D'ARTAGNAN, centre, half on his feet;
ATHOS, right, a hand on the boy's arm holding him down.

> **ARAMIS:** "With that, a man could rob a bishop, and no court in France could touch him."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then burn it and let's go. Every minute we sit here, she's closer to Constance."
>
> **ATHOS:** "We keep it. When we catch his lady, the Cardinal will want us hanged, and that paper will stop him."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy wants to run and Athos is holding him, and there is a shout from the walls.
