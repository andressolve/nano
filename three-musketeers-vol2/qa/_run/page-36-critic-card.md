## Page intent

## PAGE 36

Illustrated prose. Four riders on a long straight road through flat country in driving rain, poplars bending, a post inn behind them, the yellow horse in front. The King gave them leave that same afternoon; it is six days to Béthune; they change horses where they can, except the yellow one, which will not be changed and keeps up. The boy does not talk. He promised Constance he would not come before the Queen said she was safe, and he is coming anyway, doing the sum at every milestone: half a day was nothing, half a day was everything. Far ahead, small under a moon, a single rider in black with a red feather gallops north. The dominant turn: leave given, six days north, he is breaking his promise and she is half a day ahead. The riders in the rain own the page. The reader turns because somebody is ahead of them both.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 36 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The four prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, as one field, with no other text. A paragraph that does not read, is missing, doubled, or reordered is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant of the same word are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. The run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue is nonblocking.

### C2 — The ride owns the page
Illustrated-prose mode. Four riders in driving rain on a long flat road, the yellow horse in front, own the page; the prose sits over sky, never over the riders. Prose over a rider's face is blocking.

### C3 — Four riders, one yellow horse
Exactly four riders in the rain, the yellow horse leading and the only yellow animal. A fifth rider with them, or the yellow horse missing, is blocking.

### C4 — The rider ahead
A single small rider in black with a red feather galloping at night under a moon. If he is missing or reads as one of the four, the reason to turn is lost: blocking.

### C5 — Integrity
Horses and riders coherent at small scale; no carriage on the road.
