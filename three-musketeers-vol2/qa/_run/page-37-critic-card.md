## Page intent

## PAGE 37

An illustrated-prose page. The dominant turn: a kind widow comes to the convent, and Constance trusts her. The large picture shows the quiet cloister, Constance with her basket looking up at the gate as a nun lets in a tall lady in a plain dark widow's dress, pale gold hair under a pushed-back veil; below, the two women sit on a stone bench and the lady holds both of Constance's hands. The prose owns the reading and carries the irony: the reader recognises the widow at once, and Constance, who warned the boy about kind ladies, never asks why. The reader turns because the reader knows and she does not. Subordinate: the nun, the well, the roses; they are the quiet place, not the story.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 37 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the convent at Béthune.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** A small cloister of pale stone with roses and a
well. CONSTANCE in her grey dress and white cap, a straw basket on her arm,
looking up at the gate; at the gate, a nun letting in a tall lady in a plain
dark widow's dress, a black veil pushed back from pale gold hair.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The two women on a stone bench in the cloister, the lady
holding both of Constance's hands.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the pale cloister wall, never over the women)*
> "At the convent at Béthune, Constance was safe, and very bored, and very lonely. The nuns were kind and did not talk. She weeded the roses and watched the road.
>
> Then one afternoon a lady came to the gate in a plain widow's dress and asked the nuns to hide her. She said the Cardinal's men were hunting her. She said she had nobody in the world.
>
> She was the kindest person Constance had met since Paris. Constance, who had told a boy to beware of ladies who are kind for no reason, never once asked herself why. They sat in the cloister until the bell rang. Constance thought they were both hiding from the same man."

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows who the kind widow is, and Constance doesn't.

### C1 — Prose transcription fails
All three prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, in one prose field, with no balloon, caption, or other text on the page; a sentence that does not read, is altered, dropped, doubled, or reordered is blocking, and a paragraph that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes or apostrophes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The mode or the turn does not land
The page must read as illustrated prose: the field owns the reading over a picture of the widow being let in at the gate while Constance looks up, and the two women together on the bench with the widow holding Constance's hands. If the widow's arrival or the trust between them cannot be found by eye, or the field covers a face or the clasped hands, it is blocking.

### C3 — The two women collide
The widow must read as the tall pale woman with pale gold hair bound under a pushed-back black veil, in dark widow's clothes; Constance as the small freckled girl with dark curls under a white cap, in light grey with a white collar. If a reader could mistake one for the other, it is blocking.

### C4 — Wrong costume state
Constance wearing an apron or keys, or the widow in plum silk, breaks the disguise the page depends on and is blocking. Exact trim is nonblocking.

### C5 — Generation integrity
A duplicated named figure, a man in the cloister, or gross anatomy on a focal face or the clasped hands is blocking. Rose and stone texture are not.
