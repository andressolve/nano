## Page intent

## PAGE 57

An illustrated-prose page in the attic, in the evening. The dominant image is the boy standing at the attic window in his new Musketeer's blue cassock with the white cross over his old ochre doublet, the ring on his hand, looking not out of the window but down through the open door at the stair, where nobody is moving any more. The prose says what the picture cannot: the cloak is too big in the shoulders and exactly the blue of the other three; he came to Paris wanting to be let in, and the grand lady's front door cost him Constance; he would give the cloak back to hear her downstairs. The dominant turn is quiet and private. The second image is the foot of the stair, Constance's dark door, and his hand lighting the lamp beside it himself. The reader turns because his friends are waiting in the street.

>

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 57 — card

### C1 — Prose transcription fails
All three paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, in one prose field. A paragraph that does not read is blocking; text that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text, a balloon, or a caption is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order must match. No object carries scripted words.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read: the boy alone in the attic in his new blue, looking down at the empty stair, then his hand lighting the lamp at Constance's dark door. If the boy in blue at the window is not the image the eye finds, or the prose sits over his face or body, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — d'Artagnan fails identity or state
Beardless, black hair in his eyes, the blue cassock with the white cross over the ochre doublet. No blue cassock, a beard or moustache, or the ochre doublet with no blue over it is blocking.

### C4 — The house is not empty
Nobody else on the page: no Constance, no figure on the stair or at the door. A second figure is blocking.

### C5 — The lamp
In the lower image the lamp at the door is being lit by his hand, two hands, one action. A lamp already blazing with no hand at it, or a malformed focal hand, is blocking.
