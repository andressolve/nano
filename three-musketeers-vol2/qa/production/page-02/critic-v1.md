## Blind read
D’Artagnan owns the page: he stands alone in a rough attic bedroom at night, poised at the head of a descending stair and looking back toward the room and the open window over Paris. The prose establishes his Gascon origins, arrival in Paris, three Musketeer friends, service to the Queen, and affection from Constance, then turns on the danger that the Cardinal has learned and remembered his name. A lower strip of four hands crossing rapiers shifts the solitary portrait into the friends’ shared oath.

## Visible text
Narrator prose block at upper left (no speaker name or other name is separately lettered):

His name was d’Artagnan. He was
eighteen, and a Gascon — which only
means he came from Gascony, in the far
south of France, where boys are brought
up on garlic and pride and told never to
back down from anyone.

In the spring he had ridden to Paris on a
yellow horse that everyone laughed at.
In one month he had made three friends,
Athos, Porthos and Aramis, the King’s
Musketeers, and the four of them had
sworn: all for one, and one for all. Together
they had saved the Queen from the Cardinal,
the most powerful man in France. The Queen
had given the boy a ring. Constance, the girl
downstairs, had given him her smile.

And the Cardinal had read the boy’s name
off a letter, and remembered it.
