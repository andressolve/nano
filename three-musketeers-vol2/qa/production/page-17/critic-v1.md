VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
The boy confronts the seated musketeer and identifies him as the count; the man answers that the count is dead and names himself Athos. Athos owns the page through the largest central image: he stands and takes down his sword while warning that Milady will kill the boy, then brings the boy under the group’s protection with a rule about shared food and drink. The boy asks about Constance and says where he told Milady she was. The scene then turns from private confession and protection to war when two excited Musketeers arrive with a drummer visible in the street and announce that the King, the Musketeers, and the Cardinal are leaving for La Rochelle.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: Only seven of the eight required lines are visible. Athos’s required line “Then the Cardinal will find out which one. So must we.” is absent after the boy’s Constance line.
- Material reader harm: The missing response removes the causal bridge from the disclosed convent to the Cardinal’s departure, so the reader never learns that both the Cardinal and the Musketeers must search for Constance and the intended page-turn meaning that the war includes her does not land.
- Redraw justification: This is an omitted exact-script line that materially breaks the page’s concluding story turn; releasing the page without it is a greater risk than replacing the complete page.
