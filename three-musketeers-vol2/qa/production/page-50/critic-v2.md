VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
A mustached man in blue, chest-deep in storm water, reaches toward a blonde woman on the dock and offers her lawful judgment in daylight instead of another hanging. The large middle panel, and therefore the page, belongs to the blonde woman: she refuses his offered hand, asserts that she survived his earlier attempt to hang her, and orders the boy holding his belt to release him because he would drown for her. In the final panel, the boy keeps his grip and answers with a flat refusal, turning her command into a test of his loyalty.

## Visible text
- Mustached man in blue in the top panel; balloon tail points to his mouth; no name is lettered: “Take my hand. You’ll have a judge and a trial, in daylight. Not a rope in the dark.”
- Blonde woman on the dock in the middle panel; balloon tail points toward her mouth; no name is lettered: “You hanged me once, and I lived. I won’t owe you my life now.”
- Blonde woman on the dock in the middle panel; the balloon has no visible tail, but its placement beside her attributes it to her; no name is lettered: “Let go of his belt, boy, and he comes with me. He’d drown for me. He always would.”
- Young man behind the mustached man in the bottom panel; balloon tail points to the young man; no name is lettered: “Never.”

## Findings

### Finding C5
- Observation: A coil of rope is visibly lying on the raft beside the lantern in the offer panel.
- Material reader harm: The rope reintroduces the instrument of Athos's threatened hanging at the moment he promises “Not a rope in the dark,” undermining the required nobody-harmed object state and making his offered rescue read as less safe.
- Redraw justification: C5 expressly blocks any shown rope or noose, and this rope sits beside the focal lantern in the offer scene, so the contradiction is consequential enough to warrant replacing the page.
