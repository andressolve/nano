## Stage 1: the artifact

I opened only the named candidate.

- **Lettering, labels, watermarks:** none. There is no signature, caption, name plate or mark anywhere on the plate.
- **Borders:** none of the blocking kind. There are no ruled lines, frames or panel dividers. The outer edge is a ragged laid-paper wash edge, the same "feathered into one warm laid-paper ground, no rules or frames" treatment the plan sets for the other inherited plates. It is a painted paper edge, not a frame.
- **Figures:** none. No person, animal, silhouette or reflection.
- **Consequential objects:** the only one is the rapier hanging on the wall. Its state (hanging, sheathless, on the wall) is the state pp 15–16 need. On p 17 Athos takes it down, which is page action drawn from words. No page that attaches this plate forbids the sword being on the wall. No other object is shown, so nothing is in a forbidden state.
- **Thumbnail and grayscale:** at phone and thumbnail size the plate still reads as a bare whitewashed room with one window, a table and chair at left, a narrow bed at right, and a sword on the wall. In grayscale, the pale wall against the dark timber and the dark vertical blade against the plaster keep the sword the brightest clear accent. The room's identity does not depend on colour.

## Nonblocking observations

1. The ragged paper-wash edge could push a page model toward vignetted, edge-faded panels if the plate is attached whole. The packets for pp 15–17 might want to say the plate is a setting reference only and the panels are full-bleed or ruled per the page's mode. This is a page-packet matter, not a plate defect.
2. On p 17, where Athos takes the sword down, the page card may want to name the hanging rapier against d'Artagnan's too-long sword, so the two blades don't merge in the same panel.
3. The exposed ceiling timbers and diagonal brace are not in the prose description. They are architecture, not props, and they sit on Volume I's approved pages already. Fine to carry.
