# Script gate — round 06 — *The Three Musketeers, Volume II: Milady*

## Cold read

A boy from Gascony, d'Artagnan, has three Musketeer friends. All three wear the King's blue cloak and he doesn't, and he wants one badly. In his red room the Cardinal, who remembers the boy's name from Volume I, takes the boy away from Rochefort, who has failed four times, and gives him to Milady. She stages a runaway carriage, the boy stops it, and she invites him in by the front door, which is the thing he has always wanted. That same night Constance is sent into hiding at a convent in the north. She warns him about ladies who are kind for no reason, and she makes him promise not to come for her until the Queen says she is safe. He promises, then goes to Milady and tells her about the convent in the north.

He overhears Milady reporting to Rochefort, attacks her, sees the lily brand on her shoulder, and escapes through a window into a hay cart. Athos asks "Which shoulder?", says "Then she isn't dead," and tells the story of the count who hanged his wife. The army marches on La Rochelle with Milady's carriage in the Cardinal's train. Poisoned wine arrives with the three friends' names on it. At the Red Dovecote, Athos listens through a broken stovepipe while the Cardinal gives Milady the girl at Béthune and a paper that pardons anything she does. Athos goes up the stair, takes the paper, and lets Milady go. The boy is furious. To get the King's leave, the four eat breakfast in a bastion under the enemy's guns, and the boy counts the minutes the whole time. The Cardinal works out where they are going and sends Rochefort north ahead of them. Milady befriends the lonely Constance at Béthune. Constance sees the yellow horse coming, refuses to run, and drinks to d'Artagnan. She dies in his arms minutes after he arrives. At a flooded ferry the four say her crimes to her face. She cuts her own rope, refuses Athos's hand and the offer of a trial, and the river takes her. The boy gives the Cardinal back his own pardon. The Cardinal burns it and gives him a commission with the name left blank. Each of the three refuses it, and Athos writes the boy's name. In the attic the boy lights Constance's lamp himself.

I could tell that story to someone else after one read. **I stopped once.** On p26 Athos's speech runs across three balloons with nothing from the boy between them: "Then they shoot the three of us for following." / "Good. Hate me on the road…" I read the "Good." twice. Taken in order, it answers his own line about the three of them being shot. I also noticed I never saw the boy decide to stay. When p27 opens he is still in the tent and I had to supply the decision myself.

Smaller hitches:
- "the Louvre" (p7) is a word a seven-year-old won't know.
- "the work we spoke of" (p22) goes nowhere.
- Rochefort disappears between p39 and p53.

None of these three stopped my reading.

## Is it alive?

**Movement I (pp 1–9). Alive.** The front door carries the movement. The boy's appetite drives it, Milady's interest feels like being let in, and I understood why he goes back.
- Milady's subtext is exact: "How very clever of her" (p9).
- Constance's warning about "ladies who are kind for no reason" (p7) is the best plant in the book.
- p8's "I can promise you two of those" gives the boy a voice of his own.
- The want-from is clear on every page:
  - Milady wants what the boy knows.
  - The boy wants to be admired.
  - Constance wants him to be careful while she can't watch him.

**Movement II (pp 10–18). Alive, and the best-built movement.**
- The empty chair (p10) earns its prose page.
- "I don't know that name" (p11) is a lie the reader feels without being told.
- The window (p14) is the right size of joke after a real threat.
- "Then she isn't dead" (p15) is a page turn I could not resist.
- Athos's "a friend" story (p16) is told in his voice and stays out of the scene.
- Attribution-strip: covering the names, I can tell Porthos ("Of course, I'm me"), Aramis ("I'm trying to think about God") and Athos ("Her name.") apart from each other and from the boy.

**Movement III (pp 19–25). Alive.**
- The grass going black (p20) is enacted rather than narrated, and Porthos's "That was good grass" is the right voice at the right moment.
- The stovepipe is logistics, but it is one line and it pays off right away, so it passes.
- p24 has real heat. "We were both misinformed" is in character for both of them. Athos letting her go is a moral choice the book later makes him pay for.

**Movement IV (pp 26–36). Alive, with one flaw at its hinge.** The bastion is a delight for these readers: Porthos on the barrel, the chicken, the wall, the handkerchief flag. It is not a digression, because the boy keeps the clock running through it ("Forty-five more, and all that time she's riding"). p35 gives the Cardinal a want he doesn't say outright, "I would rather have that boy in my red than in the ground," and it carries straight through to the end of the book. The flaw is on p26, the confrontation that opens the movement. The boy's fury is real, but his decision to obey is never shown, and the new "Good." lands on the wrong line (Finding 1).

**Movement V (pp 37–45). Better than good.** Constance chooses on every page: to trust, to stay at the window, to drink. Each choice comes from the same love and sense that the book has given her. Milady's line on p38, "Lady de Winter. I've heard of her. You were right to warn him," is the cruelest subtext in the script. "Then let's drink to him first" (p40) gives a real chill. p43's "You came. You promised, and you came" turns the boy's broken promise into her comfort, and that hurts in the right way. On p44 the silent page comes before anyone speaks of the chase. On p45 Athos takes the blame, and "You didn't pour the wine either" sets up the Cardinal's evasion on p54.

**Movement VI (pp 46–52). Better than good.** The judgment at the ferry has real heat because each charge is spoken by a different voice. "That one is mine to forgive" makes Athos's past public in a single line. The chain of four men in the flood puts the friendship on screen as the climax. "He'd drown for me. He always would." / "Never." works as a complete answer to a complete demand. The "Good." on p52 is a strong close, as long as p26 sets it up properly.

**Movement VII (pp 53–58). Alive.**
- The boy returning the paper is a choice that costs him something.
- "I named a convent. I did not pour the wine." reads as the Cardinal himself, not the writer speaking through him.
- Athos's "Too much for Athos. Too little for the man I was." is the best refusal in the book.
- p57 links the front door to the lamp, and the ending is earned.

**Overall.** This is a good book, and from Béthune to the end it is better than good. I felt it. The one thing between it and approval is a single page where the book's sharpest relationship turns on a beat the reader has to fill in.

## Mandatory findings

**1. p26: the boy's choice to stay is never made on the page, and the newly added "Good." answers the wrong line.**

*What the script does.* Panel 2 ends with the boy: "Then I'll do it for you. I'm going now." Athos then speaks three balloons in a row: "Walk away from the siege without the King's leave, and they shoot you on the road." / "Then they shoot the three of us for following." / (panel 3) "Good. Hate me on the road, if you like. But get leave first, and go alive." The boy says nothing after "I'm going now." Panel 3 shows only Athos's hand on his sword arm. On p27 the boy is still in the tent, "still angry."

*What a first-time reader has to repair:*
- **The meaning of "Good."** Read aloud in order, it follows directly after Athos's own line about the three friends being shot, so a listener can hear "Good" as a comment on that. The intended meaning, "good, be angry with me," depends on the boy's anger, and panel 3 neither stages it nor gives it a line. This is the tic p52 pays off ("He always said it when things were bad"), so it has to land clearly the first time. A "Good." whose meaning only becomes clear after p52 is the "understandable after looking back" case the mandate flags for revision.
- **The boy's decision.** The page's turn is whether he deserts now or waits for leave, and that turn happens off the page. The reader has to work out from p27 that he gave in. This breaks §4.2 ("important decisions receive enough panels and dialogue to feel chosen rather than reported"), and here the decision is not even reported. It also weakens the boy's appetite at exactly the point where he loses an argument he cares about most.

*Fix.* Add one balloon to the boy in panel 3, before Athos's line. In it the boy chooses to stay and says what that costs him, so "Good." answers it directly. For example:

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then get us leave today, Athos. And I'm not forgiving you for this."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Good. Hate me on the road, if you like. But get leave first, and go alive."

Restage panel 3 so the boy's furious face sits beside Athos's hand on his sword arm. The boy needs his sword hand back too, to show he has stood down: he can slam his sword back into its scabbard, or drop his hand from the hilt. Optionally, move "Then they shoot the three of us for following" into panel 2 as a separate beat after a reaction from the boy, so that Athos's two threats don't run straight into the "Good." The page stays inside its text budget either way.

## Nonblocking observations

1. **p7.** "Everyone at the Louvre knows her carriage." Young readers won't know what the Louvre is. Use "Everyone at the palace…" or "Everyone at the Queen's palace…".
2. **pp 24 / 26.** Athos's judgment line appears almost word for word twice in three pages ("I judged you once, alone, in an hour. I won't do it twice." / "…I did it alone, in an hour, without one question. I won't do that twice."), and p47 echoes it a third time. A refrain is fine. Still, on p26 the line would hit harder if it were about the boy, for example "I won't make you what I made myself in that wood."
3. **p22.** "do the work we spoke of" is never mentioned again. It works as a secret the reader may wonder about, but this volume never sends Milady to England. If it isn't planted for Volume III, cut the clause.
4. **Rochefort between p39 and p53.** He brings the carriage at first light and then disappears. The empty carriage and the coachman who "had run" (p46) leave a reader wondering whether he was with her. Adding one clause would settle it, for example on p45: "…Rochefort rode ahead of it."
5. **p47.** Why Milady is still on a ferry she could have taken across is only implied by "The ferryman has run." A few more words would make it plain, for example "…and I can't work it alone in this flood."
6. **p35 metadata.** The turn line says "the four have their leave," but at that point they don't have it yet, because the Cardinal is only predicting it. It doesn't render, so this is housekeeping only.
7. **Five-word audit, all 31 balloons:** every one is either a complete thought or a deliberate answer to a complete line, has one visible referent, and needs no notes. p26's "Good." is not in the audit list because its balloon is longer than five words. It is handled in Finding 1.
8. **Budget and architecture:**
   - **Modes:** 37 dramatic, 12 prose, 9 spectacle (64 / 21 / 16).
   - **Locations:** no page has more than two.
   - **Text budget:** the densest dramatic pages are p30 and p54 at about 92 words each, under the ~105 ceiling. The longest prose fields are p36 (~135 words) and p2 (~131), both inside the 140 exceptional limit.
   - **Cast and crowds:** the cast is held at 8, and there is no functional crowd.
   - **Chronicle:** the mandate opts out, which is correct.

Findings: 1 (p26).
