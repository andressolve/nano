# Script gate, round 2: *The Three Musketeers, Volume II: Milady*

## Cold read

**What happens, as I read it first.** It is the summer after the Queen's ball. D'Artagnan is eighteen. He has three Musketeer friends, a ring from the Queen, and a girl downstairs. He wants the blue cloak, the reward for "something nobody in France can forget." In his red room the Cardinal sets Milady on him, and Milady stages a runaway carriage so the boy will rescue her. She invites him in by the front door, and that is exactly the welcome he has wanted since Volume I. Constance is sent to a hidden convent "in the north." She leaves him a warning about ladies who are kind for no reason. On his first visit he tells Milady where Constance is.

He later overhears Milady with Rochefort and sees the lily branded on her shoulder. He gets away through the window. Athos asks "Which shoulder?", then tells the story of the count who hanged his sixteen-year-old wife. Then the war. At La Rochelle someone sends the boy poisoned wine. Through a broken stovepipe Athos hears the Cardinal give Milady the girl at Béthune as her price, along with a blank pardon. Athos goes upstairs, takes the paper, and lets her go. The bastion breakfast wins the King's leave. Rochefort rides ahead of them. At Béthune Milady becomes Constance's only friend. Constance sees the yellow horse and refuses to run, so Milady poisons her. Constance dies in the boy's arms. At the flooded ferry the four accuse Milady. She cuts herself loose, refuses Athos's hand and goes over the weir. In Paris the boy gives the Cardinal back his pardon, unasked. The Cardinal answers with a commission with the name left blank, and Athos writes "D'Artagnan."

**Where I stopped or reread:**
- **p17, "She keeps that kind of promise."** I went back looking for the promise. It is on p15, before the whole flashback.
- **p25, "She's a day ahead of us already."** I had just watched her carriage leave before sunrise.
- **p26.** I could not tell whether the boy knows Athos had Milady at sword's point and let her go. I had to wait until p44 to find out, and even there it is unclear.
- **p29, "hanged for this".** Hanged for what? For the breakfast?
- **p33, "One more minute? She's a day closer."** I tripped on the arithmetic.
- **p34, Rochefort.** How does he know the tall one stayed behind at the Red Dovecote? He was not there.
- **p36, "They were both hiding from the same man."** For a moment I thought Milady had broken with the Cardinal.
- **p53.** I waited for the boy to say Constance's name to the man who gave her away. He never does.
- **p56, "let in by a gate, and a fort".** Which gate?

## Is it alive?

Yes. This is a good script with a real heart. The fixes below are mostly local. One of them, p53, is the climax.

**Movement I (pp 1–9).** It works. The front door is a real temptation and I understood why he goes back: "he nearly turned round to see who was behind him" earns the boy's foolishness. Constance's farewell has life ("I can promise you two of those"). On p9 his hesitation, followed by the slip, is the right size of mistake. On p3 the opening banter is light on heat but sets the boy's appetite, which is acceptable for the opening scene.

**Movement II (pp 10–18).** The best stretch in the book. On p11 Athos sets his glass down and says "I don't know that name": real subtext that plays without a word of explanation. "Which shoulder?" / "Then she isn't dead." is a superb page turn. The p16 confession is told in Athos's voice and is restrained. The window jump is joyful. One leak: the antecedent on p17.

**Movement III (pp 19–25).** It's alive. The poisoned grass, "I have never in my life sent anyone wine. I drink it.", the stovepipe and "We were both misinformed." are all excellent. The weak spot is what this movement does to the boy. Athos has his wife at the end of his sword and lets her go, but the one person with the most at stake never gets to react on the page. That is the biggest missing heat in the book outside p53.

**Movement IV (pp 26–35).** Fun, loud and properly motivated now that "The King rewards what the whole army saw" ties the bastion to the leave. Porthos is fully himself ("I left half a chicken behind it"). The boy's urgency runs under the comedy as it should. Two small time and logic slips (p33, p34).

**Movement V (pp 36–44).** Strong, and the hardest to get right. Constance repeating her own warning to Milady's face (p37) is chilling. "Then let's drink to him first." is the scariest line in the book. "You didn't pour the wine either." is the emotional climax of the friendship. The p36 narration briefly lies to the reader.

**Movement VI (pp 45–51).** The chain of four men in the flood is the image of the book. "He'd drown for me. He always would." is cruel and true. "Never." lands. The callback on p51 to Athos saying "Good." pays off cleanly.

**Movement VII (pp 52–57).** Competent, and in the wrong place cold. The boy stands in front of the man who handed Constance to her murderer as a price, a fact Aramis said aloud in front of him on p47. The scene is spent on the paper's wording and a threat of prison. Constance is not mentioned once in the Cardinal's study. The boy then accepts a commission from that hand with no beat of resistance. The ledger plans the Cardinal "telling himself he did not pour the wine", and that line, which echoes p44, is not on the page. I felt the twist of giving the paper back. I did not feel the boy's grief and anger at the one man left to aim them at. That is a `REVISE` on its own.

## Mandatory findings

### 1. p17, panel 2: an antecedent lost across the flashback
- **What fails:** Athos says "She keeps that kind of promise." The promise is Milady's threat on p15 ("Nobody who's seen it stays alive"). It was spoken before the p16 flashback about the marriage and the hanging.
- **What the reader has to repair:** Right after a story about a wedding, "that kind of promise" invites the wrong reading (her vows? the count's?). This line is what motivates "eat only what we eat," which sets up the poisoned wine on p19. A listener would ask "what promise?"
- **Fix:** Name it. For example: **ATHOS:** "She swore you wouldn't live, because you've seen the lily. She keeps that kind of promise."

### 2. pp 25–26: the boy never reacts to Athos letting Milady go
- **What fails:** On p24 Athos has Milady at sword's point and lets her go. On p25 the prose says only that Athos told the others she will stop at Béthune. On p26 the boy talks only about leave. On p44 Athos confesses ("I had her at the end of my sword … and let her go") and the boy forgives him at once.
- **What the reader has to repair:** Did the boy know since p25 and say nothing? Then his appetite has been written out at the worst moment. Or does he learn it only on p44? The reader cannot tell, and either way the most charged disagreement between the two leads is never staged. This is a heat and want failure: the boy's want from Athos right now ("why didn't you finish it?") is the scene's engine, and it is missing.
- **Fix:** Open p26 with it, before the tactics. For example:
  - **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You had her at the end of your sword, and you let her ride away toward Constance?"
  - **ATHOS:** "I judged her once, alone, in an hour. I won't do it twice. Hate me on the road."

  Then move to the leave problem. This also turns "You didn't pour the wine either." on p44 into a true payoff: forgiveness of something he was angry about. Add the page or restage as needed; do not cut causal lines to make room.

### 3. p25 and p33: the "a day ahead" arithmetic
- **What fails:**
  - On p25 Athos says "She's a day ahead of us already." The reader has just seen her leave before sunrise, a few hours earlier.
  - On p33 the boy says "One more minute? She's a day closer to Constance!" Read aloud, a minute cannot make anyone a day closer.
- **What the reader has to repair:** Both lines carry the urgency that drives Movement IV, and both make the reader stop and do sums.
- **Fix:**
  - p25: "By the time we get leave to follow her, she'll be a day ahead."
  - p33: "One more minute? She's already a day ahead of us, and getting closer to Constance every minute!" (or similar)

### 4. p29, panel 3: "hanged for this"
- **What fails:** "One day the Cardinal will want us hanged for this, and that paper will stop him."
- **What the reader has to repair:** "This" could be the breakfast, the bet, the paper or the chase. This line is the only setup for why the paper is kept, and pp 52–53 depend on it (it is a shield, and the boy gives the shield up). A listener asks "hanged for what?"
- **Fix:** "One day the Cardinal will want us hanged for whatever happens when we catch his lady, and that paper will stop him."

### 5. p34, panel 1: Rochefort knows something he was never told
- **What fails:** **ROCHEFORT:** "The tall one is the one who stayed behind at the Red Dovecote."
- **What the reader has to repair:** Rochefort was not at the Red Dovecote. Only the Cardinal and the three Musketeers were, and Milady has gone north. A character is acting on information the reader never saw him get. It also makes the Cardinal ask a question he can answer himself, which breaks his ledger voice.
- **Fix:** Give the deduction to the Cardinal. For example:
  - **CARDINAL:** "The tall one rode with me to the Red Dovecote, and his horse threw a shoe. He heard me name Béthune."
  - Rochefort supplies only "Three Musketeers and the Gascon, Eminence."

### 6. p36: the narration states Milady's lie as fact
- **What fails:** The prose ends: "They were both hiding from the same man."
- **What the reader has to repair:** Narration is asserting that Milady is hiding from the Cardinal. The reader knows she serves him, so the line makes them wonder whether she has broken with him, until p38 sets it right. That is "recoverable," which is a revision flag. The dramatic irony works better when the reader is sure.
- **Fix:** Anchor it to Constance's belief: "Constance thought they were both hiding from the same man."

### 7. pp 53–55: the climax with the Cardinal has no heat about Constance
- **What fails:**
  - The Cardinal opens with "A lady in my service is dead." The boy answers only defensively ("We never touched her …"). The scene turns on the paper's wording.
  - He never says Constance's name to the man who traded her life as Milady's price (p22, repeated aloud in his presence on p47).
  - On p55 he accepts a commission written by that man without a beat of resistance. Athos's justification, "Nobody in France will forget that," refers to a private act in a closed study.
- **What the reader has to repair:**
  - Why the boy, who drew his sword to kill Milady on p44, has no anger left for the man who handed her the girl.
  - Why he would wear anything that man signed. He has just said "I don't want anything you've signed", and then he takes the commission.
  - The ending's moral fork is left for the reader to build.
- **Fix:**
  - **p53:** Make the boy's want from the Cardinal in this room explicit. For example:
    - **D'ARTAGNAN:** "She was in your service when she poisoned Constance. You gave her Constance as her price. Aramis heard you."
    - **CARDINAL:** "I named a convent. I did not pour the wine."

    This deliberately echoes p44 and gives the Cardinal his own moral evasion on the page. Then the boy lays down the paper, refusing the shield because of who signed it. Split p53 across two pages if the text needs room.
  - **p55:** Let the boy refuse the commission for that reason ("I won't wear his name on me"). Athos answers why he should: for example, "It isn't his. It's the King's blue, and you earned it at the bastion with the whole army watching. Wear it, and make him look at you in it." Then Athos writes the name. That ties the payoff back to the p3 rule ("the King gives it, when you do something nobody in France can forget") and to the p57 caption.

### 8. p56: the closing interior beat is muddled
- **What fails:** "He had been let in by a gate, and a fort, and a front door he should never have gone through, and now by the King's Musketeers."
- **What the reader has to repair:** Which gate? What does it mean to be "let in by a fort"? This is the book's last interior sentence, and a reader stops on it to decode the list. That is the writer's pattern speaking, not the boy.
- **Fix:** Make it concrete. For example: "He had come to Paris wanting to be let in. A grand lady had let him in by her front door, and it had cost him Constance. Now the King's Musketeers had let him in, and he would have given the cloak back to hear someone moving about downstairs."

## Nonblocking observations

- **p22, "do my errand there."** The errand in England is never named. It works as a sealed secret, but the Cardinal saying it to his own agent sounds coy. Consider "do the work we spoke of." Also on p22, "The girl is no use to me now" carries his indifference well, but a clause such as "now that she's shut away from the Queen" would remove the "why not?"
- **Mandate departure on pp 36–40 and p38.** The volume mandate says Milady appears "only where one of the four can see her or hear her," except pp 4 and 22. Pp 36–40 (with Constance) and p38 (with Rochefort) break that. The script is right and the pages are among its best: Constance is an active chooser and nothing here is the villain's own book. Reconcile by amending the mandate line, not the script.
- **p52, "It was always yours."** The antecedent wobbles between the paper and "this part." Both readings land the same feeling, but "This was always yours to finish" is cleaner.
- **p54.** The Cardinal quotes his paper in words that differ from p29's printed text. Either quote it exactly or have him say "My own words."
- **p54/p55.** "Lieutenant's commission" on p54 becomes "A Musketeer's commission" on p55. Pick one. "Lieutenant" also quietly makes the boy his friends' superior, which cuts against p56's "exactly the blue of the other three."
- **p47.** Porthos and Aramis hear "the day I married you" for the first time and have no reaction. A glance between them in panel 2 would cost nothing.
- **p2.** The prose is about 132 words, inside the exceptional ceiling. Keep it there.
- **Five-word audit.** All 34 balloons pass apart from the one noted at p52: each is a complete thought or a direct answer to a complete question in the same panel, with one referent.
- **Architecture.** Every page has exactly one mode, a named turn, one dominant panel and at most two locations. Dialogue pages are within budget. The mode balance is 36/12/9, about 63/21/16, which is acceptable.
