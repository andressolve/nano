# Script gate, round 7: *The Three Musketeers, Volume II: Milady*

**Read:** `08-FULL-SCRIPT.md`, all 58 pages, in order and cold, before any other document. After that read: `qa/script/audit-r07.txt`, `00-VOLUME-MANDATE.md` and `04-CHARACTER-LEDGER.md`. I judged the whole script fresh. I did not count any earlier round's approval toward this one.

---

## 1. Cold read

A Gascon boy called d'Artagnan is eighteen. He has three Musketeer friends and wants a blue cloak of his own. The Cardinal has given up on his scarred man Rochefort and sets a beautiful woman, Milady, on the boy instead. She fakes a runaway carriage so that he will rescue her. Then she asks him to visit by the front door. The same night Constance, the girl downstairs, is sent to a hidden convent "in the north". She warns him about "ladies who are kind for no reason" and makes him promise not to come for her until the Queen says it is safe. On his first visit, dazzled, he tells Milady about the convent. He keeps going back and drops his friends.

Days later he arrives early and overhears her report to Rochefort. He confronts her, and in the struggle her sleeve tears and he sees a lily branded on her shoulder. He escapes through a window into a hay cart. Athos asks only "Which shoulder?" and then tells, as a story about "a friend", how he married her and hanged her. Then war takes them all to La Rochelle. A bottle of poisoned wine arrives with the friends' names on it. Through a broken stovepipe at an inn, Athos overhears the Cardinal give Milady the girl's convent (Béthune) as her price, along with a blank pardon. Athos goes upstairs, takes the pardon and lets her go. The boy is furious. To earn leave from the King, the four eat breakfast in an enemy fort under fire for a bet, and Porthos pushes a wall onto the attackers. The Cardinal watches, guesses what they heard, and sends Rochefort north to warn her.

At Béthune, Milady poses as a hunted widow and befriends Constance. Constance even tells her she warned the boy against "Lady de Winter". The four come into sight, Constance refuses to run, and Milady poisons her wine and leaves by the back gate. Constance dies in the boy's arms. They chase Milady through a storm to a ferry. Each of them names what she did. She cuts the rope. Athos wades into the flood, held by a chain of his three friends, and offers his hand and a fair trial. She refuses, and the river takes her over the weir. Back in Paris the boy gives the Cardinal his own pardon back. The Cardinal tears it up and writes a commission with the name left blank. The boy offers it to each friend in turn, and Athos writes the boy's name on it. He lights the lamp at Constance's door himself and goes out to his friends.

**Where I stopped or reread.** There were three places. None of them made me reconstruct the story.
- **p4:** "Find out where the girl goes." For one beat I read "goes" as where she goes each day. Three pages later Constance's departure settles it.
- **p26:** "But get leave first." The boy has just said "get us leave today". Athos's "But" pushes against a point the boy has already given up. It is a small bump for the ear.
- **p53:** Nothing in the staging has Athos hand over the paper. The page-turn line assumes the boy has it, and p54 confirms it.

## 2. Is it alive?

Yes. This is a good book, and I wanted to turn its pages.

**Movement I (pp 1–9).** The appetite is real. The boy wants the cloak and wants to be let in, and the front-door motif makes his slip with Milady something the reader respects rather than mere foolishness. Milady's want from him on p9 is clear and cold ("And is there a girl … There's always a girl."). His reply is the most damaging line in the book, and he says it while smiling. Constance's goodbye (pp 7–8) has heat and subtext: "I can promise you two of those." Athos's "Good. Nor was I." on p3 marks him out in four words.

**Movement II (pp 10–18).** The tavern on p11 is the best use of subtext so far. Athos asks "Describe her," then "Her name," says "I don't know that name," and leaves his wine untouched. The lily, the window and "Then she isn't dead" make a run of pages a ten-year-old will hold his breath through. The story on p16 is told as Athos's own speech and is properly restrained.

**Movement III (pp 19–25).** "That's my name on a bottle I never sent. I have never in my life sent anyone wine. I drink it." and "That was good grass." are two voices you could tell apart with the names covered. The stovepipe scene puts the reader in the room as the Cardinal trades a girl's life away, and Milady's "Then I shall do a great deal" is chilling. On p24 both of them want something from the other, and the confrontation runs hot: "We were both misinformed."

**Movement IV (pp 26–36).** p26 now works. The boy chooses not to walk out, and Athos's "Good." means something here because it is said at a bad moment. The bastion is the book's playground: the bet, the chicken, the wall, "one minute more, for good manners". It never lets go of the boy's clock ("Fifteen minutes. Forty-five more, and all that time she's riding."), so the fun pulls against his urgency instead of replacing it. The Cardinal's hill scene (p35) shows him wanting something, the boy "in my red", and Rochefort's single line deflates him. p36's "Half a day was nothing. Half a day was everything." is the boy's appetite turned into prose.

**Movement V (pp 37–45).** This is where the book is better than good. p38 is the best dramatic page in the script. Constance tells Milady, by name, that she warned the boy about Milady. Milady answers "You were right to warn him," and the reader can hardly bear it. The death is quiet and exact ("You came. You promised, and you came."). In the aftermath (p44) each friend grieves in his own voice. On p45 Athos takes the blame and the boy gives it back ("You didn't pour the wine either"), which repairs the rift from p26 without a speech.

**Movement VI (pp 46–52).** At the ferry, each accuser names a different crime. Athos's "That one is mine to forgive. The others aren't." is the volume's moral centre, and he says it in character. The chain of four men holding each other in the river is the right climax for a book about four friends. Milady's refusal ("I won't owe you my life now") is her choice and nobody else's. p52's "Good." lands as a recognition. The one weakness is that its last clause explains what the reader has already felt (see observations).

**Movement VII (pp 53–58).** The boy's return of the paper is a choice, not a plot device, and the Cardinal's "I named a convent. I did not pour the wine." gives him his own evasion, echoing Athos. The commission scene (p56) gives all three friends their own voices: "Too much for Athos. Too little for the man I was." / "I'd have to read things." / "Next year. Probably." In p57's lamp the boy ends the book having gained the thing he wanted at the cost of the person he wanted.

**Presence tests.**
- **Want-from:** passes in every scene. The weakest is p27, where Aramis mainly wants them to stop talking, and that is enough for a three-panel bridge.
- **Logistics:** p27's "two things … I know where to get both" is a plan, but it comes with Athos's appetite behind it and pays off as a joke ("Are you hungry?"). No page is mostly arrangements.
- **Aphorism:** none blocks. The Cardinal's "Men who can't be bought are rare…" is epigrammatic, but it is his habit of speech and it answers a direct question.
- **Attribution-strip:** passes on every dramatic page I tested (pp 11, 20, 30, 31, 48, 56).
- **Enacted, not narrated:** the poisoning, the lily, the wall, the chain and the refusal are all on panel.
- **Page-turn:** every line names a real question or threat.

## 3. Mandatory findings

NONE.

What I checked:
- **Introductions:**
  - Rochefort is named and given his want on p4.
  - Milady is named on pp 4 and 6.
  - Constance and the Cardinal are introduced on p2.
  - The three Musketeers are named to their faces on p3.
  - The Count de la Fère is set up on pp 16–17 before p24 depends on it.
- **Causal chain:** the convent leak runs p9 → p12 → p22. The paper runs p23 → p24 → p30 → p53–55. Leave runs p26 → p28 → p35 → p36. The warning runs p35 → p39 → p40.
- **Clock:** "half a day" is consistent on pp 25, 34 and 36. Rochefort says "by noon" on p39, the four arrive "at nine" on p42, and the carriage is at first light on p40.
- **Five-word audit:** all 31 short balloons pass. Each is either a direct answer to the line before it or an imperative with a visible object. "Never." answers "Let go of his belt"; "The Gascon?" answers "Four of them"; "Constance?" is spoken over her visible body.
- **Architecture:** one mode per page, at most two locations per page. On pp 22–23 the cut between two floors is itself the eavesdropping turn, so the two-location rule is met.
- **Mode balance:** 37 dramatic / 12 illustrated prose / 9 spectacle, which is 64% / 21% / 15.5%. Illustrated-prose pages are present.
- **Mandate:** the volume opts out of Chronicle pages. Milady appears only where one of the four sees or hears her, or where she is working on Constance.
- **Cast:** no functional crowd, and all 8 characters earn their place.

## 4. Nonblocking observations

1. **p26, Athos's last balloon.** "But get leave first, and go alive" pushes back against a demand the boy has just made himself ("Then get us leave today, Athos"). Something like "Good. Hate me on the road, if you like. I'll get us the leave — and we'll all go alive." would keep the "Good." and make Athos take on the boy's demand.
2. **p53, panel 3 staging.** Add Athos putting the folded paper into the boy's open hand. That makes the handover visible, so the page-turn line ("the Cardinal's own paper in his coat") is true of what is shown.
3. **p52, last clause.** "…and for the first time the boy understood that it was the only way Athos could bear them" explains a recognition the reader has already made from pp 3, 26 and 45. You could cut it after "He always said it when things were bad," or keep it. It is the narration's one moment of pointing, and it is gentle.
4. **p4, "Find out where the girl goes."** Optionally: "Find out where the Queen hides that girl." That closes the momentary misreading and sets up p7.
5. **p27/p28, the reason for privacy.** The only private business at the bastion is the decision to keep the paper (p30). The leave is the real engine and it reads clearly. If you revise anything here, give one more line of the bastion talk to something they could not have said in a tent.
