# Volume Architecture — 58 pages

Paris, La Rochelle, and the north of France; the summer and autumn after the
Queen's ball (1625, by Volume I's calendar; history's siege date is moved to
the story, not the story to the siege).

---

## I — THE LADY IN THE CARRIAGE · pp 1–9

**Emotional entry:** restless. **Exit:** a boy in a golden salon, telling a
smiling woman exactly what she came for.

A month after the ball, nothing has happened, and d'Artagnan, the only one of
the four without a blue cloak, is itching for something nobody can forget.
The reader meets Milady before he does: in the Cardinal's study, where
Rochefort has been beaten four times by the boy and the Cardinal wants a
different kind of hand. She promises the boy in a week. Her horses bolt by
the river; he stops them; she opens her front door to him. Constance is sent
north by the Queen that night, will not tell him where, and warns him about
ladies who are kind for no reason. On Thursday, in the grandest room he has
ever stood in, he tells Milady that the girl he loves is safe in a convent in
the north.

**The turn:** the boy hands Milady the first half of what the Cardinal wants.
**Page-turn out:** the reader knows who she is; he doesn't.

## II — THE LILY · pp 10–18

**Emotional entry:** dazzled. **Exit:** an army on the road south, and a
carriage in it.

He goes back every day; Athos asks him to describe her and goes very still.
Arriving early, the boy overhears Milady and Rochefort: the girl is in the
north, and when Milady is done with the boy he is Rochefort's. She draws a
knife; in the struggle her sleeve tears and he sees a lily burned on her
shoulder; she swears he will die for having seen it; he goes out of the
window. In Athos's room Athos asks which shoulder, sits down, and says, "Then
she isn't dead" — and tells the story he has never told. The King marches on
La Rochelle; the four march with him, and so does a carriage the reader
knows.

**The turn:** the boy knows what Milady is, and Athos's secret is out.
**Page-turn out:** she has sworn to kill him, and she is going where he is
going.

## III — THE RED DOVECOTE · pp 19–25

**Emotional entry:** hunted. **Exit:** Athos at dawn with the Cardinal's
paper, and a carriage gone north.

At the camp a bottle arrives "from your friends"; Athos never sent wine in
his life; the grass where they pour it goes black. That night the three
Musketeers escort a cloaked man to an inn called the Red Dovecote; through a
broken stovepipe Athos hears the Cardinal send Milady to London and hears her
price: the boy's life, and the girl in the convent at Béthune. The Cardinal
writes her a paper that makes anything she does his. Athos climbs the stair:
the woman he hanged looks up, and he takes the paper at the point of his
sword. At dawn she is gone up the north road. Béthune is on the way to the
sea.

**The turn:** the four know where she is going and that she is a day ahead.
**Page-turn out:** they cannot leave the army, and they cannot write.

## IV — THE BASTION · pp 26–36

**Emotional entry:** trapped. **Exit:** four horses on the north road.

Deserters are shot on the road and every letter crosses the Cardinal's desk.
The boy rounds on Athos for letting her go; Athos takes it. Then he does the one thing that buys privacy and leave at once: the four bet
the camp they will breakfast for an hour in the ruined bastion under the
city's walls. They walk out with a basket while the army watches, make their
plan between mouthfuls, beat back two attacks, and Porthos brings the cracked
wall down between them and the city. Aramis's handkerchief flies on a pike.
The Cardinal, watching from the hill, wants the boy in red; Rochefort says he
would rather die. The leave is given. Rochefort is sent north ahead of them.

**The turn:** the four earn their road with the whole army watching.
**Page-turn out:** six days north, and she has a day.

## V — BÉTHUNE · pp 37–45

**Emotional entry:** dread. **Exit:** the boy on his knees in a cloister.

At the convent Constance is lonely and the new lady is kind: a widow hiding
from the Cardinal too. Constance tells her that a Gascon is coming for her.
Rochefort brings word at night: four riders, one day behind. In the morning
Constance sees the yellow horse on the road — nobody else in France would ride
a horse that colour — and refuses to run. Milady pours two glasses and twists
her ring over one. The four come through the gate as the carriage goes out of
the back. Constance dies in his arms, saying she knew his horse.

**The turn:** Constance is dead, and the boy helped Milady find her.
**Page-turn out:** the carriage went north, toward the river, and Athos says
get up.

## VI — THE RIVER · pp 46–52

**Emotional entry:** fury. **Exit:** grey dawn over an empty river.

In the storm they run the carriage down at a flooded ferry. Milady is on the
raft; the ferryman has fled. The boy draws to kill her; Athos stops him — "I
did that once, alone, in the dark" — and the four name her crimes on the bank.
She cuts the rope. The ferry swings into the flood toward the weir. Athos
wades in, d'Artagnan holding his belt, Porthos holding the boy, Aramis
holding Porthos, and Athos holds out his hand and offers her a trial in
daylight. She will not owe her life to him. The lantern goes into the dark.

**The turn:** Milady chooses the river over Athos's hand, and the boy chooses
to hold on to his friend.
**Page-turn out:** the four go home, and the Cardinal has lost his second hand.

## VII — THE PAPER · pp 53–58

**Emotional entry:** hollow. **Exit:** four blue cloaks, four abreast.

At the Paris gate Rochefort arrests the boy — the fifth time, and this time
with an order. The Cardinal says a woman in his service is dead. The boy
hands him the paper: what the bearer has done was done by the Cardinal's
order, for the good of France. The Cardinal reads his own words, tears the
paper, and writes another: a lieutenant's commission in the King's
Musketeers, the name left empty. The boy offers it to Athos, Porthos and
Aramis; each refuses; Athos writes the boy's name. In the attic he puts on
the blue cassock for the first time. Four blue cloaks walk away down a Paris
street, and the man in red watches from a window.

**The turn:** the boy is given the cloak he wanted, by the friends who
wouldn't take it.
**Page-turn out:** none; the book ends on the fork.

**No movement runs more than five dramatic pages without a prose or spectacle
page.** The prose and spectacle pages are what buy the dramatic pages their
room; they cannot be cut for budget.

## The two structural rules this architecture is built to satisfy

**Emotional duration.** Grief, mechanism, and revelation never share a page.
Every consequential beat gets its aftermath before the plot moves — including
the protagonist's. Constance's farewell has two pages (7–8); the lily has its
own page (13) and Athos's story its own (16) with an aftermath page (17);
Constance's death (43) is followed by a silent page (44) before the chase
(45); the river (51) by a dawn (52).

**One dominant turn per page.** Every page in `07-PAGE-CONTRACT.md` names the
one thing that changed. A page whose turn cannot be stated in one sentence
comes back here, not to the prompt.
