# Character Ledger — 8 locks, hard ceiling

Identity hierarchy, in order of priority:
**1.** face shape and apparent age · **2.** hair and facial-hair mass ·
**3.** body silhouette and habitual posture · **4.** costume value pattern ·
**5.** colour accent, prop, repeated gesture.

> Costume and colour may reinforce a character; they may never be the only
> cues. Separation is built through skull, jaw, nose, eye set, hairline,
> apparent age, body mass and posture — the traits that survive grayscale and
> thumbnail reduction. **Every page prompt names both the positive lock and the
> nearest forbidden lookalike.**

Seven of the eight return from Volume I; their structural locks below are
Volume I's, carried verbatim except where a state is new, and their Volume I
approved sheets (`refs/parents/`) are the identity anchors. One is new:
Milady.

The cast divides into five silhouettes that must never blur: **the boy** (the
only beardless young man); **three Musketeers in the same blue** (tall and
still, huge and loud, slim and neat); **two dark men in the Cardinal's
service** (one seated in red, one standing in black with a scar); **the girl
in grey**; and **the lady** (tall, pale gold, in deep plum). Danger pairs:
Athos/Rochefort (dark, grave, the same dark hair to the shoulder; only Athos is bearded), Milady/Aramis (fair hair, fine
features, both slim and elegant), Milady/Constance (the only two women, shared
pages at Béthune), and, on pp 57–58 only, d'Artagnan/Aramis when the boy wears
blue.

## 1 · D'ARTAGNAN — eighteen

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/01-dartagnan.png` (Volume I, approved).

**Wants (volume):** the blue cloak — to do "something nobody can forget". From
Milady, at first, to be let in by the front door of a grand house; later, to
get to Constance before she does; at the river, to kill her. **Wants
(secretly):** to be somebody's equal, not somebody's boy.

**Moral position:** believes he is clever enough for Paris now. Is actually
still the boy who wants to be let in, and the one door he walks through
gladly is the trap. At the river he chooses to hold on to his friend rather
than to his rage. Nobody says this.

**Arc:** restless → saves a lady → let in → tells her about the convent →
overhears → sees the lily → learns Athos's secret → the wine → the bet →
holds the bastion → rides north → one hour late → Constance dies in his arms
→ draws on Milady → holds Athos's belt in the flood → arrested → hands the
Cardinal his own paper → offers the commission away → given the cloak.

**Structural lock:** a boy, not a man: long narrow face, sharp chin, a long
straight Gascon nose, dark brows, dark brown eyes set close and quick,
sun-brown skin. **No beard, no moustache — the only clean-shaven male face in
the book.** Black hair, thick, unruly, falling into his eyes, cut roughly to
the jaw. Lean, all elbows and knees, narrow shoulders, a head shorter than
Athos and half the width of Porthos. Never stands still: weight forward on
the balls of his feet, one hand always on the hilt. Habitual gesture: pushing
his hair out of his eyes with the back of his sword hand.

**Costume:** a doublet of Gascon ochre-yellow wool, faded and patched, with
brown breeches and worn brown riding boots; a grey felt hat with a broken
feather; his father's sword, an old long rapier with a plain swept hilt, too
long for him; the Queen's ring (Volume I's, from `obj-casket-pouch-ring`) on his
right hand throughout. No cloak, pp 1–56. **Pp 57–58 only:** the
Musketeer's blue cassock with the white cross over the same ochre doublet,
the same grey hat. Travel and bastion: dust; at Béthune and the river, mud
and rain.

**Never:** a beard or moustache of any kind (every other man). Blue before
p 57 (the three). Black clothes, a scar (Rochefort). Blond hair (Aramis,
Milady). Broad shoulders (Porthos).

**Voice:** says the biggest thing first and the reason after, if at all. Asks
questions he already knows the answer to. Never more than two sentences
before he moves.

## 2 · ATHOS — thirty, looks older

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/02-athos.png`.

**Wants (volume):** from d'Artagnan, at first, nothing; then for the boy not
to make his mistake. From Milady, at the inn, the paper and her absence; at
the river, for her to take his hand. **Wants (secretly):** to undo one hour of
his life, which he cannot, so he does the next thing: he does not judge alone
again.

**Moral position:** believes the count he was died in a wood with the woman
he hanged. Is actually the only man in the book who knows exactly what
Milady is and still offers her his hand.

**Arc:** "good" → asks the boy to describe her → "Which shoulder?" → "Then
she isn't dead" → tells it → "We never sent you wine" → the stovepipe → "Do
you know me?" → takes the paper → the bet → "Get up" at Béthune → stops the
boy's sword → wades into the flood → writes the boy's name.

**Structural lock:** tall, straight as a post, the tallest man in the book by
a hand. Long pale face, high forehead, straight nose, grey eyes, hooded and
level, deep vertical lines beside the mouth. Dark brown hair to the shoulder,
straight, pushed back; a short pointed beard and a neat moustache, dark.
Lean, broad-shouldered, no stomach. Stands with both hands folded on the
pommel of his sword, point on the ground, and does not shift his weight.
Habitual gesture: stillness; the one man in any panel who is not moving.

**Costume:** the Musketeer's blue cassock with the white cross, worn open over
a black doublet; black hat, one white plume; a plain steel-hilted sword. No
sling in this volume. In the river (pp 49–50): hatless, straight hair clumped
wet against his temples and neck; the cassock, collar and black doublet soaked,
visibly darkened and clinging through the chest.
In the memory (p 16): a young lord in black and silver on a grey horse, the
same face younger, beardless — drawn small and from behind.

**Never:** a scar, black clothes without the blue, a thin moustache with no
beard, a red feather (Rochefort). A stomach, red hair, a gold baldric
(Porthos). Fair hair, a book (Aramis). Never seen running.

**Voice:** the fewest words on any page he is on. Says "good" when things are
bad. Never explains — except once, on p 16, and then only as "a friend".

## 3 · PORTHOS — thirty-five

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/03-porthos.png`.

**Wants (volume):** from the camp, to be looked at, which is why the bet is
his to shout; from breakfast, breakfast. **Wants (secretly):** for the four to
stay four.

**Moral position:** believes he is the strongest. Is the one who holds on.

**Arc:** "Always. Why?" → shouts the bet → eats under fire → brings the wall
down → at Béthune, carries nothing and cannot speak → holds the chain in the
flood → refuses the commission ("I'd have to read things").

**Structural lock:** enormous: the widest man in the book by half, a head
taller than d'Artagnan, chest like a barrel, big hands. Round ruddy face,
heavy jaw, small bright blue eyes, a big nose. Red-brown hair in thick curls
to the collar, a great red-brown moustache waxed out to points, no beard.
Stands chest out, fists on hips, feet planted wide. Habitual gesture: a hand
smoothing the front of the baldric.

**Costume:** the blue cassock with the white cross, strained across the
chest, over a doublet of crimson; a wide shoulder-baldric embroidered with
gold in front; a black hat with the largest plume in the book, three ostrich
feathers, red. A heavy sword with a gilt hilt. At the bastion, the breakfast
basket.

**Never:** lean, still, pale, quiet (Athos). A beard. Anything neat (Aramis).
Never seen without the baldric.

**Voice:** the loudest on any page; puts himself in every sentence, and is
generous in the same breath.

## 4 · ARAMIS — twenty-four

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/04-aramis.png`.

**Wants (volume):** to be believed when he says he is leaving for the Church;
at the camp, for nobody to write anything down. **Wants (secretly):** to be
useful as the priest he keeps saying he will be, and at Béthune he is.

**Moral position:** believes he is the clever one. Is the one who knows what
to say over a girl who has died.

**Arc:** a great lady's front door sent him toward the Church → "Every letter out of this camp crosses
the Cardinal's desk" → the handkerchief on the pike → the prayer at Béthune →
names the second charge → holds Porthos in the flood → refuses the
commission ("next year, the Church. Probably.").

**Structural lock:** slim, of middle height, a hand shorter than Athos, half
the width of Porthos. Fine oval face, smooth high forehead, straight small
nose, large dark eyes with long lashes, pale clear skin, a small mouth.
Blond hair, fair and fine, curled to the collar and always in order; a
small neat blond moustache and a tiny tuft under the lip, no beard. Stands
with his weight on one hip, one hand at his moustache. Habitual gesture: two
fingers touching the moustache before he speaks.

**Costume:** the blue cassock with the white cross, immaculate, over a
doublet of dove grey with a white lace collar; black hat with one white
plume; a slim sword with a silver hilt; a small book in a strap at his belt;
a white lace handkerchief (on the pike at the bastion, pp 33–34).

**Never:** a beard, dark hair, height, stillness (Athos). Bulk, red, a
baldric (Porthos). Long hair past the collar, a gown, plum, pale-blue ice
eyes (Milady). Never dishevelled, even in the flood.

**Voice:** soft, polite, and sly; says "as the Fathers tell us" and "in
theory"; says the opposite of what he is about to do.

## 5 · CONSTANCE — twenty

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/05-constance.png`.

**Wants (volume):** from the boy, to be careful while she cannot watch him;
at Béthune, a friend, and then him. **Wants (secretly):** to be told he is
coming, and she is: she sees his horse.

**Moral position:** believes she is safe because nobody knows where she is.
Is actually the one person who suspected Milady from the start, undone by
the one face she never saw.

**Arc:** sent north → "ladies who are kind for no reason" → the farewell →
lonely at the convent → trusts the kind widow → "A Gascon is coming for me" →
sees the yellow horse → refuses to run → drinks → "I knew your horse."

**Structural lock:** small, quick, a head shorter than d'Artagnan. Round face,
soft chin, short straight nose, wide-set hazel eyes, dark brows, a scatter of
freckles. Dark brown hair in curls, pinned up under a white linen cap with
strands always escaping. Slight, straight-backed, hands never idle. Habitual
gesture: looking up — the stair in Paris, the road from the convent window.

**Costume:** pp 7–8: the plain dress of dove grey with a white apron (the
small gold-embroidered crown on its bib) and white cuffs, a ring of keys at
her waist, a travelling cloak of brown wool over it on p 8. At Béthune
(pp 37–43): the same grey dress, no apron, no keys, a white collar, the white
cap; a convent garden's straw basket.

**Never:** finery, jewels, plum, silk, pale-gold hair, a fan (Milady). Never
taller than the boy.

**Voice:** practical, fast, Parisian, and truthful about everything except
herself.

## 6 · THE CARDINAL — forty-five

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/06-cardinal.png`.

**Wants (volume):** from Milady, the boy removed from his way and the Queen's
people found; from the war, La Rochelle; from the boy, at the end, the boy.
**Wants (secretly):** a servant who cannot be bought, and he has just met
one, and it is the wrong one.

**Moral position:** believes he is serving France. Is actually signing papers
that let a woman kill a girl, and telling himself he did not pour the wine.

**Arc:** "Four times" → sets Milady on the boy → at the Red Dovecote, names
Béthune and signs the paper → on the hill: "I would rather have him than bury
him" → sends Rochefort north → reads his own paper → writes the commission
with the name left blank → at the window.

**Structural lock:** a thin ascetic man, seated whenever possible: long
narrow face, hollow cheeks, high forehead, a thin high-bridged nose, pale
grey eyes, hooded, that do not blink; pale skin. Grey hair cropped close
under the cap; a short pointed grey beard and a thin grey moustache, both
precise. Narrow-shouldered, long-fingered. Habitual gesture: one hand
stroking a grey cat on his lap; the hand never stops.

**Costume:** a cardinal's robe and cape of one saturated red, a red skullcap,
a small gold cross on a chain. The only red robe in the book. At the Red
Dovecote (p 22): a plain dark travelling cloak thrown over the red, hood
down. On the hill at La Rochelle (p 35): on a grey horse, the red cape
showing under the cloak.

**Never:** black clothes, a scar, a sword, a hat with a feather (Rochefort).
Dark hair (Athos). Never seen with a raised hand or a raised voice. Seated in
every scene except on horseback (p 35).

**Voice:** quiet, complete sentences; never asks a question he does not
already know the answer to.

## 7 · ROCHEFORT, the man with the scar — forty

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/07-rochefort.png`.

**Wants (volume):** from the Cardinal, to be the hand again; from the boy,
one win. **Wants (secretly):** to stop counting.

**Moral position:** believes he is serving France. Is actually the Cardinal's
messenger in a game he no longer leads, and he knows it.

**Arc:** "Four times" (he says it, not the Cardinal) → Milady's man at her
house → chases the boy out of the window → on the hill: "He'd rather die" →
rides north to warn her → arrests the boy: "Five times, and this time I have
an order" → at the end, nearly smiles.

**Structural lock (relocked as Volume I's approved sheet, 2026-10-17 — this
text describes that sheet; it supersedes an advisor rewrite that the parent
contradicts):** a dark man of middle height, taller than d'Artagnan and a
head shorter than Athos; lean and wiry under a full black cloak. A hard,
high-cheekboned face of middle length, an angular jaw, a moderate chin; heavy
black brows, black eyes, sallow olive skin, and **one pale scar anatomically
fixed to the left side, running from the left temple down across the
cheekbone toward the jaw, near the corner of the mouth**. It is visible only
when the left facial plane is exposed; his right-cheek profile is clean and
must never inherit or mirror the scar. Black hair, wavy, falling past the
collar to the top of the shoulders. One thin black moustache turned up at
the ends; **the chin clean — no beard, no goatee, no lip tuft** (pages ignore
any mark beneath the lower lip on the reference). Sits a horse sideways,
stands with his weight back, one hand on his hip, his chin up. Habitual
gesture: a laugh through the nose.

**Costume:** black from hat to boot: a black doublet, a full black cloak, a
black hat with a single tall red feather; gilt sword guard and pommel, a
brass belt buckle and cloak clasp; the red feather is his only colour. Pages
use the sheet's views (a)–(c) only: never wet, hatless with the arm held to
the chest, or torn (view (d) is a Volume I state). No sling in this volume.
Never the guards' red cassock.

**Never:** a beard, goatee, lip tuft or chin stubble; blue, a white cross, a
pointed beard, stillness with both hands folded on a pommel (Athos). Red
robes (the Cardinal). A crown of several plumes (Porthos). A scar anywhere
but the left side of the face, including any mark on the right cheek in a
right-cheek profile.

**Voice:** clipped and mocking, and always one word too many, which is how
the boy knows he is not calm.

## 8 · MILADY (Lady de Winter) — twenty-eight, looks younger

**Anchor:** new.

**Wants (volume):** from the Cardinal, the paper and her price; from the boy,
at first, what he knows; after the lily, his death; from Constance, nothing
but the boy's pain. From Athos, never to owe him anything. **Wants
(secretly):** for nobody alive to have seen the lily.

**Moral position:** believes she was made what she is by a man who hanged her
without a question, and that she owes the world nothing. Is actually the
only person in the book who chooses every harm she does, and chooses the
river rather than mercy. Nobody says either thing.

**Arc:** in the study ("Give me a week") → the runaway carriage → the front
door → "a convent in the north" → with Rochefort → the knife, the sleeve, the
lily → the carriage in the army's train → the wine → the Red Dovecote: "the
boy, and the girl" → "Do you know me?" → north at dawn → the kind widow at
Béthune → the ring over the glass → the ferry → "You hanged me once."

**Structural lock:** tall for a woman — as tall as d'Artagnan — slender, very
straight, long neck. A heart-shaped face with high fine cheekbones, a small
pointed chin, a short straight nose, a full mouth that smiles before the eyes
do. **Pale ice-blue eyes**, large, under fine dark-gold brows. **Hair pale
ash-gold, almost silver-gold, in long loose ringlets well past the shoulders,**
two ringlets brought forward over the right shoulder; a single dark ribbon.
Very pale skin. Habitual gesture: turning the dark-stoned ring on her left
hand with her thumb.

**Costume:** a gown of deep plum-violet silk with a square neckline and a
falling collar of silver lace; long sleeves slashed with silver; a dark
stone (black-violet) in a gold ring on her left hand, the ring that opens. On
the road and at the river: a hooded travelling cloak of dark slate grey over
the plum. At Béthune: a plain dark grey widow's dress and a black veil pushed
back, the ring still on her hand, the ringlets bound up. **The lily:** a
small dark fleur-de-lis brand on the back of her left shoulder, seen only on
p 13 where the left sleeve has torn at the shoulder seam.

**Never:** blue (the Musketeers), red (the Cardinal), ochre, the dove-grey
dress and white cap (Constance), a beard or moustache of course, black hair,
dark eyes. Never short hair. Never a man's hat.

**Voice:** warm, amused, and exact; asks people about themselves; never raises
her voice; the one cruelty she lets herself is to tell the truth at the end
of a sentence.

## Scenery with a signature (unlocked, no balloons)

- **The Cardinal's guards.** Red cassocks with a white cross, no faces, no
  balloons.
- **The army at La Rochelle.** King's soldiers in buff coats and steel
  helmets, Musketeers in blue in the far crowd (never a pointed beard, never a
  red-brown waxed moustache, never blond curls); faces generic, turned away.
- **The Rochelais.** Townsmen-soldiers of the besieged city in dun and black
  with steel caps and ladders; seen from the bastion, at a distance, never a
  face close; nobody drawn hit.
- **The nuns of Béthune.** In black with white wimples, seen at a distance
  across the cloister; no balloons, no faces close.
- **Milady's coachman, the ferryman, innkeepers.** Bareheaded, aproned or
  cloaked, faces turned away, no balloons.
- **The young count and his wife** (Athos's story, p 16): drawn small, from
  behind and at a distance — a man in black and silver on a grey horse, a
  pale-gold-haired girl beside him; never close.

## Reserved design space

No background or unnamed figure may inherit a complete identity stack:

- beardless youth + black hair in the eyes + ochre doublet → **d'Artagnan**
- tall + dark pointed beard + blue cassock → **Athos**
- huge + red moustache + gold baldric + red plumes → **Porthos**
- slim + blond curls + small moustache + blue cassock → **Aramis**
- grey dress + white cap + small, freckled → **Constance**
- red robe + grey beard + cat + chair → **the Cardinal**
- black clothes + scar + red feather → **Rochefort**
- pale-gold ringlets + ice-blue eyes + plum silk + dark-stoned ring → **Milady**

| Porthos / Rochefort | low | a crown of three red plumes vs one red feather; blue and crimson vs all black; huge vs lean |
