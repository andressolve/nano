# Salt and Stone — Life Four Style Guide

## Style Block (Copy Verbatim to Every Prompt)

```
Art Style: Cinematic painterly realism with restrained brush texture, historical graphic novel aesthetic
Color palette: winter silver-gray, frost blue, amber firelight, spring gold-green, warm earth
Effects: Vast steppe landscapes, natural light, atmospheric depth, wind and frost
```

## Palette Notes

Life Four's palette evolves with the seasons — the first volume in the series to span a full winter-to-spring cycle.

- **Silver-gray** — the dominant winter steppe. Dormant grass, overcast sky, frost
- **Frost blue** — pale cold sky, ice on felt tents, breath in cold air
- **Amber firelight** — yurt interiors, campfires, the warmth of family against the cold
- **Mediterranean gold** — intrudes when Greek traders arrive. Bronze, olive oil, warm stone tones. Foreign, nostalgic, unsettling.
- **Spring gold-green** — the rebirth palette for the final act. Green returning to the grass, warm sunlight, the most hopeful light in the series.
- **Warm earth** — horses, leather, faces, the constant ground

### Palette Transitions Across the Chapter

- **Pages 1-6 (Winter steppe):** Silver, gray, frost blue, amber interiors. Beautiful but austere. The steppe in its harshest season — wind, cold, hard ground. Firelight scenes feel like islands of warmth.
- **Pages 7-9 (Traders arrive):** Mediterranean gold and terra cotta bleed into the winter palette. Warm foreign colors against the cold steppe. Visually jarring — the past invading.
- **Pages 10-14 (Late winter / reckoning):** Muted. Gray sky, muddy earth, bare ground. The steppe stripped bare. Not beautiful — honest.
- **Pages 15-17 (Death and mourning):** Frost and silence. Pale, cold, still. The quietest palette in the series.
- **Pages 18-20 (Spring / resolution):** Gold-green floods in. Warm sunrise, new grass, gentle wind. The visual opposite of the opening pages. Life returning.

## Panel Rules

- **3-4 panels per page maximum**
- Black panel borders, consistent weight
- Clean white gutters
- No decorative borders
- Caption boxes: simple rectangular, muted parchment background, dark text
- Speech bubbles: classic white with black outline
- **FULL-PAGE PANELS:** Three pages use single full-page panels — the winter steppe vista (page 1), Targitaos's burial (page 16), and the final page (page 20).

| Low Angle | Horses, riders against sky, Targitaos's authority |
| High Angle | Small figures on vast steppe, vulnerability |
| POV | Looking south toward unseen sea, starfield, child's eye level |

## What This Should Look Like

- Winter steppe: Alejandro Gonzalez Inarritu's "The Revenant" — cold, real, breath visible, harsh beauty
- Family/interior scenes: Andrei Tarkovsky's warmth — amber light, close faces, intimacy
- Traders' arrival: color disruption, like a Terrence Malick flashback bleeding into the present
- Targitaos's death: Akira Kurosawa's stillness — wide frames, silence, dignity
- Spring finale: continuation of Life Three's Malick feel — wind, grass, light, freedom

## What This Should NOT Look Like

- NOT a cozy winter cabin story (the winter is real and hard)
- NOT children's book illustration at any point
- NOT romanticized "noble savage" imagery
- NOT anime or manga
- NOT sentimental — the emotion should come from restraint, not amplification
- The steppe in winter is beautiful but punishing — never let it look comfortable
