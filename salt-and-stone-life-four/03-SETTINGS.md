# Salt and Stone — Life Four Settings

## THE WINTER STEPPE (Pages 1-6)

```
SETTING: PONTIC STEPPE — WINTER
- The same vast grassland from Life Three, but in deep winter. The grass is silver-gray and dormant. Frost covers everything at dawn. The sky is pale and enormous — whitish-blue, sometimes heavy gray.
- The Scythian camp: felt tents with smoke rising from central openings. Wagons drawn into a circle. Horses huddled together, breath steaming. Dogs curled by fires. Snow dusts the ground in patches — not deep, but persistent.
- Interiors: warm amber firelight, felt walls, furs and wool, cooking smoke, the close warmth of family. The visual opposite of the vast cold outside.
- Palette: silver-gray, frost blue, warm amber (interiors), earth brown, pale sky.
- CRITICAL: Winter steppe is beautiful but harsh. Wind never stops. Cold is real. This is NOT a cozy winter scene — it's survival made habitual.
```

## THE TRADING POST (Pages 7-9)

```
SETTING: EDGE OF CAMP — TRADERS' ARRIVAL
- A small Greek trading caravan: two wagons, pack horses, guards. They carry bronze goods, amphorae of wine and olive oil, bolts of dyed cloth. Mediterranean objects against the winter steppe.
- The visual collision of two worlds: Greek clothing (himation, sandals) alongside Scythian leather and fur. Bronze gleaming against gray sky.
- The traders set up outside the camp — not inside. They're tolerated, not welcomed.
- Palette: Mediterranean gold and terra cotta BLEEDING INTO the winter silver-gray. The warm colors feel foreign, intrusive, nostalgic.
- The contrast should be immediately visible — these colors don't belong here, just as Nikos once didn't.
```

## THE CAMP — INTERIOR LIFE (Pages 10-14)

```
SETTING: SCYTHIAN CAMP — LATE WINTER
- Inside the main tent: low fire, furs on the ground, weapons on the walls, dried meat hanging. Functional, warm, lived-in.
- Nikos's family tent: smaller, with a child's sleeping furs, a small carved wooden horse in progress on a work surface, Asha's bow, Nikos's leather tools.
- The camp edge: where Nikos sits to work with his hands, carving. Looking out at the steppe.
- Palette: amber interior, gray exterior. The late-winter limbo between cold and thaw.
- The ground is muddy where snow has melted. Not pretty. Honest.
```

## THE BURIAL MOUND (Pages 15-17)

```
SETTING: STEPPE — BURIAL SITE
- A low rise on the open steppe where the Scythians build a kurgan (burial mound) for Targitaos. Earth piled over a timber-lined chamber. The camp gathers around it.
- The sky is heavy, gray, pressing down. No wind for once — the steppe holding its breath.
- The mound is modest — not a royal tomb. A chief's burial, done by his people's hands.
- Palette: muted earth, gray sky, frost white. The coldest, most austere palette in the series. Quiet and still.
```

## THE STARFIELD (Pages 18-19)

```
SETTING: OPEN STEPPE — NIGHT
- Nikos and Kian sitting outside the tent under the stars. The steppe at night — no light pollution, the Milky Way blazing overhead. The camp's fires are low embers behind them.
- The sky is the subject. Enormous, ancient, indifferent, beautiful.
- A small fire between father and son. Amber light on their faces. The shell necklace catching starlight.
- Palette: deep night blue, star white, amber firelight, warm skin tones. Intimate warmth against cosmic vastness.
```

## THE SPRING STEPPE (Page 20)

```
SETTING: PONTIC STEPPE — EARLY SPRING
- The steppe transformed. New green pushing through silver-gray dormant grass. The sky is pale blue with warm undertones. Morning light is golden. Everything feels alive after the long winter.
- The camp is moving — tents folded, wagons loaded, the caravan stretching out across the green.
- This is the visual opposite of page 1's winter camp. The same place, reborn.
- Palette: gold-green, warm sunrise, pale blue, earth brown. The warmest, most hopeful palette in the entire four-volume series.
- The horizon is unbroken. The future is open.
```
