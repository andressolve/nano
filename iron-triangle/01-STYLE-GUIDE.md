# 01 — STYLE GUIDE

## Register (REUSE — same gritty military-tech look as "The Gun That Aims Itself")

Not glossy aerospace. This reads like a **frontline field manual crossed with conflict photojournalism** —
gritty, desaturated, documentary. Same family as the turret book so the two sit together on the shelf.

### STYLE BLOCK (paste verbatim into every page prompt, block 1)

> Gritty modern military-technology illustration — the look of a frontline field manual crossed with
> conflict photojournalism. Desaturated palette: gunmetal gray, olive drab, concrete, cold steel-blue,
> dust and haze in the air. A single hot accent per image — muzzle-flash orange, tracer line, or warning
> amber. Harsh directional light, deep shadow, grain and grit, handheld documentary framing. Painted
> photographic realism. NOT glossy, NOT a glamour poster, NOT a video-game render.

### REGISTER / ANTI-DRIFT BLOCK (paste verbatim, block 2)

> Serious adult military-technology book. Real hardware, realistic proportions, realistic materials.
> NOT a comic. NO halftones, NO cel shading, NO ink linework, NO clean vector look. NOT a children's book,
> NOT a cartoon, NOT cute.

## Two page modes

**CINEMATIC** (10 pages) — single composed photographic scene. Frontline field, mud, dust, smoke, hard
light, one hot accent. Caption bands top and/or bottom.

**SCHEMATIC POSTER** (4 pages: P3, P5, P7, P12) — a **dark technical field-manual plate**, NOT warm ivory.
Dark slate / charcoal background, the central object (or diagram) painted in the round, thin **amber or
steel-blue technical leader lines** to callout boxes. Callout boxes: dark, pale bold sans text. This is the
same "military schematic" plate validated on the turret book's POSTER pages.

## Caption / lettering formula (hold across the whole volume)

- **Caption bands & boxes:** "dark charcoal caption band, pale bold sans-serif text, high contrast, readable."
  Full-width top/bottom bands on cinematic hero pages (≈30–70 words each). One formula, held every page.
- **On-image labels** (gun parts, armor layers, crew roles): short, ALL-CAPS, pale text on a dark tag.
- **No speech bubbles needed** in most pages (machines, not dialogue). If a human line appears, round dark
  bubble, pale text, under 12 words, NO inner quotation marks.
- Open the lettering block with `LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:` and close with the restrictions block:
  `All words spelled correctly. Do not duplicate text. Do not invent extra captions. NO modern logos, NO watermarks, NO spurious signage, NO national flags or insignia. Every word in plain English with NO accent marks on any letter.`

## Palette / accent

- Base: gunmetal #2b2f33, olive #4b4f3a, concrete #8a8f8c, steel-blue #3a4a5a.
- Hot accents (one per image): muzzle orange #e8772e, warning amber #d98a3d, tracer white #eef2e8.
- **Reader accent color: warning amber `#d98a3d`** (Sky Duel blue, Scattergun brass, turret phosphor-green —
  this is amber, the muzzle-flash / hazard-stripe signature). Dark flipper reader, same family as the others.

## Safety framing (non-negotiable)

Machines are the subjects. **No human casualties depicted.** Crew shown standing with / operating the tank
for scale, calm and competent. Gun barrels point up-range or across empty ground, never at a person. The
Somme history beat shows the tank crossing trench and barbed wire, never what it shot. **No real national
flags, insignia, or unit markings** — keep the hero tank a generic modern MBT. This framing kept the turret
book and Scattergun at near-zero safety-filter rejections; hold it here.

## Generic-hardware rule
The hero tank is a **composite generic modern main battle tank** — angular welded turret, 120 mm smoothbore
gun with a thermal sleeve, slatted/composite side skirts, modern optics. Do NOT name or exactly replicate a
specific real model (no "Abrams / Leopard / Merkava" string in prompts). Same for the WWI tank: a generic
**rhomboid Mark-I-style** British heavy tank, not a labeled museum piece.
