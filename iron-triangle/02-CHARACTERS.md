## 1. THE TANK — "Bastion" (HERO)

A generic modern main battle tank. The protagonist of the book. Appears on almost every page.

**LOCK:**
> A modern main battle tank, **about 60 tonnes**. Low, angular **welded steel turret** with flat sloped
> faces; a long **120 mm smoothbore main gun** with a pale **thermal sleeve** sheath along the barrel and a
> bore evacuator bulge mid-barrel. Boxy hull on **wide continuous tracks** with road wheels and **composite
> side skirts**. A **commander's sight and a gunner's sight** as armored boxes on the turret roof, plus a
> roof-mounted machine gun and stowage baskets. Matte **olive-drab and gunmetal**, dust-caked, weathered,
> chipped paint, mud on the running gear. Reads heavy, purposeful, modern. **Generic — NOT a specific named
> model, NO flags or insignia.** Realistic military hardware, NOT a cartoon, NOT a video-game render.

Signature marks: the long thermal-sleeved gun, the angular sloped turret, the wide muddy tracks, the boxy sights.

## 2. MARKO — the tank commander (HUMAN ANCHOR)

One recurring human so the reader has a face and a sense of scale. A tank commander.

**LOCK:**
> A man tank commander, **about 35**, **olive-green tanker coveralls** and a padded **tanker's helmet** with
> a boom microphone, dark stubble, weathered hands, a headset cable. Calm, focused, competent. Often shown
> **standing in the commander's hatch** chest-up, or beside the hull for scale. Realistic adult proportions.
> Generic uniform — describe the coveralls/helmet, **never a named army or flag**.

Appears on the human-scale pages: P2, P6 (interior), P10 (crew), P13. The machine is shown alone on the
schematic and pure-hardware pages.

## 3. THE MARK I — the first tank (history, P11 only)

**LOCK:**
> A generic **rhomboid First-World-War heavy tank** in the British Mark I style: a long **lozenge/diamond
> shape** with caterpillar **tracks running all the way around the hull**, riveted steel plates, a small
> side **sponson with a gun**, no top turret, a tail of two trailing steering wheels. Drab gray-brown,
> mud-caked, ~28 tons, slow. 1916 look. Generic, NOT a labeled museum tank.

## 4. THE FPV DRONE — the new threat (P14 only, small/distant)

Described in-prose on P14 (no dedicated ref needed — appears small and in numbers):
> small black **carbon-fiber quadcopter FPV drones**, about the size of a dinner plate, four motors, a stub
> warhead underneath, diving from above. Cheap, field-improvised, menacing. (Same threat as the previous book.)
