# 01 — STYLE GUIDE

## Register
Oil-painting realism, matching the Newton / Gauss / da Vinci / Tesla / Darwin volumes. This is a
19th-century **monastery-and-garden** drama — the quiet world of the cloister, the greenhouse, the
ordered vegetable bed, the candlelit cell, the lecture hall of a provincial scientific society. The
visual signature is **the living pea-green and blossom-violet of growing things breaking into the
monk's grey-stone, black-habit, aged-paper world**, and a recurring **order/counting motif** (rows of
peas, ruled tallies, the grafted tree, the grid) that culminates in the closing pea-square.

## STYLE BLOCK — paste verbatim into every page prompt
> Oil-painting realism, cinematic period illustration. Muted mid-19th-century Central-European palette: monastery stone-grey and cloister shadow, the deep black of an Augustinian friar's habit, aged paper and candle-amber interiors — pierced by the living pea-green of garden foliage and the soft violet of pea blossom as the warm accent. Painterly brushwork, soft natural light, film-still composition, shallow depth of field. The Austrian Empire, Moravia and Silesia, 1820s–1880s — orchard, cloister, greenhouse, study, lecture hall. Visible canvas texture, no photographic sheen.

## REGISTER BLOCK — paste verbatim into every page prompt
> Oil-painting realism. NOT a comic. NO halftones, NO cel shading, NO ink linework. Painted brushwork, cinematic lighting, muted period palette.

## ANTI-DRIFT DIRECTIVE — paste verbatim into every page prompt
> NOT a children's book. Serious mature graphic novel, realistic proportions, natural lighting, cinematic composition.

## Palette
- **Base:** monastery stone-grey, cloister shadow, the matte black of the Augustinian habit, aged-paper cream, candle/oil-lamp amber for interiors, weathered farm browns.
- **Nature / the accent:** the **living pea-green** of leaves, pods, garden foliage and the **soft violet** of the pea blossom (with white blossom as its recessive partner). Wherever *life and growing things* are the subject, this green-and-violet appears against the grey-black-brown world. This is the through-line: *the hidden order of life.*
- **Garden / greenhouse pages:** warm sun through glass, green rows, terracotta, the violet-and-white of the pea flowers.
- **Cloister / study / late pages:** candle-amber, stone-grey, ink and paper, the black habit — patient, quiet, enclosed.

## The order / counting motif (quiet through-line)
Let a **figure of order** sit somewhere in many pages — the grafted tree (two joined into one), ruled rows of peas, a tally of marks in a notebook, pinned pods in a tray, a grid, beads on a counting frame, the violet-and-white pattern of blossoms. Never labeled, never forced. It primes the eye for the **P17 pea-square**, where the motif becomes the structure of the page: heredity as arithmetic.

## Camera language
- Cinematic single-image pages (default). Eye-level intimacy for the cell, the study, the greenhouse; warm over-the-shoulder for the counting beats (hands, pods, notebook); a wide quiet hall for the lectures.
- Soft natural light — window light, candlelight, sun through greenhouse glass, grey cloister light. Gentle and contemplative, like Darwin; this is a patient, interior book, not a melodrama.
- Montage finale (P17): the pea-square grid as the page's structure, with the factors / peas / Mendel's life plotted across it.

## Narration treatment (the Honda formula)
- **Caption boxes:** "off-white box, dark serif text, readable." Hold this style across the whole volume. Period variant allowed: "aged-cream paper with serif ink."
- **Hero pages (T4–T5):** a top caption band sets the moment, a bottom band closes it; 50–80 words each is fine on gpt-image-2.
- **Speech bubbles:** round, off-white, dark serif, tail explicitly described. Under 15 words. **No quotation marks inside bubbles — the bubble shape IS the quote.**
- **Quote / number artifacts:** the **"5474 : 1850"** count and the **3:1 ratio** (P10), the German title **"Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden"** with English gloss (P13), and the closing **"My time will come"** (P15/P17) are rendered as the page's emotional artwork — period serif / period handwriting / chalk.
- **Lettering block** opens with `LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:` and lists each element by position + exact string. Close every prompt with the restrictions block.

## In-image text → always clear to a first-time reader
- All captions stand alone: a reader who has never heard of Mendel must follow on first read. No teaser captions, no withheld punchlines, no ungrounded names (gloss "Abbot Napp," "Doppler," "Nägeli," "Darwin," "the Brünn society" on first use).
- **German always gets an English helper** (per the project-wide rule): the paper's title, any "dominant/recessive" terms, "Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden — Experiments on Plant Hybridization."
- Period/technical vocabulary glossed on first use in body captions: "Augustinian friar," "novice," "graft," "true-breeding," "dominant / recessive," "heredity."

## Restrictions block — close every prompt with this
> All words spelled correctly. Do not duplicate text. Do not invent extra captions. NO modern logos, NO watermarks, NO spurious signage, NO modern clothing or objects in period scenes.

## Period-accuracy guardrails
- 1822–1900, Austrian Empire (Moravia/Silesia → today's Czech Republic) and Vienna. Biedermeier → mid-Victorian Central-European dress: dark frock coats, high collars, cravats, waistcoats; peasant smocks and shawls for the farm; full skirts and bonnets for women.
- **Mendel's habit:** the **black Augustinian (Canons Regular of St. Augustine) friar's habit** — a long black cassock/tunic, NOT a brown Franciscan robe, NOT a Benedictine cowl. As abbot: a black habit with a **pectoral cross** on a chain and sometimes a black biretta or a sash.
- **The pea science:** a monastery kitchen-garden and a lean-to **greenhouse**; ruled rows; pea plants on sticks; pods, a fine brush for hand-pollination, tweezers, paper bags over blossoms, a magnifier, a field notebook full of tallies. NOT a modern lab.
- **NO anachronisms:** no DNA / double-helix imagery, no microscopes-as-hero (he worked with the naked eye and a hand lens, by counting — not by peering at cells), no 20th-century clothing, no modern greenhouse glazing, no Punnett square as a printed modern diagram (render any grid as his own penciled working).

## MENDEL FACIAL/DRESS DISCIPLINE (the wig-on/wig-off rule for this volume — load-bearing)
Mendel ages from boy to old abbot and his ICONIC look is the stout, bespectacled, clean-shaven monk. **State the phase explicitly in EVERY prompt:**
- **Boy Mendel (P1–P2): ~12–16, NO glasses, NO habit** — a peasant farm boy / poor student, plain country clothes.
- **Young Mendel (P3–P6): ~21–25, clean-shaven, just taking the black habit; glasses optional/just appearing.** Slimmer than the iconic phase.
- **Mid / Experimenter Mendel (P7–P13): ~34–43 — the ICONIC look.** Stout/full-figured, round full face, clean-shaven, **gold oval wire-rimmed spectacles**, short dark hair receding, the **black Augustinian habit**. This is the most-used phase.
- **Abbot / Old Mendel (P14–P17, cover): ~46–61 — the iconic face plus abbot's regalia:** heavier, greyer, the same round clean-shaven spectacled face, **black habit with a pectoral cross on a chain**, sometimes a black biretta or watch-chain. Without the explicit "abbot, pectoral cross," the late pages drift back to the plain-friar phase.
- **Clean-shaven in EVERY phase** — Mendel had no beard. State it; the model loves to add 19th-century facial hair.

## Anti-drift watchlist
- Mid Mendel must read **stout, round-faced, clean-shaven, gold oval spectacles, black habit** — the friendly, heavy, bespectacled monk, NOT a gaunt ascetic and NOT a bearded sage.
- Keep the green-and-violet accent disciplined: it marks where *life / the peas* are the subject. Don't flood every page green.
- The habit is **black Augustinian**, never brown Franciscan, never a Benedictine hood.
- The book is contemplative and patient, not melodramatic. Soft light, quiet framing. No heroic posturing — Mendel's heroism is patience.
- Old Mendel (P14–P17) must look genuinely middle-aged-to-older, heavier and greyer, with the **pectoral cross** marking the abbot.
- NO modern science imagery (DNA, chromosomes-as-we-draw-them, printed Punnett squares). All "diagrams" are his own period penciled working.
