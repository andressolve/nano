## Life dates & places
- Born **Johann Mendel, 20 July 1822**, in **Heinzendorf bei Odrau** (now **Hynčice**, Czech Republic), then Austrian Silesia.
- Peasant farming family. Father **Anton**, mother **Rosine** (née Schwirtlich). Father was **crushed in the chest by a rolled log during feudal corvée labor (~1838)** and could no longer work the farm — deepened the family's poverty.
- **Sister Theresia gave up part of her inheritance/dowry** so Johann could continue his studies. (He later helped fund her sons.)
- **Philosophical Institute, Olomouc (Olmütz), ~1840–1843.** Severe poverty; supported himself by private tutoring; recurring stress/overwork illness (first episode at ~16 in Troppau).
- Entered the **Augustinian Abbey of St. Thomas, Brno (Brünn), 1843** (9 Oct 1843); took the religious name **Gregor**. **Ordained priest 1847** (use 1847).
- Patron: **Abbot Cyrill Franz Napp** — an enlightened abbot who ran St. Thomas as a center of science and teaching, and sponsored Mendel's studies.
- **Poor at pastoral work** — "seized by an unconquerable timidity" when visiting the sick (Napp's words). Redirected to teaching and research.
- **University of Vienna, 1851–1853.** Physics under **Christian Doppler**; botany under **Franz Unger**. Picked up the mathematical / combinatorial / statistical habit of mind that defined his method.
- **Failed the teacher-certification exam TWICE — 1850 and 1856.** In 1850 he did well in physics but weak in **natural history** (the irony). In 1856 he **fell ill during the exam** and withdrew. Never got the certificate.

## The pea experiments
- **Monastery garden, Brno, ~1856–1863.** ~7 years of crossing + counting (some sources 1856–1863/1865).
- **About 28,000 pea plants** (standard figure — not 29,000, not 10,000).
- **Two years up front just to establish pure-breeding ("true-breeding") lines** before the real crosses — the patience beat.
- **Seven either/or traits** (dominant form / recessive form):
  1. Seed shape — **round / wrinkled**
  2. Seed (cotyledon) color — **yellow / green**
  3. Flower (seed-coat) color — **purple (gray-violet) / white**
  4. Pod shape — **inflated / constricted**
  5. Unripe pod color — **green / yellow**
  6. Flower position — **axial (along stem) / terminal (at tip)**
  7. Stem length — **tall / short (dwarf)**
- **Dominant & recessive** — Mendel's own coined terms.
- The recessive trait **vanishes in the first cross (all offspring show the dominant), then reappears in ~1 of 4 of the next generation.**
- **~3:1 ratio.** His signature count: **5474 round : 1850 wrinkled = 2.96:1.**
- He proposed paired hereditary **"factors"** (German **"Elemente" / "Merkmale"**) — one inherited from each parent — that **separate** and **recombine** independently. Now called the **Law of Segregation** and the **Law of Independent Assortment** (names applied later).
- He did **NOT** use the word **"gene"** — coined by **Wilhelm Johannsen in 1909.** Do not put "gene" in Mendel's mouth.

## Publication & reception
- Read **"Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden"** ("Experiments on Plant Hybridization") to the **Natural History Society of Brünn** on **8 February and 8 March 1865**. **Published 1866** in the society's proceedings (*Verhandlungen des naturforschenden Vereines in Brünn*).
- **Largely ignored / not understood.** Polite reception, no real follow-up.
- Wrote to **Carl von Nägeli** (famous Munich botanist), who was unconvinced and steered Mendel toward **Hieracium (hawkweed)** — which, because of **apomixis** (asexual seed), did **NOT** reproduce the pea ratios and produced confusing results. Years lost.
- **Darwin almost certainly never read Mendel.** ⚠️ AVOID the "uncut Mendel offprint in Darwin's library" myth — unfounded. The real, poignant, verified fact: **Mendel READ Darwin** (he owned an annotated German copy of *Origin*, translated 1863) — but Darwin never knew Mendel existed. The two halves of the theory of inheritance lived at the same time and never connected.

## Late life & death
- **Elected abbot 1868.** Later years consumed by abbey administration and a long government dispute over **monastery taxation**; he largely abandoned the science.
- Other interests: **beekeeping, meteorology** (daily weather records — effectively the local meteorologist), **sunspots**, observed a **tornado in 1870**.
- **Died 6 January 1884, Brno**, of **chronic nephritis (Bright's disease) + heart disease.**
- His **papers were burned after his death** (traditionally attributed to his successor / tied to ending the tax dispute). Phrase as reported/traditional, not forensic certainty.
- **"My time will come"** (German *"Meine Zeit wird schon kommen"*) — **traditionally attributed** (reportedly to colleague Gustav von Niessl), **not firmly documented.** Render as "he is said to have told a friend…", never as a hard quote.

## Vindication
- **1900: independently rediscovered** by **Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns, and Erich von Tschermak** — sixteen years after Mendel's death. Each found Mendel had reached the laws first, ~35 years early. Genetics is built on his pea arithmetic.

## Myths to AVOID stating as fact
- "Simple, unlettered, lucky monk" — FALSE. He had Vienna physics/math training and designed a rigorous quantitative program.
- The "Darwin had Mendel's uncut paper" tale — FALSE (it was Focke's 1881 book, with uncut pages). Use "no evidence Darwin read Mendel."
- **Fisher's 1936 "too good to be true" statistical critique** — DELIBERATELY OMITTED from the narrative (not a milestone; a nuanced statistical controversy that would muddy a kids' triumphant-but-honest arc; modern scholarship rejects deliberate fraud, attributing the close fit to unconscious classification bias / stopping rules).
