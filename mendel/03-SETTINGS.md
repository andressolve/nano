# 03 — SETTINGS

Recurring locations. Keep these consistent across pages so the world feels continuous.

## The Silesian farm — Heinzendorf (P1–P2)
- Era: 1830s, rural Austrian Silesia (today's Czech-Polish border country).
- A modest peasant farmstead: a low whitewashed cottage, a fruit **orchard** with grafted trees, a
  vegetable plot, a wooden fence, rolling green fields and dark woods beyond.
- Light: open daylight, the green-gold of an orchard in leaf.
- Props: grafting knife and waxed cloth, young fruit trees, baskets, a beehive, simple farm tools.
- Mood: hard-working, poor, close to growing things. The boy's first laboratory is the orchard.

## The Philosophical Institute — Olomouc (P2)
- Era: ~1840. A provincial Austrian college town.
- A bare student's garret or a plain lecture room: cold, ink, candle-stubs, a few borrowed books,
  thin bedding. Outside, grey baroque streets.
- Light: thin candlelight, cold window light.
- Mood: hunger, exhaustion, determination. Poverty made visible.

## St. Thomas Abbey — Brno (Brünn) (P3–P4, P11–P15)
- Era: 1843 onward. A large Augustinian abbey in Moravia — grey stone, vaulted cloisters, a fine
  **library**, a refectory, a chapel, private cells, a courtyard.
- The abbey is a **house of science and teaching**, not gloom: globes, herbarium presses, books,
  instruments, framed botanical plates. Abbot Napp's enlightened establishment.
- Light: cool cloister light, warm candle/oil-lamp in the cells and library.
- Props: black habits, crucifixes, books, ink, a pectoral cross (the abbot), herbarium folios.
- Mood: ordered, calm, scholarly, a refuge.

## The monastery garden & greenhouse — Brno (P7–P10) — the heart of the book
- Era: 1856–1863. (See `ref_garden.png`.)
- A walled kitchen-garden: ordered rectangular beds, **ruled rows of staked pea plants** climbing
  sticks and string, gravel paths, a lean-to **glass greenhouse** against the old stone wall,
  terracotta pots, paper bags tied over chosen blossoms.
- Light: warm sun through greenhouse glass; green, orderly, patient.
- Props: a fine camel-hair **brush** for hand-pollination, tweezers, **paper pollination bags**, a
  **magnifier/hand-lens**, a **field notebook full of tallies**, pinned/sorted pea pods, labelled
  rows. NO modern lab gear.
- Mood: the patient laboratory. This is where the counting happens. Green and violet against grey
  stone — the visual heart.

## The University of Vienna (P5)
- Era: 1851–53. A grand imperial lecture hall / physics cabinet.
- Tiered wooden benches, a demonstration bench, brass physics instruments, a blackboard with
  equations, tall windows.
- Light: cool clean north light, a more rigorous and modern register than the cloister.
- Mood: rigor, numbers, the wider world of science. Where Mendel learns to think mathematically.

## The Brünn society lecture hall (P11–P12)
- Era: February–March 1865. A modest provincial scientific society's meeting room.
- A plain hall, rows of chairs, a lectern, a small audience of perhaps thirty–forty dark-coated
  gentlemen-scientists, oil lamps, a few specimen plates and a chalkboard.
- Light: warm lamplight, a little drab — an ordinary evening meeting.
- Mood: the most important biological lecture of the century, delivered to polite, uncomprehending
  silence. Quiet anticlimax.

## Mendel's study / cell — late (P14–P15)
- Era: 1860s–1884. The abbot's study: a desk piled with abbey accounts and tax papers, a window over
  the garden, the black habit, a candle, a few beehives visible outside, weather instruments, a
  shelf with his bound pea paper unread.
- Light: candle-amber, a tired evening warmth; greyer and heavier as he ages.
- Mood: the great work behind him; administration, bees, weather, sunspots; patient waiting. "My
  time will come."

## Closing / the rediscovery (P16–P17)
- P16: a turn-of-the-century botanist's study (1900) — three different rooms implied, gas-lamp
  modernity creeping in, journals, a copy of Mendel's 1866 paper rediscovered on a desk.
- P17: NOT a literal place — the **pea-square grid** as the structure of the page, Mendel's life and
  the peas plotted across it; the green-and-violet of the peas joined to the branching tree of life.
- Mood: vindication, light breaking in, his time arriving.
