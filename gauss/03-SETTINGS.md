# 03 — SETTINGS

## The Gauss home, Brunswick (~1777–1788)
A cramped poor laborer's house on a narrow Brunswick lane. One low room: rough plaster
walls, a single small leaded window, a hearth with a black cooking pot, a plain scrubbed
table, a tallow candle. Bundles of tools (the father's trades). Warm candlelight inside,
cold blue street outside. Modest, clean, hard-pressed. Half-timbered houses crowd the lane.

## Büttner's schoolroom, St Katharine's, Brunswick (~1784)
A crowded poor village-town schoolroom: low beamed ceiling, rows of rough benches packed
with ragged boys, individual writing slates, a high master's desk and lectern, a cane, a
small window of cold light, a slate or chalkboard at front. Chalk dust in shafts of light.
The atmosphere of fear and drudgery — into which one boy's answer detonates.

## Collegium Carolinum, Brunswick (1792–1795)
A respectable provincial academy: paneled lecture rooms, tall windows, shelves of books,
globes and instruments, warmer and richer than the schoolroom. The first place Gauss is
treated as a scholar, not a poor boy.

## The Duke's audience chamber, Brunswick (1792)
An enlightened prince's reception room: tall windows, parquet floor, gilt-framed paintings,
a writing table with papers, warm light on rich but restrained furnishings. Where a poor
boy is given a future.

## University of Göttingen (1795–1798)
- **The aula / lecture hall:** a fine university hall, tall windows, dark wood, rows of
  benches, a lectern.
- **Heyne's study (WORDS):** lined floor-to-ceiling with classical books, scrolls, a marble
  bust of a Greek or Roman, warm lamplight, a globe — the seductive life of letters.
- **Kästner's dull math room:** dustier, sparer, a tired old professor, a chalkboard of rote
  problems — the unglamorous life of numbers, where the real treasure secretly waits.
- **Gauss's student lodging:** a small bare rented room, a narrow bed, a table by a window,
  a candle, papers and a slate. Cold gray scholar's light. Where the 17-gon happens at dawn.

## The 17-gon morning (30 March 1796)
Gauss's student room at first light. Cold blue pre-dawn giving way to a single warm shaft
of dawn through the window — the visual turn of the whole book. Papers, compass and
straightedge, the new construction taking shape, the diary open to its first entry.

## The night sky / observatory (1801, Ceres)
A winter night sky thick with stars; a telescope on a stand; star charts and dense columns
of computation spread on a table by lamplight. The math on paper and the sky above in one
frame — Gauss finding a lost world with numbers.

## Recurring palette logic
Poverty = warm candle + cold outside. Patronage = richer warm interiors. Göttingen
indecision = cold gray light. The 17-gon = a single dawn shaft (the turn). Fame = starlight.
