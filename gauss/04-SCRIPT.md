## QUIZ (5 questions, tests WHY not WHAT; correct answer NOT the longest; shuffled positions)

shuffled: b, c, a, c, b.

Q1. Why was Carl able to add 1 to 100 so fast, when the other boys added one number at a time?
  a) He had secretly memorized the answer from an older book the assistant gave him.
  b) He noticed the numbers form pairs that each add to the same total, 101.  ★CORRECT
  c) He was simply faster at adding long columns than any other boy in the school.
  FEEDBACK (correct): Right — he paired 1+100, 2+99, and so on into fifty pairs of 101, then multiplied. He found a shortcut instead of grinding through the sum.

Q2. Why did the Duke of Brunswick's offer change everything for Carl?
  a) The Duke taught Carl advanced mathematics himself in the palace each week.
  b) The Duke made Carl a nobleman so he no longer had to obey his strict father.
  c) Carl's family was poor and could never have paid for school; the Duke paid instead.  ★CORRECT
  FEEDBACK (correct): Right — a laborer's son could never have afforded college or university. The Duke's money set Carl free to think for sixteen years.

Q3. Why did building a seventeen-sided shape matter so much?
  a) No one had added a new shape buildable with compass and straightedge for two thousand years.  ★CORRECT
  b) It was the very first shape anyone had ever drawn using a compass and a straightedge.
  c) It was the largest shape that anyone had ever managed to fit inside a circle.
  FEEDBACK (correct): Right — the Greeks had stopped two thousand years earlier. Carl found the next buildable shape, and proved which ones are possible at all.

Q4. Why did the 17-gon settle Carl's choice between words and numbers?
  a) Professor Heyne told him philology was full and he should study mathematics.
  b) The Duke's money would only continue if Carl agreed to study mathematics.
  c) It showed him he was not following the great mathematicians — he was ahead of them.  ★CORRECT
  FEEDBACK (correct): Right — until that morning he might have become a scholar of languages. The proof showed him where his real gift was, and he chose numbers.

Q5. Why was finding Ceres again such an astonishing feat?
  a) Carl built a far more powerful telescope than any astronomer in Europe owned.
  b) From a tiny scrap of its path, his arithmetic alone predicted where the lost world would reappear.  ★CORRECT
  c) He was the first person to ever calculate the orbit of a planet around the sun.
  FEEDBACK (correct): Right — every telescope had lost Ceres in the sun's glare. With only Piazzi's few measurements and patient calculation, Carl said exactly where to look.
