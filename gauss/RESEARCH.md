# RESEARCH ARCHIVE — Young Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777–1801)

Verified against three independent sources: Wikipedia, MacTutor (St Andrews), and
Encyclopedia.com = *Dictionary of Scientific Biography* (K. O. May). This archive is
GROUND TRUTH for the script. Cross-check every date, place, and quote against it.

## Two locked dates (models corrupt these most)
- **Birth: 30 April 1777**, Brunswick (Braunschweig), Duchy of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel.
- **17-gon: 30 March 1796**, age **18** (turned 19 one month later on 30 April). NOT 19 on the day.

## Family
- Father **Gebhard Dietrich Gauss** (1744–1808): poor laborer — gardener, bricklayer, butcher, waterworks foreman, treasurer of a funeral fund. Took whatever work he could.
- Mother **Dorothea Benze** (also Bentze): daughter of a peasant stonemason, worked as a maid, **nearly illiterate** but intelligent, devoted to Carl.
- Uncle **Friedrich Benze**: master weaver, recognized and encouraged the boy's gift (keep modest — popular accounts romanticize him).
- Genuinely poor working class. One elder half-brother.

## Prodigy anecdotes (present as beloved tradition, hedge the drama)
- **Age 3, payroll correction**: "well-authenticated" (DSB) but rests on Gauss's own recollection. Anecdote, not documented fact.
- **Schoolboy summation**: sum 1+2+…+100 = 5050 by pairing into 50 pairs of 101. Teacher **J. G. Büttner**. Assistant **Johann Martin Bartels** (1769–1836) got him advanced books. Age **7 or 8** (sources disagree — use "about seven"). Wikipedia flags the dramatized version as apocryphal; the pairing trick predates Gauss. Tell it as a treasured story.

## Patronage
- **Carl Wilhelm Ferdinand, Duke of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel** (Charles William Ferdinand).
- Stipend began **1792**; introduced to court via Collegium professor **E. A. W. Zimmermann**.
- Funded Gauss for ~16 years: Collegium, Göttingen, doctorate, early research. Ended when the Duke was mortally wounded at Jena-Auerstedt (1806) — outside our window.

## Education
- Catharineum gymnasium from 1788.
- **Collegium Carolinum (Braunschweig): 1792–1795** — rediscovered known results independently.
- **University of Göttingen: 1795–1798**.
- **Abraham Gotthelf Kästner** — uninspiring math professor. Gauss's jab: "the leading mathematician among poets, and the leading poet among mathematicians."
- **Christian Gottlob Heyne** — celebrated classicist/philologist; strongly attracted Gauss.
- **Philology-vs-mathematics tension is REAL and documented** (DSB): Gauss "planned to be a philologist," had exceptional command of ancient languages, was undecided — until the 17-gon. This is the narrative spine and it is factually anchored.

## The 17-gon (regular heptadecagon)
- **30 March 1796**, age **18**. First entry in his mathematical diary.
- Proved the regular 17-sided polygon is **constructible with compass and straightedge** — the first new constructible regular polygon since antiquity (Greeks had 3, 4, 5, 15 and power-of-2 multiples). Later generalized: regular n-gon constructible iff n = power of 2 × distinct Fermat primes; 17 is a Fermat prime.
- This resolved the philology-vs-math decision; he chose mathematics.
- **Tombstone story (HEDGE)**: he *wished* a heptadecagon on his tombstone; it was **never** placed on his actual grave (Göttingen). A **17-pointed star** is on a **monument in Brunswick**. The "stonemason said it'd look like a circle" line is traditional, medium confidence. Do NOT say his grave has a 17-gon.

## Mathematical diary (Tagebuch)
- Started **30 March 1796** (17-gon was entry 1), kept to 1814. Terse Latin, 146 results. Rediscovered 1897, published by Felix Klein 1903.

## Doctorate
- **1799, University of Helmstedt** (NOT Göttingen), in absentia. Advisor **Johann Friedrich Pfaff**. Topic: first proof of the **Fundamental Theorem of Algebra**.

## Disquisitiones Arithmeticae
- Published **1801** (written ~1798), age **24**. Foundational number theory: modular/congruence arithmetic (≡ notation), quadratic reciprocity, binary quadratic forms, §VII cyclotomy/polygon construction. Won him recognition as the "prince of mathematicians."

## Ceres
- Discovered **1 January 1801 by Giuseppe Piazzi** (Palermo), tracked ~40 days, lost in the sun's glare.
- Gauss developed a new **orbit-determination method** from the short arc (method of least squares) and predicted where it would reappear.
- Refound **31 Dec 1801 by Franz Xaver von Zach**, confirmed **1 Jan 1802 by Heinrich Wilhelm Olbers**, at nearly Gauss's predicted spot.
- Made him **internationally famous** beyond mathematics. Age **24**. (Legendre/least-squares priority dispute is a LATER event — not part of the 1801 story.)

## Quotes (verbatim, sourced)
- **"Mathematics is the queen of the sciences and number theory is the queen of mathematics."** — Sartorius von Waltershausen, *Gauss zum Gedächtniss* (1856). Second-hand, LATE life. Do NOT put in teenage Gauss's mouth. Usable only as a closing authorial epigraph, attributed carefully.
- **Seal motto "Pauca sed matura"** — "Few, but ripe." Lifelong publishing philosophy. Fine as a closing motif.
- **SAFEST real quote near the window** — letter to Farkas Bolyai, **2 Sept 1808**: *"It is not knowledge, but the act of learning… which grants the greatest enjoyment."* Genuine, dated, from his own pen, only a few years past our window. Bolyai was his Göttingen friend.
- No verbatim quote survives from teenage Gauss. Any "young Gauss said…" dialogue is invented and must read as natural, not as a famous line.

## Appearance (INFERRED — no portrait of young Gauss exists)
- Adult traits for back-projection: blue eyes, fair/light-brown hair, broad forehead, sturdy/stocky build, average-to-short height, robust not delicate.
- ~7 (poor schoolboy, ~1784): coarse linen shirt, simple breeches, woolen stockings, rough shoes, no wig, unkempt fair hair, thin but healthy — a laborer's son.
- ~15 (Collegium, newly patronized ~1792): modest respectable student dress — dark woolen frock coat, waistcoat, breeches, plain linen stock, buckled shoes, hair tied back (queue), no powder. Serious.
- ~19 (Göttingen, 1796): engaged student — dark coat, high collar, simple cravat, natural short/tied hair (1790s moving away from wigs). Intense, inward.
- ~24 (1801): young gentleman-scholar — better-cut dark coat, white stock, waistcoat, clean-shaven, short side-swept Empire-era hair. Composed, confident.
- The visible upgrade in dress from ~7 to ~15 is a storytelling device for the patronage turn.

1. Age-3 payroll story — anecdote from his own recollection.
2. Summation drama + exact age (7 vs 8) — apocryphal in dramatized form; use "about seven."
3. Age at 17-gon — he was **18**, not 19.
4. Tombstone — wished for, never placed on grave; 17-pointed star is on the Brunswick *monument*.
5. "Queen of the sciences" — second-hand, late life; not teenage Gauss.
6. "Woke and conceived the 17-gon on rising" — atmosphere, lightly sourced.
7. Uncle's depth of influence — keep modest.
8. All young-Gauss appearance — inferred from adult portraits + period costume.
