# 00 — PROJECT BRIEF

## Title
**The Boy Who Chose Numbers**
Subtitle: *Carl Friedrich Gauss, Book One*

(Fits the nano "The Boy Who ___" biography pattern: …Chased Engines / …Watched Birds / …Mapped the Wind.)

## One-sentence window
This bio covers Carl Friedrich Gauss from his birth into poverty in Brunswick (1777) to the year he became internationally famous (1801) — a boy who could not stop counting, pulled between a life of words and a life of numbers, who let a single geometric proof decide his future.

## Spine (decided with user)
**The boy who chose numbers over words.** The hinge of the whole book is 30 March 1796: an 18-year-old torn between philology (the brilliant classicist Heyne) and mathematics (the dull professor Kästner) constructs the regular 17-gon — the first new constructible polygon in two thousand years — and lets that one morning decide his life. The summation trick (age ~7) and the 17-gon construction are the two re-enactable hooks: both can be done by the reader on paper.

## Curricular hook (Lyceum, Collection II)
The kids are working through **Euclidean geometry — compass-and-straightedge constructions**. The 17-gon is exactly that, taken to its frontier: which regular polygons CAN be built with only compass and straightedge? The Greeks stopped at the 15-gon; a teenager 2000 years later added the 17-gon. The summation also gives pure paper agency (pair the numbers, get 5050). This is the Descartes formula: a concept they are learning RIGHT NOW, with something they can DO on paper, inside one human arc.

## Audience
Francisco (10) and Sebastian (8). Per the standing framing rule: NOT pitched down by age. Write so any first-time reader who has never heard of Gauss can follow on first read — no withheld facts, period terms glossed on first use, names grounded with a one-line role. "Clear, not dumbed down."

Biographical mode. 3:2 landscape (1536×1024). Oil-painting realism, matching Newton / Honda / da Vinci / Name of the Rose register. Narration baked into the image as off-white serif caption boxes (the Honda formula). Dark-theme page-flipping reader, 5-question WHY-quiz.

## Page count
Cover + 17 pages (target). Expand or stop as the arc lands. The arc closes on 1801 fame; the Göttingen man, Johanna, grief, and later science are explicitly a plantable Book Two — NOT promised here.

## Deliberate editorial choices (traceable)
- **Hedged anecdotes flagged in-script.** The age-3 wage correction and the summation are told as treasured stories; captions use "they say" / "the story goes" framing rather than asserting them as documented fact. The 17-gon, patronage, Disquisitiones, and Ceres are hard fact.
- **Age at 17-gon stated as 18.** Many sources say 19; we use 18 (turned 19 a month later) per the locked diary date.
- **Tombstone NOT claimed.** The closing motif uses the 17-gon as a *wish* and as the structure of the final page, not as a real engraving on his grave.
- **No teenage "queen of the sciences."** That line, if used at all, appears only as a closing authorial epigraph, correctly attributed to his later life.
- **Emotional ordering:** strict chronology is preserved at the life-event level. The philology/mathematics tension (P7–P8) is foregrounded before the 17-gon (P9) so the proof lands as a decision, not a footnote.
