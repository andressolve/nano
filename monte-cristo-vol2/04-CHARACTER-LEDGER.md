# Character Ledger — 8 locks, hard ceiling

Identity hierarchy, inherited from Volume I, in order of priority:
**1.** face shape and apparent age · **2.** hair and facial-hair mass ·
**3.** body silhouette and habitual posture · **4.** costume value pattern ·
**5.** color accent, prop, repeated gesture.

> Costume and color may reinforce a character; they may never be the only cues.
> **Every page prompt names both the positive lock and the nearest forbidden
> lookalike.**

---

## 1 · THE COUNT OF MONTE CRISTO — Edmond Dantès, 42

**Anchor:** `monte-cristo-expanded/refs/02-count-v2.png`. Aged forward nine
years from Volume I's 1829 Count.

**Wants (volume):** to take Fernand Mondego apart and to enjoy every hour of
it. **Wants (secretly):** for Mercédès to see him do it and understand why.
**Moral position:** he believes he is an instrument of justice. He is not; he
is a man with an appetite, and the volume is honest about the difference
without ever letting a character *say* so.

**Arc:** appetite → the first flinch (Mercédès knows) → hardening → the price
→ he pays it and continues. He does not learn his lesson in this volume. That
is the point.

**Structural lock:** deep-set black-brown eyes; strong straight brow; long
clean nose; high cheekbones; **slight asymmetry at the left corner of the
mouth**; hollow temples; cultivated pallor; **clean-shaven**; swept-back black
wave with the first grey at the temples; tall columnar stillness; the poised
right hand before a decision. 1838 addition: slightly fuller than the 1829
Count — the starvation now reads as discipline rather than damage.

**Costume:** black, always. Unrelieved. He is the only unbroken black vertical
in every room he enters.

**Never:** Fernand's heavy moustache and thickened soldier's build; Villefort's
narrow pale inverted triangle; **and never Albert's chestnut hair, light
waistcoat, or open mobile face.**

## 2 · MERCÉDÈS, COMTESSE DE MORCERF — 42

**Anchor:** `monte-cristo-expanded/refs/07-mercedes-1838-v2.png`. Already the
correct year; needs no ageing, only the Paris wardrobe.

**Wants (volume):** at first, to be wrong about who he is. Then, to get her son
out of the building before it falls. **Moral position:** she has known what her
husband was since 1815 and married him anyway, and she has never forgiven
herself for it. She does not ask the Count for mercy on Fernand. She never
once defends her husband.

**Arc:** recognition → twenty pages of silence → she says the name → she takes
nothing and leaves.

**Structural lock:** the same decisive eyes and straight nose as 1815; visibly
early forties; lean mature cheeks; **lower-lid and temple lines**; restrained
grey threads at the temple; dark hair sculpted into formal 1838 dress;
burgundy-black vertical gown; still, upright carriage.

**Never youth-wash her.** Every prompt states early forties, visible temple and
lower-lid lines, and restrained grey. A beautiful but smoothed face is a
blocking defect, inherited verbatim from Volume I's ledger.

**Never:** Haydée's unbound black hair, gold embroidery, or late-twenties
face.

## 3 · FERNAND MONDEGO, COMTE DE MORCERF — 46

**Anchor:** `monte-cristo-expanded/refs/05-fernand-1815-v2.png`, **aged
twenty-three years.** This is the volume's largest ageing job.

**Wants (volume):** to be believed. Everything he does — the staircase, the
decorations, the invitation to a famous stranger, the demand to be heard by the
Chamber — is a man buying certification he knows he has not earned.
**Moral position:** he sold a city and a family for money, and has spent
twenty-three years constructing a person who could not have done that.

**Arc:** vanity → suspicion → a genuinely strong public defense → annihilation
→ an empty house.

**He must be able to almost win.** A villain who cannot nearly get away with it
is not worth destroying. His Chamber defense is real and the reader should feel
it working.

**Structural lock:** broad square jaw; heavy black brows set low and close;
deep-set close dark eyes; weathered ruddy-olive Catalan skin, coarser than the
Count's; **heavy iron-and-black military moustache** (grown after 1815 — the
primary anti-collision marker); black hair receding at the temples and iron-grey
at the sides; thick neck; heavy upright soldier's build; chest of decorations.

**Never:** the Count's clean-shaven face, full swept-back wave, pallor, or
columnar slimness. The moustache and the receding hairline are load-bearing and
appear in every prompt.

## 4 · ALBERT DE MORCERF — 22 · NEW

**Wants (volume):** to be liked by the interesting stranger; then, to defend
his father's name; then, to be able to live with himself. **Moral position:**
the only person in the volume with nothing to hide — which is precisely why
destroying his father is unbearable.

**Arc:** warmth → humiliation → fury → the truth → he gives up the name.

**He is not a prop.** His final choice is his own and it costs him everything
he was born with.

**Structural lock:** his mother's wide-set direct eyes and mouth; his father's
jaw, softened and un-weathered; **chestnut-brown hair — never raven black** —
worn short with a neat side part in the 1838 fashion; fair-olive skin, several
values lighter than the Count's; slim, upright, unmarked by any work; **open,
mobile, quick-to-smile face**; clean-shaven, no side whiskers.

**Costume:** the volume's brightest values — pale waistcoats, coloured
neckcloths. He is the light figure where the Count is the black one.

**Never — highest collision risk in the volume:** Albert must never inherit
young Edmond's identity stack from Volume I (loose raven curls, olive skin,
open white shirt, red sash, sailor posture), and must never read as a young
version of the Count. Hair *colour*, skin *value*, costume *value*, and default
expression all separate them, and all four are stated in every prompt where
both appear.

## 5 · HAYDÉE — 27 · NEW

**Wants (volume):** Fernand Mondego destroyed, publicly, by name, with her
mouth. Not the Count's mouth. Hers. **Moral position:** she has no French
manners, no interest in whether revenge is philosophically defensible, and no
patience. She is the only character who says exactly what she means, which is
what makes her the one voice you can identify with the names stripped off.

**Arc:** she overrules him in Movement I, arms him in Movement III, fires the
gun herself in Movement IV, and is the only person still exultant in Movement V
— which is how the reader learns what triumph looks like from outside.

**She is the confidant.** The Count has no valet, no steward, no majordomo. He
has her. Every private thing he says in this volume, he says to her.

**Structural lock:** twenty-seven — eleven at Janina in 1822, and the
volume's dates are checkable; long unbound black hair or one heavy
braid; large wide-set very dark eyes; straight brows; olive-gold skin; small
straight nose; full mouth; slight build; direct, unornamented stillness.

**Costume:** Epirote — deep crimson and gold embroidery, loose vertical
silhouette, never a French 1838 waist. In the Chamber (P32–33) she is the only
person in the room not dressed as a Parisian, and that is the image.

**Never:** Mercédès' sculpted French coiffure, burgundy-black fitted gown, or
forty-year-old face. Fourteen years separate them and the faces must show it. Age, hairline, and silhouette carry the distinction at
thumbnail size.

## 6 · BARON DANGLARS — 55

**Anchor:** `monte-cristo/refs/11-danglars-1815.png`, aged twenty-three years.

**Wants (volume):** to be taken seriously by a man richer than he is. That
vanity is the entire mechanism of Movement III — he writes to Janina to
demonstrate his own prudence to the Count, and destroys his co-conspirator
without ever intending to.

**Moral position:** he wrote the letter in 1815 and has not thought about it
since. He does not recognize the Count and he does not connect the Janina
report to himself. His obliviousness is the joke, and the joke is load-bearing:
Volume IV will collect it.

**Structural lock:** heavy fleshy face; small shrewd close-set eyes; thin
mouth; high colour; thinning sandy-grey hair combed across; **full side
whiskers, no moustache**; short and thickening; expensive clothes that fit
badly; rings.

**Never:** Villefort's narrow pale rigidity; Fernand's military moustache and
upright carriage.

## 7 · BEAUCHAMP — 28 · NEW

**The single young-man-about-town.** Collapses Debray, Château-Renaud, Franz
d'Épinay, and Beauchamp into one face.

**Wants (volume):** to be right. He prints the paragraph because it is true, he
offers to retract it because Albert is his friend, and then he goes to Janina
and verifies it because he is a journalist — and the verification is what kills
Fernand. His decency is the murder weapon.

**Moral position:** the volume's only neutral party, and its conscience by
accident.

**Structural lock:** tall, thin, slightly stooped; sandy-light brown hair,
untidy; **small oval spectacles — primary anti-collision marker**; long face;
ironic mouth; ink-stained fingers; plain dark practical clothes, unfashionable
and slightly worn.

**Never — second-highest collision risk:** Beauchamp and Albert are the
volume's only two young men and appear together on four pages. Separation is
carried by hair colour (sandy vs chestnut), spectacles (always vs never),
costume value (plain dark vs bright pale), and posture (stooped vs upright).
All four stated in every prompt where both appear.

## 8 · GÉRARD DE VILLEFORT — 53

**Anchor:** `monte-cristo-expanded/refs/01-villefort-1815-v2.png`, aged
twenty-three years.

**Appears on two pages only** — P9, where he shakes the Count's hand and feels
a chill he cannot place, and P49, where the Count looks up at his roof.

He is here because the volume's best single image is three men who destroyed
Edmond Dantès taking his hand in one evening and none of them knowing, and
because the last panel of this volume must have somewhere to point. He is not
developed. Volume III is his.

**Structural lock:** long narrow inverted-triangle face; very high forehead;
close-set grey-hazel eyes; thin brows; convex aquiline nose; pointed chin; cool
pale skin; chestnut hair gone iron-grey, still a hard side part; deep vertical
lines; rigid high-necked black silhouette; clean-shaven.

**Never:** any Edmond trait — no curls, no olive skin, no broad cheekbones, no
left-mouth asymmetry. Inherited verbatim from Volume I, where Edmond and
Villefort collided badly enough to force a full reference redesign.

## Reserved design space — extended from Volume I

No background or unnamed figure may inherit a complete identity stack:

- unrelieved black columnar dress with swept-back hair and pallor → **the
  Count**;
- heavy iron-black military moustache with receding temples and decorations →
  **Fernand**;
- crimson-and-gold Epirote embroidery with unbound black hair → **Haydée**;
- small oval spectacles with untidy sandy hair and plain dark worn clothes →
  **Beauchamp**;
- bright pale waistcoat with neat chestnut side part on a young man →
  **Albert**;
- loose raven curls, open white shirt, red-brown sash → **young Edmond,
  Volume I. Reserved and not used in this volume at all.**

## Collision matrix — pairs requiring adversarial boards

| Pair | Risk | Carried by |
|---|---|---|
| **Albert / the Count** | **highest** | hair colour, skin value, costume value, age, expression default |
| **Albert / Beauchamp** | **high** | spectacles, hair colour, costume value, posture |
| Fernand / the Count | medium | moustache, hairline, build, pallor |
| Haydée / Mercédès | medium | age, hairline, silhouette, palette |
| Danglars / Villefort | medium | face shape, build, whiskers, colour |
