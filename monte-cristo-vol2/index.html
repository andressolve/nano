After the story

Test your understanding

  1. Why does Haydée insist that Fernand come first?

    Correct: Haydée wants her testimony to confront the man responsible for her family's ruin.

    Not quite. She insists Fernand come first so her evidence can expose him directly.

  2. How does the Count use Danglars and the newspaper?

    Correct: the newspaper turns the evidence into a public summons Fernand cannot ignore.

    Not quite. The paper publishes the Janina evidence, driving Fernand to demand a public hearing.

  3. Why does Mercédès ask Edmond not to fight Albert?

    Correct: Mercédès intervenes to protect Albert, who is acting from a false understanding of his father's honor.

    Not quite. She asks Edmond to spare Albert, her son.

  4. Why does Albert withdraw and reject the Morcerf name and fortune?

    Correct: Albert cannot keep a name and fortune stained by Fernand's betrayal.

    Not quite. He rejects the inherited identity because Fernand's betrayal makes it morally intolerable.

  5. What do the Count's final “One” and Villefort's lit window mean?

    Correct: “One” marks the completed revenge, while Villefort's lit window promises another reckoning.

    Not quite. The ending marks one completed revenge and points toward another.
