# Settings and Objects

Palettes inherit the Volume I world-palette system in
`monte-cristo/01-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. **Paris 1838 is already written there** and is
used verbatim as this volume's base:

> **Paris, 1838** — Lacquer black, burgundy, bottle green, ivory, polished
> walnut, wax red, and old gold. Mirrors, doorways, documents, and furniture
> should make social rooms feel like mechanisms.

Every location below is a variation on that base **except Janina**, which must
look like a different world.

Each palette names one restrained accent that does dramatic work. That is the
Volume I pattern and it is not decoration — the amber lamp in Volume I's prison
was the moral boundary of a page.

---

## Locations

### 1 · The Count's house, 30 Champs-Élysées — P1–6, P14–15, P17–18, P20, P22–23, P26, P29, P37–40, P44–46
Lacquer black, ivory, cold grey daylight, unpolished new gold.
**Deliberately underfurnished** — enormous, correct, and unlived-in. A stage
set for a man who does not live anywhere. No family objects, no clutter, no
warmth. Tall uncurtained windows with the city in them.
**Accent:** the city lights outside the window — the three roofs. They are the
only warm thing in the room and they are other people's houses.

### 2 · The Morcerf house — P6–13, P16, P26, P28, P41, P47
Burgundy, polished walnut, wax red, old gold, dense candle amber.
The opposite of the Count's house: **overstuffed with purchased legitimacy.**
Too many portraits, too much gilt, a general's staircase built for a man who
was not born to one. Warm, crowded, and trying too hard.
**Accent:** wax red — the seals, the ribbons, the decorations on Fernand's
chest. Volume I's wax motif, now worn as jewellery.
**P47 reverses it:** the same rooms, cold, wardrobes standing open.

### 3 · Janina, 1822 — P19–20
**Must not look like Paris.** White limestone, cypress black, Ionian blue,
sun-bleached ochre, and fire.
High hard Mediterranean light where Paris is candlelit and interior. Horizontal
where Paris is vertical. This is Haydée's memory and it is the only place in
the volume with a sky in it.
**Accent:** fire — the burning fortress, and the one lamp in the pavilion.

**P20's last panel is a match cut out of Janina and back to Paris,** held in one
frame: the knocked-over pavilion lamp on one side, and on the other, nearer, the
same shape of lamp on the Count's black table with his two hands flat beside it.
The two worlds have to be rendered so that the cut reads as *the same lamp* and
the collision of palettes is the point. It is the only frame in the volume that
holds both.

**P29 returns to Janina in 1838** — the same white limestone and the same hard
light, sixteen years on and unburned, with Beauchamp in it. Same world, no
fire. The reader must recognize the place on sight; that recognition is what
makes his verification land. **P29's last panel cuts back to the Count's black
room** with the printed result in his hands, rhyming P26's first panel: the same
lamp, the same two hands, the same paper.

### 4 · The slave market, Constantinople — P21
Dust, bleached white, heat-glare, indigo shadow.
**Restraint is mandatory.** The subject is a child watching her mother sold and
then dying. No nudity, no chains as spectacle, no crowd leering. The image is a
face and a hand let go of.

### 5 · Danglars' study — P24–25
Bottle green baize, brass, ledger calf, gaslight, coin-yellow.
Money rendered as furniture. Mechanisms everywhere: a clock, a strongbox, a
letter scale.
**Accent:** brass — everything that counts, weighs, or locks.

### 6 · Beauchamp's newspaper office — P27, P35
Ink black, newsprint grey, tallow, bare board.
The only unluxurious room in Paris in this book. Cheap light, paper everywhere.

### 7 · The Chamber of Peers — P31–34
The volume's largest room. Crimson benches, heavy gold, dark oak, cold high
daylight from above.
Designed as a mechanism, per the style guide: tiered, symmetrical, and built to
process a man.
**Accent:** Haydée's crimson-and-gold Epirote dress, which is the *only* thing
in the room that is not French — and which rhymes with the benches, so that
when she enters she looks like she belongs there and Fernand does not.

**P30 is the outside of this building** — a stopped carriage in a side street
beside it, then its front steps and the narrow public stair at its flank. The
Chamber must be recognizable from outside on P30 so that P31's interior is the
same building and not a new one.

### 8 · The Bois, dawn — P42–44
Pale grey-green, mist, wet black trunks, thin gold at the horizon.
Volume I's "the ending" palette, borrowed early and deliberately: *air, dawn,
open distance.* The one place in the volume where the world breathes.

### 9 · The street outside the Morcerf house, night — P48–49
Lacquer black, wet cobble, gas-yellow, and one departing carriage lamp.
**Accent:** the carriage lamp going away — the last warm thing, leaving.

### 10 · The foyer of the Opera — P36
Gilt, mirrors, gaslight, massed pale silk and black coats. The only crowd in
the volume that is not a party in a private house.
It exists for one image: **the Count perfectly still in the middle of a moving
crowd** while a boy shouts at him. Everyone else is a blur of value; he is the
one unbroken black vertical, and the room does not touch him.

## Objects and their page-by-page state

### The untouched glass — **the volume's primary motif**
The Count will not eat or drink under the roof of an enemy.

| Page | State |
|---|---|
| P8 | Fernand toasts him. The Count raises the glass, holds it, sets it down full. Nobody notices. |
| P11 | Mercédès offers him apricots grown in her own garden. He refuses. **She notices, and it is the moment she becomes certain.** |
| P12 | The full glass sits between them through the whole scene, and is still there in the last panel after he has walked away. |
| P15 | In his own empty house, a poured glass nobody drank — the habit is not a performance for enemies. It is what he is. |
| P45 | In his own house, with Fernand in front of him, **he pours and drinks it empty** — and then says what the two refusals meant. The roof is his. |

This is a Dumas detail, kept because it does more work than a page of dialogue:
it is a courtesy, an insult, a confession, and a piece of evidence, and it never
has to be explained.

### Haydée's proof — the bill of sale
A document bearing Fernand's signature and a **red wax seal**, carrying Volume
I's wax motif forward from imprisoning order → cancelled debt → now, evidence.

P18 she produces it · P22 both their hands are on it · P23 he holds it while he describes the
trap he will build around it · **P30 it passes back into her hands in the
carriage, and for one panel both of them are holding it again** · P32 she
carries it through the door · P33 a clerk reads it aloud. It is **large, and its
essential content is spoken aloud on P33** — no story logic ever depends on
reading small handwriting (Volume I's tiny-prop rule).

### Fernand's decorations
P8 worn, polished, displayed · P31 worn to the bar of the Chamber · P34 still
on his chest in an emptying hall · P47 on the floor of an empty room.

### The Janina correspondence
P24 Danglars' outgoing letter, sealed by his own hand · P25 the reply,
opened · P26 the same words set in newsprint. **The chain is visible and
unbroken** — the reader watches one piece of paper become a public fact.

### The general's staircase
P6 lit and crowded · P8 the Count climbs it beside its owner · P16 its top,
very late, with Mercédès below him on it · P47 unlit and empty, with Fernand
climbing it alone.

### Doorways
Volume I used doorways to measure Edmond's relationship to freedom. Volume II
uses them to measure his relationship to other people's houses: he enters
Morcerf's door as a guest (P7) and Danglars' as a peer (P24), but he never
walks onto the floor of the Chamber — on P30 he puts Haydée out of the carriage
at the front steps and takes the narrow public stair himself, and from then on
he is only ever above that room (P31, P33). **He is at the killing and not in
the room**, which is the whole shape of the man.

### Hands
Volume I's moral-action motif continues. **P9 is built on it:** three
handshakes in one page, none of them knowing.
