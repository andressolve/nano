# Portrait Typography System — Volume II

## Inheritance

**Parent authority:** `monte-cristo-expanded/06-PORTRAIT-TYPOGRAPHY-SYSTEM.md`.

**Every number below is carried forward from Volume I unchanged.** This volume
relaxes nothing. There is no clause here that lowers a value, widens a
tolerance, or softens a check relative to the parent, and none may be added
without naming the parent rule it relaxes and why.

This is hygiene. It is settled, it is not a topic for critic rounds, and no
gate round is spent discussing it. **No typography number on this page is a gate.**
Every number here is a construction instruction for the builder, and it belongs
in the page prompt. The only typography question ever asked at the page gate is
the transcription test: *did every string read?* Nothing on this page is
measured, at any size, on the source or on the proof.

---

## Canvas and safe area

- Finished canvas: **1024 × 1536 portrait, 2:3.** Never landscape, never
  square, never mixed.
- Outer safe margin: **64 px** every side.
- Minimum panel gutter: **24 px**.
- No essential text below **72 px** from the bottom edge.
- No balloon, caption, or prose field crosses a panel border.
- Page numbers belong to the reader interface, never to the story image.

## Speech balloons

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Normal lettering height | **44–50 px** |
| Short-reply lettering height | **48–54 px** when space allows |
| **Minimum approved lettering height** | **40 px — hard floor** |
| Comfortable balloon width | **240–390 px** |
| Maximum words in one balloon | **~24** |
| Internal padding | ~one capital-letter height between text and outline |

Warm ivory fill, never pure digital white. Restrained charcoal-brown painted
outline. Tails end in open space immediately beside the speaker's mouth. A
balloon belongs unmistakably to the person on its own side of the panel.
Silent characters receive no balloons and no tail fragments.

**Opposed speakers:** first speaker takes the upper position on their own side;
the reply takes the opposite upper or middle position; a second line from the
first speaker drops lower on the original side. **If that pattern is still
ambiguous, split the exchange across panels.**

## Illustrated-prose fields

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Prose lettering height | **36–42 px** |
| Line length | **38–52 characters** including spaces |
| Paragraph length | **2–5 lines** |
| Fields per page | **one or two** — never scatter sentences across many boxes |
| Field width | **78–88% of canvas** |
| Minimum internal padding | **42 px** |

Matte parchment field integrated into the page design. Left-aligned, calm
ragged right edge. **No busy art, faces, hands, maps, or documents behind the
prose.**

Parchment colour comes from the page palette: warm cream in the Morcerf house;
cold ivory in the Count's house; sun-bleached ochre at Janina; pale grey-green
at the Bois.

## Lettering family

One restrained editorial family at three levels:

1. **Speech** — large, narrow, upright, hand-lettered mixed-case
   serif/sans hybrid.
2. **Narrative prose** — legible literary serif, moderate contrast, mixed case.
3. **Small sound or object label** — the same family, simplified and enlarged.

**Banned:** condensed comic-display lettering; modern geometric UI fonts;
cursive body text; all-capital prose; faux-aged or distressed letterforms;
tiny handwritten documents carrying essential facts; the thin old-style italic
serif that the abandoned volume rendered at 27 px.

## Hierarchy

Exactly **three** visible levels: narrative prose, speech, small sound/object
label. Production titles, page titles, scene titles, speaker names, density
codes, and editorial labels **never** appear in finished art.

Boldface, italics, enlarged shouting, and decorative colour are exceptional.
Meaning comes first from staging, expression, silence, and the words.

## Per-mode application

**Dramatic scene** — balloons only unless a brief transition is
indispensable. Reserve balloon lanes *before* placing faces and objects.
Prefer 45–80 total words; ~105 only with large protected fields.

**Illustrated prose** — compose the prose field first. One dominant image or a
continuous-time sequence. 50–120 words, 140 exceptional. Speech only if the
live interruption is the page turn.

**Spectacle or silence** — text under 15% of visual attention. One short line
may anchor scale or identity. Never add prose to defend an image whose causal
setup belongs on the previous page.

## How this is enforced

**Never measure lettering. At any size.** Not glyph extent, not x-height, not
cap height, not line pitch, on the source or on the proof. A line that reads
well is fine whatever it measures. Measuring type against these targets is the
tolerance-exercise failure mode that drove one Volume I page to v77 across 143
candidates, and in this volume it took page 1 to a v4 ceiling on four reports
that each said the text was fully readable.

Reader comfort is proved by the **transcription test** (`10-CRITIC-OPERATIONS.md`),
not asserted. The critic transcribes every string from the 600 × 900 desktop
proof with the script closed. A string that cannot be transcribed is blocking.

**If text will not survive transcription, restage or split the page. Never
shrink the type.** An extra page is cheap; there is no page budget that
outranks this.

## Reader-size test

Inherited verbatim, including Volume I's 2026-08-07 display-target correction:

1. inspect the 1024 × 1536 source;
2. inspect at **600 × 900 desktop** — binding;
3. inspect at **768 × 1152 tablet** — secondary;
4. read the sequence without production notes;
5. reject any page requiring magnification, source knowledge, or a guess about
   balloon ownership.

**Phone-size and 390 px proofs are not approval gates.**

## Rejection conditions

Reject and regenerate the full page when: any word differs from the exact
script · a letter is malformed, missing, duplicated, or replaced · lettering
must be interpreted rather than read · a tail points at the wrong speaker, a
silent person, an object, or empty space · the first balloon encountered
belongs to a later speaker · the reading path crosses backward · text touches a
panel edge, face, hand, document, or decisive object · prose sits over
patterned or high-detail art · a story-world document carries essential
information only through handwriting · the canvas is not 1024 × 1536 portrait.

**Never patch a balloon or tail onto a flattened page. Regeneration is always
whole-page.**
