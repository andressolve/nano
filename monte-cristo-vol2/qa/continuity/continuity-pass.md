# Pages 1–10 — visual continuity gate rerun

I inspected the current deterministic `qa/continuity/contact-sheet.png` as one image at thumbnail scale, then compared the first and last promoted full pages and the approved identity boards where needed.

## Mandatory defects

None.

## Brightness, gloss, detail and saturation

No page jumps out as a visual outlier. Pages 1–5 remain deliberately cold, dark and underfurnished. Page 6 becomes much warmer and brighter because the story crosses into the overstuffed, candlelit Morcerf house, and Pages 7–10 sustain that location’s range. The shift is spatial and narrative rather than an isolated exposure or saturation failure. Matte surface texture, edge handling, shadow mass and detail remain coherent; no page becomes glossy, flat, airbrushed or conspicuously more detailed than its neighbours.

## Principal distinguishability at thumbnail scale

All principals who appear in Pages 1–10 remain distinct. Haydée’s long dark hair and crimson-gold vertical silhouette separate her from mature, burgundy-gowned Mercédès. Fernand’s moustache, receding hairline, decorated soldier mass and ruddy value separate him from the Count. Danglars’ fleshy face and side whiskers separate him from narrow, high-foreheaded Villefort. Beauchamp does not yet appear.

The highest-risk pair passes decisively on Pages 7 and 10: Albert remains the chestnut-haired, fairer, twenty-two-year-old open face with an ivory waistcoat and navy coat, while the Count remains the older, pallid, angular, swept-back black-and-grey-haired figure forming an unbroken black vertical. They do not merge in the contact sheet or any reduced panel.

## Different-book check

No page reads as though it belongs to a different book. The illustrated-prose opener, architectural spectacle and intimate dialogue pages share the same Velvet Cinema material language and shadow structure. The Count-house-to-Morcerf-house palette transition is coherent and intentional.
