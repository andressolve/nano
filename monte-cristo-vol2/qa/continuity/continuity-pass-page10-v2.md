# Pages 1–10 — visual continuity gate

I inspected the deterministic `qa/continuity/contact-sheet.png` at thumbnail scale, then used the promoted full pages and approved identity boards for direct comparisons where needed.

## Mandatory defects

None.

## Brightness, gloss, detail and saturation

No page jumps out as a rendering outlier. Pages 1–5 remain deliberately cold, dark and underfurnished; Page 6 becomes much warmer and brighter because it crosses into the Morcerf-house palette, and Pages 7–10 sustain that warmth. The shift is clearly spatial and narrative rather than an isolated saturation or exposure failure. Surface detail, edge handling and matte painterly texture remain coherent from the first page to the tenth; no page becomes glossy, flat, airbrushed or conspicuously more detailed than its neighbours.

## Principal distinguishability at thumbnail scale

All principals who appear in this batch remain distinguishable. Haydée’s long dark hair and crimson-gold vertical silhouette separate her from the mature burgundy-gowned Mercédès; Fernand’s moustache, receding hairline, decorated soldier mass and ruddy value separate him from the Count; Danglars’ fleshy face and side whiskers separate him from narrow, high-foreheaded Villefort. Beauchamp does not yet appear.

The highest-risk pair passes decisively on Pages 7 and 10: Albert is the chestnut-haired, fairer, twenty-two-year-old open face anchored by the pale waistcoat, while the Count is the older, pallid, angular, swept-back black-and-grey-haired figure forming an unbroken black vertical. They do not merge in the contact sheet or in any reduced panel.

## Whole-sequence visual read

No page reads as though it belongs to a different book. The architectural spectacle pages, intimate dialogue pages and illustrated-prose opener all retain the same Velvet Cinema material language and shadow structure. The Count-house-to-Morcerf-house palette transition is coherent and intentional.
