## PAGE 31 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Chamber of Peers. Crimson benches, heavy gold, dark oak, cold
high daylight from above.

**Panel 1 — 11%.** The same room from inside, from above and behind: tiered,
symmetrical, three hundred men in it, built to process one man. Fernand alone at
the bar. No caption; the reader arrived with him.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Fernand standing, full figure, decorations across
his chest, both hands on the rail. He is good at this. He has been good at this
for eleven years.

> **FERNAND:** "I am accused by a newspaper of selling a fortress."
>
> **FERNAND:** "In a war France did not fight. In a country none of you has
> seen."
>
> **FERNAND:** "On the strength of a letter written in a language none of you
> reads."

**Panel 3 — 18%.** Faces on the benches, leaning toward him — and among them
Danglars, nodding along, entirely untroubled.

> **FERNAND:** "I have sat in this house eleven years. I was at Ligny."
>
> **FERNAND:** "There is not a man here who has not had my hand on his
> shoulder."

**Panel 4 — 12%.** Fernand turning, opening his arms to the whole room, and one
peer on the front bench already half out of his seat to answer him. It is
working.

> **FERNAND:** "I ask the man who wrote the paragraph to stand up and put a name
> to it."

**Panel 5 — 14%, wide band.** High in the public gallery, alone at the rail: one
black figure, leaning slightly out over three hundred heads. He does not look
worried. He looks hungry.

> **COUNT:** *(to nobody, under the noise)* "Go on. Win it."

**Why you turn the page:** Fernand is winning, the whole room is coming over to
him, and the man in the gallery wants him to.

## PAGE 32 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Chamber of Peers.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Three hundred faces, all turned the same way, at the sound of
a door.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 70%.** The great door at the back of the hall standing
open, and Haydée in it: small, alone, in crimson-and-gold Epirote embroidery,
unbound black hair, a loose vertical silhouette in a room of French tailoring.
The dress rhymes with the crimson benches. She looks as though the room was built
for her — and Fernand, tiny at the far end, does not.

**Why you turn the page:** you already know who she is and nobody in that room
does.

## PAGE 33 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Chamber of Peers.

**Panel 1 — 11%.** Haydée at the bar, alone, hands empty, entirely still.

> **HAYDÉE:** "My name is Haydée. My father was Ali Tebelen, Pasha of Janina."

**Panel 2 — 10%.** The President of the Chamber, half-risen.

> **PRESIDENT:** "Mademoiselle. Do you know the accused?"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 47%.** Haydée turned to look down the whole length of the
hall at Fernand, and Fernand looking back. Both in one frame with the entire
crimson room between them.

> **HAYDÉE:** "He is fatter."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "He was an officer of my father's guard."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "He had the keys of the eastern gate, and he ate at our table for
> two years."

**Panel 4 — 18%.** The President of the Chamber reading aloud from the document,
holding it up, the red seal visible to the room.

> **PRESIDENT:** "'Received of the merchant El-Kobbir, four hundred thousand
> francs—'"
>
> **PRESIDENT:** "'—for a Christian slave of eleven years named Haydée, and her
> mother, wife of Ali Tebelen.'"
>
> **PRESIDENT:** "Signed, Fernand Mondego."

**Panel 5 — 14%.** One frame, two figures, the height of the room between them:
Fernand frozen at the bar, not moving at all — and above and behind him the
gallery rail, where the Count has both hands on the wood and is leaning out over
the whole hall. For one panel he looks exactly like what he is: a man at a
killing he paid for.

**Why you turn the page:** it is over, and you have not seen what it looks like
afterwards.

## PAGE 34 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Chamber of Peers, emptying.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** The crimson benches nine-tenths empty under cold
light from above. Fernand alone in the middle of the tiers, still standing, still
wearing every decoration.

> **PROSE FIELD 1**
> "The vote took four minutes. Nobody spoke to him on the way out. Two men who
> had dined at his house in April went past him on the stair and looked at the
> stair.
>
> He stayed where he was for some time after the hall was empty, because he had
> not been given anything to do next, and for eleven years there had always been
> something to do next."

**Panel 2 — 35%, lower band.** The empty doorway at the back of the hall.

> **PROSE FIELD 2**
> "By six o'clock the name had been taken off the door.
>
> His son heard it in the street, from a man selling papers."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is going to want to know who did this.

## PAGE 35 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the newspaper office, night.

**Panel 1 — 17%.** Albert in the doorway. Beauchamp does not get up.

> **ALBERT:** "The girl. Who is she?"
>
> **BEAUCHAMP:** "Albert—"

**Panel 2 — 18%.**

> **ALBERT:** "She walked into the Chamber of Peers with a sixteen-year-old
> receipt and the name of a town nobody in France can find."
>
> **ALBERT:** "Somebody put her there."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Beauchamp, who does not lie and is not going to start tonight.

> **BEAUCHAMP:** "She lives in a house on the Champs-Élysées."
>
> **ALBERT:** "Whose house."

**Panel 4 — dominant, 50%.** Beauchamp's face, and Albert understanding it before
he is told.

> **BEAUCHAMP:** "You've dined in it."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy has just found out that the man he liked did
this to his father.

## PAGE 36 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the foyer of the Opera. Crowded, gilt, public.

**Panel 1 — 15%.** Albert coming through a crowd that opens for him. Forty people
in evening dress, already turning to look.

> **ALBERT:** "You came into my father's house. You took his hand on his own
> stairs. You ate at his table—"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 55%.** The Count, black, absolutely still in a bright
crowd, and the small flat pleasure of the line landing.

> **COUNT:** "I ate nothing at your father's table."

**Panel 3 — 12%.** Albert, struck. The cruelty is worse than a denial would have
been.

**Panel 4 — 10%.** A glove in the air.

> **ALBERT:** "Tomorrow. Eight o'clock, the Bois. Pistols."

**Panel 5 — 8%.** The Count's hand taking it out of the air without hurry, and his
face above it — and for one frame, after the pleasure, something else.

> **COUNT:** "As you like."

**Why you turn the page:** he said yes too quickly and something went across his
face.

## PAGE 37 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's house, the black room.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Haydée lit up, alive, moving — the only warm figure in the
volume's coldest room.

> **HAYDÉE:** "They have scraped his name off the door of the Chamber. I went to
> watch them do it."

**Panel 2 — 15%.** A laid table. The Count is at it and is not eating.

> **HAYDÉE:** "You are not eating."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** Haydée standing over him; the Count turned
three-quarters away toward the window. She is triumphant. He is looking at a
wall.

> **HAYDÉE:** "You have wanted this since before I was born."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "It is on the table in front of you and you are looking at the
> wall."

**Panel 4 — 15%.**

> **COUNT:** "The boy is twenty-two."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "I was eleven."
>
> **COUNT:** "Yes."

**Why you turn the page:** she is right, he has no answer, and he has agreed to
shoot the boy at eight in the morning.

## PAGE 38 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's house, the black room. Night.

**Panel 1 — 13%.** A servantless hall. Mercédès alone in the doorway in
travelling black, having come herself, at night, with no card and nobody to
announce her.

> **COUNT:** "Madame la Comtesse. At this hour."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Don't."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** She comes into the room. She does not sit.

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "I have sat in that house since the newspaper, while men I have
> known twenty years found reasons not to look at me."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "I have not once had to wonder who was doing it."

**Panel 3 — 12%.** The enormous black underfurnished room, and Mercédès small in
the middle of it. The room makes her small and it does not make her less.

**Panel 4 — dominant, 55%.** Two faces, and nothing else in the frame. This is the
sentence the whole volume has been holding.

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Edmond."
>
> **COUNT:** "Nobody has said that name to me in twenty-three years."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "I have said it every day."

**Why you turn the page:** the secret the volume is built on has just been said
out loud, and there are ten pages left.

## PAGE 39 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the same room, continuous.

**Panel 1 — 12%.**

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Don't kill my son."
>
> **COUNT:** "Your son called me out in front of forty people."

**Panel 2 — 14%.**

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Then let him miss."
>
> **COUNT:** "He will not miss. He has been shooting since he was ten."
>
> **COUNT:** "His father taught him."

**Panel 3 — 13%.**

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Then don't fire."
>
> **COUNT:** "You are asking me to stand still in a field and let a Mondego
> shoot me."

**Panel 4 — dominant, 46%.** Two faces very close, both of them wrecked. The
largest panel in the volume of two people simply looking at each other.

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "You did not do this for justice, Edmond."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "You did it so that I would see it."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "I have seen it."

**Panel 5 — 15%.** The poised right hand, opening; his face above it, and nothing
held in reserve behind it for the first time in nine years.

> **COUNT:** "…Very well."
>
> **COUNT:** "I will stand where his second puts me, and I will not raise my
> hand."

**Why you turn the page:** he has agreed to die, and it is eleven hours to eight
o'clock.

## PAGE 40 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Count's house, one lamp, three in the morning.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** The Count at a bare table, writing, in a pool of
lamplight in an enormous dark room. Everything else is black.

> **PROSE FIELD 1**
> "He wrote until three. The estate to Haydée, entire, with a man in Trieste
> named to see it done. A letter to a shipowner in Marseille who was old now and
> would not understand any of it. Instructions about a house on an island that
> nobody else had ever seen the inside of.
>
> It took him under two hours to put down everything he had made in nine years,
> and there was nobody on the list he had known before 1815."

**Panel 2 — 35%, lower band.** The pistol case on the table, closed, and his hand
resting on the lid.

> **PROSE FIELD 2**
> "Then he sat with the case shut in front of him and did the one piece of
> arithmetic he had been avoiding since April:
>
> what he had spent, and what he had bought with it."

**Why you turn the page:** it is nearly dawn and he intends to let a boy shoot
him.
