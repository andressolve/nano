## Page intent

## PAGE 33

Haydée calmly identifies Fernand, supplies the facts of his betrayal, and lets
his own signed sale finish him. The whole Chamber lies between accuser and
accused. Above Fernand, the Count leans out of the gallery with unmistakable
appetite: Haydée speaks, but he built the killing ground.

The page is about testimony becoming public fact. It is not a test of whether a
tiny wax seal or handwriting can be inspected; the President reads the evidence
aloud.

**Location:** the Chamber of Peers.

**Panel 1 — 11%.** Haydée at the bar, alone, hands empty, entirely still.

> **HAYDÉE:** "My name is Haydée. My father was Ali Tebelen, Pasha of Janina."

**Panel 2 — 10%.** The President of the Chamber, half-risen.

> **PRESIDENT:** "Mademoiselle. Do you know the accused?"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 47%.** Haydée turned to look down the whole length of the

>
> **HAYDÉE:** "He was an officer of my father's guard."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "He had the keys of the eastern gate, and he ate at our table for
> two years."

**Panel 4 — 18%.** The President of the Chamber reading aloud from the document,
holding it up, the red seal visible to the room.

> **PRESIDENT:** "'Received of the merchant El-Kobbir, four hundred thousand
> francs—'"
>
> **PRESIDENT:** "'—for a Christian slave of eleven years named Haydée, and her
> mother, wife of Ali Tebelen.'"
>
> **PRESIDENT:** "Signed, Fernand Mondego."

**Panel 5 — 14%.** One frame, two figures, the height of the room between them:
Fernand frozen at the bar, not moving at all — and above and behind him the
gallery rail, where the Count has both hands on the wood and is leaning out over
the whole hall. For one panel he looks exactly like what he is: a man at a
killing he paid for.

**Why you turn the page:** it is over, and you have not seen what it looks like
afterwards.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named/focal identity, consequential continuity/object state, focal
generation integrity, or dominant dramatic relationship. Every correction
replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop, geometry,
color, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking. Omit them.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 33 — appendix

### C1 — Testimony text or ownership fails
Any required script string cannot be transcribed comfortably from the 600 × 900
proof, is altered/duplicated/reordered, or cannot be assigned confidently to
Haydée or the President. Fernand and the Count must remain silent.

### C2 — The testimony does not build to conviction
The page does not read in order as Haydée naming herself, identifying Fernand,
the President reading the sale, and the room reaching the end of the case.

### C3 — The accuser–accused relationship fails
Haydée and Fernand are not held in one readable hall-spanning relationship, one
is unrecognizable, or the staging makes another figure the apparent accuser or
accused.

### C4 — The evidence action fails
The President is not visibly reading and presenting a document, so the spoken
sale appears ungrounded. Exact handwriting and microscopic seal resolution are
nonblocking when the raised evidence action is clear.

### C5 — The Count's position changes the story
The Count is absent from the final relationship, appears on the Chamber floor,
is confused with Fernand, or does not read as an intent observer above Fernand.
Exact hand placement, railing geometry, and facial micro-expression are not
gates when that relationship lands.

### C6 — Consequential generation failure
A duplicated named figure, identity collision, gross focal anatomical break, or
corruption materially disrupts the testimony. Anonymous crowd repetition,
background hands, and decorative artifacts are nonblocking unless they dominate
attention or change who is present.
