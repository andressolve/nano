## Page intent

## PAGE 43

Albert publicly states what Fernand did to Edmond Dantès, gives up his quarrel,
and refuses the Count's prepared response. Then he leaves the field with
Mercédès, takes her name, and removes both of them from the Count's frame.

The page belongs to Albert's moral choice. The Count's sudden lack of a line is
important, but the departing mother and son own the final image.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Albert, and behind him at the edge of the field, Mercédès

>

grass toward the carriage, backs to frame, small under a wide dawn sky. The Count
is not in the panel at all.

> **ALBERT:** "I'll take my mother's name. She was Mercédès Herrera before she
> was anything of his."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is out of it and the father is still alive.
