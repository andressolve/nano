# Exact owner-controlled script — Pages 41-49

Read only after the uninterrupted blind sequence read.

## PAGE 41 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Albert's room, the Morcerf house.

**Panel 1 — 15%.** Albert at a table with a pistol in pieces in front of him,
cleaning it because there is nothing else to do with his hands. Mercédès in the
door, still in the travelling black.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "The same night, at the other end of Paris."

> **ALBERT:** "You've been out."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Yes."

**Panel 2 — 12%.** She sits down opposite him, which she has not done in this
room since he was a boy.

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Put that down. I am going to tell you about 1815."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** The two of them across the table with the pistol
between them and one candle. She does not soften any of it.

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "There was a boy in Marseille who was going to marry me. His
> name was Edmond Dantès."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "They arrested him at our betrothal dinner. He was nineteen."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Danglars wrote the letter. Your father carried it to the post."

**Panel 4 — 13%.** Albert not moving.

> **ALBERT:** "You knew."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "I have always known."

**Panel 5 — 10%.** Mercédès' hands flat on the table.

> **ALBERT:** "Where is he now?"
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "You are going to shoot at him at eight o'clock."

**Why you turn the page:** she has just told him who he is meeting at dawn and
there are four hours left.

## PAGE 42 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Bois de Boulogne, dawn. Pale grey-green, mist, wet black
trunks, thin gold at the horizon.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The field: figures in coats, a carriage, two seconds, a
surgeon's bag. The Count standing apart, already still.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 55%.** Albert with his hat off, in front of the seconds,
speaking loudly enough for all of them. Nobody in the frame expected this.

> **ALBERT:** "I asked for this and I'm withdrawing it. Out loud, here, so
> nobody has to hear it from somebody else later."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** A second, half-outraged, the pistol case open in his hands.

> **SECOND:** "On what grounds, monsieur?"

**Panel 4 — 10%.** Albert, steady.

> **ALBERT:** "On the grounds that the newspaper was right."

**Why you turn the page:** he is about to say the rest of it in front of
witnesses.

## PAGE 43 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Bois, continuous.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Albert, and behind him at the edge of the field, Mercédès
standing by a hired carriage in the mist.

> **ALBERT:** "My mother told me last night what my father did in 1815, to a man
> called Edmond Dantès."
>
> **ALBERT:** "He did it for money, and he did it to marry her. The man was
> nineteen years old."

**Panel 2 — 12%.** The seconds and the surgeon. Nobody is moving.

> **ALBERT:** "I've no quarrel with this gentleman. I haven't the right to one."

**Panel 3 — 13%.** The Count, one step forward, and for the first time in the
volume he has no line ready.

> **COUNT:** "Monsieur—"
>
> **ALBERT:** "Don't."

**Panel 4 — dominant, 50%.** Albert and Mercédès walking away across the wet
grass toward the carriage, backs to frame, small under a wide dawn sky. The Count
is not in the panel at all.

> **ALBERT:** "I'll take my mother's name. She was Mercédès Herrera before she
> was anything of his."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is out of it and the father is still alive.

## PAGE 44 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Bois, and then the road back into the city.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** The Count alone in the middle of the wet field,
very small under a wide pale sky. The carriages are already going: Albert's and
his mother's out through the trees, the seconds' the other way. Nobody is
looking at him. He has not moved from where he was told to stand.

> **PROSE FIELD 1**
> "He had come out to the Bois to be shot at. He had spent the night before
> putting his affairs in order so that a boy of twenty-two could kill him
> without inconveniencing anybody, and he had meant it.
>
> Nobody fired. He stood on the wet grass a long while afterwards working out
> what he felt, which took longer than he expected, and what he arrived at was
> not relief."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The inside of his own carriage going back into Paris, his
face at the window, the grey suburbs going past. He looks like a man doing
arithmetic.

> **PROSE FIELD 2**
> "At three that morning he had thought that if he were let off he would stop.
>
> He was wrong about that, and he knew it before the carriage reached the gate.
> The son had let him go. The father had not, and would be at his door before
> dark."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** The black room. His own hand setting a decanter and one glass
down on the table, squared to the edge, and going away again. Nobody has drunk
anything.

**Why you turn the page:** he was spared, he has decided it changes nothing, and
he has already put out a glass.

## PAGE 45 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's house, the black room. Afternoon.

**Panel 1 — 17%.** Fernand in the doorway, hat still on, ruined, a pistol case
under his arm. The room is enormous around him.

> **FERNAND:** "My son wouldn't fire at you. Take a pistol."

**Panel 2 — 15%.** The Count seated, unhurried — still in the coat he wore to
the Bois, the wet grass of the field still on his boots. He has not been home
long enough to change.

> **COUNT:** "No. Sit down."
>
> **FERNAND:** "You've destroyed me and you won't even—"
>
> **COUNT:** "Sit down. You are going to be told why first."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** The Count pouring, in his own house, and drinking.
The glass comes down empty. It is the first thing he has swallowed in this
volume, and Fernand does not understand why it is happening.

> **COUNT:** "You watched me set a full glass down on your own staircase and
> decided I was ill."
>
> **COUNT:** "Your wife watched me refuse fruit out of her garden and knew what
> it meant inside a minute."
>
> **FERNAND:** "What?"

**Panel 4 — 18%.** The empty glass set down on the black wood; the Count's face
above it.

> **COUNT:** "It is a rule of mine. I don't eat or drink under the roof of an
> enemy."
>
> **COUNT:** "This is my roof."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just told him he was an enemy, and not why.

## PAGE 46 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the same room, continuous.

**Panel 1 — 12%.**

> **COUNT:** "Do you know the name Dantès?"

**Panel 2 — 18%.** Fernand's face, beginning.

> **COUNT:** "February, 1815. The *Pharaon.*"
>
> **COUNT:** "You could not write, so Danglars wrote it for you."
>
> **COUNT:** "You carried it to the post yourself, because you did not trust him
> to."

**Panel 3 — 18%.** Fernand on his feet, the case forgotten under his arm.

> **COUNT:** "I have been in your house. I have taken your hand on your stairs."
>
> **COUNT:** "I have had your son's arm through mine."

**Panel 4 — dominant, 46%.** The Count's face full in the frame, unmasked. No
performance left in it anywhere.

> **COUNT:** "Look at me, Fernand."
>
> **COUNT:** "I am Edmond Dantès."

**Panel 5 — 6%, narrow lower band.** The empty doorway, and the marble top where
the pistol case was set down. It is not there any more.

**Why you turn the page:** he has gone, and he took the case with him.

## PAGE 47 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Morcerf house, dusk.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The general's staircase, unlit and empty, the front door
standing open behind Fernand's back as he climbs it with the case.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 65%.** The bedroom: wardrobes standing open and emptied,
jewel cases open on the table with everything still in them, drawers pulled out,
a woman's gown left across a chair. Fernand alone in the middle of it with the
case open in his hands.

> **CAPTION**
> "His wife and son had taken nothing of his."

**Panel 3 — 15%, lower band.** The front of the house seen from the street below:
black, shuttered, dead — except one upstairs window, which for the length of this
panel is white. No figure, no caption, no balloon.

> **SOUND LABEL** *(small, third text level, set against the dark masonry beside
> the white window — not a balloon and not a caption)*
> "CRACK"

**Why you turn the page:** the house went off like a shot and every other window
in it is dark.

## PAGE 48 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the street outside the Morcerf house, night. Wet cobble,
gas-yellow, one hired carriage.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Mercédès and Albert at the carriage door with one small bag
between them. Behind them the great house, entirely dark.

> **ALBERT:** "We could have taken the plate. It was my grandmother's."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "He bought it in 1821, from a dealer in the rue Vivienne. There
> was no grandmother."

**Panel 2 — 12%.** Albert, absorbing that this is going to keep happening.

> **ALBERT:** "You know where all of it came from."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "All of it."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 55%.** Both of them stopped and half-turned back toward the
house, faces up, at the window that has gone dark again. Nothing else in the
street has changed.

> **ALBERT:** "Mother—"
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "I know."

**Panel 4 — 13%.** Her hand closed on his arm, holding him exactly where he is.

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Twenty-three years I lived in that house."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Get in the carriage, Albert."

**Why you turn the page:** she will not go in and she will not let him go in, and
the carriage is already moving.

## PAGE 49 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the street, night. Continuous.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The far pavement, deep in the dark: one black figure, alone,
who has been standing there the whole time.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 65%.** The street receding — the carriage going away from
the frame, its single lamp small and warm and getting smaller, wet cobble, gas
light, and the enormous shuttered house on the left. The last warm thing in the
volume, leaving.

> **COUNT:** "One."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Looking up past the roofline: another house, further off, one
lit window on the second floor. Somebody is still working in it.

> **CAPTION**
> "Villefort, the King's Attorney, kept late hours."

**Why you turn the page — the last one:** you have just watched him pay for it,
it did not slow him down, and he was looking at the next roof before the carriage
was out of sight.
