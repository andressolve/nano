## Page intent

## PAGE 45

Fernand arrives ruined and armed, demanding a duel. In his own house the Count
refuses, makes him sit, pours a glass, and drinks it empty—the first thing he has
swallowed in the volume. He explains that he never eats or drinks beneath an
enemy's roof, then states the decisive fact: this roof is his.

The empty glass is consequential because of the action, not because the reader
can inspect its exact design.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 45 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's house, the black room. Afternoon.

**Panel 1 — 17%.** Fernand in the doorway, hat still on, ruined, a pistol case
under his arm. The room is enormous around him.

> **FERNAND:** "My son wouldn't fire at you. Take a pistol."

**Panel 2 — 15%.** The Count seated, unhurried — still in the coat he wore to
the Bois, the wet grass of the field still on his boots. He has not been home
long enough to change.

> **COUNT:** "No. Sit down."
>
> **FERNAND:** "You've destroyed me and you won't even—"
>
> **COUNT:** "Sit down. You are going to be told why first."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** The Count pouring, in his own house, and drinking.
The glass comes down empty. It is the first thing he has swallowed in this
volume, and Fernand does not understand why it is happening.

> **COUNT:** "You watched me set a full glass down on your own staircase and
> decided I was ill."
>
> **COUNT:** "Your wife watched me refuse fruit out of her garden and knew what
> it meant inside a minute."
>
> **FERNAND:** "What?"

**Panel 4 — 18%.** The empty glass set down on the black wood; the Count's face
above it.

> **COUNT:** "It is a rule of mine. I don't eat or drink under the roof of an
> enemy."
>
> **COUNT:** "This is my roof."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just told him he was an enemy, and not why.

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
Any of the nine exact strings is missing, altered, duplicated, reordered, or
not comfortably transcribable; or the two men's speech ownership is unclear.

### C2 — Fernand's armed demand and the Count's refusal fail
Fernand does not arrive ruined with the closed case, or the Count appears to
accept the duel rather than making him sit and hear the reason first.

### C3 — The drink action fails
The dominant moment does not clearly show the Count pouring and drinking the
glass empty in his own house. Exact glass design, liquid color, swallow pose,
and tiny object state are nonblocking when the first completed drink is clear.

### C4 — The roof/enemy meaning fails
The final two lines do not land as the explanation of the earlier refusals and
the assertion that Fernand is now under the Count's roof.

### C5 — Identity or generation integrity fails materially
The Count and Fernand collide, a principal is duplicated, gross focal anatomy
or corruption disrupts the scene, or the case visibly opens/weapon is raised
and changes the event. Grass specks, décor, glass detail, and minor hands do not.
