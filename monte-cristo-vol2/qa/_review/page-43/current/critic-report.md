In the Bois, with Mercédès and a carriage waiting behind him, he publicly recounts what his father did to Edmond Dantès in 1815 for money and marriage, then declares that he has no right to quarrel with the gentleman before the witnesses. When the Count begins to answer, Albert stops him. Albert then chooses his mother's name and leaves together with Mercédès toward the carriage, removing them from the confrontation.

## Visible text
ALBERT: "My mother told me last night what my father did in 1815, to a man called Edmond Dantès."

ALBERT: "He did it for money, and he did it to marry her. The man was nineteen years old."

ALBERT: "I've no quarrel with this gentleman. I haven't the right to one."

COUNT: "Monsieur—"

ALBERT: "Don't."

ALBERT: "I'll take my mother's name. She was Mercédès Herrera before she was anything of his."
