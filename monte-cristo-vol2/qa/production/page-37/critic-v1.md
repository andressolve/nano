VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Haydée returns animated by Fernand's public humiliation and finds the Count seated before an untouched meal, withdrawn from the victory. She confronts his inability to enjoy what he wanted; the exchange turns when his concern for the twenty-two-year-old boy meets her memory of being eleven, leaving the Count with only assent.

HAYDÉE: "It is on the table in front of you and you are looking at the wall."

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: In the final panel, the "Yes." balloon sits on Haydée's side and its tail points rightward into Haydée rather than clearly reaching the Count at the far right, so the line cannot be assigned confidently to the Count.
- Material reader harm: The visible attribution makes Haydée appear to answer her own "I was eleven," obscuring the Count's assent and breaking the closing exchange.
- Redraw justification: The Count's ownership of the final word is the page's decisive dramatic action; ambiguity at that exact beat materially changes who concedes and is substantial enough to risk replacing the page.

### Finding C3
- Observation: Although the three final strings are present in order, the tail on "Yes." visually assigns or strongly associates the response with Haydée rather than the Count.
- Material reader harm: The ages no longer culminate clearly in the Count's acknowledgment, so the intended reason he cannot celebrate does not land as a completed moral exchange.
- Redraw justification: This is the page's turn-ending reader event, and losing the Count's unmistakable assent defeats that event strongly enough to warrant a full redraw.
