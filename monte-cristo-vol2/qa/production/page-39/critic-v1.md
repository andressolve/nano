VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Mercédès asks Edmond to spare her son. He answers each plea by insisting that the duel leaves him no safe alternative. She then exposes that his revenge was meant for her to witness; after she says she has seen it, he yields and agrees to stand in the field without raising his hand.

## Visible text
- Mercédès: “Don't kill my son.”
- Count: “Your son called me out in front of forty people.”
- Mercédès: “Then let him miss.”
- Count: “He will not miss. He has been shooting since he was ten.”
- Count: “His father taught him.”
- Mercédès: “Then don't fire.”
- Count: “You are asking me to stand still in a field and let a Mondego shoot me.”
- Mercédès: “You did not do this for justice, Edmond.”
- Count, by the right-pointing tail: “You did it so that I would see it.”
- Unclear ownership: “I have seen it.”
- Count: “…Very well.”
- Count: “I will stand where his second puts me, and I will not raise my hand.”

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: In the dominant fourth panel, “You did it so that I would see it.” has a tail pointing toward the Count, while “I have seen it.” has no clear speaker tail. Both lines belong to Mercédès.
- Material reader harm: The page's decisive accusation and Mercédès' declaration that she has witnessed his revenge lose clear ownership at the emotional hinge of the exchange.
- Redraw justification: Misassigning or obscuring two consecutive core lines changes who defeats whom and materially breaks the scripted surrender, which is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
