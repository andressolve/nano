VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Albert confronts Beauchamp in the newspaper office about the girl who entered the Chamber of Peers. He presses for who placed her there and where she lives. The exchange narrows to a familiar house, and Beauchamp's final answer makes Albert realize the implication.

## Visible text
- Albert: "The girl. Who is she?"
- Beauchamp: "Albert—"
- Albert: "She walked into the Chamber of Peers with a sixteen-year-old receipt and the name of a town nobody in France can find."
- Albert: "Somebody put her there."
- Visually attributed to Albert by the balloon tail: "She lives in a house on the Champs-Élysées."
- Visually attributed to Beauchamp by the balloon tail: "Whose house."
- Beauchamp: "You've dined in it."

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: In Panel 3, the tail for "She lives in a house on the Champs-Élysées." points down-left toward Albert, while the tail for "Whose house." points right toward Beauchamp. The two balloons are therefore visibly assigned to the opposite speakers from the exact script.
- Material reader harm: The reversal makes Albert appear to supply the address and Beauchamp ask whose house it is, breaking the interrogation's ownership and obscuring that Beauchamp reluctantly gives Albert the clue.
- Redraw justification: This is a material attribution failure in the pivotal deduction exchange, not a cosmetic tail issue; leaving it would invert who knows and who asks at the moment that sets up Albert's comprehension.
