He had come out to the Bois to be shot at.
He had spent the night before putting
his affairs in order so that a boy of
twenty-two could kill him without
inconveniencing anybody, and
he had meant it.

Nobody fired. He stood on
the wet grass a long while
afterwards working out what
he felt, which took longer than
he expected, and what he
arrived at was not relief.

carriage reached the gate.
The son had let him go.
The father had not, and
would be at his door
before dark.
