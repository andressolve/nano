## Reader read

The conversation is effortless to follow, the case handoff reads clearly, and Haydée's discovery of the Count's timing lands. The page's visual hierarchy does not fully serve that turn: the bright monumental façade at the top competes with the central handoff instead of leaving Panel 3 as the one unmistakable dominant field. The final split also stops a beat early because Haydée has not yet begun climbing.

## Mandatory defects

1. **Page hierarchy:** The large, bright façade establishment in Panel 1 competes with—and at a glance can own the page over—the Panel 3 case transfer. Panel 3 must be the unmistakable single dominant field carrying the handoff and the Count's timing reveal; the establishing view may not compete with that turn.
2. **Panel 5 stair action:** Haydée remains on the pavement in front of the building. She must already be climbing the great front steps with the case under her arm while the Count stays off those steps and turns toward the separate flank stair.
