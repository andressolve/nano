VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Mercédès arrives alone at night and confronts the seated Count. She refuses the social title, describes the isolation she has endured since the newspaper, then crosses the room's immense distance by calling him Edmond. His persona breaks as he admits that nobody has used the name in twenty-three years; she answers that she has said it every day. Mercédès owns the confrontation and the final recognition changes it from formal accusation to intimate shared history.

## Visible text
Mercédès (balloon tail): "Madame la Comtesse. At this hour."

Mercédès: "Don't."

Mercédès: "I have sat in that house since the newspaper, while men I have known twenty years found reasons not to look at me."

Mercédès: "I have not once had to wonder who was doing it."

Mercédès: "Edmond."

Count: "Nobody has said that name to me in twenty-three years."

Mercédès: "I have said it every day."

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: In Panel 1, the balloon containing "Madame la Comtesse. At this hour." has its tail aimed left toward Mercédès rather than right toward the Count, visibly assigning the Count's line to Mercédès.
- Material reader harm: The page opens as though Mercédès addresses herself by her own title before saying "Don't," so the intended formal greeting and her immediate rejection of it are not clearly exchanged between the two speakers.
- Redraw justification: This is an exact ownership failure in the opening confrontation, and it materially obscures the social-title break that establishes the page's dramatic movement; correcting that attribution warrants replacing the page.
