VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Mercédès arrives alone at night and refuses the Count's formal address. Standing while he remains seated, she confronts him over the deliberate ruin surrounding her household. The vast empty room isolates her without diminishing her resolve. In the final close confrontation, she calls him Edmond; his public persona breaks, and their shared twenty-three-year history becomes intimate.

## Visible text
- COUNT: "Madame la Comtesse. At this hour."
- MERCÉDÈS: "Don't."
- MERCÉDÈS: "I have sat in that house since the newspaper, while men I have known twenty years found reasons not to look at me."
- COUNT, by visible balloon tail: "I have not once had to wonder who was doing it."
- MERCÉDÈS: "Edmond."
- COUNT: "Nobody has said that name to me in twenty-three years."
- MERCÉDÈS: "I have said it every day."

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: In Panel 2, the balloon containing "I have not once had to wonder who was doing it." is placed beside the Count and its tail points to him, visibly assigning Mercédès' second accusation to the Count.
- Material reader harm: The ownership error turns Mercédès' uninterrupted statement of certainty into a reply from the Count, changing the confrontation's meaning and weakening her control of the scene immediately before the name reveal.
- Redraw justification: This is one of the seven exact scripted strings and its visible speaker attribution is materially wrong; correcting that core dialogue ownership warrants replacing the page.
