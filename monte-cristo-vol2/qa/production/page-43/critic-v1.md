## Blind read
Albert confronts the older man in the Bois while Mercédès waits near a carriage. He recounts what his father did to Edmond Dantès for money and marriage, cuts off the older man's attempted reply, then leaves with his mother and rejects his father's name in favor of hers. Albert owns the page through that repudiation and choice; the departing mother and son own the final image. A separate panel of motionless witnesses contains no visible dialogue or action that explains a further change.

## Visible text
ALBERT: "My mother told me last night what my father did in 1815, to a man called Edmond Dantès."

ALBERT: "He did it for money, and he did it to marry her. The man was nineteen years old."

COUNT: "Monsieur—"

ALBERT: "Don't."

ALBERT: "I'll take my mother's name. She was Mercédès Herrera before she was anything of his."

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The required Albert line "I've no quarrel with this gentleman. I haven't the right to one." is entirely absent from the otherwise silent witnesses panel.
- Material reader harm: One of the six exact strings is missing, so Albert never explicitly gives up the quarrel or states that he lacks the right to pursue it.
- Redraw justification: This omission removes the decisive withdrawal that connects Albert's moral account to his interruption of the Count and departure; restoring that complete scripted beat is substantial enough to risk replacing the page.

### Finding C2
- Observation: Albert states the 1815 betrayal, money, and marriage, but never states that he has no quarrel and no right to one.
- Material reader harm: The visible sequence reads as accusation followed by cutting off the Count and leaving, rather than making Albert's moral account clearly function as the reason he withdraws from the quarrel.
- Redraw justification: Albert's moral choice is the page's owner-controlled event, and the missing causal declaration materially weakens that event enough to justify a complete redraw.
