## Blind read
Albert publicly recounts what his father did to Edmond Dantès, renounces his quarrel with the Count, stops the Count's attempted response, and chooses his mother's name. Albert owns the moral decision; he and Mercédès then walk away together, leaving the Count behind.

## Visible text
Albert: "My mother told me last night what my father did in 1815, to a man called Edmond Dantès."

Albert: "He did it for money, and he did it to marry her. The man was nineteen years old."

Albert: "I've no quarrel with this gentleman. I haven't the right to one."

Count: "Monsieur—"

Albert: "Don't."

Albert: "I'll take my mother's name. She was Mercédès Herrera before she was anything of his."

## Findings

### Finding C4
- Observation: In the final image, Albert and Mercédès walk away into the open path while the carriage is visibly behind them at far left; their direction is away from it rather than toward it.
- Material reader harm: The dominant departure does not show the specified exit toward the waiting carriage, so the page's final consequential action reads as the pair abandoning the carriage and walking into the woods rather than completing their removal from the field by carriage.
- Redraw justification: The contradiction occupies the page's concluding image and changes the visible destination of the departure, making it a dominant story-action failure substantial enough to replace the page.
