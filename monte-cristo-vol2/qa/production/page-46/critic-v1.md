VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
The seated Count methodically reconstructs Fernand's 1815 betrayal, moving from the name Dantès through the letter and later encounters until he orders Fernand to look at him and reveals himself full-faced as Edmond Dantès. The Count owns the page and changes the encounter from interrogation into unmasking; the final empty doorway and cleared room show that Fernand has departed with the case.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The balloons reading "February, 1815. The Pharaon." and "You carried it to the post yourself, because you did not trust him to." sit beside Fernand and have tails pointing left toward him, so two lines assigned exclusively to the Count visibly appear to be Fernand's dialogue.
- Material reader harm: The misattribution breaks the Count's uninterrupted reconstruction of Fernand's betrayal and makes Fernand appear to supply incriminating facts himself, materially changing both ownership and dramatic logic of the exchange.
- Redraw justification: Correct speaker ownership is a blocking requirement for every line, and these two visibly directed balloons alter the central accusation rather than a cosmetic detail; that core reader harm is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
