VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Albert challenges Mercédès about leaving the household plate behind, and she reveals that its supposed family history was invented and that she knew the origin of everything in the house. They pause and look in different directions; Albert appeals to her, and she answers that she knows. Mercédès owns the page: she closes her hand on Albert's arm, states the cost of her twenty-three years in the house, and decisively orders him into the carriage, changing the exchange from disclosure to departure under her protection.

## Visible text
ALBERT: "We could have taken the plate. It was my grandmother's."

MERCÉDÈS: "He bought it in 1821, from a dealer in the rue Vivienne. There was no grandmother."

ALBERT: "You know where all of it came from."

MERCÉDÈS: "All of it."

ALBERT: "Mother—"

MERCÉDÈS: "I know."

MERCÉDÈS: "Twenty-three years I lived in that house."

MERCÉDÈS: "Get in the carriage, Albert."

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: In Panel 3, Mercédès turns up and right toward the house while Albert turns left toward the carriage/driver side; they do not visibly share a turn toward the same dark window. On a blind read, `Mother—` / `I know.` continues naturally from her admission that she knew where the possessions came from, so no newly heard off-panel shot is apparent.
- Material reader harm: The page's pivotal event—the two of them immediately hearing and understanding the shot—does not land. The exchange reads as a pause in the provenance argument before Mercédès orders their departure, removing the moment that makes her refusal to return an active response to Fernand's death.
- Redraw justification: This is the central dramatic turn and the causal bridge into Mercédès holding Albert outside. Because the principals visibly orient in opposite directions and the dialogue has a complete alternate meaning, the missing event cannot be recovered from the finished page without replacing it.
