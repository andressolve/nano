VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Outside the Morcerf house at night, Albert suggests they could have taken the family plate, but Mercédès reveals that the supposed inheritance was purchased and that there was no grandmother. Albert realizes she knows the origin of everything. Mercédès owns the page: she discloses that she lived in the house for twenty-three years, keeps hold of Albert, and orders him into the carriage, changing the moment from his questioning into her decisive departure.

## Visible text
Albert: “We could have taken the plate. It was my grandmother’s.”

Mercédès: “He bought it in 1821, from a dealer in the rue Vivienne. There was no grandmother.”

Albert: “You know where all of it came from.”

Mercédès: “All of it.”

Albert: “Mother—”

Mercédès: “I know.”

Mercédès: “Twenty-three years I lived in that house.”

Mercédès: “Get in the carriage, Albert.”

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: The `Mother—` / `I know.` exchange occurs while Albert and Mercédès face one another in the second panel; their shared turn toward the dark house occurs only in the following panel, paired with Mercédès’s statement that she lived there for twenty-three years. No visible cue connects the turn to a newly heard off-panel event.
- Material reader harm: The page reads as a revelation about the possessions and Mercédès’s history with the house, not as Albert and Mercédès immediately hearing and understanding the shot. The central off-panel death event is therefore absent from the reader’s understanding of the scene.
- Redraw justification: Hearing and silently comprehending the shot is the page’s dominant dramatic relationship and the cause of Mercédès preventing Albert’s return. Because that causal event does not land in a blind read, the intended final refusal and protection lose their essential stakes, which is substantial enough to replace the complete page.
