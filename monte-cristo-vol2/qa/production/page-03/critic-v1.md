## Mandatory defects

1. **Page architecture — no panel reaches the required dominant range.** The intended dominant fourth panel begins at approximately 58% of the page height and occupies only the bottom approximately 42%, below the mandatory 45–70% range. The three upper bands collectively outweigh the page’s turn instead of leaving one compliant dominant panel.
2. **Dominant turn — the Count’s raised hand reads as authority, not failure.** In the fourth panel the large, sharply rendered foreground palm is rigidly vertical with widely spread fingers, producing a forceful stop command that visually dominates the small distant Haydée. It does not read as a poised stopping hand whose authority is failing against her, so the appendix’s required reversal is not carried by the image.

- **The pike and head are spoken, never depicted:** Pass. No head, pike, blood, flashback, shadow shape, wall image, or window silhouette visualises the atrocity.
- **The Count’s raised right hand must fail:** REVISE. Its rigid, sharply focused open-palm posture and visual scale make it command the fourth panel rather than visibly fail against Haydée.
- **No tail on the hand; `Haydée.` first:** Pass. `Haydée.` is the highest element in the fourth panel and reads before Haydée’s two balloons. Its tail runs into the Count’s dark shoulder edge and remains clear of the hand.
- **Haydée at the edge of the light in the Epirote silhouette:** Pass. At every scale she remains the approved 27-year-old identity, with unbound dark hair and a loose crimson-and-gold vertical costume rather than a French waist, coiffure, or shawl.
- **Panel 1 text field judged by transcription:** Pass. The first balloon is comfortably readable in the desktop proof; the blind transcription was exact.

## Reader response

The first three beats read clearly: Haydée sets the cup down, the Count turns in annoyance, and she quietly exposes the flaw in his priorities. The final exchange is also easy to follow, and the spoken image of Janina lands without being gratuitously pictured. But the upper bands take too much page area from the intended dominant turn, while the foreground hand remains the strongest visual assertion in that turn. Instead of Haydée’s stillness defeating the Count’s habitual authority, the composition leaves his stop gesture in command. I would turn the page for her accusation, but the page has not yet rendered the reversal its script requires.
