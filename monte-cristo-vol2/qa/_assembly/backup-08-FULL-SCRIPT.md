# Full Script — 48 pages

Paris, 1838. **Round 5**, after script-gate rounds 1, 2, 3 and 4.

**Changes from round 4.** Round 4 returned three findings and asked for no page
split, no added page, no cut episode and no restaging.

**Page 16 is dated on the page.** It steps back to the night of the party after
two pages in the Count's house at dawn and in the morning; it now opens with an
orientation caption in the same register page 40 uses. **Page 28's last panel
cuts back to Paris** — the printed instalment in the Count's own hands, rhyming
page 25's first panel — which ends the volume's only three-page Count-absent run
and puts Beauchamp's honest eleven days into the hand that aimed him.
**Page 37 no longer says *three weeks***; Movement V is thirty continuous hours
and the ruin is two days old, so the line dates itself from the newspaper
instead.

Also: page 20's match cut goes 15% → 20% (panel 1 55%) so the frame that holds
both worlds has room; page 46's caption names its antecedent; page 7 says *the
general's son*; the second *eleven days* on page 29 becomes eight; page 43's
decanter is set down by his own hand, because he has no servants.

**How to read this document.** Each page is written as a scene first. The spec
under it is three lines and no more: mode, location, and why the reader turns the
page. Panel notes are staging, not metadata. Panel shares sum to 100 on every
page and the dominant panel is always 45–70%.

**Text discipline.** Every string between quotation marks in a `BALLOON`,
`PROSE`, `CAPTION`, or printed-object line renders exactly as written. Nothing
else in this document renders.

**Changes from round 3**, in one place so no later gate has to reconstruct them.
Round 3 cleared the register — no logistics dialogue, no aphorism dialogue,
nothing inverted — and returned six findings, all structural.

**Janina is now stated to be in Greece the first time the word is used** (3), and
Fernand's own eastern history is bound to it on 5. Page 8's needle — *"In
Spain?" / "In Greece."* — no longer asks the reader to supply the geography that
makes it a wound.

**The Count is no longer off-page through his own mechanism.** Page 20's last
panel is a match cut from the burning pavilion to his two hands flat on the
Paris table, so he is *in* the Janina triptych being fed it. Page 25 now opens on
the same edition in his hands at dawn before it reaches the Morcerf breakfast
tray — the sheet of newsprint is shown in the hand that caused it and the hand it
destroys, on one page.

**New page 43** — illustrated prose, the aftermath of the duel. He was spared,
he works out what he feels, and he decides before the carriage reaches the city
gate that it changes nothing. Every other major beat in this volume gets its
aftermath page; the protagonist's was the one denied it, and it is the beat the
verdict on page 48 is measured against.

Also: page 29 now carries its own orientation caption and names *the President
of the Chamber* on the page that needs it, so page 30 opens on Fernand at the
bar instead of on exposition · Haydée's antithesis on 22 is flattened into her
own literal register · the Clerk is folded into the President (32), removing an
unlocked face · Haydée is twenty-seven, not twenty, and the Count *found and
bought her back* years after the market rather than standing in it · every
speech balloon is at or under 24 words and no panel under 18% of its page
carries more than 20 words. **Nothing was fixed by cutting causal language.**

# MOVEMENT I — THE INVITATION · pp 1–7

---

## PAGE 1 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Count's house, 30 Champs-Élysées — first-floor window, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Interior, from behind: an enormous unlit room with
almost nothing in it, and one tall uncurtained window. A man in black stands at
the glass, small in the frame. Beyond him, Paris at night — and three roofs close
enough to count.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper third, over the dark wall, never over the window)*
> "Nine years after a shipowner in Marseille walked down to the harbour and
> found a ship he had lost sitting at anchor, a stranger bought the house at
> number thirty, Champs-Élysées.
>
> He paid in gold, he paid at once, and he furnished it the way a man furnishes
> a room he does not mean to live in. Within a month Paris had decided he was
> the most interesting person in France. Nobody could say where the money came
> from. Nobody asked twice."

**Panel 2 — 35%, lower band.** The three roofs alone in a wide horizontal strip:
one with a copper gutter, one with a flagpole, one with every window lit.

> **PROSE FIELD 2**
> "From that window he could see three roofs.
>
> He had chosen the house for that."

**Why you turn the page:** three roofs, and a sentence that says he picked the
house to look at them, and no reason given yet.

## PAGE 2 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the same room.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** The Count at the window, back to the room, one hand
flat on the glass. Far behind him in the dark, HAYDÉE sits with a cup she is not
drinking from.

> **COUNT:** "The copper gutter is the banker. The slate roof with the flagpole
> is the King's Attorney."

**Panel 2 — 15%.** His hand on the glass; through it, the third house, every
window burning.

> **COUNT:** "And that one entertains. Every night, all season. He is very
> anxious to be liked."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Haydée, watching him and not the window.

> **HAYDÉE:** "Which one first?"

**Panel 4 — 15%.** The Count three-quarters, lit from outside. Pleasure in the
face — not calm. A man reading a menu.

> **COUNT:** "The banker. A man who lives on credit comes apart in one season,
> and quietly."

**Why you turn the page:** he has three enemies inside a single window frame and
he has already decided which one he opens first.

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the same room.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Haydée sets the cup down and stands. She does not come closer.

> **HAYDÉE:** "Then take him apart in the spring."

**Panel 2 — 12%.** The Count, half-turned. He was enjoying himself and has been
interrupted.

> **COUNT:** "You are not listening to me."

**Panel 3 — 12%.**

> **HAYDÉE:** "I listened. You said the banker was simple. You did not say he

**Panel 4 — dominant, 56%.** Haydée full-figure against the dark room, small and

>
> **HAYDÉE:** "In Greece, in a lake town called Janina, they carried my

>

> this room. And you want to begin with a bank."

**Why you turn the page:** she has put a head on a pike into a drawing room and
he has not answered.

# MOVEMENT II — THE HOUSE OF MORCERF · pp 8–17

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the general's staircase.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 48%.** Two men climbing a wide gilt staircase side by side.
FERNAND — heavy, upright, iron-black moustache, decorations across his chest —
one step ahead and enjoying being ahead. The Count beside him, black, taller,
unhurried.

> **FERNAND:** "A month in Paris, and everyone has had you at their table but
> me."
>
> **COUNT:** "I wanted to see the house first. I walked past it four times."

**Panel 2 — 8%.** Fernand half-turned, flattered and not sure why.

> **FERNAND:** "Four times. Why?"

**Panel 3 — 18%.** The Count's hand on the banister rail — the poised hand.

> **COUNT:** "I like to know what a man built before I take his hand. You have a
> general's staircase."
>
> **FERNAND:** "I earned it."

**Panel 4 — 8%.** Tight on the Count. Nothing on his face at all.

> **COUNT:** "In Spain?"
>
> **FERNAND:** "In Greece."

**Panel 5 — 18%, wide lower band.** A raised glass, and the Count's hand setting
an identical glass down on a marble top, still full. Fernand's toast going on
above it, unnoticed.

> **COUNT:** "Of course. Greece."
>
> **COUNT:** "Forgive me. I am a stranger here and I get your wars wrong."

**Why you turn the page:** he made him say it out loud, and nobody in the house
noticed the glass.

# MOVEMENT III — WHAT HAPPENED AT JANINA · pp 18–24

---

## PAGE 18 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's house. A small table, two chairs, one lamp.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Haydée sitting down opposite him, deliberately, with a flat
leather case in her hands.

> **HAYDÉE:** "You have kept me four years and asked me for nothing. I have had
> this for all four of them."

**Panel 2 — 15%.**

> **COUNT:** "You never showed me."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "No. I thought you would take it."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 55%.** The document flat on the table between them under

>

>

yet.

## PAGE 21 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the slave market, Constantinople.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Heat-glare, dust, bleached white walls, indigo shadow. A wide

**Panel 2 — dominant, 65%.** A child's hand and a woman's hand coming apart. That

**Panel 3 — 15%.** A ledger on a table, a wax seal, and coins being counted, seen

grass toward the carriage, backs to frame, small under a wide dawn sky. The Count
is not in the panel at all.

> **ALBERT:** "I'll take my mother's name. She was Mercédès Herrera before she
> was anything of his."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy is out of it and the father is still alive.

## PAGE 43 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Bois, and then the road back into the city.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** The Count alone in the middle of the wet field,
very small under a wide pale sky. The carriages are already going: Albert's and
his mother's out through the trees, the seconds' the other way. Nobody is
looking at him. He has not moved from where he was told to stand.

> **PROSE FIELD 1**
> "He had come out to the Bois to be shot at. He had spent the night before
> putting his affairs in order so that a boy of twenty-two could kill him
> without inconveniencing anybody, and he had meant it.
>
> Nobody fired. He stood on the wet grass a long while afterwards working out
> what he felt, which took longer than he expected, and what he arrived at was
> not relief."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The inside of his own carriage going back into Paris, his
face at the window, the grey suburbs going past. He looks like a man doing
arithmetic.

> **PROSE FIELD 2**
> "At three that morning he had thought that if he were let off he would stop.
>
> He was wrong about that, and he knew it before the carriage reached the gate.
> The son had let him go. The father had not, and would be at his door before
> dark."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** The black room. His own hand setting a decanter and one glass
down on the table, squared to the edge, and going away again. Nobody has drunk
anything.

**Why you turn the page:** he was spared, he has decided it changes nothing, and
he has already put out a glass.

# MOVEMENT VI — THE COST · pp 44–48

## PAGE 44 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's house, the black room. Afternoon.

**Panel 1 — 17%.** Fernand in the doorway, hat still on, ruined, a pistol case
under his arm. The room is enormous around him.

> **FERNAND:** "My son wouldn't fire at you. Take a pistol."

**Panel 2 — 15%.** The Count seated, unhurried — still in the coat he wore to
the Bois, the wet grass of the field still on his boots. He has not been home
long enough to change.

> **COUNT:** "No. Sit down."
>
> **FERNAND:** "You've destroyed me and you won't even—"
>
> **COUNT:** "Sit down. You are going to be told why first."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** The Count pouring, in his own house, and drinking.
The glass comes down empty. It is the first thing he has swallowed in this
volume, and Fernand does not understand why it is happening.

> **COUNT:** "You watched me set a full glass down on your own staircase and
> decided I was ill."
>
> **COUNT:** "Your wife watched me refuse fruit out of her garden and knew what
> it meant inside a minute."
>
> **FERNAND:** "What?"

**Panel 4 — 18%.** The empty glass set down on the black wood; the Count's face
above it.

> **COUNT:** "It is a rule of mine. I don't eat or drink under the roof of an
> enemy."
>
> **COUNT:** "This is my roof."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just told him he was an enemy, and not why.

## PAGE 45 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the same room, continuous.

**Panel 1 — 12%.**

> **COUNT:** "Do you know the name Dantès?"

**Panel 2 — 18%.** Fernand's face, beginning.

> **COUNT:** "February, 1815. The *Pharaon.*"
>
> **COUNT:** "You could not write, so Danglars wrote it for you."
>
> **COUNT:** "You carried it to the post yourself, because you did not trust him

**Panel 3 — 18%.** Fernand on his feet, the case forgotten under his arm.

> **COUNT:** "I have been in your house. I have taken your hand on your stairs."

> **COUNT:** "I have had your son's arm through mine."

> **COUNT:** "Look at me, Fernand."
>
> **COUNT:** "I am Edmond Dantès."

**Panel 5 — 6%, narrow lower band.** The empty doorway, and the marble top where

**Why you turn the page:** he has gone, and he took the case with him.

## PAGE 46 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Morcerf house, dusk.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The general's staircase, unlit and empty, the front door
standing open behind Fernand's back as he climbs it with the case.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 65%.** The bedroom: wardrobes standing open and emptied,
jewel cases open on the table with everything still in them, drawers pulled out,
a woman's gown left across a chair. Fernand alone in the middle of it with the
case open in his hands.

> **CAPTION**
> "His wife and son had taken nothing of his."

**Panel 3 — 15%, lower band.** The front of the house seen from the street below:
black, shuttered, dead — except one upstairs window, which for the length of this
panel is white. No figure, no caption, no balloon.

> **SOUND LABEL** *(small, third text level, set against the dark masonry beside
> the white window — not a balloon and not a caption)*
> "CRACK"

**Why you turn the page:** the house went off like a shot and every other window
in it is dark.

## PAGE 47 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the street outside the Morcerf house, night. Wet cobble,
gas-yellow, one hired carriage.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Mercédès and Albert at the carriage door with one small bag
between them. Behind them the great house, entirely dark.

> **ALBERT:** "We could have taken the plate. It was my grandmother's."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "He bought it in 1821, from a dealer in the rue Vivienne. There
> was no grandmother."

**Panel 2 — 12%.** Albert, absorbing that this is going to keep happening.

> **ALBERT:** "You know where all of it came from."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "All of it."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 55%.** Both of them stopped and half-turned back toward the
house, faces up, at the window that has gone dark again. Nothing else in the
street has changed.

> **ALBERT:** "Mother—"
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "I know."

**Panel 4 — 13%.** Her hand closed on his arm, holding him exactly where he is.

> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Twenty-three years I lived in that house."
>
> **MERCÉDÈS:** "Get in the carriage, Albert."

**Why you turn the page:** she will not go in and she will not let him go in, and
the carriage is already moving.

## PAGE 48 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the street, night. Continuous.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The far pavement, deep in the dark: one black figure, alone,
who has been standing there the whole time.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 65%.** The street receding — the carriage going away from
the frame, its single lamp small and warm and getting smaller, wet cobble, gas
light, and the enormous shuttered house on the left. The last warm thing in the
volume, leaving.

> **COUNT:** "One."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Looking up past the roofline: another house, further off, one
lit window on the second floor. Somebody is still working in it.

> **CAPTION**
> "Villefort, the King's Attorney, kept late hours."

**Why you turn the page — the last one:** you have just watched him pay for it,
it did not slow him down, and he was looking at the next roof before the carriage
was out of sight.

> **Edmond Dantès destroyed the man who took everything from him, and it worked.
