# Page 32 pilot — page intent

## Reader event

At the sound of the great door, the Chamber turns as one. Haydée appears alone
in the doorway and, without speaking or acting theatrically, takes possession of
the room. The reader knows who she is; the assembled men do not. Fernand, at the
far end, suddenly belongs less in his own world than she does.

## What must land

- The first image is a collective reaction, not a collection of unrelated faces.
- The reveal is one solitary woman facing a vast, visibly occupied chamber.
- The room may dwarf Haydée physically, but it must not diminish her dramatic
  authority.
- Haydée's crimson-and-gold presence should feel naturally at home against the
  crimson chamber, while Fernand feels isolated and subordinate at the far end.
- The page is silent spectacle: no words, balloons, captions, labels, signatures,
  or page number.

## What this page is not about

This page is not about documentary evidence or a wax seal. A document's
presence, absence, or recognizability—and a seal's visibility—has no bearing on
whether Page 32 works. If a prop happens to appear, any actual readable words on
it still fall under the page's zero-text rule. The evidence becomes reader-facing
on Page 33.

Exact percentages, coordinates, prop placement, and literal compliance with
generation-prompt adjectives are not the page intent.
