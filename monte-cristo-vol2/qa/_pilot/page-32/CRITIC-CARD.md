## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 32 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Chamber of Peers.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Three hundred faces, all turned the same way, at the sound of
a door.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 70%.** The great door at the back of the hall standing
open, and Haydée in it: small, alone, in crimson-and-gold Epirote embroidery,
unbound black hair, a loose vertical silhouette in a room of French tailoring.
The dress rhymes with the crimson benches. She looks as though the room was built
for her — and Fernand, tiny at the far end, does not.

**Why you turn the page:** you already know who she is and nobody in that room
does.

## Numbered blocking criteria

A `REVISE` verdict is valid only for one or more of these:

### C1 — The page event does not read

The two images fail to communicate one connected event: the chamber reacts to
the door, then Haydée is revealed. A reader cannot tell what changed, or another
event becomes the apparent subject.

### C2 — The chamber relationship is wrong

The upper crowd does not read as a collective reaction, or the lower reveal
reads as an empty room rather than the same occupied public chamber. Background
faces need not share identical eye angles; the blocking question is whether the
room responds collectively and remains present around the reveal.

### C3 — Haydée does not carry the reveal

Haydée is absent, duplicated, accompanied in the doorway, unrecognizable, or
visually diminished as a dramatic presence. The architecture may make her
physically small, but the page must still belong to her. Exact scale, centering,
lighting, pose, and coordinates are not gates.

### C4 — Fernand or the Count changes the story

Fernand is absent from the far-end relationship, becomes a foreground co-star,
or is confused with the Count; or the Count appears anywhere on the page.
Fernand need not be facially detailed at that distance, but he must register as
the isolated, subordinate man at the other end of the chamber.

### C5 — The silent page contains readable text

Any word, caption, balloon, label, signature, sound effect, or page number is
legible from the 600 × 900 proof. Unreadable incidental texture is not text.

### C6 — Consequential generation failure

A duplicated person, gross anatomical break, corrupted image, wrong canvas, or
identity collision materially disrupts reading the page. Minor background-hand
or crowd-face imperfections that do not attract attention or alter the event
are nonblocking.

## Explicitly nonblocking

Do not reject for:

- the presence, absence, visibility, color, or recognizability of a document or
  seal; any actually readable word still falls under `C5`;
- literal prompt compliance or any detail remembered from a generation prompt;
- exact panel percentages, coordinates, scale, or measured geometry;
- whether every background face points at precisely the same angle, provided
  the chamber's collective turn reads immediately;
- exact hue matching, provided Haydée feels naturally at home in the chamber
  and Fernand feels displaced;
- tiny facial or decorative detail on distant Fernand;
- cosmetic finish or minor background generation artifacts that do not impair
  the story event.

If the page communicates the intent, preserves the exact silent script, and
passes `C1`–`C6`, the verdict is `APPROVED` even when the image differs from
generation instructions the critic has not seen.
