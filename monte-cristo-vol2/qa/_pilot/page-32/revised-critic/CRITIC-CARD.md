# Page 32 — revised script and critic card

Read only after completing the blind image read.

## Page intent

At the sound of the great door, the Chamber turns as one. Haydée appears alone
and, without speaking or theatrical action, takes possession of the occupied
room. The reader knows her; the assembled men do not. Fernand, at the far end,
is diminished inside his own world.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 32 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Chamber of Peers.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Three hundred faces, all turned the same way, at the sound of
a door.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 70%.** The great door at the back of the hall standing
open, and Haydée in it: small, alone, in crimson-and-gold Epirote embroidery,
unbound black hair, a loose vertical silhouette in a room of French tailoring.
The dress rhymes with the crimson benches. She looks as though the room was built
for her — and Fernand, tiny at the far end, does not.

**Why you turn the page:** you already know who she is and nobody in that room
does.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a reader-facing release gate, not a defect collector. A visible
problem blocks only when it materially damages the story event, exact text,
attribution, named/focal identity, consequential continuity, focal anatomy, or
dominant dramatic relationship.

Every correction replaces the complete page and risks destroying successful
story, text, identity, and composition. Do not demand that risk for a minor
background, cosmetic, or tiny-prop flaw. A technically correct observation may
still be nonblocking.

## Numbered blocking criteria

### C1 — The connected page event fails

The two images do not communicate one event: the Chamber reacts to the door,
then Haydée is revealed; or another event becomes the apparent subject.

### C2 — The chamber relationship fails

The upper crowd does not read collectively, or the lower reveal reads as an
empty room rather than the same occupied public chamber.

### C3 — Haydée does not carry the reveal

Haydée is absent, duplicated, accompanied in the doorway, unrecognizable, or
dramatically diminished. Exact scale, centering, light, and pose are not gates.

### C4 — Fernand or the Count changes the story

Fernand is absent from the far-end relationship, becomes a foreground co-star,
or is confused with the Count; or the Count appears on the page.

### C5 — The silent page contains readable text

Any word, balloon, caption, label, signature, sound effect, or page number is
legible from the 600 × 900 proof. Incidental unreadable texture is not text.

### C6 — Consequential generation failure

A duplicate named/focal character, gross focal anatomical break, corruption,
wrong canvas, or material identity collision disrupts reading the page.
Anonymous background repetition blocks only if it creates a grotesque
attention-dominating pattern, changes who is in the scene, or prevents the crowd
from reading as a collective Chamber. Similar or repeated anonymous crowd faces
are otherwise nonblocking, even when visible.

## Explicitly nonblocking

- Presence, absence, visibility, color, or recognizability of a document or
  seal; actual readable words still fall under `C5`.
- Similar or repeated anonymous background crowd faces that do not change the
  event or dominate the page.
- Minor background anatomy, hands, faces, texture, or finish.
- Prompt compliance, exact percentages, coordinates, scale, measured geometry,
  hue matching, or distant decorative detail.
- Any true observation that lacks material reader harm substantial enough to
  justify replacing the full page.

If the reader event lands and `C1`–`C6` pass at this threshold, return
`APPROVED` and omit minor observations.
