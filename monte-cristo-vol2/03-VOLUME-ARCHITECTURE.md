# Volume Architecture — 49 pages, six movements

Paris, 1838. Nine years after the *Pharaon* came home.

---

## I — THE INVITATION · pp 1–7

**Emotional entry:** anticipation. **Exit:** a door opening on the enemy's house.

The Count is in Paris and his three men live within sight of his windows. He
means to begin with Danglars, because Danglars is easy and quiet. Haydée
overrules him, and in overruling him she names the real reason he was leaving
Morcerf for later: he is afraid of Fernand's wife. He concedes. Fernand — who
collects fashionable people — invites him to dinner.

**The turn:** Haydée changes the order. Morcerf first.
**Page-turn out:** across a crowded room, Mercédès has stopped moving.

## II — THE HOUSE OF MORCERF · pp 8–17

**Emotional entry:** appetite. **Exit:** the first crack in it, papered over.

He takes Fernand's hand on Fernand's own staircase and makes him say *Greece*
out loud. He touches Danglars and Villefort in passing and none of the three
knows him. Then two things go wrong. Albert is a delight — warm, generous,
proud of his father — and the Count likes him. And Mercédès looks at him across
her own drawing room and knows exactly who he is, and neither of them says so.
The movement closes with him offered a way out and refusing it out loud: he
does not want Fernand dead, he wants to be at the table when it happens.

**The turn:** Mercédès recognizes him — and he hardens rather than stop.
**Page-turn out:** he has now said what he actually wants.

## III — WHAT HAPPENED AT JANINA · pp 18–25

**Emotional entry:** cold purpose. **Exit:** a loaded gun in another man's hand.

Haydée tells him what she can prove, and the volume goes to Janina in 1822 to
show it: a fortress sold, a pass opened, a head delivered, a widow and a child
sold in a market. Then the mechanism. The Count does not accuse anyone. He goes
to Danglars and needles his vanity until Danglars decides, entirely by himself,
to write to Janina and check a fellow peer's credit. The reply is the weapon.
Danglars, who does not know what he has, sells it to a newspaper.

**The turn:** Danglars loads the gun that kills his own co-conspirator, for
free, out of vanity.
**Page-turn out:** it is in print.

## IV — THE FALL · pp 26–34

**Emotional entry:** dread. **Exit:** a man destroyed in public.

Albert reads the paragraph and goes to Beauchamp, who agrees to verify it
honestly — and does. Fernand and Mercédès face each other, and he discovers
that his wife has known what he is for twenty-three years and simply never said
it. He demands to be heard by the Chamber of Peers, certain he can win. He
almost does; give him a real defense and let him nearly get away with it. On
the morning itself the Count arms Haydée in a carriage outside the building,
sends her up the front steps, and takes the public stair to the gallery
himself. Then the door at the back of the hall opens — and he is above it,
leaning out over the rail, watching a killing he paid for.

**The turn:** Haydée testifies. Fernand is finished.
**Page-turn out:** the boy is going to want to know who did this.

## V — THE SON · pp 35–44

**Emotional entry:** triumph. **Exit:** the Count has agreed to lose.

Albert traces it back and challenges the Count publicly. Haydée is exultant;
the Count is not, because winning now means killing Mercédès' son. Mercédès
comes to his house and says *Edmond* out loud for the first time in
twenty-three years, and asks him for her son's life. He refuses — until she
names the thing he has not admitted to himself. He agrees to stand still and
let the boy kill him. Four hours before the duel she tells Albert everything,
on the page, and the reader watches him receive it. On the ground the next
morning, Albert apologizes publicly instead, and gives up his father's name in
front of witnesses.

Nobody fires. The movement closes on a prose page: the Count alone in the wet
field afterwards, working out what he feels, and deciding before the carriage
reaches the city gate that being spared changes nothing.

**The turn:** Mercédès names him, and he agrees to die — and then is not made to.
**Page-turn out:** the son let him go, the father will not, and he knows it.

## VI — THE COST · pp 45–49

**Emotional entry:** exposure. **Exit:** unease.

Fernand comes to demand satisfaction. In his own house the Count pours and
drinks in front of him — the first thing he has swallowed in the volume — tells
him what the two refusals meant, and then takes the persona off: face, name,
and all. Fernand rides home to a house where the
wardrobes are open and the jewel cases are full and his wife and son are gone,
having taken nothing of his. The Count stands in the street and watches their
carriage go, and it costs him more than he expected.

And then he looks up at another roof, and names the next man.

**The turn:** he wins, he pays, and it does not stop him.
**Page-turn out:** the reader is now afraid of the person they are rooting for.

## Mode distribution

| Mode | Pages | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **Dramatic** | 2–5, 7–12, 14, 16–18, 22–25, 27–28, 30–31, 33, 35–39, 41–43, 45–46, 48 | 34 |
| **Illustrated prose** | 1, 13, 15, 20, 29, 34, 40, 44 | 8 |
| **Spectacle / silence** | 6, 19, 21, 26, 32, 47, 49 | 7 |

Each page's single declared dominant mode is fixed in `07-PAGE-CONTRACT.md` and
is a blocking check at the page gate.

**No movement runs more than five dramatic pages without a prose or spectacle
page.** The longest runs are 2–5 plus 7 (broken by 6), 8–12 (broken by 13),
34–38 (broken by 39), 40–42 (broken by 43) and 44–45 plus 47 (broken by 46).
Every one is at or under the limit, which is why **pages 6, 13, 40 and 44 cannot
be cut for budget.**

---

## The two structural rules this architecture is built to satisfy

**Emotional duration.** Grief, mechanism, and revelation never share a page.
Fernand's public destruction (P33) gets its own aftermath page (P34) before the
plot moves. Mercédès' recognition (P12) gets P13 alone. The unmasking (P46) is
not asked to share a page with the arrival that sets it up (P45). Fernand's
death gets P47 to itself. **And the duel that does not happen gets P44** — the
protagonist is the last character in the volume to be given his own aftermath,
and the volume's closing verdict is only legible because he was.

**One dominant turn per page.** Every page in `07-PAGE-CONTRACT.md` names the
one thing that changed. A page whose turn cannot be stated in one sentence goes
back to the architecture, not to the prompt.
