# The Count of Monte Cristo — Volume II
## Project Brief

**Working title:** *The Count of Monte Cristo, Volume II — The House of Morcerf*

**Form:** cinematic illustrated novel, 49 portrait pages, 1024 × 1536.
**Parent:** `monte-cristo-expanded/` (Volume I, 55 pages, shipped).
**Predecessor to avoid:** `monte-cristo-vol2-abandoned/` — written off. Do not
read, cite, inherit, or reuse. Its refs and pages are not assets.

---

## The adaptation stance

> This volume is an adaptation. Fidelity to the source is not a goal, not a
> tiebreaker, and not a defense. Every element earns its place by what it does
> for this reader in this book, or it is cut.

This is stated first because it is the rule that gets abandoned first. The
cut list in `02-SOURCE-MAP.md` is long on purpose.

---

## The engine

**A man who spent fourteen years turning himself into a weapon walks into the
house of the men who made him — and discovers how much he is going to enjoy
this.**

Protected absolutely. Everything else is held loosely.

The operative word is **enjoy**. Volume I earned the reader's sympathy for
Edmond completely. Volume II spends it. The Count is not serene, not above it,
not a philosopher of justice — he is hungry, he is playing with his food, and
the reader should be having a wonderful time right up until the moment the bill
arrives.

**If the Count reads as calm and above it all for more than a page at a stretch,
the engine has been written out.** That is a script-gate blocker.

---

## The spine

Volume I ended on **mercy**: Edmond used his power first to save Morrel, and
stepped out of the center. Its closing line was *"the choice between mercy and
judgment lies ahead."*

Volume II is **judgment** — and the discovery that judgment has a price, paid
by people who did nothing.

One target, start to finish: **Fernand Mondego, Comte de Morcerf.** Chosen,
opened, destroyed, and paid for.

---

## What this volume is

- One kill, fully dramatized, with its cost.
- A two-hander at its heart: two people who both know who he is, neither
  saying it, for twenty pages.
- The volume where the reader starts to be afraid of the protagonist.

## What this volume is not

- Not a tour of Paris society.
- Not a setup volume for Volumes III and IV. It has its own reversal.
- Not a travelogue. There is no Rome in it.
- Not an ensemble. Eight faces, hard ceiling.

---

## Cast ceiling — 8. Hard.

The Count · Mercédès · Fernand · Albert · Haydée · Danglars · Beauchamp ·
Villefort.

Everyone else in Paris is scenery: unnamed, unlocked, no balloons. See
`04-CHARACTER-LEDGER.md`.

Two collapses are structural, not cosmetic:

**Debray + Château-Renaud + Franz d'Épinay + Beauchamp → Beauchamp.** One face
does four jobs: Albert's friend, the press that prints Janina, the second at
the duel, and the outside world's opinion.

**Bertuccio + Ali + Baptistin → nobody.** This is the cut that kills the
logistics register at the root. The abandoned volume spent a page on a
bell-ringing protocol *because it had locked servants who needed lines.* Delete
the servants and that page cannot be written. **Haydée takes the confidant
function** — a promotion, not a workaround.

---

## Length

**49 pages.** Reached by cutting, never by compressing. A scene that survives
the cut list gets the room it needs.

Planned at 43 and grown to 49 across three script-gate rounds and one page split, every addition
structural rather than decorative: a page where the Count is offered a way out
and refuses it out loud (P17), a page where Mercédès tells Albert the truth on
camera instead of off it (P41), the split of the unmasking off the page that
sets it up (P45/P46), a page where the Count arms Haydée, sends her through the
front door of the Chamber and takes the public stair himself (P30), and the
aftermath of the duel he was let out of (P44).
**None of the five is compression relief; each one puts a beat on the page that
was previously only asserted** — and the last one exists because every other
major beat in the volume gets an aftermath page and the protagonist's did not.

Six movements; see `03-VOLUME-ARCHITECTURE.md`.

Mode balance: **33 dramatic · 8 illustrated prose · 7 spectacle.**
A volume of nothing but dramatic scenes has lost the form; the prose and
spectacle pages are what buy the dramatic pages their room.

## Series position

| Volume | Arc | Ends on |
|---|---|---|
| I — *shipped* | The making of the weapon | Mercy. The *Pharaon* restored. |
| **II — this volume** | **The first use of the weapon** | **The price. Morcerf dead, Mercédès gone.** |
| III — planned | Villefort: the buried child, Benedetto, the poisonings, Valentine, Maximilien | — |
| IV — planned | Danglars' ruin, Haydée, the renunciation, the ending | — |

**Volume II reorders Dumas deliberately: Morcerf falls first, not third.**
Justification is in `02-SOURCE-MAP.md` and it is an arc argument, not a
convenience.

---

## The reader

A first-time reader who has read Volume I and will never read Dumas. They must
never have to solve the adaptation, supply a missing causal step, or recognize
a character the book has not introduced.

"Recoverable," "eventually clear," and "understandable after looking back" are
revision flags, not passing grades.
