# 03 — SETTINGS

Recurring locations for Book Two. Keep these consistent across pages so the world holds.

## The Brunswick / early study (1801–1806)
Modest scholar's room: whitewashed walls, a small window, a plain wooden desk crowded
with papers, a brass astronomical instrument or two, candle and oil lamp, shelves of
calf-bound books. Warmer and better-furnished than Book One's poverty interiors — he is
patronized and rising, but not rich. Night-study amber.

## The Göttingen Observatory (1807 onward; new building 1816)
A neoclassical stone observatory: pale ashlar facade, tall arched windows, a low dome
and a long-focus refracting telescope on a mahogany mount inside a circular instrument
room. Brass meridian circle, clocks, charts of star positions on the walls. Cold blue
night light through the dome slit, warm lamplight at the working desk. This is Gauss's
home and workplace for the rest of his life — it appears in the heavens pages, the
magnetism pages, and as one terminus of the telegraph wire.

## The Gauss family home (1805–1810s)
A respectable middle-class German household: a warm parlor with a tiled stove (Kachelofen),
a cradle, a window onto a Göttingen street, simple good furniture. The ONLY fully warm,
domestic setting in the book — used for the Johanna love page and, inverted into cold
emptiness, for the grief page.

## The battlefield edge / the Duke's death (Auerstedt, Oct 1806)
NOT a battle scene — the aftermath at the edge of frame: smoke on a gray horizon, a fallen
banner, an aristocratic officer carried on a litter, an autumn field churned to mud. Cold,
desaturated, distant. The violence is implied, never shown explicitly (safety-filter rule:
lead with the litter and the grieving figure, not wounds).

## The Hanover survey field (1820s)
Open northern-German countryside: low green-gray hills, heath, scattered woods, a big
weather sky. A wooden survey signal-tower or a tripod-mounted theodolite (a brass angle-
measuring telescope) on a hilltop station; a chain or rod laid out as a measured baseline
across a field; distant hills receding to haze. Daylight, working weather — sun-and-cloud,
sometimes drizzle. The heliotrope's reflected-sun spark is the one hot point of light.

## The physics cabinet / magnetism lab (1831–1833)
Weber's domain across the rooftops from the observatory: a darker laboratory with coils of
copper wire, a suspended magnetic needle in a glass case, voltaic apparatus, a long bench.
Night work by lamplight; copper and brass; the warm small glow of the first telegraph
signal. Bare copper wire runs out a high window and over the tiled Göttingen rooftops to
the observatory.

## The Göttingen rooftops (the telegraph line, 1833)
A night view over steep tiled roofs, chimneys, the observatory dome at one end and the
physics building at the other, a single bare copper wire strung between them under the
stars. About a kilometre (do NOT state an exact length in-image). This is the visual of
"the first electric message."

## The closing / montage world (P18)
A wide luminous unifying field bringing together the three things he measured: a starlit
sky with a recovered planet-point (the heavens), a triangulated landscape with the
heliotrope spark (the Earth), and the telegraph wire / magnetic needle (the invisible),
with Gauss small and present at the center. The single most composed page in the book.
