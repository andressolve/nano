# 02 — CHARACTERS

Locks describe the VISUAL only — never the name. Re-Read each ref PNG before using it in
a page prompt; write a one-line verbatim observation from the pixels.

Ref generation: 1536×1024 landscape, neutral warm-toned plain background, portrait +
partial full body, neutral expression, "no text, no labels." Style/register/anti-drift
blocks prepended to every ref prompt. Real portraits of mature Gauss exist (Ritmüller 1828,
Jensen 1840) — keep the **penetrating blue eyes** constant across all three age phases.

## Object / prop refs

### ref_triangulation.png — the triangulation diagram (the DO-hook) — pages 9, 10, 18
A clean surveyor's construction on warm ivory paper / pale field map: a single large clear
TRIANGLE drawn over a simple landscape — one side marked as a measured BASELINE along the
ground, the two base angles drawn as clean arcs, the apex reaching a distant tower or
hilltop you could not walk to. Faint additional triangles mesh outward to fainter hills
beyond, suggesting a whole grid. Drawn in dark ink with a pair of brass dividers and a
straightedge resting beside it. Large, separated, readable — a child could copy it. NOT a
crowded technical chart. Plain background.

### ref_heliotrope.png — the heliotrope instrument — pages 11, 18
A small early-1800s brass surveying instrument on a wooden tripod: a flat mirror and a small
sighting telescope on a geared brass mount, angled to catch the sun. A single hot spark of
reflected sunlight flares off the mirror toward a distant hill. Mahogany and brass, precise,
handmade. Plain neutral outdoor background, cool distance behind the one hot point of light.

### ref_telegraph.png — the Gauss–Weber electromagnetic telegraph — pages 14, 18
An early-1830s laboratory apparatus: a coil of copper wire and a suspended magnetic needle
in a glass-fronted wooden case mounted on a bench, a small mirror on the needle, bare copper
wire leading away out of frame. Beside it, a slip of paper with a simple dot/line signal
code. Copper, brass, dark wood, warm lamplight. Plain background. (This is a working
philosophical instrument, NOT a modern telegraph key — no Morse sounder.)

### ref_observatory.png — the Göttingen Observatory (setting anchor) — pages 6, 14, 18
A neoclassical stone observatory building under a night sky: pale ashlar facade, tall arched
windows, a low dome with a slit, a long brass refracting telescope visible within. Calm,
institutional, early-19th-century. Establishes the place Gauss lived and worked for the rest
of his life. Plain night-sky background, a few stars.

## Multi-character strategy per page (NO composite plate needed — max 2 named characters/page)
- **P4 (Gauss young + Johanna):** lock JOHANNA's face (the harder/softer face to keep
  consistent), describe Gauss young in prose (dark blond Empire hair, blue eyes, dark coat).
- **P7 (Gauss young + Johanna, grief):** Johanna absent/dying at edge of frame or implied;
  anchor on Gauss young alone with the cradle. Single ref.
- **P8 (Gauss young + Minna):** lock MINNA's face (must read as clearly different from
  Johanna — darker hair, gray eyes), describe Gauss in prose.
- **P5 (Gauss young + the Duke):** lock the DUKE (most distinctive — aged, bandaged,
  uniform), describe Gauss in prose at the litter's side.
- **P13 / P14 (Gauss older + Weber):** lock WEBER (distinctive — young, dark-haired,
  1830s), describe Gauss older in prose (white hair, velvet cap on the indoor lab page).
- **All other pages:** single locked character (correct age phase) via edit_image, or an
  object ref for the instrument-hero pages.
- **If any script revision puts 3+ named characters on one page → STOP and build a
  composite plate first (and surface it).**
