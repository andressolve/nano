# The Count of Monte Cristo — Dialogue Prototype Scripts

## Purpose

These are finished visual-development tests, not final numbered Volume I pages.
All use the locked 1536 × 1024 landscape canvas and Velvet Cinema style.

The prototypes test whether information can remain dramatic when carried by
dialogue, gesture, objects, and changing power.

## Prototype A — The Examination

**Accepted output:** [`pages/prototype-01-examination.png`](pages/prototype-01-examination.png)

### Burden

Political stakes without a history lecture; Edmond and Villefort remain clear;
the letter changes control of the room; native lettering and tails remain
unambiguous.

### Layout

Four panels:

1. wide upper panel, Edmond standing before Villefort's desk;
2. lower-left, opposed medium close-ups across the letter;
3. narrow center inset, Noirtier's name and Villefort's eyes;
4. large lower-right, the letter burning as the guards enter behind Edmond.

### Exact lettering and balloon map

1. **Villefort**, upper-left, tail to his mouth: “Your name?”
2. **Edmond**, upper-right, tail to his mouth: “Edmond Dantès. Mate of the
   *Pharaon*.”
3. **Villefort**, left: “You carried a letter from Elba.”
4. **Edmond**, right: “My captain was dying. I gave him my word.”
5. **Villefort**, small near the letter: “To Monsieur Noirtier...”
6. **Edmond**, opposite: “You know him?”
7. **Villefort**, beside the flame, tail clearly to him: “No. And now no one
   will.”
8. **Edmond**, near the open side of the panel: “Then I may go?”
9. **Villefort**, lowest and isolated: “Guard.”

No explanatory caption.

### Turn

Edmond thinks destruction of the dangerous letter has freed him. The same flame
that appears to save him seals his imprisonment.

## Prototype B — Faria Names the Conspiracy

**Accepted output:** [`pages/prototype-02-faria-conspiracy.png`](pages/prototype-02-faria-conspiracy.png)

### Burden

Exposition becomes investigation, grief, moral judgment, and a decision to
learn.

### Layout

Seven panels inside the tunnel-school:

1. wide upper panel, Faria and Edmond over a hand-drawn relationship diagram;
2. Edmond alone counts the first three names;
3. Edmond alone asks about Villefort;
4. Faria alone connects Villefort to Noirtier;
5. Edmond alone realizes the design;
6. Faria alone names despair as the remaining jailer;
7. Edmond alone lifts his head into newly opened lamplight.

The one-speaker blocking in panels 2–7 was adopted after an earlier composition
produced ambiguous tails. The full page was restaged and regenerated.

### Exact lettering and balloon map

1. **Faria**, upper-left: “Who profited when you vanished?”
2. **Edmond**, upper-right: “Danglars wanted my command. Fernand wanted
   Mercédès.”
3. **Edmond**, alone: “Caderousse knew. He did nothing.”
4. **Edmond**, alone: “And Villefort?”
5. **Faria**, alone, pointing to the diagram: “He burned a letter addressed to
   Noirtier. His father, perhaps. Your innocence threatened his future.”
6. **Edmond**, alone: “Then four men built this cell.”
7. **Faria**, alone: “They opened the door. Despair kept you here.”
8. **Edmond**, alone, with a short clean tail: “Then teach me to defeat it.”

No explanatory caption.

### Turn

Edmond receives the names he will one day judge, but his first response is not
revenge. It is the decision to learn.

## Prototype C — Mercédès Recognizes Edmond

**Accepted output:** [`pages/prototype-03-mercedes-recognizes-edmond.png`](pages/prototype-03-mercedes-recognizes-edmond.png)

### Burden

Adult identity consistency, restrained acting, shot/reverse-shot dialogue, and
subtext in a quiet Paris room.

### Layout

Eight panels:

1. wide establishing panel, Mercédès and the Count alone after a salon has
   emptied;
2. Mercédès alone remembers Edmond's fear response;
3. the Count alone declares Edmond dead;
4. Mercédès alone notices the Count's right hand becoming still;
5. the Count alone tries to recover control at the doorway;
6. Mercédès alone names the danger to Albert;
7. the Count alone repeats the lie;
8. Mercédès alone owns the final panel.

### Exact lettering and balloon map

1. **Mercédès**, upper-left: “Everyone believes you are the Count of Monte
   Cristo.”
2. **Count**, upper-right: “Everyone is usually satisfied with a name.”
3. **Mercédès**, alone: “I knew a man who held his breath before speaking
   when he was afraid.”
4. **Count**, alone: “That man died at Château d'If.”
5. **Mercédès**, alone: “No. He learned to stand very still.”
6. **Count**, alone at the doorway: “Why are you here?”
7. **Mercédès**, alone: “To ask whether Edmond Dantès has returned to kill my
   son.”
8. **Count**, alone: “Edmond Dantès is dead.”
9. **Mercédès**, alone, final balloon: “Then why did he just lie to me?”

No explanatory caption.

### Turn

The Count enters the scene as its controller. Mercédès ends it speaking to the
man he insists is dead.
