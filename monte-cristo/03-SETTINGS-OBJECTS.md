# The Count of Monte Cristo — Settings and Object Grammar

## Purpose

Settings and objects exist to intensify decisions. They are not historical
exhibits.

## Marseille and the *Pharaon*

- Working commercial harbor, not pirate fantasy.
- Masts, yards, rigging, wet stone quays, warehouses, small craft, workers,
  merchants, and Notre-Dame de la Garde in the distance.
- The *Pharaon* is a three-masted merchant vessel returning from Smyrna,
  Trieste, and Naples.
- Shipboard actions must make Edmond's competence instantly legible.

## The Catalans and the Betrothal

- Sun, limewashed walls, rough timber, nets, simple tables, shared food, open
  doors, and community proximity.
- The arrest should rupture warmth and human closeness, not enter a generic
  tavern.

## Villefort's Examination Room

- Formal but not grand: desk, green or dark leather writing surface, papers,
  wax, seal, sand shaker, candle or fire, controlled daylight.
- The Bonapartist letter is the visual center.
- Edmond begins with more open space; after Noirtier's name appears, the desk
  and guards divide him from the exit.

## Château d'If

- Sixteenth-century coastal fortress: thick limestone, small openings, damp,
  salt, iron, uneven floors, and the sea heard or glimpsed rather than enjoyed.
- The cell is not a fantasy dungeon with chains everywhere.
- Passing time appears through body, light, food, scratches, repeated guard
  actions, and changing hope.

## Faria's School

Improvised materials:

- sharpened metal fragment;
- fishbone or wood pen;
- soot/food-based ink;
- cloth strips and threads;
- hand-drawn diagrams;
- concealed pages or manuscript;
- tunnel debris and a hidden work rhythm.

The setting gradually changes from cell to workshop to school without becoming
clean or safe.

## Escape Objects

- Faria's burial sack;
- coarse rope closure;
- heavy shot or weight tied near the feet;
- concealed knife or sharpened metal;
- wet cloth clinging underwater.

The escape page must make orientation and sequence clear without gore.

## Monte Cristo

- Black rock, narrow openings, dangerous footing, heat, blue sea, shadow, and a
  concealed chamber whose geometry feels discovered rather than magical.
- Treasure includes coin, ingots, jewels, vessels, and historical containers.
- The image centers Edmond's transformed understanding of possibility, not
  inventory.

## Paris Mechanisms

- visiting cards;
- sealed letters;
- newspapers;
- ledgers and credit instruments;
- mirrors and opposed doorways;
- dinner tables as tactical terrain;
- carriages, horses, servants, and clocks as command of time;
- wax red as a recurring signal of a decision entering the world.

## First Object Reference Plate

The initial locked plate should include:

1. Captain Leclère's folded letter with plain wax seal and no readable invented
   writing;
2. Villefort's candle flame, sealing wax, and desk tools;
3. Faria's improvised pen, ink, blade, cord, and diagram;
4. the burial-sack rope and cutting tool;
5. the red silk purse;
6. a visiting card, ledger, and sealing ribbon from the Count's Paris system.
