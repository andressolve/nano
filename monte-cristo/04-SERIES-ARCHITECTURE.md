# The Count of Monte Cristo — Dramatic Series Architecture

## Decision

Use a **five-volume working architecture**. The distribution follows the
117-chapter public-domain source map in
[`research/CHAPTER-MAP.md`](research/CHAPTER-MAP.md), but source coverage never
determines page quality.

## Volume I — *Edmond*

Source movement: Chapters 1–30.

### Dramatic question

Can an innocent man survive being erased without losing the part of himself
capable of love?

### Major turns

- Edmond returns capable, loved, and on the verge of command and marriage.
- Danglars, Fernand, and Caderousse form the human trap.
- Villefort recognizes Edmond's innocence and chooses his own future.
- Prison destroys Edmond's sense of time and nearly his will to live.
- Faria gives him friendship, knowledge, the truth of the conspiracy, and a
  future beyond the walls.
- Edmond escapes through Faria's death, finds the treasure, and becomes capable
  of remaking reality.
- He first uses that power to save Morrel.

### Ending

The restored *Pharaon* enters the harbor. Julie holds the red silk purse.
Edmond's benevolence is experienced before vengeance becomes his governing
project.

## Volume II — *The Return*

Source movement: Chapters 31–56.

### Dramatic question

What does Edmond become when everyone encounters the legend before the man?

Rome introduces Sinbad and the Count from outside. Albert's rescue opens Paris.
The Count enters the Morcerf, Danglars, and Villefort worlds; Auteuil and
Bertuccio reveal the buried child; Haydée and the Morrels establish competing
uses of power; Andrea Cavalcanti enters society as a manufactured identity.

Ending: Andrea is accepted into the marriage market. The Count has placed a
living falsehood inside the households built on older lies.

## Volume III — *The Web*

Source movement: Chapters 57–83.

### Dramatic question

Can the Count keep each punishment confined to the person he judges guilty?

Noirtier defeats Villefort through will and memory. Telegraphy, markets,
marriage, poison, Yanina, and Caderousse begin crossing between households.
Haydée's history becomes active evidence. Caderousse refuses his last chances
and dies after naming Edmond.

Ending: Caderousse recognizes the man beneath the Count as the first revenge
target falls. The Count still calls the pattern divine.

## Volume IV — *Judgment*

Source movement: Chapters 84–103.

### Dramatic question

What remains of perfect vengeance when the innocent stand inside it?

Beauchamp verifies Yanina. Albert challenges the Count. Mercédès recognizes
Edmond and forces him to speak as himself. Haydée's testimony destroys
Morcerf's public honor. Fernand dies. Eugénie refuses the marriage transaction.
Valentine appears to die and Maximilien's trust is placed in the Count.

Ending: Maximilien accepts the Count's impossible demand to wait. The audience
can no longer be sure whether Edmond is saving him or repeating cruelty.

## Volume V — *Providence*

Source movement: Chapters 104–117.

### Dramatic question

Can Edmond surrender judgment before vengeance finishes consuming the innocent?

Danglars flees. Benedetto names Villefort in court. Héloïse and Édouard die.
The Count's belief that he acts as Providence collapses. He pardons Danglars,
restores Valentine to Maximilien, transfers power away from himself, and leaves
with Haydée only after her choice is given dramatic space.

Ending: “wait and hope” transforms prison patience into a humane future rather
than a guarantee that suffering was necessary.

## Adaptation Discipline

- Preserve functions, not every transaction.
- Combine conversations only when each character's objective remains clear.
- Do not compress away the Morrels, Haydée's agency, Mercédès's recognition,
  Noirtier's will, Eugénie's refusal, or Édouard's moral consequence.
- Whenever a financial, legal, or political mechanism enters, identify the
  person who wants it, the person endangered by it, and the visible consequence
  before writing explanatory dialogue.
