Five Questions

Choose an answer, then check it. The explanation matters more than the score.

1. Why does Villefort imprison Edmond after deciding that he is innocent?

Exactly. Villefort recognizes Edmond’s innocence, then sees his father Noirtier’s name on the letter. Edmond becomes dangerous not because he is guilty, but because the truth could expose Villefort’s family and ruin his rise.
Look again at the burning letter. Villefort imprisons an innocent man because the addressee—Noirtier—is his own father, and that truth threatens the future Villefort wants.

2. What changes when Faria helps Edmond reconstruct the betrayal?

Right. Danglars wanted the command, Fernand wanted Mercédès, Caderousse refused courage, and Villefort protected himself. Faria gives Edmond names—but Edmond’s first answer is, “Teach me.”
Faria turns pain into an investigation. Edmond sees that four different failures converged, then chooses learning and self-command before revenge.

3. Why is Edmond able to escape inside Faria’s burial sack?

Yes. The plan almost becomes his death when the guards add an iron weight. Faria’s tools, Edmond’s preparation, and his refusal to surrender turn burial into rebirth.
The guards throw him into the sea with a weight tied to his feet. Edmond survives because he planned for the sack, kept a blade, and cuts both cloth and rope underwater.

4. What is Busoni really buying when he offers Caderousse the diamond?

Exactly. The diamond makes greed speak. Behind the priest’s disguise, Edmond learns that Morrel fought for him, his father died, Mercédès waited, and the betrayers prospered.
Busoni’s diamond is a test and a price for testimony. Edmond is not shopping for property; he is discovering who remained loyal and what his enemies became.

5. Why is the restored Pharaon the right ending for this volume?

That is the moral fork. Treasure gives Edmond astonishing reach. His first great choice saves Morrel anonymously; the next volume will ask what happens when the same power turns toward judgment.
The ship really was lost, and Edmond does not reclaim his old command. He rebuilds the Pharaon for Morrel, proving his new power can restore before it destroys.
