# The Count of Monte Cristo — Velvet Cinema Style Guide

## Canvas

- **1536 × 1024, 3:2 landscape, without exception.**
- Reference sheets, prototypes, covers, and story pages use the same landscape
  canvas.
- Do not approve portrait or square generations, even when their content is
  otherwise strong.

## Medium

Velvet Cinema is mature historical graphic-novel realism painted in layered
matte gouache and opaque watercolor over sparse charcoal and ink construction.

Required characteristics:

- broad visible brushstrokes;
- simplified interlocking color shapes;
- bold shadow masses;
- selective hard edges at faces, hands, and decisive objects;
- tactile cloth, stone, wood, paper, wax, metal, and water;
- expressive, anatomically credible faces;
- cinematic blocking without photographic lens effects.

Avoid:

- glossy game-concept-art surfaces;
- airbrushed skin;
- dense engraved cross-hatching;
- oil-painting pastiche;
- modern anime or chibi proportions;
- children's-book softness;
- generic grimdark;
- steampunk decoration;
- pirate visual shorthand.

## World Palettes

### Marseille, 1815

Salt white, cobalt and working blue, warm ochre, sun-warmed skin, sailcloth,
weathered wood, and open sky. The horizon is available and life feels possible.

### Château d'If

Mineral gray, damp moss, rust, black-green depth, worn linen, and tiny sources
of amber light. Space compresses around Edmond.

### Faria's underground school

The prison palette remains, but warm earth, lamp amber, parchment, thread, and
skin enter the center of the frame. Learning creates literal visual room.

### Monte Cristo

Black rock, impossible Mediterranean blue, salt white, oxidized bronze, and
treasure rendered as concentrated molten warmth rather than a generic gold
pile.

### Rome

Torch amber, carnival red, chalk white, violet dusk, stone, masks, and the
Count's controlled black silhouette.

### Paris, 1838

Lacquer black, burgundy, bottle green, ivory, polished walnut, wax red, and old
gold. Mirrors, doorways, documents, and furniture should make social rooms feel
like mechanisms.

### The ending

Air, dawn, sea, pale gold, and open distance return. The visual world breathes
again without pretending the damage is erased.

## Edmond's Visual Continuity

Across every age and identity preserve:

- deep-set black-brown eyes;
- straight strong brow;
- long, clean nose;
- high cheekbones;
- a slight asymmetry at the left corner of his mouth;
- a poised right hand that becomes still before a difficult decision;
- the habit of occupying a doorway before entering a room.

Age, weight, beard, hair, posture, costume, and emotional access may change.
The bones and gaze do not.

## Landscape Page Grammar

- Prefer three to five panels.
- Use the wide canvas for opposed characters, doors, tables, ships, tunnels,
  and cause-and-effect across space.
- A full-width establishing panel should establish an emotional problem, not
  merely location.
- Narrow vertical insets may interrupt a wide scene for a hand, eye, letter,
  flame, key, knife, or seal.
- Panel size follows power: whoever controls the exchange should increasingly
  control the visual space.
- Quiet negative space is deliberate lettering territory, never an empty
  decorative zone.

## Lettering

- All lettering is native to the generated page image.
- Balloons are warm ivory, not pure digital white.
- Balloon outlines use a restrained charcoal-brown brush line.
- Lettering is dark, upright, highly legible hand-lettered serif/sans hybrid:
  literary without faux-antique ornament.
- Captions are matte parchment rectangles integrated into calm image areas.
- Never use blank balloons, blank captions, or HTML/SVG lettering overlays.
- No decorative French phrases or invented document text.

### Attribution

Treat speech attribution as blocking:

- place speakers in dialogue order;
- keep each balloon on the speaker's side;
- specify every balloon by ordinal, speaker, verbatim text, position, and tail
  endpoint;
- for A–B–A exchanges use A upper-left, B upper-right, A lower-left;
- if attribution remains risky, use one visible speaker and one balloon per
  panel;
- reject tails that stop at torsos, furniture, or empty space.

## Dialogue Density

- Normal page: roughly 45–90 words.
- Hard dialogue page: roughly 90–135 words only when the composition clearly
  supports it.
- Fewer words are welcome when silence is active and the story remains complete.
- Never make a page terse merely to simplify generation.
- Break or restage a page before shrinking lettering or omitting necessary
  story logic.

## Recurring Graphic Lines

- rope becomes tunnel line, map line, account line, sealing ribbon, telegraph
  wire, and finally open horizon;
- red wax marks promises, identity, transaction, evidence, and consequence;
- doorways measure Edmond's relationship to freedom and control;
- hands carry the story's moral actions: writing, failing to intervene,
  teaching, cutting free, giving, poisoning, testifying, pardoning.
