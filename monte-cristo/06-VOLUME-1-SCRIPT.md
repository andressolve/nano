# The Count of Monte Cristo — Volume I Script

## Working title

*Edmond*

Working length: 32 story pages, 3:2 landscape, 1536 × 1024.

All lettering below is exact image text. Captions are deliberately rare. Page
prompts must add ordinal balloon placement and tail endpoints without changing
the words.

## Page 1 — Bring Her Home

**Purpose:** Edmond enters through action and competence.

**Layout:** Three panels. Full-width harbor approach; opposed deck actions;
Morrel's skiff coming alongside.

**Text:**

- Caption: “Marseille · February 1815”
- Pilot: “Shoal water ahead!”
- Edmond: “Ease the mainsail! Larboard line ready!”
- Sailor: “She is answering!”
- Edmond: “Then let her answer. Bring her home.”
- Morrel: “Dantès! Where is Captain Leclère?”
- Edmond: “Dead, sir. But the *Pharaon* and every man aboard are safe.”

**Turn:** Morrel arrives expecting disaster and finds Edmond already commanding
the crisis.

## Page 2 — Captain at Nineteen

**Purpose:** Establish the future Edmond is about to receive and Danglars's
envy.

**Layout:** Four panels on deck. Morrel and Edmond; Danglars listening; the crew
responding to Edmond; private offer.

**Text:**

- Morrel: “The crew obeyed you as captain.”
- Edmond: “They obeyed because the ship needed them.”
- Danglars: “A fortunate performance.”
- Morrel: “Not fortune. Judgment.”
- Morrel: “When the *Pharaon* sails again, I mean you to command her.”
- Edmond: “Me?”
- Morrel: “At nineteen you brought my ship home. Shall I wait until you forget
  how?”

**Turn:** Edmond's happiness becomes public; Danglars discovers exactly what
Edmond is about to gain.

## Page 3 — The Empty Cupboard

**Purpose:** Make Edmond's love for his father immediate and expose the cost of
Caderousse's debt.

**Layout:** Four intimate panels in the small apartment.

**Text:**

- Edmond: “Father!”
- Louis Dantès: “You came back taller.”
- Edmond: “And you came back thinner.”
- Louis: “You left two hundred francs. I paid what we owed Caderousse.”
- Edmond: “All of it?”
- Louis: “A debt is a debt.”
- Edmond: “And food?”
- Louis: “Food can wait.”
- Edmond: “No. From today, nothing in this house waits for money.”

**Turn:** Edmond arrives with a captaincy ahead of him and finds that honor has
nearly starved his father.

## Page 4 — Mercédès

**Purpose:** Introduce Mercédès as a person who chooses, not a prize being
claimed.

**Layout:** Three panels in the Catalans. Fernand blocks a doorway; Mercédès
refuses him; Edmond appears beyond Fernand.

**Text:**

- Fernand: “He has been gone for months. The sea may keep him for years.”
- Mercédès: “The sea does not choose my husband.”
- Fernand: “I am here.”
- Mercédès: “And I have answered you.”
- Edmond, outside: “Must I ask the question too?”
- Mercédès: “Only if you have forgotten the answer.”

**Turn:** Fernand's imagined opportunity vanishes in a single look between
Edmond and Mercédès.

## Page 5 — Tomorrow

**Purpose:** Give Edmond and Mercédès enough joy that the arrest will wound.

**Layout:** Four panels moving from reunion to the betrothal table; Fernand
watches from outside the warmth.

**Text:**

- Edmond: “Morrel will make me captain.”
- Mercédès: “Then Captain Dantès must learn to arrive on time.”
- Edmond: “Tomorrow?”
- Mercédès: “For the wedding?”
- Edmond: “For everything.”
- Mercédès: “Tomorrow.”
- Louis Dantès: “To tomorrow!”
- Guests: “To Edmond and Mercédès!”

**Turn:** The future is spoken aloud and therefore becomes something the
conspirators can attack.

## Page 6 — The Letter

**Purpose:** Stage the conspiracy as three different moral failures.

**Layout:** Four panels at an outside table near La Réserve. Danglars writes;
Caderousse sees; Fernand carries the letter without touching the pen.

**Text:**

- Danglars: “A loyal subject reports a Bonapartist agent aboard the
  *Pharaon*...”
- Caderousse: “That joke has a prison inside it.”
- Danglars: “Then tear it up.”
- Caderousse: “I said it was cruel. I did not say I was sober enough to stand.”
- Fernand: “Give it to me.”
- Danglars: “I wrote nothing. You saw nothing.”
- Caderousse: “And Edmond?”
- Fernand: “Tomorrow will not come.”

**Turn:** No one can later claim the crime belonged to only one man.

## Page 7 — The Feast

**Purpose:** Let celebration continue until authority physically enters it.

**Layout:** Three panels. Wide feast; Edmond and Mercédès's private exchange
inside the crowd; armed men darken the doorway.

**Text:**

- Mercédès: “You keep looking at the door.”
- Edmond: “I am teaching myself to believe no one will call me back to sea.”
- Mercédès: “Then look at me.”
- Officer: “Which of you is Edmond Dantès?”
- Edmond: “I am.”
- Mercédès: “On whose order?”
- Officer: “The deputy crown prosecutor requires a question answered.”

**Turn:** Edmond leaves expecting a question, not a disappearance.

## Page 8 — Two Betrothals

**Purpose:** Put Edmond's arrest beside the society Villefort is protecting.

**Layout:** Four cross-cut panels. Edmond under escort; Villefort at his own
betrothal; an official whispers; Villefort sees a career opportunity.

**Text:**

- Renée: “Must justice interrupt even our betrothal?”
- Villefort: “Justice is rarely invited. That is why it enters dramatically.”
- Official: “A sailor carrying a letter from Elba.”
- Royalist guest: “A Bonapartist plot?”
- Villefort: “Or a frightened boy obeying a dead captain.”
- Renée: “You sound merciful.”
- Villefort: “Mercy is safest after one knows the name.”

**Turn:** Villefort approaches the examination believing he can control both
justice and appearance.

## Page 9 — The Examination

**Purpose:** Edmond's innocence becomes undeniable to Villefort.

**Layout:** Four panels from the accepted examination grammar, ending before
the addressee is revealed.

**Text:**

- Villefort: “Your name?”
- Edmond: “Edmond Dantès. Mate of the *Pharaon*.”
- Villefort: “You carried a letter from Elba.”
- Edmond: “My captain was dying. I gave him my word.”
- Villefort: “Did you read it?”
- Edmond: “No.”
- Villefort: “Then you do not know enough to be guilty.”
- Edmond: “I knew that when I entered.”

**Turn:** Villefort has already decided Edmond is innocent—until the envelope
changes the stakes.

## Page 10 — Noirtier

**Purpose:** Make Villefort's betrayal a visible choice.

**Layout:** Four panels matching the accepted prototype's lower sequence:
letter, eyes, flame, guards.

**Text:**

- Villefort: “To Monsieur Noirtier...”
- Edmond: “You know him?”
- Villefort: “No. And now no one will.”
- Edmond: “Then I may go?”
- Villefort: “You must never speak of this letter.”
- Edmond: “You have my word.”
- Villefort: “Guard.”
- Edmond: “Monsieur?”

**Turn:** Edmond offers the same honorable word that brought him there;
Villefort answers by burying him.

## Page 11 — The Black Island

**Purpose:** Turn a routine transfer into dawning terror.

**Layout:** Four panels in a night boat: Marseille recedes; Edmond tests the
guards; Château d'If appears; landing.

**Text:**

- Edmond: “The prison is behind us.”
- Guard: “Not your prison.”
- Edmond: “Where are you taking me?”
- Guard: “Sit down.”
- Edmond: “That is Château d'If.”
- Guard: “You know your landmarks.”
- Edmond: “I am not a state prisoner!”
- Guard: “You are whatever the order says when the door closes.”

**Turn:** The familiar fortress outside Edmond's home becomes the place from
which home cannot be reached.

## Page 12 — A Name in a Register

**Purpose:** Show Edmond resisting erasure through live exchanges.

**Layout:** Five panels across the first weeks: cell demand, jailer, governor,
petition, silence.

**Text:**

- Edmond: “Take me back to Villefort.”
- Jailer: “I carry soup, not prisoners.”
- Edmond: “Then carry a message.”
- Jailer: “Messages cost money.”
- Edmond: “M. Morrel will pay.”
- Governor: “The register says dangerous Bonapartist.”
- Edmond: “The register has never met me.”
- Governor: “It does not need to.”
- Edmond: “Tell me how long.”
- Jailer: “Long enough to stop asking.”

**Turn:** Edmond learns that every official can see the order and no official
must see the man.

## Page 13 — Nothing Can Be Done

**Purpose:** Keep the changing regime and failed appeals inside human choices.

**Layout:** Four cross-cut panels: Morrel petitions Villefort; Mercédès waits;
Villefort marks the file; Edmond receives no answer.

**Text:**

- Morrel: “You examined him. You know he is innocent.”
- Villefort: “I know he carried a dangerous letter.”
- Morrel: “On a dying man's command.”
- Villefort: “The government has changed twice. That makes the letter more
  dangerous, not less.”
- Mercédès: “Will he come home?”
- Morrel: “I will not stop.”
- Villefort, writing: “Nothing can be done.”
- Edmond: “Did anyone answer?”
- Jailer: “No one asked me to bring an answer.”

**Turn:** Villefort turns political uncertainty into a permanent sentence.

## Page 14 — The Sound

**Purpose:** Move from suicidal despair to the smallest possible reason to live.

**Layout:** Five increasingly quiet panels. Edmond rejects food; lies down;
scratches stop; a faint sound; he answers.

**Text:**

- Jailer: “Eat.”
- Edmond: “Take it.”
- Jailer: “Tomorrow you will beg.”
- Edmond: “Tomorrow is your word.”
- Sound effect, very small: “scrape”
- Edmond: “...Who is there?”
- Sound effect: “scrape · scrape”
- Edmond: “Again.”

**Turn:** A sound smaller than speech interrupts Edmond's decision to die.

## Page 15 — Prisoner Twenty-Seven

**Purpose:** Deliver the first meeting as wonder, comedy, and danger.

**Layout:** Four panels. Edmond digs; floor collapses; Faria emerges wrong-way;
the two prisoners see one another.

**Text:**

- Voice: “Move away from the stone!”
- Edmond: “You are beneath me?”
- Faria: “Regrettably.”
- Edmond: “I thought you were rescue.”
- Faria: “I thought you were the outer gallery.”
- Edmond: “We have both been disappointed.”
- Faria: “Yes. But only one of us brought a lamp.”

**Turn:** Edmond's first human encounter in years arrives with intelligence and
wit rather than pity.

## Page 16 — The Wrong Direction

**Purpose:** Make the tunnel spatially clear through argument and shared
problem-solving.

**Layout:** Four panels with a simple floor sketch, measurements, and the guard
route.

**Text:**

- Faria: “My tunnel should have reached the gallery.”
- Edmond: “Your wall is twice as thick here.”
- Faria: “Because this is not the east tower.”
- Edmond: “The sea strikes my wall before dawn. We are north.”
- Faria: “Then my beautiful calculation was beautifully wrong.”
- Edmond: “Can we correct it?”
- Faria: “We?”
- Edmond: “Unless you intend to dig through me next time.”

**Turn:** A failed escape route becomes the beginning of partnership.

## Page 17 — The School

**Purpose:** Make learning exhilarating and relational.

**Layout:** Six compact panels connected by lamplight and repeated hands:
language, mathematics, history, navigation, manners, memory.

**Text:**

- Faria: “What is power?”
- Edmond: “A captain's order.”
- Faria: “Too small.”
- Edmond: “A king's order.”
- Faria: “Still too small.”
- Edmond: “Knowing which order a man will obey.”
- Faria: “Better.”
- Edmond: “Again.”
- Faria: “Italian first. Then history. Then the mathematics you insulted
  yesterday.”
- Edmond: “The mathematics insulted me first.”

**Turn:** Edmond becomes hungry for knowledge with the same force he once
directed toward freedom.

## Page 18 — Four Men

**Purpose:** Reconstruct the conspiracy as emotional discovery.

**Layout:** Use the accepted seven-panel prototype.

**Text:**

- Faria: “Who profited when you vanished?”
- Edmond: “Danglars wanted my command. Fernand wanted Mercédès.”
- Edmond: “Caderousse knew. He did nothing.”
- Edmond: “And Villefort?”
- Faria: “He burned a letter addressed to Noirtier. His father, perhaps. Your
  innocence threatened his future.”
- Edmond: “Then four men built this cell.”
- Faria: “They opened the door. Despair kept you here.”
- Edmond: “Then teach me to defeat it.”

**Turn:** Edmond first answers betrayal by choosing mastery of himself.

## Page 19 — A New Mind

**Purpose:** Complete the education movement without an infographic montage.

**Layout:** Five panels built around tests Faria sets and Edmond solves.

**Text:**

- Faria: “A banker refuses your money. What do you buy?”
- Edmond: “His creditor.”
- Faria: “A minister refuses your request?”
- Edmond: “I discover what request he cannot refuse.”
- Faria: “A room laughs at your name?”
- Edmond: “I give them another name.”
- Faria: “And an enemy?”
- Edmond: “I learn what he loves.”
- Faria: “Why?”
- Edmond: “So I know what not to destroy.”

**Turn:** Edmond articulates a moral limit before wealth and pain tempt him to
cross it.

## Page 20 — Spada

**Purpose:** Make the treasure a gift of trust before it becomes wealth.

**Layout:** Four panels: Faria reveals the fragment; Edmond doubts; heat reveals
hidden marks; Faria names him heir.

**Text:**

- Faria: “The Spada fortune was never found because everyone searched the
  palace.”
- Edmond: “And you searched a scrap of paper?”
- Faria: “I heated it.”
- Edmond: “Monte Cristo.”
- Faria: “A cave on the eastern shore. The entrance opens when the sun reaches
  the split rock.”
- Edmond: “Why tell me?”
- Faria: “Because knowledge dies if it cannot choose an heir.”
- Edmond: “We will find it together.”
- Faria: “My son... one of us may.”

**Turn:** Edmond refuses to imagine a future that leaves Faria behind.

## Page 21 — The Sack

**Purpose:** Unite Faria's death and Edmond's escape decision without making
death a device.

**Layout:** Five panels: attack; failed care; final words; guards remove body;
Edmond sees the empty sack return.

**Text:**

- Edmond: “Stay with me.”
- Faria: “I have stayed longer than stone expected.”
- Edmond: “The tunnel—tonight I can widen it.”
- Faria: “No. Listen.”
- Faria: “Knowledge first. Treasure second. Judgment last.”
- Edmond: “Do not leave me here.”
- Faria: “Then do not remain.”
- Guard, outside: “The old madman is dead.”
- Edmond, alone: “Not mad.”

**Turn:** The burial sack enters the cell; Edmond understands Faria's final
instruction as permission to act.

## Page 22 — The Dead Man Leaves

**Purpose:** Make the body exchange legible, tense, and emotionally costly.

**Layout:** Five panels: Edmond opens seam; moves Faria; enters sack; guards
carry him; weight tied at feet.

**Text:**

- Edmond, whispering: “Forgive me.”
- Guard 1: “He weighs more dead.”
- Guard 2: “They all do.”
- Edmond, thought balloon avoided; whispered inside sack: “Knife. Knot. Air.”
- Guard 1: “Ready?”
- Guard 2: “One...”
- Guard 1: “Two...”
- Both: “Three.”

**Turn:** Edmond expected burial and feels the weight tied to his feet.

## Page 23 — First Breath

**Purpose:** Deliver the escape as a spatially clear rebirth.

**Layout:** Six action panels: fall; impact; sinking; cutting sack; cutting
weight; surfacing into storm.

**Text:**

- Sound effect: “WHUMM”
- Edmond: “Faria—”
- Sound effect: “RRRIP”
- Edmond: “Not yet.”
- Sound effect: “SNAP”
- Edmond, breaking surface: “I am alive!”

**Turn:** Edmond speaks his own existence aloud for the first time since the
state reduced him to a register entry.

## Page 24 — The Jeune-Amélie

**Purpose:** Show Edmond immediately using judgment and concealment in the
living world.

**Layout:** Four panels: smugglers spot him; rescue; questions; Edmond proves
useful during danger.

**Text:**

- Jacopo: “Man in the water!”
- Smuggler: “Or a corpse that changed its mind.”
- Jacopo: “Give me your hand!”
- Captain: “Name?”
- Edmond: “Maltese. Shipwrecked.”
- Captain: “Your French says Marseille.”
- Edmond: “My hands say sailor.”
- Lookout: “Customs sail!”
- Edmond: “Then lose three points to starboard and let the squall hide us.”
- Captain: “Do it.”

**Turn:** Edmond's first lie wins because his skill makes it useful.

## Page 25 — The Split Rock

**Purpose:** Turn Monte Cristo into a dangerous choice rather than instant
wonder.

**Layout:** Four panels: island approach; Edmond finds landmark; feigns injury;
crew leaves reluctantly.

**Text:**

- Captain: “One hour. Then we sail.”
- Edmond: “An hour is enough.”
- Jacopo: “You keep looking east.”
- Edmond: “At the goats.”
- Edmond, after falling: “My ankle.”
- Jacopo: “We will carry you.”
- Edmond: “And lose the tide? Leave water. Return at sunset.”
- Jacopo: “I do not like leaving a friend.”
- Edmond: “Then come back for one.”

**Turn:** Edmond is alone on the island with Faria's memory and no certainty the
treasure exists.

## Page 26 — Monte Cristo

**Purpose:** Deliver awe through Edmond's changed sense of possibility.

**Layout:** Four escalating panels: split rock; hidden opening; first small
coffer; full chamber with Edmond tiny against it.

**Text:**

- Edmond: “Eastern shore. Split rock. Sun at noon.”
- Edmond: “A ring.”
- Sound effect: “KRRR”
- Edmond: “Faria.”
- Edmond: “You were right.”
- Edmond, before the chamber: “What can one man become with this?”
- Faria's remembered words, small and integrated: “Judgment last.”

**Turn:** Wealth answers every practical obstacle and leaves the moral question
entirely to Edmond.

## Page 27 — Abbé Busoni

**Purpose:** Reveal the lost years through Caderousse while introducing
identity as theater.

**Layout:** Five panels in the Pont du Gard inn. Busoni offers a diamond;
Caderousse tells each fate; the Count's eyes remain Edmond's.

**Text:**

- Busoni: “Dantès left this diamond to be divided among his friends.”
- Caderousse: “Friends?”
- Busoni: “Danglars. Fernand. You. And Mercédès.”
- Caderousse: “Then the dead know how to accuse.”
- Busoni: “Tell me who deserves a share.”
- Caderousse: “Morrel fought for him. His father starved. Mercédès waited until
  hope became cruelty.”
- Busoni: “And the others?”
- Caderousse: “They became rich.”
- Busoni: “Then truth has appraised the stone.”

**Turn:** Edmond learns the original crime matured into respectable lives.

## Page 28 — The Handwriting

**Purpose:** Give Edmond proof that memory alone did not invent Villefort's
betrayal.

**Layout:** Four panels. Lord Wilmore requests the register; official leaves;
Edmond finds accusation and note; close on handwriting.

**Text:**

- Clerk: “The English gentleman seeks Abbé Faria's record?”
- Wilmore: “And the prisoner in the neighboring cell.”
- Clerk: “Edmond Dantès. Dangerous Bonapartist.”
- Wilmore: “According to whom?”
- Clerk: “The deputy prosecutor signed it.”
- Wilmore: “May I read in private?”
- Clerk: “The dead keep no secrets.”
- Edmond, alone: “Villefort.”
- Edmond: “Now neither do I.”

**Turn:** Villefort's own hand becomes the evidence Edmond will carry forward.

## Page 29 — Morrel & Son

**Purpose:** Turn financial collapse into a human deadline.

**Layout:** Four panels in the counting house: returned bills; Morrel protects
employees; Julie sees pistol case; Thomson & French representative arrives.

**Text:**

- Clerk: “The *Pharaon* is overdue. The insurers will not extend us again.”
- Morrel: “Pay the sailors' families first.”
- Clerk: “Then nothing remains.”
- Morrel: “My name remains.”
- Julie: “Father, why is the pistol case open?”
- Morrel: “Because I am cleaning what I should have sold.”
- Visitor: “Thomson & French holds your final notes.”
- Morrel: “Then you have come for the last day of Morrel & Son.”

**Turn:** Edmond finds the honorable man who defended him preparing to die
rather than default.

## Page 30 — The Red Purse

**Purpose:** Make the rescue a mission driven by Julie's courage.

**Layout:** Five panels. Anonymous letter; Julie chooses to obey; old house;
red purse; race home.

**Text:**

- Letter caption, exact readable document text: “Julie Morrel. Go alone to the
  old house in the Allées de Meilhan. Take the red silk purse from the
  mantelpiece. Return before eleven.”
- Emmanuel: “It could be a trap.”
- Julie: “Then someone built a trap that knows my father's deadline.”
- Emmanuel: “I am coming.”
- Julie: “It says alone.”
- Julie, finding purse: “This was Grandfather's.”
- Note inside purse: “For the debt of Morrel & Son.”
- Julie: “Run.”

**Turn:** The purse joins Edmond's anonymous power to the Morrel family's own
memory.

## Page 31 — Eleven O'Clock

**Purpose:** Cross-cut the financial rescue and the threatened suicide with
absolute clarity.

**Layout:** Six tightening panels: clock; Morrel writes; employees wait; Julie
runs; pistol raised; Emmanuel bursts in.

**Text:**

- Morrel, writing: “My dear Julie—”
- Clerk: “Ten minutes.”
- Creditor's agent: “I am sorry.”
- Morrel: “Do not be. A debt should arrive on time.”
- Julie, outside: “Open the gate!”
- Clock sound: “DONG”
- Morrel: “Forgive me.”
- Emmanuel, entering: “Monsieur Morrel!”
- Julie: “The debts are paid!”

**Turn:** The pistol lowers—but the impossible rescue is not finished.

## Page 32 — The *Pharaon*

**Purpose:** End Volume I with restoration, wonder, and Edmond choosing to give
life anonymously.

**Layout:** Three panels. Counting house erupts; harbor crowd points; full-width
final image of the new *Pharaon* entering beneath open sky while an unseen
Edmond watches from a departing boat.

**Text:**

- Clerk: “A ship at the harbor mouth!”
- Morrel: “Not now.”
- Sailor, outside: “The *Pharaon*!”
- Morrel: “The *Pharaon* was lost.”
- Julie: “Then look.”
- Emmanuel: “Same name. Same cargo. Every sailor paid.”
- Morrel: “Who could do this?”
- Edmond, small from the departing boat: “A friend.”

**Turn:** The volume closes on benevolent power. The action creates the moral
fork and leaves the reader to feel it without a final explanatory caption.

## Volume I Lettering Audit

- Page 1 contains the volume's only conventional caption: time and place.
- Page 30 contains readable story-world document text because the letter drives
  Julie's actions.
- No page uses a caption to explain Restoration politics, prison systems,
  navigation, finance, or the conspiracy.
- The hardest native-text pages are 6, 9–10, 13, 17–20, 27, and 30–32; each
  must be prototyped or generated with deliberate calm lettering areas.
