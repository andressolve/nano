## The Promise

This is a sweeping, dialogue-driven adventure about a gifted young sailor who
is betrayed, imprisoned, educated, reborn through escape and treasure, and
eventually discovers that revenge cannot obey him.

The reader should experience:

- the joy of Edmond's life before it is taken;
- the shock and precision of the betrayal;
- the physical impossibility of Château d'If;
- the exhilaration of friendship, learning, escape, and treasure;
- the fascination of the Count's theatrical mastery;
- the pleasure of plans and identities converging;
- the growing fear that innocent lives are entering his design;
- mercy as the final act of greater power.

## Audience

- Francisco, age 10.
- Sebastian, age 7.
- Both can follow dense plots, recurring characters, moral ambiguity, and
  consequential dialogue.
- Never talk down to them and never require them to fill omitted story logic.
- Darkness should be truthful but not lurid.

## Locked Format

- **3:2 landscape, 1536 × 1024.**
- Finished dialogue and captions baked into every page image.
- Reader supplies navigation and quiz only.
- Graphic direction: **Velvet Cinema**.

## Adaptation Form

Working series architecture: five volumes.

| Volume | Working title | Core movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | *Edmond* | Joy, betrayal, prison, Faria, escape, treasure, and benevolent return |
| II | *The Return* | Rome, the Count's legend, Paris entrance, and the first construction of the web |
| III | *The Web* | The hidden households connect; social, financial, and inherited fault lines activate |
| IV | *Judgment* | Morcerf falls, Mercédès reaches Edmond, Valentine is endangered, and revenge exceeds its columns |
| V | *Providence* | Benedetto, Villefort, Édouard, Danglars, pardon, restoration, and “wait and hope” |

These titles are working labels, not cover locks.

## Volume I Target

Working length: **32 landscape story pages**, plus cover and quiz.

Volume I begins with capability and happiness, not foreboding. Its ending is not
the treasure. It is the restored *Pharaon*: Edmond has learned that his new
power can give life before he chooses to use it for vengeance.

The emotional movement is:

> homecoming → promise → trap → erasure → friendship → new mind → death and
> rebirth → apparent omnipotence → a first act of grace.

## Page-Level Standard

Most pages are live scenes. Each page must contain a desire, obstacle,
discovery, reversal, decision, consequence, intimacy, wonder, or spectacle.

Historical and technical information appears only when a character:

- needs it;
- discovers it;
- hides it;
- challenges it;
- acts upon it;
- pays for it.

Captions may establish time, place, compression, or atmosphere. They may not
summarize the drama the images and dialogue should perform.
