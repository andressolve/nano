## Part I — The decisions that made the book

**The quarrel at its breaking point.** Volume II's quarrel (may you hurt
someone to protect someone?) became a trial: three faces, three blows, three
uses of the words, and banishment. The mandate's countermeasure — *the monk is
wrong for a reason the reader respects* — carried the whole script gate. He
cannot see what Wukong sees; to him every fallen disguise is a still body.
Every critic called Movement II the heart of the book.

**The seeing rule.** The reader sees what Wukong sees, always, and only inside
the flare of his red-ringed eyes. Round 1 found the third blow staged in plain
sight (the monk would have seen a shrieking skull); round 2 found the hollow
skin visible to everyone. Fixed by one rule applied on pp 6, 10, 11, 15:
Wukong's view is framed as his; the monk sees a body, or has his face buried in
the horse's neck. **For IV: any sight-based secret needs its rule written into
the writer notes and made blocking on the cards.**

**Faces over faces.** The demon wears three kind ones; the Yellow Robe wears a
demon's over a star's; the monk is made a tiger and keeps his rule inside it;
Bajie wears a liar's and takes it off. Nobody says it. The payoff is p45:
Wukong sees the monk's face inside the tiger.

**Going back as the climax.** The shoe turned round: the pig who caused the
banishment flies east and has to tell the truth to get help; the monkey who
was sent away decides to go back. "He waited for the pig" (p40) is the
reconciliation, done as action.

**Cuts that paid.** The whole Baoxiang kingdom (princess, king, court,
framing), the trip to Heaven and the constellations, the princess disguise (no
repeated trick from II), the ginseng tree and the four sages. Seven locks of a
ceiling of eight.

## Part II — What the script critics taught (6 rounds)

Round 1 (8 findings): the vow had to be planted in the monk's own mouth ("I've
never let anything die for my supper") so the tiger's refusal means something;
the monk's change of heart had to happen on the page (at the pillar, to
Wujing); the villain's fear had to be planted (the shutter against the sky,
"nobody ever comes looking for me"); the timeline had to count three days.
Round 2: one seeing rule; Wujing's broken promise as a choice; the Yellow Robe
named before Wukong uses the name; the pagoda door geography. Round 3: **split
p28** so the monk's turn and the tiger never share a page; give Wukong a reason
to wait for the pig. Round 4: the luggage pole on the last page contradicted
the monk's ruling. Round 5: Wukong had no reaction to the apology ("I heard you
the first time"). Round 6 approved. **Lesson, again:** after the first strong
rounds take only mandatory findings; each round found exactly one real thing.

## Part III — References (30 locks, ~62 candidates, 2 gate rounds)

- **Over-specified ornaments cost candidates.** The plan asked for a
  "seven-storey" pagoda and "seven-point" stars on the Yellow Robe's robe; the
  critics counted and blocked. Ruled nonblocking (five to seven storeys; any
  small star). Same family as method/07's counted objects: never write a number
  into an uncounted detail.
- **The reveal view on a disguise sheet** (the old woman, view d: a skull inside
  a living face) failed six candidates; the living disguise passed every time.
  Relocked on views (a)–(c) by a fresh critic; pages draw the reveal from the
  true-shape sheet and words.
- **The returning dragon** failed six candidates on equine snout and the wound
  view; relocked as Volume II's approved sheet unchanged (same character,
  already gated); the wound is drawn from words.
- **The horse's wound view** (a red band) was the whole-cast gate's only
  finding; relocked on the lifted-hoof pose, wound as page instruction.
- Wukong locked on his first candidate from the Volume II anchor.

## Part IV — Pages (49 pages)

- Most pages landed first or second candidate.
- **Safety filter (new):** p28 (the monk and Wujing tied to a pillar, "bruised",
  tears) was refused three times by the in-app tool — no candidate, driver
  stopped. Restaged in storybook terms (sitting against the pillar inside one
  loose rope, no bruises, no tears) in the prompt only; passed first time.
- **Red wound read as blood** on pp 32–34 (continuity gate); edited to a dry
  charcoal slash. The p32 edit then drifted out of the register (glossy oil,
  bright fires) — regenerated with a register-emphasis section.
- **Wrong name lettered** on p45 v2 ("Wujing" for "Wukong") — full reset.
- **Bajie drift** (whole-book gate): two different pig faces across the book,
  the softer one with no tusks and sometimes a cap; edited on eight pages.
  Bajie's sheet was attached on only some pages — pages drawing him from words
  drifted toward a cartoon pig.

## Part V — For Volume IV

Threads this volume planted or left open:

- **The new promise:** "if you tell me a face is a demon's, I'll believe you,
  even when I can't see it." / "Then we'll be wrong together." Volume IV can
  test it — the first time Wukong is wrong.
- **The band is still on.** The monk knows no words to take it off.
- **The horse talks** "only when it matters", and refuses to talk again (p48).
- **Bajie told the truth unasked** and carried the whole pole; Wukong now waits
  for him.
- **Wujing** said "I didn't keep you safe" and was told nobody could have.
- **The Yellow Robe** went home to the sky; the White Bone Lady is bones.

Source ground for IV (Journey to the West ch. 32 onward), as evidence only: the
Gold and Silver Horned Kings and the gourd that answers to your name (ch 32–35);
the Red Boy and his fire (ch 40–42); the Cart Slow kingdom and the three Daoist
immortals (ch 44–46). The gourd that swallows whoever answers to his name is
the natural next test of the promise: a trick Wukong cannot see through.
