starting with `00-CRAFT-MANDATE.md`. This file carries only what is specific to
this volume. **It relaxes no method rule.** If a future editor adds a clause
that lowers a number, widens a tolerance, or softens a gate, that clause must

## The adaptation stance — say it first

> This volume is an adaptation. Fidelity to the source is not a goal, not a
> tiebreaker, and not a defense. Every element earns its place by what it does
> for this reader in this book, or it is cut.

## The specific failure this volume must avoid

**The fool monk and the flawless monkey.**

The White Bone Demon is the most famous episode of the whole source, and it
is told badly almost everywhere: a monk too stupid to see what is in front of
him, a monkey who is simply right, and a lesson ("listen to the clever one")
that makes the monk contemptible and the book a sermon. Then the second demon
becomes a fight-of-the-week, and the return a formality.

The countermeasure is fixed here and enforced at the script gate:

- **The monk is wrong for a reason the reader respects.** He cannot see what
  Wukong sees. He sees a girl with a basket, a mother looking for her
  daughter, an old man praying, and a monkey with an iron staff. His rule —
  nobody may be killed for him — is the same rule that made him brave in
  Volume II. A pig he trusts tells him the monkey is tricking him, and the pig
  has been tricked by Wukong himself (the bride, Volume II). Every time the
  monk says the words, the reader must understand why he does.
- **The reader sees what Wukong sees.** The White Bone Lady's true shape is
  shown to the reader before the first disguise and under every disguise.
  The suspense is never "is she a demon?"; it is "why won't he believe him?"
  and "what will Wukong do the third time?"
- **Wukong is not flawless.** The first time, he swings before he says one
  word. The third time he asks first, is refused, and strikes anyway to save
  the monk's life, knowing what it will cost. That choice is the book.
- **Everyone in this book wears a face over a face.** The demon wears three
  kind ones; the Yellow Robe wears a demon's over a star's; the monk is made
  to wear a tiger's and keeps his rule inside it; Bajie wears a liar's and
  takes it off. The monk learns to see Wukong's. Nobody states this.
- **Going back is the climax, not a bridge.** The pig who caused the
  banishment flies back to ask for help and has to tell the truth to get it;
  the monkey who was sent away has to decide to go back for the one who sent
  him. Both choices are dramatized in full, on the page.
- **Wukong is never above it.** The series blocker stands: he is loud, hurt,
  delighted, insulted, on every page he appears. The wound under the noise in
  this book — *the one person he chose to stay for chose to send him away* —
  is visible at least once per movement he is in.
