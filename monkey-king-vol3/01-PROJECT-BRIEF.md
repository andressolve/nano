# Monkey King, Volume III: Sent Away — Project Brief

**Form:** cinematic illustrated novel, portrait 1024 × 1536, 48 pages.
**Audience:** Francisco (10) and Sebastian (7).
**Source:** *Journey to the West* (Xiyouji), attributed to Wu Cheng'en, 16th
century, chapters 27–31 (the White Bone Demon; the Yellow Robe Demon; Bajie
sent to Flower-Fruit Mountain). Consulted in the Anthony C. Yu and W. J. F.
Jenner translations as evidence of incident, never for wording. Sequel to
`../monkey-king-vol2/` and `../monkey-king-vol1/`.

---

## The engine

**The only one who can see the demon's real face is the one the monk has just
sent home — and someone has to go back for him.**

Volume I asked whether anything could stop him. Volume II asked whether
anything could keep him. This one asks what happens when the person he
stayed for sends him away. People still tell this part of the story because
it is the one where the hero is right and is punished for it, and everyone
who has ever been blamed for something they did not do feels it in the
stomach. The fun is a shape-changing demon, a tiger in a cage, a horse that
talks once, a pig on a cloud. The heart is that the monkey goes back.

**If the protagonist reads as calm and above it all for more than a page at a
stretch, the engine has been written out.** That is a script-gate blocker.

## The spine

Months west of the sand river, on a ridge of white rock with no village and no
food, a demon made of white bone watches the travellers from a crag: she has
heard that one bite of this monk will let her live forever. Wukong flies off
to fetch peaches and leaves the monk with Bajie and Wujing. The demon puts on
a kind girl's face and walks down with a basket of rice. Wukong comes back,
sees the skull under the face — the smoke of a furnace in Heaven got into his
eyes long ago, and since then no disguise can fool them — and brings the staff
down without a word. The demon slips out of the girl's empty skin and away,
and all the monk sees is a girl on the ground. Bajie, who has been bossed by
the monkey for months and who was once tricked by him in a bride's face, tells
the monk the monkey is tricking him now; the monk says the three words and
the gold band crushes Wukong's head. It happens again with an old woman
looking for her daughter. The third time, an old man praying, Wukong asks the
monk first and is told no, and the old man's hand is reaching for the monk;
Wukong strikes anyway, faster than she can slip out, and the old man falls
into a heap of white bones with her name cut into the skull. Bajie says it is
a trick. The monk says the words, and then the worst words: go, you are not
my disciple. Wukong makes three more of himself so the monk cannot turn away
from his bow, tells Wujing to walk at the monk's back, and flies home to
Flower-Fruit Mountain crying, with the band still on.

At home he is king again, and happy, he tells everyone, and every evening he
climbs the peak and looks west. On the road the monk walks alone into a golden
pagoda in a pine wood and is caught by the Yellow Robe, a demon nobody can
beat: Bajie runs, Wujing is tied up, and the demon, who thinks a monk who will
not hurt anything is the funniest thing he has ever caught, turns him into a
tiger to see how long that lasts. The tiger will not touch the meat. At night
the white horse becomes a dragon again and is wounded. At dawn, for the first
time since he joined, the horse speaks: go and get our big brother. Bajie, who
caused it all, flies east, lies to Wukong, is seen through, and tells the
truth. Wukong goes back. He beats the Yellow Robe, sees the star under the
demon's face and lets him flee into the sky rather than leave the monk one
minute longer, and turns the tiger back into a man. The monk, on his knees,
says he could not see the demon's face and should have believed the one who
could. The last page is the road west, and the monk walking beside the
monkey instead of behind him; when the next stranger comes smiling down the
road, the monk looks first at Wukong.

## What this volume is

- A complete story: the ridge to the road west again, the company whole. No
  cliffhanger the reader must resolve elsewhere.
- The quarrel at its breaking point: the monk's rule against the monkey's
  sight, and a third member of the company — the pig — whose jealousy breaks
  it, and whose courage mends it.
- Dramatic irony from the first disguise: the reader always sees what Wukong
  sees.
- The three tricks of Volume I, used for new reasons: the cloud to leave and
  to come back; the shape to make four of himself for one bow and to undo a
  demon's shape-spell; the staff, which he asks permission to use.
- A book where the horse speaks once, the pig is brave once, and the monk is
  wrong once, on the page, and says so.

## What this volume is not

- Not a demon catalogue. Two demons; each changes what Wukong or the monk
  chooses.
- Not a book where the monk is a fool. He is wrong for reasons the reader
  respects, and he is the one who refuses the tiger's meat.
- Not a sermon about obedience or about listening to the clever one. The
  band is still a mistake, and what brings Wukong back is not the band.
- Not a recap of Volumes I–II. Every returning rule and face is
  re-introduced in story; a reader of this book alone must follow every beat.

**Collapses.** The local spirits Wukong summons to witness the third blow
**merge into Wukong's own speed**: he is faster than the demon's escape.
The Princess of Baoxiang, her father's court, and the plot to frame the monk
**collapse into the Yellow Robe's cruelty**: he makes the monk a tiger to
test his rule, not to fool a king. The Jade Emperor and the star-roll in
Heaven **collapse into Wukong's eyes**: he sees the star under the demon's
face and says it, and the star goes home. The provoking lie Bajie tells in the
source ("the demon insulted you") **becomes the truth Bajie tells**.

**Cut to nobody**: Guanyin (named once, as the reason the company exists),
the four sages and their test, the ginseng-fruit tree and the Temple of the
Five Villages, the Princess of Baoxiang, the King of Baoxiang and his court,
the Yellow Robe's children, the mountain gods, the hunters of Flower-Fruit
Mountain, the Jade Emperor, the twenty-eight constellations.

**Source names in these chapters:** about twenty. **Locked here: seven.**

## The audience promise

**What these readers already enjoy.** A hero they love who is right when the
grown-up is wrong. A shape-changing villain they can see through. A gross
basket (rice into maggots, noodles into frogs). A big brother sent away and a
little brother who has to go and get him. A tiger. A horse that talks once.
A fight nobody else can win.

**The repeatable pleasures.** The demon's three faces and the reader's
"it's HER!". Wukong's mouth, now in real pain. Bajie's appetite, now with
consequences. The band — and the reader's dread every time the monk's hands
come together.

**The emotional promise.** The reader knows what it is to be blamed for
something you did to help, and to be asked, afterwards, to help the person
who blamed you. Wukong goes back. The reader sees why, and that it is not the
band.

**The central dramatic question.** *Will he come back?* Yes — and the monk
has to see his real face to deserve it.

**Complexity budget.** Seven names, two of them new. One realm: the road, a
mountain home, a pagoda in a pine wood. One time jump: the days at home.
Zero assumed knowledge: who everyone is, the band and its three words, the
no-flying rule, the bride trick, the eyes — each re-introduced in story before
the plot depends on it.

**What this project refuses to become.** A sermon about trust. A list of
demons. A book where the monk is stupid. A book where Wukong is perfect.

## Length

Working count **48**, reached by cutting: five source chapters to six
movements; the whole Baoxiang kingdom plot cut; the trip to Heaven cut. The
count follows the script and will not shrink by compression.

## The reader

A first-time reader who will never read the source. They must never have to
solve the adaptation, supply a missing causal step, or recognize a character
the book has not introduced. "Recoverable," "eventually clear," and
"understandable after looking back" are revision flags, not passing grades.
