# Settings and Objects

Mineral Ink, inherited from Volumes I–II: earth reds, ochres, malachite and
azurite on toned paper, black ink line. Each location is a variation on it;
the ridge is drained to bone and ash, the pagoda is gold outside and black
inside.

Each location names one restrained accent that does dramatic work.

---

## Locations

### 1 · WHITE TIGER RIDGE — pp 1–19
A high dry ridge of chalk-white rock and bleached grass, a few twisted dead
pines, a narrow pale road along the spine, wide hazy blue valleys falling
away on both sides. Hard flat midday light, short shadows; the afternoon
(pp 12–19) goes long and amber. The White Bone Lady's crag is a tooth of white
rock above the road, its top a cave-mouth. Nobody owns this place; it is
hungry.
**Accent:** the **peaches** Wukong brings back — four big red-gold peaches,
the only warm round thing on the ridge; dropped in the dust on p5 and left
there. Never counted in text.

### 2 · FLOWER-FRUIT MOUNTAIN — pp 19–22, 36–40
Volume I's green peak on the eastern coast (`refs/parents/v1-set-peak.png`,
`v1-set-cave.png`): peach trees, the waterfall, the Water Curtain Cave behind
it with its stone seats, the split stone on the peak where he was born, the
old flagpole on the peak with a rag of **faded pink banner** — the red banner
of Volume I bleached by five hundred years. Wet green, white water, warm
light; the evenings orange over the western hills.
**Accent:** the faded pink banner rag on the peak, which he stands under to
look west.

### 3 · THE PINE WOOD AND THE GOLDEN PAGODA — pp 23–34, 41–46
A dark wood of tall black-green pines in deep shadow; in a clearing, a
tall tiered pagoda (five to seven storeys; the count is not a story object) whose curved roofs are plated with **gold** that gleams
through the trees; a heavy black-iron gate; a clear stony stream running past
the clearing (p33–34). Inside the pagoda, one great dark hall of black stone,
lit by a few iron lamps: a wide stone bed heaped with furs, a thick black
pillar, and against one wall an **iron cage** taller than a man. Owned by
somebody who likes to sleep and to own things.
**Accent:** the **gold** of the pagoda roof outside — beautiful bait — and, in
the black hall, a single shaft of moonlight from a high window falling on the
cage.

### 4 · THE ROAD WEST — pp 47–49
The morning after: the pine wood behind them, a long road going west through
green hills into far blue mountains. Clean early light.
**Accent:** the long shadows of the five travellers, side by side.
