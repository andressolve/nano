## Blind read
The monkey bows deeply to the departing monk, framing the gesture as repayment for the robe the monk gave him; the monk refuses to look back. The monkey then pulls out three hairs and blows on them, creating five bowing copies so that a monkey confronts the monk wherever he turns. In the final panel, the monkey quietly recruits Wujing to walk behind the monk and keep the pig from influencing him, and Wujing agrees. The large middle panel owns the page: the monk is its visual center, but the monkey owns the action and the turn through the encircling duplicates and his next instruction.

## Findings

### Finding C2
- Observation: The dominant middle panel shows five bowing Wukongs around the monk, rather than four.
- Material reader harm: Five Wukongs break the caption’s “three hairs” transformation logic, which requires the original Wukong plus three copies, and make the page’s central clever answer visually contradictory.
- Redraw justification: The wrong count is the dominant panel’s core story mechanism, so the farewell trick does not land as scripted and the error is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
