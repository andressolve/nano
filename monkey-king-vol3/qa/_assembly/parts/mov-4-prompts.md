## PAGE 20 — The king comes home

### Reader turn
Wukong lands on his own green peak and hundreds of his monkeys come pouring out from behind the waterfall to meet him.

### Ordered moments
1. One dominant illustration, about 70% of the page, a single panel: Flower-Fruit Mountain, a wet malachite peak on the eastern sea, peach trees, a waterfall roaring white down its face over the cave mouth. Hundreds of young brown monkeys stream out from behind the falling water and scramble up the rocks from lower left toward the summit at right. On the summit, beside the split stone and an old wooden flagpole carrying a small rag of faded pale-pink banner, Wukong is just landing, small but clear, golden against the green, one foot down and one up, tail out, the thin gold band glinting on his brow. Warm light, white spray.
2. The prose field, a matte parchment field in the open sky above the peak, upper part of the page, over plain sky only, never over Wukong, the monkeys, the waterfall, or the flagpole. Three paragraphs, left-aligned.

### Exact strings
PROSE FIELD 1: `Flower-Fruit Mountain stood on the eastern sea where it always had, green and loud, with the waterfall roaring down in front of the cave where he had once been king.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `The monkeys saw him from the trees. They screamed. They came pouring out from behind the waterfall, hundreds of them, the children and grandchildren of the monkeys he had left, who had heard stories about him every night of their lives.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `The Monkey King had come home.`

Render exactly these 3 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
Wukong: as the attached sheet's views (a)–(c) — short heart-shaped pale face inside golden-tawny fur, large round gold eyes with a thin red ring, two small upright cupped monkey ears, swept-back golden crown ruff; small and wiry, long tail; plain undyed hemp robe belted with a cord over the tiger-skin kilt, mended patch at the left shoulder, bare feet; the thin plain gold band flush on his brow, just above the eyes; the tiny red-and-gold needle behind his right ear. The flower crown is not on yet.
The monkeys: as the attached ensemble sheet — young, brown, bare, many sizes, all alike in kind; no clothing, no gold, no band.

The banner is a small faded pale-pink rag, never bright red, never a full banner, no glyphs. No staff in hand. No balloons, no captions, no sound cues; nothing lettered on the flag, stone, or rocks. ## PAGE 21 — The band won't come off

### Reader turn
Crowned with flowers on his old throne, Wukong declares himself free, cannot pull the band off, and will not let the monkeys take the robe.

### Ordered moments
1. Top strip, about 25%: inside the Water Curtain Cave, the white waterfall falling across the cave mouth behind, light coming through the water. Wukong sits on a rough stone throne at left-centre, one foot up on the seat, grinning; two or three young brown monkeys reach up to set a crown of flowers and leaves on his head, on top of the gold band, which still shows beneath. His balloon upper left beside his mouth, tail to his mouth.
2. Dominant middle panel, about 50%: Wukong at centre, close and large, face toward us, straining, exactly two hands gripping the gold band at his temples and hauling upward; the band does not move, flush on his brow; the flower crown knocked askew above it. Behind him a chain of young brown monkeys, each holding the one in front, pulls on his waist like a tug-of-war, feet braced. His balloon upper left beside his open mouth, tail to his mouth.
3. Bottom strip, about 25%: a small young brown monkey on the LEFT, silent, tugging at the sleeve of Wukong's hemp robe. Wukong on the RIGHT, fierce, yanking the sleeve back with one hand, the other hand pressed flat over the mended patch on his left shoulder; flower crown on, band showing. His balloon upper right beside his mouth, tail to his mouth.

WUKONG: `That's right, your king is home! No monk, no magic words, and no walking. Not ever again!`
WUKONG: `And this thing comes off too. Pull! Pull harder! It won't come off.`
WUKONG: `Leave the robe alone. My monk sewed that patch himself.`
