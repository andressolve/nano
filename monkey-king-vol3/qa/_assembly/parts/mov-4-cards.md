## Page 20 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All three prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in script order, in one prose field, with no extra text anywhere (no balloon, caption, sound cue, or writing on the flag or rocks). A paragraph that does not read, is missing, doubled, or reordered is blocking; a paragraph that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word; the words and their order are what must match. The run length of a repeated letter in any sound cue is nonblocking (none is scripted), and the count of background props or crowd figures is never a transcription matter.

### C2 — The homecoming owns the page
One illustration plainly owns the page in the illustrated-prose mode: the green peak, the white waterfall, the monkeys pouring out toward the small golden figure on the summit. Blocking if the reader cannot find the monkeys rushing to him, or the prose field covers Wukong or the rush. How many monkeys is a crowd and is never counted.

### C3 — Wukong and the band
Wukong reads as the golden monkey in the hemp robe, small but recognizable. The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in is blocking: band on. A missing band, a crown, or Wukong drawn twice is blocking; the exact width of the band at this distance is not.

### C4 — The monkeys
Every other monkey is brown, bare, and young. Blocking if any monkey wears clothing, gold, or a band, or reads as a second Wukong. Repeated monkey faces and loose background anatomy are nonblocking.

### C5 — Integrity
No blood; no absent companion appears. The banner rag, if shown, is faded pink rather than a bright red flag; its absence or exact shape is nonblocking.

Dramatic mode; the middle panel owns the page by eye: Wukong hauling at the band with both hands, the monkeys pulling him like a tug-of-war, and the band plainly staying on. Blocking if the band appears lifted, loosened, or off, or if the reader cannot tell he is failing to remove it.

### C3 — Attribution
Wukong is the only speaker. Each balloon hangs by its tail from Wukong's mouth in its own panel, his mouth visible; in the last panel he stands on the right with the balloon on his side. Blocking if a balloon points at a monkey or reads before the action it answers.

### C4 — Wukong, band, crown, robe
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in is blocking: band on, still showing under the flower crown. He wears the hemp robe in every panel and keeps it; in the last panel one hand snatches the sleeve back and one covers the shoulder patch. ### C5 — Monkeys and integrity
Every other monkey is brown, bare, young: no clothing, no gold, no band, no balloon. Wukong drawn once per panel; the focal hands on the band are exactly two. The number of monkeys in the chain or at the crowning is nonblocking. No blood.
