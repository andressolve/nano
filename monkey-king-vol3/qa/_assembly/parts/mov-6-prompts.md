## PAGE 30 — the tiger turns from the meat

### Reader turn
The monk, a tiger in an iron cage, turns his face to the wall and will not touch the meat, while the shutter is closed tight against the sky.

### Ordered moments
1. One dominant illustration, about 70% of the page, close and tall: the black stone hall lit by a single iron lamp. Centre-right, an iron cage taller than a man, door shut. Inside, a thin lean tiger sits upright with his front paws together, his head and face turned toward the stone wall at the back of the cage, away from the meat. On the cage floor in front of him, one untouched joint of meat on a bone. High above, the hall's one window with a wooden shutter closed tight over it: no sky, no moonlight in the hall. Far left, deep in shadow, Wujing tied with rope to the thick black pillar, head turned toward the cage, watching; silent, no balloon.
2. The prose field: one matte parchment field across the lower part of the page, over the plain black stone wall below the cage, never over the cage, the tiger, the meat or Wujing's face. Four paragraphs, left-aligned.

### Exact strings
PROSE FIELD 1: `The Yellow Robe put the tiger in an iron cage and threw in a joint of meat.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `The monk had never eaten meat in his life. Now he had a tiger's hunger, which is enormous, and a tiger's claws and teeth.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `He turned his face to the wall and sat with his front paws together.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `All day the meat lay there. When the demon went to bed, it was still there, and he did not laugh about it quite so loudly. Before he lay down, he pulled the shutter across the high window, as he did every night, so that no light from the sky could fall on him.`

Render exactly these 4 strings once each, in this order, as four paragraphs of one prose field; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
- Sanzang as the tiger (state pp 29–46, attached sheet): lean long-legged orange tiger with black stripes, thin from not eating; the torn red kasaya with its gold-thread grid hanging over his left shoulder and knotted at his throat; the monk's calm dark almond eyes, never tiger-yellow; sitting upright, paws together; never snarling, never eating, never a human face.
- Wujing: tall gaunt slate blue-gray ogre, long hollow face, wild red hair and red beard, sand-brown sleeveless tunic, rope round him at the pillar; no skulls anywhere on him. Small, in shadow.

### Consequential exclusions
Wukong is absent. The Yellow Robe is absent (gone to bed, off the page). The horse and the dragon are absent. The shutter is closed; no moonlight enters. The meat is whole, untouched, a single joint. No skull or necklace on Wujing. No blood. Wujing is not attached; drawn from words. No balloons, no captions; nothing lettered on the cage or walls.
