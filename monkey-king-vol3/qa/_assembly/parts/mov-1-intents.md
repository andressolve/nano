## PAGE 1

Illustrated prose. The book opens on the road west along the spine of a white, empty ridge: the monk on his white horse, the pig and the river ogre sharing the luggage pole, the pig complaining, and far ahead on a boulder the monkey with the gold band on his brow, shading his eyes. The prose owns the page and carries the turn: who these travellers are, the band and its three words, and the monkey's eyes that see the real face under any face. The picture is wide, bright and drained to bone; nobody speaks. What changes is that the reader now holds the two rules the whole book turns on, the band and the eyes. The reader turns because the ridge is too empty, and something on it is watching.

## PAGE 2

Spectacle. From the black mouth of a cave at the top of a tooth of white rock, the White Bone Lady in her true shape, a tall lady of bone with a skull for a face, green pinpoints in its hollows, long black hair and gray-violet burial silk, draws one finger across her teeth and looks down. Far below, tiny on the pale road, the four travellers and the horse walk toward her. She owns the page; the travellers are small and unaware. The one turn: she wants the monk, because one bite of him means she will never die. The caption names the waiting and her one line names the want. The reader turns because the company is walking straight toward her.

## PAGE 3

Dramatic. The travellers stop on the bare ridge. The monk is so hungry he can't see straight; Wukong, up on a dead stump, finds no village but knows of peaches to the south. The dominant moment is Wukong on a puff of cloud, pointing at the seated monk like a teacher: sit on that rock and don't move, I can't fly you but I can fly peaches, while Wujing promises to stand at the monk's back. Then Wukong is gone, a streak south, and Bajie throws down his end of the pole and grumbles about who made the monkey the boss; the monk answers mildly. The turn: Wukong flies for peaches and leaves an order behind. The grumble stays light. The reader turns because the monk is alone on the ridge with two guards and an empty stomach.

## PAGE 4

Dramatic. Up on the crag, the White Bone Lady draws a pretty girl's face down over her skull like a mask; a peach-pink jacket, a black plait and a lidded basket grow onto her. On the road the girl walks up smiling and offers the hungry monk rice and noodles; he rises, palms together, glad there is no meat, because he has never let anything die for his supper. The girl and the monk own the page; the offer is the turn. Then the girl lifts the lid, two tiny green pinpoints in her eyes for the reader alone, while Bajie leans over the monk's shoulder and accepts for him. Nobody on the road suspects anything. High behind them, a small streak of cloud comes fast from the south. The reader turns because the monk is about to eat from the demon's basket.

## PAGE 5

Spectacle. Wukong drops out of the sky onto the road, the peaches tumbling from his arms, his red staff already swinging down, the red rings of his gold eyes flaring. The reader is shown what only his eyes see: through the smiling girl's face, the faint pale ghost of a skull, and bone fingers under her hands. To the monk and the pig on the left she is still only a smiling girl; their mouths hang open at a monkey attacking her, too late to stop him, while Wujing reaches from behind. Wukong owns the page. The one turn: he comes back, sees the skull under the face, and swings without a word. The staff has not landed. The reader turns because the staff is falling.
