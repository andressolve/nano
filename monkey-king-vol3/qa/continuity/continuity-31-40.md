# Visual-continuity gate — pages 31–40

APPROVED

Pages 31–40 remain in the same book. The sequence consistently preserves the
Mineral Ink register: visible brush-and-ink contours, toothed-paper texture,
pooled flat mineral washes, firm blacks, and dimensional cast light without
slipping into glossy or airbrushed rendering. The cool moonlit pagoda sequence,
clean morning stream, wet malachite Flower-Fruit Mountain, and long orange
sea-light form a legible lighting progression rather than a palette drift.

Recurring identities and states hold against the approved locks. The Third
Prince remains the same white, pale-blue-maned, sea-green-eyed figure across
dragon and horse forms, with the clean foreleg wound carried into the horse
state. Bajie retains his pig head, broad body, black clothes, blue sash, rake,
and lumpy cloud. Wujing remains gaunt, slate-skinned, red-haired, bound, and
free of skulls; he does not collide with the much larger indigo Yellow Robe in
mustard star-patterned robes and black armour. Sanzang's tiger remains calm,
upright, and marked by the torn red-and-gold kasaya. Wukong retains the short
monkey face, gold fur and eyes, thin gold band, patched hemp robe, tiger-skin
kilt, red staff, and the flower-crown home state; the young brown bare monkeys
remain distinct from him.

The black-stone pagoda hall, gold-roofed exterior, cage, waterfall cave,
bridge, throne, and western sea approach all remain recognizable continuations
of their approved setting and object locks. Lettering weight and rounded cream
balloons are stable across the dialogue pages; serif narration remains a
consistent separate register. Minor shifts in cage geometry, crowd repetition,
and incidental prop detail do not confuse the consequential object, setting,
character identity, story state, or reading.
