## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without outside documentation. The supernatural threat, the travelers’ relationships, the reason Wukong attacks, and the reason Sanzang punishes and distrusts him are all understandable at reading pace.

## Retell of the story so far

Months into a westward journey, the monk Sanzang travels a mountain road with three disciples: Wukong, Bajie, and Wujing. Sanzang rides a white horse, Bajie and Wujing carry the luggage, and Wukong scouts ahead. Wukong wears a gold band that can tighten painfully when Sanzang says certain words, though Sanzang has promised to use them only when necessary. Wukong also keeps a magical red staff behind his ear in needle form.

A skeletal demon watches the party from the ridge and says that eating the monk will grant immortality. When Sanzang grows hungry, Wukong sees peaches on a distant southern mountain. He tells Sanzang to remain on a rock, leaves Wujing to guard him, and flies off to fetch fruit.

While Wukong is away, the demon disguises herself as a young woman carrying rice and noodles. She offers the food to Sanzang. He politely declines meat, while Bajie eagerly encourages him to accept her hospitality. Wukong returns, sees the skull beneath the disguise, and attacks immediately. The apparent girl collapses, but her spirit escapes. Her basket spills open to reveal that the noodles are frogs, confirming that she was not human.

Sanzang believes Wukong has murdered an innocent girl. Bajie worsens the misunderstanding by insisting Wukong can create false evidence and is trying to keep Sanzang from speaking the tightening words. Sanzang recites them. The gold band crushes Wukong’s head with pain until he begs Sanzang to stop. Wukong insists that the girl was a demon, but Sanzang says he saw only a girl with a basket.

The same demon returns disguised as the girl’s elderly mother, asking whether anyone has seen her daughter. Bajie immediately treats this as proof that Wukong killed a real person. Wukong recognizes the same skull beneath the new face, but Sanzang forbids him to attack. The old woman asks Sanzang to dismount and help search. As he reaches toward her, Wukong strikes again. The woman falls and her spirit escapes once more. Wukong asks whether Sanzang saw what happened, but Sanzang still believes he saw an old mother searching for her daughter.

## Cast

- **Sanzang:** A young, bald monk in layered yellow and red robes, usually carrying a ringed monk’s staff and riding a white horse. He is the protected master and intended prey. He is compassionate and courteous, but his trust in appearances makes him vulnerable to the demon and puts him in conflict with Wukong.

- **Wukong:** A lean, golden-brown monkey man with a long tail, a gold band around his head, ragged pale clothes, a spotted hide wrap, and a red-and-gold staff that can shrink to needle size. He is the swift scout and strongest defender. He alone sees through the demon’s disguises, but his speed and refusal to explain make his correct actions look murderous.

- **Bajie:** A stout pig man with pink ears and snout, a bare belly, dark robes, a blue sash, and a rake. He carries luggage, complains, loves food, and readily believes the worst of Wukong. His accusations push Sanzang toward punishing and distrusting Wukong.

- **Wujing:** A very tall, blue-gray man with wild red hair and beard, rough brown clothing, and a crescent-bladed polearm. He carries luggage and agrees to guard Sanzang while Wukong is gone. He is present as a quieter, steadier disciple but has not yet driven the conflict.

- **The demon:** A green-eyed skeleton with long black hair, first dressed in torn gray-purple robes. She wants to eat Sanzang to gain immortality. She can send her spirit out of a slain disguise and return wearing another body: first a pretty young woman with a braid, pink blouse, blue skirt, and food basket; then a frail gray-haired old woman in brown clothes with a cane.

- **The white horse:** Sanzang’s mount and part of the traveling party. Bajie briefly worries that Wukong’s departure leaves the horse exposed, but it does not yet act independently.

## Causal chain

1. The demon wants immortality, so she targets Sanzang.
2. Sanzang becomes hungry, so Wukong leaves to fetch peaches.
3. Wukong’s absence gives the demon a chance to approach in a harmless human disguise.
4. Wukong returns, sees through the disguise, and attacks without explaining.
5. The spirit escapes, leaving what looks like a dead girl, so Sanzang believes Wukong killed an innocent person.
6. The transformed food reveals the deception, but Bajie claims Wukong fabricated that evidence.
7. Bajie’s accusation and Sanzang’s certainty in what he saw lead Sanzang to use the tightening words against Wukong.
8. The demon exploits the party’s distrust by returning as the girl’s grieving mother.
9. Sanzang prevents Wukong from acting at first and moves close enough to help her, increasing his danger.
10. Wukong attacks anyway and saves him again, but the spirit escapes and Sanzang once more sees only an apparently innocent victim.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

No page required backtracking or rereading. The only deliberate uncertainty was whether Sanzang would accept the disguised girl’s food and whether he would believe Wukong once the frogs appeared; both questions were resolved by the following panels. I did not misidentify any character. The demon is shown changing into each disguise before meeting the travelers, so the reader remains ahead of Sanzang rather than being confused with him.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text or speaker attribution failed. The tightening words on page 7 are unusual, but page 1 establishes their existence and the immediate transition to Wukong clutching his head on page 8 makes their function clear without rereading. Wukong’s dialogue, Sanzang’s dialogue, and Bajie’s interventions all attach cleanly to the correct speakers.

## What I expect next

The demon will return in a third disguise, probably as another relative or authority figure whose apparent innocence makes Wukong look even more violent. Wukong will recognize and attack her again, while Sanzang’s distrust—intensified by the two bodies he believes Wukong has left behind—will bring the master-disciple relationship to a breaking point. I expect the next turn to force proof of the demon’s identity or separate Wukong from the group just when Sanzang most needs his protection.
