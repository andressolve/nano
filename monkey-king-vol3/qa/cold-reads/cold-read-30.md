The story lands without outside documentation. I could follow the characters, the repeated deception, Wukong's dismissal, the party's resulting vulnerability, and the new danger through page 30 on a single normal-paced read.

## Retell of the story so far

A monk named Sanzang is traveling west on a white horse with three supernatural disciples: Wukong, the Monkey King; Bajie, a pig-man; and Wujing, a large river ogre. Wukong is immensely fast and carries a gold-banded staff that can shrink to a needle. Smoke trapped in his eyes long ago also lets him see through disguises. The monk controls him with three secret words that make the gold band around Wukong's head tighten painfully.

A skeletal demon waits on a mountain ridge because she believes eating the monk will grant immortality. When the monk grows hungry and Wukong flies away to fetch peaches, the demon disguises herself as a young woman carrying rice and noodles. Wukong returns, sees the skull beneath her face, and strikes her. Her spirit slips out, leaving the apparent girl's body behind. The monk believes Wukong has murdered an innocent person. The food changing into maggots and frogs does not persuade him, especially after Bajie claims Wukong could have faked that too. The monk recites the headband words, causing Wukong terrible pain, and orders him never to do it again.

The demon returns as the girl's grieving old mother. Wukong recognizes her and strikes again; the spirit escapes again, so the monk sees only a second innocent-looking corpse. The monk warns Wukong that one more attack will end their relationship. The demon then returns as an old man looking for his wife and daughter. Wukong recognizes the same creature a third time and chooses to attack even though he knows the cost. This time he is faster than her escaping spirit and destroys her. Her body collapses into bones, and her skull bears the name White Bone Lady. Bajie immediately claims Wukong scratched the name there himself. The monk trusts what he thinks he saw over Wukong's explanation and dismisses Wukong from his service.

Wukong protests that the monk would already have died three times without him and asks for the headband to be removed, but the monk does not know how. Wukong bows farewell, asks Wujing to protect the monk and Bajie not to mislead him, then flies home in tears. At Flower-Fruit Mountain, a huge community of monkeys welcomes its returned king. Wukong declares that he is happy and free, yet cannot remove the headband, keeps the robe the monk patched for him, and watches the western horizon every evening while pretending he is only checking the weather.

Without Wukong, the remaining travelers are slower and hungrier. Bajie disappears with the begging bowl. The monk sends Wujing to find him, then enters a pagoda alone in hope of meeting monks and getting food. The pagoda actually belongs to a huge demon called the Yellow Robe. He captures and binds the monk. When the monk asks him to spare his friends, the demon laughs at the idea that a captive can set conditions.

Bajie and Wujing reach the pagoda and demand their master back. The Yellow Robe notices that the famous monkey guardian is absent, then defeats both rescuers almost instantly. Wujing is tied beside the monk and admits that he went after Bajie instead of guarding the monk's back as Wukong asked. The monk answers that he himself sent away the one person who might have saved him, finally acknowledging that the girl, mother, and father were the same threat Wukong warned him about.

The Yellow Robe mocks the monk's refusal to hurt anyone and says that everyone has a beast beneath the surface. He spits a magical spray over the monk and transforms him into a tiger. He cages the tiger and throws in meat, expecting hunger to break the monk's principles. The transformed monk refuses it, turns toward the wall, and holds his front paws together in prayer. The demon is less amused when the meat remains untouched. Before sleeping, he closes the high window so that no light from the sky can enter.

## Cast

- **Sanzang, the monk:** A young, slim, bald man in yellow robes with a red patterned outer robe. He rides a white horse, carries a ringed monk's staff and a woven pack, avoids meat and violence, and is the pilgrimage's master. He is compassionate and principled but too ready to trust appearances and Bajie's insinuations. By page 29 he has been transformed into a tiger that still wears the remnants of his robes.
- **Wukong, the Monkey King:** A lean golden-brown monkey man with a tail, amber eyes, a gold headband, patched pale robes, and a tiger-patterned wrap. He carries a red staff with gold ends, rides a cloud, can cross enormous distances, makes duplicates from his hairs, and sees through disguises. He is the party's strongest protector and is emotionally devoted to the monk despite their conflict.
- **Bajie:** A heavyset pig-man with pink skin, a snout, tusks, large ears, dark clothes, and a blue sash. He carries a rake and often complains about hunger or work. He is comic but materially unreliable: he accepts suspicious food, repeatedly undermines Wukong's truthful warnings, disappears with the begging bowl, and is quickly routed by the Yellow Robe.
- **Wujing:** A very tall, muscular blue-gray river ogre with long wild red hair and beard, ragged brown clothes, and a crescent-bladed polearm. He carries the luggage and tries to guard the monk. He is steadier than Bajie and loyal to Wukong's request, but the monk sends him away at the crucial moment and he is defeated and captured.
- **The white horse:** Sanzang's calm, blue-maned mount, visually present through most of the mountain journey and left outside the pagoda.
- **White Bone Lady:** A skeletal female demon with long black hair, green-lit eyes, and decayed gray-purple robes. She wants to eat the monk for immortality and assumes three convincing human forms: a young woman with a food basket, her grieving old mother, and an old male relative. Wukong destroys her on the third attempt.
- **The Yellow Robe:** A massive blue-skinned, black-haired and bearded demon with tusks, black armor, a fur mantle, and a gold robe patterned with pale stars. He rules the false pagoda, carries a broad curved sword, overwhelms Bajie and Wujing, and transforms Sanzang into a tiger in an attempt to expose a hidden appetite for violence and meat.
- **The monkeys of Flower-Fruit Mountain:** A large, lively community, including many young monkeys, who regard Wukong as their returned king and crown him with flowers.

## Causal chain

1. Sanzang becomes hungry, so Wukong leaves to fetch peaches.
2. Wukong's absence gives White Bone Lady the chance to approach in disguise with tempting food.
3. Wukong's special sight reveals the demon, but her ability to abandon each false body makes his rescues look like murders.
4. Bajie's accusations reinforce Sanzang's trust in appearances, so Sanzang punishes Wukong and forbids another strike.
5. White Bone Lady exploits that mistrust with two more disguises. Wukong kills her for good but knowingly triggers his own dismissal.
6. Wukong returns home, while Sanzang's party loses its fastest scout and strongest defender.
7. Hunger and Bajie's absence lead Sanzang to send Wujing away and enter the apparent pagoda alone.
8. The pagoda is the Yellow Robe's lair, so Sanzang is captured. With Wukong gone, Bajie and Wujing cannot rescue him and Wujing is captured too.
9. Captivity forces Sanzang to recognize that his three apparent innocents were one demon and that he expelled the protector who understood the danger.
10. The Yellow Robe tests Sanzang's moral identity by turning him into a hungry tiger and offering meat, but Sanzang refuses it.

## What I expect next

The closed high window and the emphasis on keeping out light suggest that moonlight, sunlight, or another celestial influence could undo or expose the Yellow Robe's magic. Bajie is still free and is the likeliest person to seek help. Because Wujing is captured and the party has now learned that it cannot manage without Wukong, I expect Bajie to travel to Flower-Fruit Mountain and ask Wukong to return. Wukong will probably resist out of hurt and pride, but his continued attachment to the monk and his nightly westward watch make a rescue inevitable. The immediate problem will be freeing Sanzang from the tiger form without harming him, followed by a confrontation with the Yellow Robe.
