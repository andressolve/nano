## Retell of the story so far

Months into a westward pilgrimage, the monk Sanzang travels with three unusual disciples: the Monkey King Wukong, the pig-man Bajie, and the blue river ogre Wujing. Wukong is powerful enough to cross the world in a somersault, but he must walk at his master's pace. Sanzang controls him with a gold headband and three secret words he has promised to use only when necessary. Wukong's supernatural sight is imperfect because smoke from a heavenly furnace once damaged his eyes, but he can still recognize hidden evil.

A skeletal demon called the White Bone Lady waits on the mountain ridge, hoping to eat Sanzang and gain immortality. When Sanzang becomes hungry, Wukong spots peaches to the south and flies to fetch them, leaving Wujing to guard the monk. The demon disguises herself as a young woman carrying rice and noodles. Sanzang politely accepts her kindness, and Bajie eagerly supports her, but Wukong returns, sees the skull beneath her face, and strikes before explaining. The demon escapes as mist and leaves the girl's body behind. Sanzang believes Wukong has murdered an innocent person. The basket's food changes into frogs and maggots, but Bajie claims Wukong may have staged that trick too.

Bajie feeds Sanzang's distrust by describing Wukong as a shameless shape-changer. Sanzang speaks the secret words, and Wukong's gold band crushes his head in pain. Although Wukong insists the girl was a demon, Sanzang says that he saw only a girl and warns Wukong never to kill again.

The hungry demon returns disguised as the girl's elderly mother, supposedly searching for her daughter. Bajie immediately treats her as proof of Wukong's guilt. Wukong recognizes the same demon under another face, but Sanzang forbids him to attack. When Wukong strikes anyway, the demon again escapes as mist, leaving an old woman's corpse. Sanzang tortures Wukong with the words a second time and threatens to expel him if he strikes one more person.

The company continues in bitter silence. The White Bone Lady then appears as an old man searching for his missing wife and daughter. Wukong recognizes her third disguise and asks Sanzang to let him act, but Sanzang sees only an old man praying for his family. Wukong chooses to save his master even if it costs him his place. He strikes faster than the escaping mist and destroys the demon in her true skeletal form.

The remaining bones and a skull marked "WHITE BONE LADY" prove Wukong's account to the reader, but Bajie tells Sanzang that Wukong scratched the name there himself. Sanzang invokes the headband again and judges the three disguises to have been three kind people whom Wukong killed. Crying, he expels Wukong. Wukong answers that Sanzang would have died three times without him and asks for the band to be removed, but Sanzang does not know how.

Before leaving, Wukong bows to his master. When Sanzang refuses even to look at him, Wukong blows on three hairs and creates three more monkeys so that Sanzang is surrounded by his final bow. Wukong then asks Wujing to guard the monk and not let Bajie talk him into danger. He flies home on a cloud, crying all the way. At Flower-Fruit Mountain, the monkey descendants who have remembered their absent king for generations pour out joyfully to welcome the Monkey King home.

## Cast

- **Sanzang:** A young, bald human monk in yellow robes and a red patterned outer robe, usually riding a white horse and carrying a ringed pilgrim's staff. He is Wukong's master and the protected pilgrim. He is compassionate and sincerely opposed to killing, but unable to see through the demon's disguises. His authority over Wukong comes from the gold headband's secret words.
- **Wukong / the Monkey King:** A lean, golden-brown monkey warrior in patched pale clothes and a tiger-patterned wrap, with a gold band around his head and a red-and-gold staff. He is the group's fastest and strongest protector. He sees the demon beneath her human faces, repeatedly saves Sanzang, and accepts banishment rather than let his master be eaten.
- **Bajie:** A large, round-bellied pig-man in dark robes with a blue sash, carrying a rake and much of the luggage. He is hungry, credulous, resentful of Wukong, and the decisive voice encouraging Sanzang to distrust and expel him.
- **Wujing:** A tall, muscular blue-skinned river ogre with wild red hair, carrying a crescent-bladed polearm. He is the quieter disciple and dependable rear guard. Wukong trusts him with Sanzang's safety when he leaves.
- **The White Bone Lady:** A towering skeletal demon with long black hair, green-lit eyes, and ragged gray-purple robes. She wants to eat Sanzang to live forever. She successively disguises herself as a young woman with food, that woman's elderly mother, and an old man presented as the woman's father.
- **The white horse:** Sanzang's mount and the group's steady traveling companion.
- **The monkeys of Flower-Fruit Mountain:** Wukong's numerous descendants and former subjects, who have waited through generations for their king to return.

## Causal chain

Sanzang's hunger gives the White Bone Lady an opening. Wukong leaves to fetch food, allowing her to approach in disguise. Because Wukong sees the hidden demon but Sanzang sees a harmless girl, Wukong's rescue looks like murder. Bajie's suspicion turns that difference in perception into active distrust. Sanzang's headband spell and warning force Wukong to choose between obedience and protecting him. The demon exploits the same blind spot twice more, and each rescue appears to confirm Wukong's guilt. On the third attempt, Wukong knowingly sacrifices his place in the pilgrimage to kill her before she can escape. Bajie's final accusation lets Sanzang dismiss even the evidence of the bones, so Sanzang expels the disciple who saved him. Unable to remove the band or reconcile with his master, Wukong returns in grief to the kingdom that still loves him.

## What I expect next

Wukong will initially receive the homecoming and kingship he has been denied on the road, but his grief and continued bond to Sanzang will prevent the return from feeling complete. Meanwhile, the pilgrimage will continue without its strongest protector. Bajie's influence or a new danger will expose what Wukong was contributing, and Wujing may be the one who resists Bajie's bad advice or seeks Wukong's help. I expect the story to force Sanzang to confront the cost of judging only by appearances before master and disciple can be reconciled.
