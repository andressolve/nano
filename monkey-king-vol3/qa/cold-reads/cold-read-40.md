# Cold read — pages 1–40

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands from the pages alone. I did not need outside documentation to understand the cast, the danger, the central mistake, or why Wukong agrees to return. I found no page-specific comprehension or attribution failure causing material reader harm.

## Retell

The monk Sanzang is travelling west through the mountains with three disciples: Wukong, the Monkey King; Bajie, a pig-man; and Wujing, a blue river ogre. Sanzang rides a white horse while the others walk and carry the luggage. Wukong is immensely powerful and can fly on a cloud, but he walks because this is the monk's journey. He also wears a gold band around his head. If Sanzang says three words, the band tightens painfully.

A skeletal demon waits above their road. She wants to eat Sanzang because she believes his flesh will grant immortality. When the hungry monk stops to rest and Wukong flies away to fetch peaches, the demon disguises herself as a young woman carrying rice and noodles. Bajie eagerly welcomes her. Wukong returns, sees the skull beneath her face, and strikes before explaining. The demon escapes as mist, leaving what looks like a dead girl behind. The food is exposed as maggots and frogs, but Sanzang believes Wukong killed an innocent person. Bajie makes the misunderstanding worse by claiming Wukong can fake such evidence. Sanzang says the words that tighten Wukong's band and warns him never to kill again.

The demon tries a second time as the girl's grieving old mother. Wukong recognizes her, but Sanzang refuses to believe him and orders him not to strike. The demon lures Sanzang close; Wukong hits her, and she escapes again as mist. Because Sanzang sees only a dead old woman, he punishes Wukong once more and threatens to expel him if he attacks another person.

The demon returns as an old man searching for his wife and daughter. Wukong identifies this as her third face and asks Sanzang to make the decision, but Sanzang sees only an old man saying his prayers. Wukong decides that being sent away is better than letting his master be eaten. He strikes fast enough to catch the demon before she can escape. Her disguises collapse into a heap of bones and a skull marked "White Bone Lady." Even then, Bajie claims Wukong scratched the name onto it. Sanzang punishes Wukong again and dismisses him as a disciple.

Wukong points out that Sanzang would have died three times without him, but the monk will not relent or remove the gold band. Before leaving, Wukong bows to Sanzang, creates three copies from his hairs so that four monkeys can bow together, and quietly charges Wujing with guarding the monk and resisting Bajie's influence. Wukong flies home to Flower-Fruit Mountain in tears.

His monkey subjects welcome their king joyfully. Wukong boasts that he is free of monks, magic words, and endless walking, though he cannot remove the gold band. He eats, swims, rests, and tells everyone he is happy. Yet every evening he climbs high and watches the west, claiming only to be looking at the weather.

Without Wukong, the pilgrims' road is slower and more dangerous. Bajie disappears with the begging bowl, and Sanzang refuses to let Wujing leave him alone to search. Seeing a pagoda, the monk goes inside expecting monks and supper. Instead he finds the Yellow Robe demon, a huge blue-black warrior in a yellow robe and armor. Yellow Robe ties Sanzang to a pillar and intends to eat him. Sanzang offers himself if the demon will spare his friends.

Bajie and Wujing reach the pagoda, find Sanzang's staff outside, and challenge the demon. Yellow Robe is surprised that the famous monkey protector is absent. He defeats Bajie and Wujing with one breath; Bajie runs while Wujing is captured. Bound beside Sanzang, Wujing apologizes for failing to protect him. Sanzang admits that he sent away the one person who might have saved him and remembers that Wukong correctly warned him three times.

Yellow Robe mocks Sanzang's belief that people are gentle beneath their outward forms. He spits water on him and transforms him into a tiger, then locks him in an iron cage with meat. Though the transformed monk has a tiger's hunger, claws, and teeth, he refuses the meat and sits with his paws together. The demon habitually closes a high shutter so no sky light can enter.

The white horse witnesses the others' defeat. At night he reveals that he was once a dragon, transforms back into that form, and breaks into the pagoda to rescue Sanzang. Yellow Robe intercepts him and strikes him through the high window into the stream. Injured, the dragon can no longer hold his shape and becomes a horse again.

Bajie, hiding near the stream, finds the wounded horse and is astonished when it speaks. The horse tells him Sanzang is a tiger in a cage, Wujing is tied up, and neither of them can defeat Yellow Robe. He orders Bajie to go to Flower-Fruit Mountain, tell Wukong the truth, and bring their big brother back.

Bajie flies badly eastward on a sagging cloud and rehearses telling the truth. At Flower-Fruit Mountain, the monkeys rough him up before taking him to Wukong. Wukong pretends not to know him. Bajie first lies that Sanzang misses Wukong, cries for him, and sent Bajie to fetch him. Wukong sees through this immediately and makes him try again.

Bajie then gives the real confession: he knew the bones belonged to the demon and that her name was visibly written on the skull, but he lied because he resented Wukong for being faster, bossier, and Sanzang's favourite. His lie got Wukong expelled. Now Yellow Robe has transformed Sanzang into a tiger, tied Wujing up, and wounded the horse, who sent Bajie for help. Wukong asks why he should return to a master who dismissed and tortured him. Bajie has no polished answer; he blurts out that he hates flying and hates Wukong ordering him around, but still came all that way to bring him back. That blunt truth persuades Wukong. He agrees to return and makes the journey at Bajie's slower pace because only Bajie knows the way.

## Cast

- **Sanzang:** A young, slender, bald monk in yellow robes with a red-and-gold outer robe. He rides a white horse and carries a ringed monk's staff. He is compassionate and principled, but too ready to trust appearances and too slow to trust Wukong's supernatural sight. His punitive control of Wukong drives the first half of the story, and his recognition of his mistake gives the later rescue its emotional force. By page 29 he has been transformed into a tiger while retaining his conscience and discipline.

- **Wukong / the Monkey King:** A lean golden-brown monkey-man with bright eyes, a long tail, a gold headband, patched pale clothing, a spotted fur wrap, and a red-and-gold staff. He is the group's fastest and strongest protector. He can see through disguises, ride a cloud, change hairs into copies of himself, and move faster than the White Bone Lady's mist. He is proud, blunt, and quick to violence, but also fiercely devoted to Sanzang; his tears, westward watching, and eventual return reveal what his boasting tries to conceal.

- **Bajie:** A heavy pig-man with large ears, tusks, a bare belly, dark patched clothes, a blue sash, and a rake. He is hungry, jealous, lazy, frightened, and habitually dishonest. His spite helps Sanzang misread Wukong and causes the group's catastrophe. His role then changes: he makes the difficult flight to Wukong, finally confesses the ugly truth without excuses, and becomes the person who brings the protector back.

- **Wujing:** A very tall, muscular blue-grey ogre with wild red hair and beard, ragged brown clothing, and a crescent-bladed polearm. He is steady, quiet, loyal, and literal. He accepts Wukong's charge to guard Sanzang, refuses to abandon the monk when Bajie vanishes, fights Yellow Robe, and honestly accepts responsibility after being captured.

- **The white horse / dragon:** A white horse with a pale blue mane, green-blue eyes, ornate red tack, and saddle. He is Sanzang's mount but was previously a long white dragon with blue mane-like fins and gold horns. He watches the group's defeat, attempts a rescue in dragon form, is wounded, and then sends Bajie to Wukong. His unexpected speech begins the successful response to the crisis.

- **The White Bone Lady:** A skeletal female demon with long black hair, green-lit eyes, and ragged purple-grey robes. She wants Sanzang's flesh for immortality and uses three human disguises: a young woman with food, her old mother, and an old man presented as the missing husband and father. She repeatedly sheds a false body and escapes as mist until Wukong destroys her real skeletal form.

- **Yellow Robe:** A huge blue-black demon with tusks, black hair tied high, black armor, a fur mantle, a yellow robe, and a broad curved sword. He occupies the pagoda, overpowers Bajie and Wujing, transforms Sanzang into a tiger, and wounds the horse-dragon. He is physically dominant, amused by hypocrisy, and confident enough to toy with prisoners.

- **The Flower-Fruit monkeys:** A large community of ordinary monkeys living among waterfalls, fruit trees, pools, and caves. They are Wukong's subjects and family. Their wholehearted welcome contrasts with his rejection by Sanzang, while their treatment of Bajie reflects loyalty to their king.

## Causal chain

1. The White Bone Lady wants Sanzang's immortality-giving flesh and waits until Wukong leaves to approach in disguise.
2. Wukong sees through her disguises, but his immediate strikes leave Sanzang seeing only apparently murdered humans.
3. Bajie's jealousy and lies reinforce Sanzang's worst interpretation of Wukong's actions.
4. Sanzang uses the headband spell repeatedly and expels Wukong even after the demon's bones are exposed.
5. With Wukong gone, the party loses its strongest protector and Sanzang enters Yellow Robe's pagoda expecting shelter.
6. Yellow Robe captures Sanzang, defeats Wujing and Bajie, and turns the monk into a tiger.
7. The horse reveals his dragon form and tries to rescue Sanzang, but Yellow Robe wounds him.
8. Unable to fight, the horse tells Bajie that only Wukong can save them and sends him east.
9. Wukong rejects Bajie's first plea because it is another lie.
10. Bajie's full confession, followed by his unpolished admission that he came despite resenting Wukong, finally gives Wukong a truth he can trust.
11. Wukong agrees to return, setting up the rescue and confrontation with Yellow Robe.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

None required. Two reveals caused a brief, intended recalibration without forcing me backward: the bowing monkeys on page 18 are immediately explained as copies made from Wukong's hairs, and the horse's dragon identity on page 31 is stated on the page as the transformation occurs. I did not misidentify any continuing character.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

All narration and dialogue were readable at normal pace. I did not lose a speech balloon's speaker or confuse who was accusing, lying, confessing, or issuing an order. The transformations, changes of location, and time transitions were also clear at first reading.

## What I expect next

Wukong and Bajie will reach the pagoda together. Wukong will free Wujing and the tiger-shaped Sanzang, defeat or outwit Yellow Robe, and find a way to restore the monk's human form. The repeatedly mentioned shutter and exclusion of sky light may be a weakness or clue that matters in the rescue. Emotionally, I expect Sanzang to face Wukong again with the knowledge that Wukong told the truth three times, while Wukong must decide whether returning also means accepting the gold band and the monk's authority. Bajie's confession should alter the group's balance even if it does not end his lying or rivalry with Wukong.
