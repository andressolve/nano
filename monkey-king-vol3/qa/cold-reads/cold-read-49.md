# Cold read — canonical pages 1–49

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without documentation. I understood who everyone was, what each character wanted, why Wukong was expelled, why the others later needed him, and what changed when he returned.

## Retell of the story so far

The monk Sanzang is travelling west with three disciples: Wukong the Monkey King, Bajie the pig, and Wujing the river ogre. The monk rides a white horse while the others walk and carry the luggage. Wukong can cross the world in one somersault, but he is required to stay with the slower group. A gold band around his head tightens painfully when the monk says three special words.

On a mountain ridge, a skeletal demon waits to eat the monk because doing so is said to grant immortality. When the hungry monk asks whether food is nearby, Wukong flies away to fetch peaches. The demon disguises herself as a young woman carrying rice and noodles. Wukong returns, sees the skull beneath her face, and strikes her before explaining. Her spirit escapes, leaving an empty skin and a basket whose food is revealed as bones, maggots, and frogs. The monk sees only that Wukong has killed a seemingly kind girl. Bajie, jealous of Wukong, insists that Wukong faked the evidence, and the monk punishes Wukong with the tightening spell.

The demon tries again as the girl's grieving old mother. Wukong recognizes her and strikes her, but the spirit escapes again. The monk sees another innocent-looking person dead, repeats the spell, and warns that one more attack will end Wukong's discipleship. The demon then appears as an old man searching for his wife and daughter. Wukong understands that if he saves the monk a third time he will be expelled, but chooses to do it anyway. This time he is faster than the escaping spirit and destroys the White Bone Lady. Her skeleton and marked skull reveal the truth, but Bajie claims Wukong carved the name himself. The monk believes three kind people have been murdered, expels Wukong, and cannot bring himself to remove the gold band. Wukong bows, leaves protective hair-clones behind, asks Wujing to guard the monk, and flies home crying.

At Flower-Fruit Mountain, Wukong is joyfully welcomed by the monkeys who remember him as their king. He loudly celebrates being free of the monk, the spell, and the journey, though the band will not come off. He enjoys himself by day but looks west every evening and pretends he is only watching the weather.

Meanwhile, the remaining travellers are slower and less secure. The monk refuses Wujing's suggestion that they retrieve Wukong and enters a pagoda expecting monks and supper. Instead he meets the Yellow Robe, a huge blue-black demon in yellow robes and armor. The demon captures the monk. Bajie and Wujing come to the rescue, but Yellow Robe defeats both easily. Wujing is tied up, Bajie escapes, and the monk admits that he sent away the one person who could have protected him.

Yellow Robe mocks the monk's insistence that people are not beasts underneath, then spits magic on him and turns him into a tiger. He cages the tiger with meat, expecting hunger to make him abandon his principles, but the monk refuses to eat it for three days. The white horse reveals that he was once a dragon, breaks into the pagoda, and tries to free the monk. Yellow Robe wounds him and knocks him into a stream. By morning he can only hold his horse form, but he can still speak when it matters. He tells Bajie that only Wukong can save the monk and orders him to go to Flower-Fruit Mountain and tell the truth.

Bajie makes the difficult flight east and is brought before Wukong. He first offers a flattering lie that the monk misses Wukong, but Wukong sees through him and demands the truth. Bajie confesses that he knew the White Bone Lady's bones were real, lied out of jealousy, and caused Wukong's expulsion. He also explains that Yellow Robe has turned the monk into a tiger, tied up Wujing, and wounded the horse. Wukong asks why he should return to someone who rejected him. Bajie cannot give a noble reason; he admits he hates flying and being bossed around but came all that way for Wukong anyway. That honest answer is enough. Wukong agrees to return and waits for the slower Bajie to guide him back.

At the pagoda, Wukong meets the wounded horse, challenges Yellow Robe, and discovers that brute force cannot defeat him. Wukong's special sight reveals that the demon is actually a runaway star from Heaven. He threatens to tell Heaven where the missing star is hiding. Yellow Robe abandons the fight and streaks back into the sky rather than be exposed.

Wukong frees Wujing and finds the tiger monk still refusing the meat. Using his knowledge of shape-changing magic, he washes the spell away and restores the monk. The monk says Wukong came back for him; Wukong deflects the emotion with a joke. The monk then admits that he was the beast in the cage because he saw only outward appearances, apologizes for doubting Wukong, and says he sent him away for saving his life. Wukong reveals that he heard the monk's regret the first time, despite being gone.

Bajie openly confesses his lie to the monk. His consequence is carrying all the luggage, which he accepts. The monk promises that when Wukong identifies a demon he will believe him even if he cannot see the truth himself. When Wukong asks what happens if he is wrong, the monk answers that they will be wrong together rather than continue without him. The group resumes its westward journey at the wounded horse's pace. When another woman approaches with a lunch basket, the monk looks to Wukong instead of judging alone. Wukong studies her carefully, decides she is genuinely human, and tells the monk he may thank her.

## Cast

- **Sanzang, the monk:** A young, slim, bald man in golden-yellow robes under a red patterned outer robe. He carries a ringed monk's staff and initially rides the white horse. He is compassionate and principled but too trusting of appearances. His central mistake is refusing Wukong's clearer sight; his central change is learning to trust Wukong and share the risk of being wrong.
- **Wukong, the Monkey King:** A small, athletic golden-brown monkey-man with bright eyes, a long tail, a gold headband, ragged pale robes, a tiger-patterned wrap, and a red-and-gold staff. He is the group's fastest, strongest, and most perceptive defender. He is proud and sharp-tongued but repeatedly chooses the monk's safety over his own pride or comfort.
- **Bajie:** A broad, heavy pig-man with a pink snout, tusks, large ears, a bare belly, dark clothes, a blue sash, and a rake. He complains, loves food, and envies Wukong's favored status. His jealous lie causes the separation; his eventual confession and arduous trip to retrieve Wukong help repair it.
- **Wujing:** A very tall, muscular blue-gray river ogre with wild red hair and beard, ragged brown clothing, and a crescent-bladed polearm. He is quiet, loyal, and dependable. Wukong entrusts the monk's back to him, but Yellow Robe overpowers and binds him.
- **The white horse / dragon:** A fine white horse with a pale blue mane, green-blue eyes, and ornate red tack; in his original form he is a long white dragon with a blue mane and gold horns. He carries the monk, watches the group closely, and reveals his hidden nature to attempt a rescue. Though wounded, he directs Bajie to the one rescuer who can succeed.
- **The White Bone Lady:** A skeletal female demon with green-lit eye sockets, long black hair, ragged gray-purple robes, and a mass of bones. She wants to eat the monk and disguises herself successively as a young woman, an old mother, and an old man. Those three forms are clearly one predator, not three additional cast members.
- **Yellow Robe:** A gigantic blue-black, tusked demon with black hair, heavy black armor, a vast yellow robe, and a curved sword. He captures the party, transforms the monk, and defeats Bajie, Wujing, and the dragon. Beneath that form he is a runaway star from Heaven; exposure, not physical defeat, drives him away.
- **The monkeys of Flower-Fruit Mountain:** A large community of ordinary monkeys who regard Wukong as their returning king. They supply his loving home and make clear that returning to the monk is a genuine choice rather than his only refuge.

## Causal chain

1. The White Bone Lady wants to eat the monk and exploits his hunger and compassion with three human disguises.
2. Wukong's sight lets him recognize her, but his habit of acting before explaining makes each rescue look like a murder.
3. Bajie's jealousy turns that misunderstanding into certainty by falsely dismissing the demonic evidence.
4. The monk uses the headband spell, issues an ultimatum, and finally expels Wukong after the third apparent killing.
5. Wukong leaves, so the diminished party cannot protect the monk from Yellow Robe.
6. Yellow Robe defeats the remaining disciples and horse, transforms the monk into a tiger, and tries unsuccessfully to make him eat meat.
7. The wounded horse recognizes that only Wukong can save them and sends Bajie to retrieve him.
8. Wukong refuses Bajie's flattering lie but responds to his complete, painful confession and honest loyalty.
9. Wukong returns, uses perception rather than force to expose Yellow Robe as a fugitive star, and drives him back to Heaven.
10. Wukong restores the monk; the monk and Bajie both confess their failures; trust is rebuilt on a promise to believe Wukong and accept mistakes together.
11. The next woman with a basket repeats the original visual test, but the monk now waits for Wukong's judgment, and Wukong now pauses long enough to be sure.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

None. I did not need to return to an earlier page to identify a character, reconstruct an action, or understand a motivation. The successive disguises, Wukong's expulsion, the horse's dragon identity, Yellow Robe's star identity, and the restoration of the monk were all clear on first reading.

I did not misidentify any recurring character. In particular, Wujing and Yellow Robe remained visually and functionally distinct despite both having blue-gray skin, and the young woman, old woman, and old man read as successive forms of the already-established skeletal demon.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text failed at reading pace. Every narration block, speech balloon, and sound effect was legible in the 600 × 900 proofs. Balloon order and speaker attribution were clear throughout, including the exchanges among several characters on pages 7–10, 16–18, 33–39, and 45–48.

## What I expect next

I expect the group to continue west with a new working trust: the monk will rely on Wukong's supernatural perception, Wukong will pause and explain before striking when circumstances allow, Bajie will remain comic and argumentative but carry the consequence of his lie, Wujing will continue as the steady rear guard, and the horse will recover while the group matches his slower pace. The woman with lunch suggests the immediate lesson has been learned, but future threats will probably test the monk's promise when Wukong's judgment is less easily confirmed than it was with the White Bone Lady.
