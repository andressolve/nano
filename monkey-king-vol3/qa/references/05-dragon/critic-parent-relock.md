**Costume-free face crop.** The dragon has no costume, so the face crop is the full test. What remains:
- a narrow, scaled lizard head;
- two smooth gold horns, curved and unbranched;
- large round sea-green eyes;
- no ears and no whiskers;
- a pale-blue mane that starts at the crown.

No approved face shares this structure. The horse has ears, a deep equine jaw, large flared nostrils and a bridle. Every other approved face is human, monkey, skull, ogre or tiger.

**Thumbnail silhouette.** The long serpentine S-body with small legs and a feathered tail is unique in the system. No other approved sheet has a horizontal serpent mass.

**Grayscale.** The dragon reads as a near-white body with a mid-value mane and mid-value horns. The only other near-white masses in the system are the horse and the Bone Lady's mist form in `11-bone-lady` view (d). Both are covered in the matrix.

**Strict profile.** The snout projects moderately and ends in a small scaled tip. Its length and depth are reptilian, not equine. The horns and mane silhouette carry the profile.

**Reaction state.** In (d) the open jaw shows teeth and a pink mouth. It reads as a dragon roaring. It does not read as horse or tiger.

**Same-panel opposition.** Only the Yellow Robe shares panels with the dragon (the fight, pp 30–31): indigo skin, black topknot and a mustard robe against a white serpent. That is maximal separation.

**Re-entry after a long absence.** The dragon appears on pp 30–31 only, after 29 pages in horse form. The eyes and mane carry the reader across the transformation, which is the intended identity carrier.

## Stage 2: ledger and plan

**Permanent lock.** The Third Prince's dragon form is causally important on pp 30–31: it fights, is cut, and turns back into the horse. A lock is planned in manifest row 05. Under the relock ruling this sheet is that lock.

**Nearest lookalike named.** Yes. Ledger line 445 (horse/dragon continuity row) and the identity-stack line 428 name the horse as the nearest lane. The relock ruling restates the separators: serpent body, four small clawed legs, the two short horns, no tack. The sheet carries all four.

**Structural lock, trait by trait (ledger §5, dragon form):**
- **Body:** slim, white as river foam, long and lithe, serpentine. Present.
- **Head:** a compact young lizard head. Present. The written lock also asks for a "blunt wedge, almost no projecting muzzle, shield nasal scale". The sheet's snout projects moderately and its nostril is small and near the tip. The relock ruling explicitly accepts the Volume II head unchanged and moves the horse separation to body, legs, horns and tack. Under the ruling this is not blocking (see observation 1).
- **Beard and jaw fins:** no beard. Small scale-spikes sit at the cheek behind the jaw in (a); they read as cheek scales, not a jaw fin or a fin beard.
- **Horns:** two pale-gold unbranched horns, not antlers. They are longer and more crescent-shaped than "short spikes". That is acceptable under the ruling.
- **Eyes:** large, round, sea-green. Present.
- **Mane:** pale blue, along the crown and spine. Present.
- **Legs:** four small clawed legs. Present.
- **Costume/tack:** none. Present.

**Never list (ledger line 284):**
- No antlers, whiskers, fin beard, blue-green scales or gold eyes.
- No robes, crown or human body.
- Not brown, not dappled.
- No wound artifact, bandage, wrap, ring, red on a limb, blood or open flesh. The red inside the open mouth in (d) is the mouth, not blood.
- "Long horse-like rostrum / rounded fleshy muzzle / terminal horse nostril": the snout is scaled, moderate in length and narrow, with a small reptilian nostril. It does not read as a horse rostrum, and the ruling has accepted this head.
- No violation.

**Reserved identity stack leakage.** None. The stack "white serpent + pale-blue mane + sea-green eyes" appears only on this sheet and the horse, which is the same character.

**Returning-character continuity.** This is the parent's approved `05-dragon`, unchanged, so it reads as the same character by construction.

## Nonblocking observations

1. **The written head lock now disagrees with the locked pixels.** Ledger §5 (dragon form, lines 264–271), the ledger Never list (line 284) and the 05 prompt in the plan (lines 456–485) still describe a blunt wedge head with almost no muzzle, a shield nasal scale and "short" horn spikes. The relock ruling sits beside them but does not strike them. A page critic whose card quotes "shield nose, almost no projecting muzzle" could fail a faithful p30–31 render drawn from this sheet. Recommendation: when the ledger is next touched, put "superseded by the relock ruling" on the head-structure sentence and the rostrum/muzzle Never items, and write the pp 30–31 critic cards against the ruling's separators (serpent body, four clawed legs, two horns, no tack).
2. **The plan tables are stale.** The anchor table (line 81) and manifest row 05 (line 102) still ask for a view (d) recoil with the right-foreleg cut. The ruling removes that. The tables should be annotated so a later gate does not read the missing wound view as a defect. The cut on pp 30–31 must come from words in the page prompt, with `06-white-horse` view (d) as the wound reference, per the ruling.
3. **Scenery on the sheet.** Views (b) and (d) carry gold-touched sea cliffs, pines and crashing surf. The dragon is on two pages only, so this cannot spread across forty pages. But a page model given this sheet may pull toward a sea-cliff setting. Pp 30–31 happen at the black pine wood, pagoda and stony stream (`set-pagoda-exterior`), so their page prompts should state the setting firmly and attach the set plate.
4. **Cheek spikes in view (a).** These small white scale-spikes behind the jaw are within tolerance. If a page render grows them into a fringe along the jaw, that fails the Never item "beard of fins" at the page gate.
