## 1. Cold read

On a ridge of white rock, the monk Sanzang, his pig, his river ogre and his dragon-horse are walking west. Wukong runs ahead. He wears a gold band that squeezes his head when the monk says "Gold, hold fast," and since the furnace his eyes see through any disguise. A lady made of bone watches from a crag and wants one bite of the monk, because that bite would make her live forever. Wukong flies off for peaches. She comes down as a girl with a basket. He comes back, sees the skull, and strikes without explaining. The girl falls as an empty skin and the basket turns out to hold maggots and frogs. Bajie convinces the monk that Wukong faked it, and the monk says the words. The same thing happens with an old woman: Wukong strikes, the monk chants longer, and then warns that one more strike means exile. Wukong walks at the back. The demon comes a third time as an old man. This time Wukong asks first, is refused, and strikes anyway: "Then send me away, Master. You'll be alive to do it." She becomes a heap of bones with her name carved on the skull. Bajie says it is a trick, and the monk sends Wukong away. Wukong bows four ways with his hair-copies, tells Wujing to guard the monk's back, and cries all the way home.

At home he is crowned with flowers, cannot get the band off, refuses to give up the robe the monk patched, and looks west every evening. Meanwhile the monk, left alone, walks into a golden pagoda that belongs to the Yellow Robe. The demon catches him, beats Bajie and Wujing, and turns the monk into a tiger. The tiger will not touch the meat. The horse becomes a dragon, attacks, and is wounded. At dawn the horse speaks for the first time and sends Bajie to fetch Wukong, telling him to tell the truth. Bajie flies east and lies. Wukong sees through it, and Bajie confesses why: he wanted to be the favourite. Wukong goes back, fights the Yellow Robe to a standstill, sees that he is a runaway star, and the demon flees into the sky. Wukong turns the tiger back into the monk. The monk apologises and Bajie confesses. The monk promises to believe Wukong even when he cannot see the demon himself. On the road a woman with a basket comes toward them, the monk looks at Wukong first, and Wukong says she's just a lady with lunch.

**Where I stopped, reread or guessed:**
- **p15:** I saw a skull shrieking in the open, in front of the monk. Why does he still not believe?
- **p23:** Bajie is asleep in a ditch in panel 1, but in panel 2 Wujing says he has to go and look for him. Is Bajie nearby? Where is this?
- **p26:** The monk's staff is at the gate, but he was grabbed inside. And the horse was tied at the edge of the wood, not at the gate.
- **p28:** "When the tiger eats meat, I'll eat the tiger." I reread this. Why does the meat matter, and why wait?
- **p31→p32:** The dragon is cut inside the hall and the cage is still shut. Then a horse lies in a stream at dawn. How did he get out?
- **p42–43:** Why does a demon who has just beaten everyone run away because he has been recognised?
- **p44:** "A whole day hungry." By my count it has been two to three days.
- **p46:** When did the monk change his mind? He never saw the star or the bones' proof again, and Bajie confesses *after* the apology.

## 2. Is it alive?

**Movement I (pp 1–5): yes.** It sets up quickly and cleanly. The three rules (the band, the eyes, no flying the monk) are planted on p1 and every one of them pays off. Bajie's "I'll say yes for you" is the first real laugh, and the swing on p5 is a strong page-turn.

**Movement II (pp 6–19): yes, and this is the best of the book.** The three faces escalate the way a fairy tale should. The first strike is a reflex. The second goes against "don't you dare." The third is asked for, refused, and chosen. "Then send me away, Master. You'll be alive to do it" is a protagonist with real appetite making a real sacrifice. The monk's reply "I don't know any words for that" (p17) is the best line in the script: tender, terrible, and in character. The four bows and "He cried the whole way home" moved me. Bajie's lie has a motive the reader can feel from p3 onward. One soft spot: p13 panel 3 spells out the dilemma that p14 then acts on.

**Movement III (pp 20–22): yes.** It is short, sad and funny. "He told the monkeys he was looking at the weather" does the whole emotional job without saying it.

**Movement IV (pp 23–33): mostly, with a hole in the middle.** The pagoda, the tiger and the talking horse are wonderful ideas, and the tiger turning its face to the wall (p29) is the monk's finest moment in the series. But the monk himself goes quiet just when his story should turn. He is silent on p28, while he is being transformed and while Wujing confesses to him, and his change of heart never happens on the page. The rule the tiger keeps is never stated as *his* rule. The Yellow Robe's game is not clear. There are also mechanical holes: p23's ditch, the horse's escape.

**Movement V (pp 34–39): yes, and it surprised me.** The pig is the book's secret heart. He practises the truth all the way there and "It sounded terrible every time." He tries a lie first, is seen through, and then tells the truth plainly. Then the best image in the volume: "One somersault would have been faster. He waited for the pig." It is badly let down by one line of writer-talk on p38.

**Movement VI (pp 40–48): the reconciliation is earned and lovely, but the villain's defeat is thin.** The Yellow Robe loses because he is recognised, and the book never planted a reason recognition should frighten him. So the climax lands on a rule the reader has to make up. "Then we'll be wrong together. I'd rather that than walk without you" is a perfect resolution of the volume's question, and the basket on p48 is a perfect closing echo.

**Overall:** this is good. It is warm and funny, and in Movement II it is genuinely painful. It is not yet ready, because its second half leans on reasons the reader has to supply: why the monk changes his mind, why the demon flees, why the meat matters.

## 3. Mandatory findings

**1. p15: the skull is visible to the monk, which breaks the central logic of Movement II.**
- **Defect:** The staging says "the White Bone Lady's skull face shrieking, caught, the mist with nowhere to go … Sanzang thrown back against the white horse." Everywhere else the script carefully marks the true shape as Wukong's view or the reader's: p5 "the reader sees what he sees," p10 "in the reader's view," p14 "for the reader." The ledger also says the mist is "seen only by Wukong and the reader." This page has no such marker, so the shrieking skull happens in plain sight, right in front of the monk.
- **Reader harm:** On pp 16–17 the reader watches a monk who has just seen a screaming skull refuse to believe in demons. The monk stops looking honest-but-blind and starts looking stupid. That dissolves the sympathy the volume depends on, and it makes Bajie's "fingernail" lie (p16) impossible to swallow.
- **Fix:** Mark the skull and the burst explicitly as Wukong's view. For example, frame it through the red ring of his flaring eye, or put the monk's back to the strike with his face buried in the horse's neck and his eyes shut, so that all he sees is an old man there and bones afterward. Then add one beat to p16 panel 1 so the monk's view is clear: "I saw you strike an old man, and then there were bones."

**2. p23 (carrying into p26 and p30): an impossible ditch, a third place, and the horse and staff in the wrong spots.**
- **Defect:** Panel 1 shows "Bajie asleep in a ditch." Panel 2 has Wujing saying "Bajie went to beg food an hour ago … I'll go and find him." The location line declares two places (the road and the wood's edge). If the ditch is where they are standing, Wujing's line makes no sense. If it is somewhere else, the page has three places, which is a blocker. Separately: on p23 the monk ties the horse "at the edge of a dark wood" and sees the pagoda far off "through the trees." But on p26 the horse is tied at the pagoda gate, and on p30 it has "seen the pig run and the ogre dragged inside." On p26 the monk's ringed staff lies "at your gate," yet he was seized inside the hall (p25), and the staff is never staged on p23 or p24.
- **Reader harm:** The opening page of the movement makes the reader work out where everyone is. Later, the horse's knowledge (p30) and Wujing's evidence (p26) depend on the reader quietly fixing the geography.
- **Fix:**
  - Cut panel 1. Open on the monk and Wujing on the road with the caption, and let Wujing's line carry Bajie's absence. If the nap is too good to lose, move it to the top of p26 as Wujing hauling Bajie out of the ditch in front of the gate.
  - On p23 panel 3, stage the pagoda gate as visible a short way up the path, and have the monk tie the horse to a pine *in sight of the gate*, staff in hand.
  - On p24, stage the staff falling from his hand on the threshold.
  - On p26, the line "His staff is lying at your gate" then works.

**3. pp 4 / 25 / 28 / 44: the monk's rule is never stated, so the tiger's test and the Yellow Robe's plan do not add up.**
- **Defect:** p28: "A monk who won't hurt anyone! Let's see how gentle you are with claws and teeth … And when the tiger eats meat, I'll eat the tiger." p44: "You kept your rule, even like this." The only thing close to a rule is on p25, "you may not hurt anyone else," and that is a demand the monk makes of the *demon*, not a vow about himself. Nowhere does the book say the monk eats no meat, or that nothing may die for his supper. A joint already on a bone in a cage is not "hurting anyone." And why would the demon wait for the meat before eating him?
- **Reader harm:** The monk's one heroic act in the book, refusing the meat on p29, only means something if the reader knows what it costs him and what it proves. As written the reader has to guess the vow and also guess the demon's logic. p44's "your rule" points to nothing on the page.
- **Fix:**
  - Plant the vow early, in the monk's own mouth. On p4, after looking into the girl's basket: "Rice and noodles — no meat? Then thank you. Nothing has ever died for my supper." (This also makes the maggots on p6 sting more.)
  - On p25 panel 2, make the vow his: "I have never eaten meat and never hurt anyone. Eat me if you must, but you may not hurt my friends."
  - On p28, rewrite the second Yellow Robe balloon so his bet is explicit: "Everybody's a beast underneath, monk. When you eat that meat, you'll know it too — and then I'll eat you with a clear conscience."
  - p44's "You kept your rule" then lands.

**4. p28 → p46: the monk's change of heart happens offstage.**
- **Defect:** The monk believes his eyes on p17. On p46 he says "I couldn't see her face, and you could … I should have believed you." Nothing in between shows him turning. He never sees the star, Bajie confesses *after* the apology, and on p28, in his worst hour, he has no line at all. Wujing says "Wukong told me to walk at your back, Master … I didn't keep you," and the monk does not answer him.
- **Reader harm:** The volume's second arc (being wrong about what one sees) turns where the reader cannot see it, so p46's apology arrives as a report rather than a result. A first-time reader asks: "What made him change his mind?" Wujing's secret want, that someone say his failure was not his fault, also goes unanswered at the moment it is raised.
- **Fix:** Give the monk his turn on p28, before the spray. Add a balloon answering Wujing: "No, Wujing. I sent away the one who could have kept me. He told me three times what she was, and I said the words three times." Optionally, the Yellow Robe can twist the knife: "Everybody on this road wants to eat you, and you sent your monkey home?" Then p46 confirms a change the reader has already watched happen, and can be rewritten so it does not repeat the realisation but builds on it.

**5. pp 29 / 34 / 39 / 44: the timeline contradicts itself and undersells the monk's endurance.**
- **Defect:** p29 says "All day the meat lay there. All evening." Then the dragon fights at night (p30–31), Bajie wakes at dawn (p32), and he "flew east all day" (p34). The return flight is at the pig's pace with Wukong waiting (p39), and they arrive at dusk (p43). That is at least two to three days. Yet p44 says "a whole day hungry." The page contract says "three days" on p29, but the script does not.
- **Reader harm:** An attentive reader counts days and catches the contradiction. Worse, the script shrinks the monk's finest act. Three days of a tiger's hunger is heroic. "A whole day" is not.
- **Fix:**
  - p29: "All day the meat lay there. All night. All the next day, and the next."
  - p39 caption: add "They flew all night and all the next day. One somersault would have been faster. He waited for the pig."
  - p44: "Claws, teeth, and three days hungry, and you didn't touch the meat."

**6. p31 → p32: the dragon's escape is missing.**
- **Defect:** p31 ends with the dragon cut across the foreleg inside the hall and the cage still shut. p32 opens at dawn with "the white horse lying in the shallow stream," his bridle broken. Nothing shows how he got out, why he is a horse again, or why he is in the stream rather than at his pine.
- **Reader harm:** This is exactly the gap "recoverable from context" is meant to catch. The reader has to invent an escape, and the most important speaker of the movement arrives in an unexplained place and form.
- **Fix:** Change p31's caption to carry it: "He tried to tear the cage open. The Yellow Robe woke, and his blade drove the dragon out through the high window and down into the stream." Then add a caption to p32 panel 2: "By morning he was a horse again, because a hurt dragon can't hold his shape."

**7. p38 (and p47): aphorism dialogue at the volume's two moral hinges.**
- **Defect:**
  - p38, Wukong: "A pig came all the way back for a monkey. **Somebody always has to go back for somebody.**"
  - p47, Sanzang: "You told me the truth yourself, Bajie. **That's harder than lying.**"
- **Reader harm:** Both bolded sentences are the writer stating the theme. Strip the names and either line could belong to anyone. Wukong's ledger voice is "short sentences, and laughs in the middle of them." The monk "never preaches." p38's first sentence already does the work, in Wukong's own sardonic voice, and the second explains it. p47's line turns Bajie's hardest moment into a moral lesson.
- **Fix:**
  - p38: cut the second sentence and keep it specific and in his voice. For example: "A pig came all the way back for a monkey. On *that* cloud." Then "Fine. Let's go and get our monk."
  - p47: replace the moral with something particular to the monk and the pig. For example: "You told me yourself, Bajie, and nobody made you. You can carry my end of the luggage today," with Bajie answering "...That's fair."

**8. p42 (planting on pp 24–29): the climax resolves on a fear the book never planted.**
- **Defect:** The Yellow Robe, who has beaten the pig, the ogre and the dragon, gets "Go home, star, before Heaven comes looking for you," and simply leaves. Nothing earlier in the book suggests he is hiding, or that being found would cost him anything. His secret want, "never to be found and sent home," exists only in the ledger. The silver stars on his robe and his silver eyes are staging that the reader cannot read as clues.
- **Reader harm:** The villain's defeat, which is the payoff of eleven pages of dread, depends on a rule the reader has to make up on the spot ("I guess stars are scared of being caught?"). The theme is right: the eyes the monk would not trust now save him. But the mechanism is unplanted, so it feels like the demon gave up rather than lost.
- **Fix:**
  - Plant one visible fear without spoiling the reveal. On p29, add a prose sentence: "Every night, before he lay down, the demon pulled the shutter across the high window, so that no light from the sky could fall on him." The high window then gains a second job for the dragon on p30.
  - On p42, make Wukong's threat concrete and aimed at that fear: "Heaven counts its stars every night, and one has been missing. Shall I tell them where you're hiding?" The Yellow Robe's "Who told you? Nobody can see that!" then lands as panic.
  - Optionally, on p25, the demon can call the pagoda "the one place nobody ever comes looking for me."

## 4. Nonblocking observations

- **p4, page-turn line:** "the sky behind her has a cloud in it." No cloud is staged. Add a small streak of cloud in the sky of panel 3 so the line is true of the page.
- **p12:** The exile ultimatum arrives as quoted speech inside the prose ("When the monk stopped at last, he said one thing more"). It reads well and the page is a strong aftermath, but consider giving the monk this line in a balloon at the end of p11, so the movement's key threat comes from his own mouth.
- **p13, panel 3:** "If I don't stop her, she eats you. If I do, you send me away" states outright the dilemma that p14's "Then send me away, Master. You'll be alive to do it" acts on, and it steals some of that line's force. Consider a silent panel 3, or an evasive line in his voice, with no explanation.
- **p30:** The audit flags it as a dramatic page with no dialogue, and it plays as a near-silent sequence held up by captions. Consider labelling it *spectacle* (sparse cinematic sequence). The mode balance would stay within tolerance.
- **p36:** "These eyes saw through three faces of a demon. Do you think they can't see through a pig?" This quietly stretches the rule from seeing true faces to seeing lies. Consider "I didn't need magic eyes for three demons' faces? I don't need them for a pig," or anything that keeps the eyes' power exactly as p1 defined it.
- **pp 33 / 37 / 46:** Bajie confesses three times, to the horse, to Wukong and to the monk. Each one costs more than the last, which is fine, but keep p33's version a single line so p37 stays the real one.
- **Contract drift:** these discrepancies should vanish when the contract is regenerated from the revised script.
  - p8: contract "never again, or you go"; the script has no "or you go."
  - p28: contract "spell of water"; the script has a mist and no cup-filling.
  - p29: contract "three days."
  - p31: contract "driven out."
- **p1:** At about 133 words this page is near the 140 ceiling and carries three rules. It is acceptable, but it must not grow in revision. If anything is added, move the eyes paragraph to a caption on p5.
