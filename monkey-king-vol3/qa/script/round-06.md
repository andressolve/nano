# Script gate, round 6: *Monkey King, Volume III: Sent Away*

**Verdict: APPROVED. Mandatory findings: NONE.** The report below is the text to save as `qa/script/round-06.md`. I did not write that file and did not edit the script.

## Cold read

I read the whole script in order before opening anything else. I skipped the round history.

**What happens, as a first-time reader would tell it.** Months after the river of sand, the monk Sanzang rides west on the white horse, which was once a dragon. Bajie the pig and Wujing the river ogre carry the luggage. Wukong runs ahead. Wukong wears a gold band that squeezes his head whenever the monk says "Gold, hold fast," and his eyes, smoked in Heaven's furnace, see the real face under any face.

The White Bone Lady wants one bite of the monk so she can live forever. While Wukong is away fetching peaches, she comes to the monk three times, as a girl, an old woman and an old man. Each time Wukong sees the skull and strikes.

- The first two times she slips away as mist. Only Wukong and the reader see the mist. The monk sees a body, and Bajie talks him into saying the words.
- The third time, Wukong asks first. The monk says no. Wukong strikes anyway: "Then send me away, Master. You'll be alive to do it."
- What is left is a heap of bones with her name carved on the skull. Bajie says Wukong scratched it on, the monk says the words again, and he sends Wukong away.

Wukong bows four times with three copies of himself, cries all the way home, is crowned at Flower-Fruit Mountain, and every evening looks west, saying he is checking the weather.

The monk, left alone, walks into a pagoda belonging to the Yellow Robe. The demon beats Bajie and Wujing, and turns the monk into a tiger to prove that everyone is a beast underneath. The tiger will not touch the meat. The horse becomes a dragon, is cut, and lies hurt in the stream. Next morning the horse speaks for the first time and sends Bajie for "our big brother."

At the mountain, Bajie lies, is seen through, and then tells the truth: he lied about the bones because he wanted to be the monk's favourite. Wukong goes back. In the fight his eyes show that the Yellow Robe is a star that ran away from the sky, and the demon flies home before Heaven counts tonight. Wukong turns the tiger back into the monk. The monk apologizes. Bajie confesses unasked. The monk promises to believe Wukong ("Then we'll be wrong together"). On the last page a woman with a basket comes down the road, and the monk looks at Wukong first.

**Where I stopped:**
- **p40, the caption "He waited for the pig all night and all the next day."** For a moment I read it as Wukong waiting at the mountain before leaving. The picture of two clouds in flight sets it right. That makes it recoverable, which is a revision flag but a small one; see nonblocking item 1.
- **p47, "That's what I did to you."** I paused for a beat to work out the comparison. The next balloon ("I should have believed you… I sent you away for saving my life") settles it at once.
- **p40's reason for the slow flight ("only the pig knew the way").** It is serviceable. I briefly wondered why Wukong couldn't just look for gold roofs west of the ridge. This did not stop my reading.

Nowhere else did I reread or guess. I could paraphrase every movement after one read.

## Is it alive?

Yes. This is a good book, and Movement II and the scene at the mountain are better than good.

**Movement I (pp 1–5).** The setup is economical and warm.
- p1 carries everything the book needs: the band, the three words, the eyes, the rule that Wukong may never fly the monk, and the needle. It does this as orientation, not as a lecture.
- p3 plants Bajie's want in the open ("Who made the monkey the boss?" / "Nobody, Bajie. He's just the fastest."). That is the seed of the whole book.
- p4 puts the monk's vow in his own mouth before it matters ("I've never let anything die for my supper").
- I wanted to turn every page.

**Movement II (pp 6–19).** Painful in the right way.
- The rule about who sees what is now completely clean. The reader sees through Wukong's eyes, and the monk sees a body. So the monk is wrong but never stupid, and the reader is torn between them. That is real heat: Wukong is fighting to be believed by the one person who cannot see what he sees.
- p13–14 is the best beat in the book. "I'm asking you first this time" shows Wukong has learned something. The monk says no, and Wukong chooses the monk's life over his own place with him: "Then send me away, Master. You'll be alive to do it." That is appetite and sacrifice in one line.
- "I don't know any words for that" (p17) is a terrible, perfect answer.
- The four bows and "Walk at his back, Wujing. And don't let the pig talk him into anything" give the exile its aftermath. So does the prose page (p12), where for the first time Wukong walks behind.
- p19's "He cried the whole way home" lands.

**Movement III (pp 20–22).** Short and right.
- The band that won't come off, and the robe he won't let go of ("My monk sewed that patch himself"), are subtext doing the work: he says he is free and acts otherwise.
- "He told the monkeys he was looking at the weather" is the funniest and saddest sentence in the volume.

**Movement IV (pp 23–34).** Wukong is off the page for twelve pages, and the absence is the engine. "Where's the monkey everybody talks about?" / "He's, um, busy." / "He was sent away." gets a laugh and a bruise at once.
- p28 is the monk's turn, and it is earned and quiet: "I saw a girl and was sure she was a girl." The silent panel after it is correct.
- The Yellow Robe has a distinct motive, which passes the want-from test. He wants to watch gentleness fail before he eats. That is different from the White Bone Lady's simple hunger.
- The tiger with its paws together facing the wall (p30) is the image of the book.
- The horse's single speech carries weight because it is single. When Bajie admits the lie to the horse first ("I knew it was a lie"), his arc starts in the right place.

**Movement V (pp 35–40).** The want-from is clearest here. Bajie wants Wukong back without paying for it, and Wukong wants the truth.
- The lie first ("He cries every night") and then "I don't need magic eyes to see through you" is subtext and comedy doing the work.
- Bajie's confession is in his own voice: "the fastest, and the bossiest, and his favourite." Strip the speaker names off pp 36–39 and every line can still be assigned.
- Wukong's decision is enacted, not explained. His hand goes to the patch, and the reason he says out loud is about the pig ("A pig came all the way back for a monkey. On a cloud like yours."). The real reason, the monk, is never spoken. That is exactly the right way round.

**Movement VI (pp 41–49).** The climax is won by seeing, not by hitting, so the volume's idea pays off as plot rather than as a lesson.
- The shutter and "nobody ever comes looking for me" are planted, so the star reveal is fair.
- p47 now belongs entirely to the apology and to Wukong being hit by it. "Get up, Master, before the pig sees you bowing to a monkey" deflects in character. "I heard you the first time" answers the monk's "he told me three times" without anyone pointing at it.
- Bajie confessing unasked the next morning completes his arc and gives the monk the factual answer about the bones.
- The basket on the last page is a strong ending, earned and unspoken.

**Voice check.** Each voice holds against the ledger:
- Wukong is blunt and jokes even when hurt.
- Sanzang is polite and stubborn, and never preaches.
- Bajie is loud, flattering and funny, then not funny for one page.
- Wujing speaks few words and says the worst thing about himself first (pp 28, 45).
- The Yellow Robe is big, lazy and cruel.

I found no dialogue that is just arrangements, and no dialogue that is the theme spoken as an epigram.

**Aftermath.** Every consequential beat gets one: the exile (pp 12, 18–22), the monk's mistake (pp 28, 47), Wujing's failure (p45), Bajie's lie (pp 38, 48), the horse's wound (p49), and Wukong's own injury of not being believed (pp 47–49).

## Mandatory findings

NONE.

I checked these specifically and found nothing that blocks:
- **Introductions.** Every returning face, rule and debt is re-identified in story before the plot depends on it: band and words on p1, eyes on p1, horse and dragon on pp 1 and 31, Bajie's exile from Heaven on p35, the bride trick on p7, the mountain on p12, the robe and patch on pp 18 and 21. The Yellow Robe is named by caption (p24) and by Bajie (p38).
- **Continuity.** The door, the dropped staff, the shutter and the boarded window, the timeline (three days), the right-foreleg wound, the luggage pole, morning on p48 and noon on p49 are all consistent.
- **Five-word audit.** All sixteen short balloons are complete thoughts or direct answers to the line before, each with one visible referent. "He's, um, busy." is a deliberate evasion of a complete question. "And what if I'm wrong?" is itself a complete question.
- **Page architecture.** Every page has one location, one dominant panel and one turn. Mode share is 30/7/12 (61/14/24%), within the roughly 60/20/13 target given the seven prose pages. Text budgets pass. p1 sits exactly at the 140-word prose ceiling and p48 at 92 dramatic words. Both are within the law.
- **Fidelity.** No functional crowd. No character earns no keep.
- **Chronicle.** None; the volume does not opt in.

## Nonblocking observations

1. **p40, caption.** "He waited for the pig all night and all the next day" can be read as standing still at the mountain. A wording such as "He kept stopping to wait for the pig, all night and all the next day" removes the beat of doubt. Wording only.
2. **p40, why the flight is slow.** "Only the pig knew the way" holds. If it is ever touched, a single clause would close the gap a sharp ten-year-old may poke at, for example "only the pig knew which pine wood."
3. **p47, the comparison.** "In that cage I was a beast outside and myself inside, and nobody could see it but you. That's what I did to you." It works with the next balloon's help, but the comparison runs backwards for a moment. A plainer version that keeps his voice: "…and nobody could see it but you. I only ever looked at your outside." This is optional; the current line passes.
4. **p23, time.** Nothing says how long Wukong has been home when the road scene starts. p22's "every evening" implies days. A three-word lead-in to the caption ("A few days later, without Wukong…") would fix it. Not required.
5. **The ledger is out of date, not the script.** Its tiger pages (pp 28–30) and spell page (p28) are one page early against the script (pp 29–30 and 45–46; spell p29). Its Bajie arc says "sent to beg, sleeps", which is not on the page and doesn't need to be. Update the ledger before reference and packet work so prompts cite the right pages.
6. **p48, density.** At 92 words across three beats, the builder prompt will need protected balloon lanes. Do not split the page; it holds its own turn.
