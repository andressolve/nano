# Sequence gate — pages 1–10

## Uninterrupted visual read

Months after crossing the river of sand, Sanzang and his companions are still traveling west through the mountains. Wukong scouts ahead while Sanzang rides, Wujing carries the luggage, and Bajie complains. The monk forbids Wukong to fly him, and the opening also establishes the gold band, the words that can tighten it, Wukong’s furnace-sharpened sight, and the staff hidden behind his ear.

A skeletal demon watches the travelers from a cave and says that eating the monk will confer immortality. When Sanzang grows hungry, Wukong spots peaches to the south, tells him to stay put under Wujing’s protection, and flies off. The demon takes the appearance of a young woman carrying a basket of rice and noodles. She offers the food to Sanzang; he refuses because he believes it may contain meat, while Bajie eagerly urges him to accept.

Wukong returns, sees the skull beneath the disguise, and strikes without stopping to explain. The woman falls and a ghostly skeleton escapes. Sanzang believes Wukong has murdered an innocent person. Wukong points to the abandoned skin and basket as proof, and the basket’s noodles turn into frogs, but Bajie reframes even that proof as one of Wukong’s transformations. Persuaded that Wukong is deceiving him, Sanzang speaks the band-tightening words. Wukong collapses in pain. Sanzang says he used the words because he had to and orders Wukong never to do such a thing again; Wukong insists that he saw a demon, while Sanzang insists that he saw a girl.

The demon survives and returns disguised as an old woman looking for the daughter who supposedly took lunch to her father. Bajie immediately identifies her as the dead girl’s mother and says Wukong is now in trouble. Wukong sees the same skull under the new face and warns Sanzang, who forbids him to act. The old woman draws Sanzang down from his horse with a plea for help. Wukong strikes again, and the spirit escapes again as mist. Sanzang sees only another dead human body and kneels beside it, while Wukong’s attempt to make him acknowledge the escaping mist fails. The batch ends with the distrust between master and disciple worse than before.

## Script comparison

The rendered sequence preserves the scripted causal chain and emotional escalation. All scripted text is present, exact, and attributed to the intended speaker across the page transitions. The named travelers remain distinct and recognizable; the White Bone Lady’s skull, disguises, basket, false bodies, and escaping mist remain legible and consequential; and White Tiger Ridge reads as one continuous setting. The focal contrast between what Wukong can see and what Sanzang believes is sustained through both attacks.

The page turns carry their intended questions forward without a broken handoff. The opening illustrated-prose page, the two spectacle pages, and the dramatic pages provide sufficient mode variation; the dramatic run does not become compressed summary because it stages the accusation, false evidence, manipulation, punishment, renewed deception, and second rupture as separate beats. The first attack receives a full aftermath through Sanzang’s accusation and punishment of Wukong, and the second attack receives immediate aftermath in the escaping mist, Sanzang’s answer, and his hands coming together for the next turn.
