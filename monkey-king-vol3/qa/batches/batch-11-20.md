# Sequence gate — canonical pages 11–20

## Uninterrupted sequence

The monk repeats the tightening spell while Wukong writhes in the dust, then warns him that one more blow will end their bond. The company continues along the ridge in a strained silence: the monk leads without looking back, Bajie and Wujing keep their places, and Wukong trails behind for the first time, chastened but still watching for danger. Meanwhile the hungry demon has one disguise left.

An old man approaches asking after his missing wife and daughter. Wukong recognizes the demon's third face and, mindful of the warning, asks permission before acting. The monk refuses. When the old man invites the monk down to pray, Wukong chooses the monk's life over his own place in the company and raises his staff despite the monk's order. He strikes before the demon can escape, exposing a heap of bones while the monk hides his face against the horse and misses the transformation.

Wukong points to the White Bone Lady's name on the skull, but the monk trusts what he thinks he saw. Bajie supplies a false explanation for both the bones and the carved name, and the monk uses the tightening spell again. In tears, he counts the three apparent killings and dismisses Wukong. Wukong answers that all three blows saved the monk and asks to have the band removed; the monk cannot remove it.

Wukong insists on one last bow. When the monk refuses even to look at him, Wukong's hair-clones make the farewell unavoidable. Before leaving, he charges Wujing to guard the monk's back and to keep Bajie from misleading him, and Wujing accepts. Wukong flies east on his cloud, crying all the way. At Flower-Fruit Mountain, the descendants of the monkeys he once ruled pour out to welcome the Monkey King home.

## Gate judgment

The batch preserves the material story and its causal chain. The warnings on pages 11–12 make Wukong's request on page 13 and choice on pages 14–15 legible; the monk's obstructed view on page 15 directly motivates his rejection of the evidence on page 16; Bajie's intervention carries that misunderstanding into the expulsion on page 17; and pages 18–20 provide a full farewell, departure, grief, and homecoming aftermath.

All scripted dialogue, captions, prose, and attributions are present and exact across the page turns. Sanzang, Wukong, Bajie, Wujing, the disguised White Bone Lady, and the welcoming monkeys remain distinct. The gold band, red staff, white horse, bones and inscribed skull, prayer beads, ridge terrain, and final move to Flower-Fruit Mountain maintain consequential continuity. The visual focus stays with the operative emotional fact of each page, and every turn pays off the preceding setup.

Pacing is strong across the ten pages. Illustrated-prose pauses on pages 12 and 20 frame a run that alternates confrontation, spectacle, evidence, judgment, farewell, and flight. The dramatic pages do not become a wall because pages 15 and 19 expand decisive actions into full-page spectacles, while the consequences of both the killing and the banishment receive sustained aftermath rather than compressed summary.
