# Batch gate — pages 31–40

## Uninterrupted visible sequence

The horse, tied outside the pagoda after seeing Bajie flee and Wujing taken
inside, changes into his white-dragon form and breaks through a high window.
Inside, he coils around the tiger's cage and tries to tear it open, but the
Yellow Robe cuts him and drives him back out through the window into the
stream. By morning he has become a horse again. Bajie emerges from hiding,
finds him hurt, and is startled when the horse speaks.

The horse tells Bajie that the master is caged as a tiger, Wujing is tied up,
and neither of them can defeat the demon. He sends Bajie to Flower-Fruit
Mountain for Wukong. Bajie admits that Wukong will not come because Bajie lied
about the bones; the horse tells him to confess the truth. Bajie flies east on
his poor cloud, frightened and reluctant, rehearsing an honesty that feels
unnatural to him.

At the Water Curtain Cave, the monkeys manhandle Bajie and Wukong pretends not
to know him, then makes clear that he remembers exactly how Bajie helped send
him away. Bajie first tries another comforting lie. Wukong sees through it and
demands the truth. Bajie confesses that he recognized the demon's name on the
skull, lied out of jealousy, and caused Wukong's banishment. He then reports
the master's transformation, Wujing's capture, and the horse's injury and
message. Wukong asks why he should return for a master who dismissed him three
times. Bajie cannot justify it; he simply admits that, despite hating flight
and Wukong's bossiness, he came all that way for him. That act of loyalty moves
Wukong to agree. Bajie offers to guide him but warns that Wukong must fly
slowly. On the return journey, Wukong does wait through the long crossing,
though Bajie still struggles behind and calls for him to slow down.

## Script comparison and gate judgment

The visible sequence preserves the material story and its causal chain. Text
and speaker attribution match the batch script across the turns from the
horse's failed rescue, to his commission of Bajie, to Bajie's confession, to
Wukong's decision and departure. Wukong, Bajie, the horse/dragon, Yellow Robe,
the tiger-master, and bound Wujing remain identifiable. The cage, ropes,
injury, bridle and saddle, Bajie's rake and weak cloud, Wukong's peach, gold
band, patched robe, flower crown, and red staff support the intended beats
without a consequential continuity break. The pagoda exterior and hall,
stream, Water Curtain Cave, and eastward/westward sea crossings read as a
clear progression of settings.

Focal control remains with the character driving each movement: the horse's
rescue attempt, Bajie's reluctant mission and confession, then Wukong's choice
to return. Every page turn advances or answers the question set up before it.
The run does not read as compressed summary. Page 35's illustrated-prose
journey gives the rescue and conversation pages room to breathe, while pages
31–32 and 40 provide spectacle around the dramatic core. The consecutive
Water Curtain Cave pages form a single escalating honesty test rather than a
wall of interchangeable dramatic scenes. Wukong's agreement receives a
visible aftermath on page 40: he is returning, he accommodates Bajie's slower
cloud, and their repaired bond is expressed through action and comedy.

No material reader harm or complete-page redraw issue found.
