# Whole-book release gate — Monkey King, Volume III (canonical pages 1–49)

## 1. Blind account (written after the 600 × 900 read, before opening the script or contract)

### Retell
Months past the river of sand, the monk Sanzang is riding west on a white horse that used to be a dragon. Bajie the pig and Wujing the river ogre carry the luggage, and Wukong runs ahead. Wukong wears a gold band that crushes his head whenever the monk says "Gold, hold fast". His eyes, smoked in Heaven's furnace, see the real face under any disguise. On a crag above them, a skull-faced lady in burial silks wants to eat the monk so she can live forever.

The monk is hungry. Wukong flies south for peaches and tells him not to move. The demon pulls on a girl's face and offers rice and noodles, and the monk accepts because nobody died for that supper. Wukong drops out of the sky, sees the skull under the smile, and strikes. Wukong sees a mist escape. The monk sees a dead girl. The basket turns to maggots and frogs. Bajie says Wukong is the trickster, and the monk says the words.

The same thing happens with an old woman "looking for her daughter". The monk says the words over and over and warns that one more strike means Wukong is no longer his disciple. That afternoon Wukong walks at the back for the first time. A third face comes, an old man at prayer. This time Wukong asks first and is told no. He strikes anyway ("Then send me away, Master. You'll be alive to do it"), and is faster than the mist. A heap of bones is left, with WHITE BONE LADY carved on the skull. Bajie says Wukong scratched it there, and the monk says the words again. Then he sends Wukong away. Wukong asks him to take the band off, and the monk says he knows no words for that. Wukong bows from four sides using hair-clones, tells Wujing to walk at the monk's back, and cries all the way home.

At Flower-Fruit Mountain the monkeys crown him with flowers. The band won't come off, and he won't let anyone take the robe with the patch the monk sewed. Every evening he looks west and says he is watching the weather.

Without him, the road is slower. Wujing leaves the monk to look for Bajie. The monk walks into a golden pagoda hoping to find monks and supper, and finds the Yellow Robe, a huge indigo demon with a scimitar. The demon ties him to a pillar and beats Bajie and Wujing at the gate in one breath. Bajie runs and Wujing is tied up. Tied to the pillar, the monk admits to Wujing what he did. The demon makes a bet: "Everybody's a beast underneath." He sprays the monk into a tiger and cages him with meat. The tiger turns to the wall, paws together, and does not eat. At night the demon shutters the window against the sky.

The horse becomes a dragon and tears through the shutter, but is cut and driven out into the stream. At dawn, back in horse shape, it speaks to Bajie for the first time and sends him to fetch "our big brother", by telling him the truth. Bajie flies badly east, practising the truth. Wukong pretends not to know him and sees through the first lie. Then Bajie tells the truth: he knew the bones were hers, and he lied because he wanted to be the favourite. Wukong goes back. The two clouds fly back, with Wukong slowing down for the pig because only the pig knows the way.

At the gate Wukong's eyes see starlight leaking through the Yellow Robe's cracks: he is a star that ran away from the sky. Threatened with being reported to Heaven, he streaks home. Wukong frees Wujing, finds the monk's face inside the tiger, notes the untouched meat, and sprays the tiger shape off. The monk apologises: "I sent you away for saving my life." Wukong answers, "I heard you the first time." Bajie confesses without being asked and gets the whole luggage pole. The monk promises to believe Wukong even when he can't see it: "Then we'll be wrong together."

In the last image, a woman with a basket comes down the road. The monk looks to Wukong first. Wukong says it's just a lady with lunch.

### Cast (as identified cold)
- **Sanzang**: shaven monk, saffron robe, red plaid kasaya, ringed staff.
- **Wukong**: monkey, hemp robe with a patch, tiger-skin kilt, gold band, red staff.
- **Bajie**: pig, black robe, blue sash, rake.
- **Wujing**: blue-grey ogre with a red beard and crescent spade.
- **The white horse / dragon.**
- **White Bone Lady**: skull face. Her disguises are the girl, the old woman and the old man.
- **Yellow Robe**: indigo face, tusks, black topknot, mustard robe with stars, scimitar.
- **The monkeys of Flower-Fruit Mountain.**

I misidentified nobody and assigned no line to the wrong speaker. The script later confirmed this.

### Causal chain
The monk is hungry, so Wukong leaves, so the demon gets her opening. The monk can't see what Wukong sees, and Bajie works on that. Three strikes and three uses of the words lead to Wukong being sent away. Without Wukong, Wujing is drawn off and the monk walks in alone. The monk is caught, then turned into a tiger. The dragon fails and is hurt. The horse speaks and sends Bajie. Bajie's truth brings Wukong back. Wukong's eyes expose the star, which flees. Wukong unmakes the tiger, the monk apologises, Bajie confesses, and a new promise is made. The basket test is passed.

### The ending: what it cost and why the final image matters
- **Cost:** Wukong's exile and tears. The monk's night as a starving tiger. The horse's cut leg, bandaged on p49 so everyone walks slowly. Bajie's confession and the luggage pole.
- **Why the final image matters:** p49 replays p4 on purpose (a woman, a basket, lunch). This time the monk looks to Wukong first, and Wukong doesn't reach for the staff. The trust has been rebuilt in both directions, and you can see it without anyone saying so. The ending lands.

### Reading-pace record
- **Movement I (pp 1–5):** quick, with clean setup. p1's prose carries the band rule and the eyes rule, and both pay off later.
- **Movement II (pp 6–19):** the triple escalation reads as rising, not repetitive. Panels split between what Wukong sees and what the monk sees (pp 6, 10, 15), so the reader knows more than the monk and feels the injustice. p12's silent afternoon is the right breath. pp 17–19 hit hard.
- **Movement III (pp 20–22):** short and warm. The look west on p22 makes the turn.
- **Movement IV (pp 23–34):** brisk. p30's prose is the longest stop in the book and earns it. The dragon pages (31–32) read clearly from their captions.
- **Movement V (pp 35–40):** Bajie's arc is the emotional surprise and the best-paced stretch.
- **Movement VI (pp 41–49):** the villain's defeat is quick, but the shutter (p30) and "nobody ever comes looking for me" (p25) set it up, so it reads as earned, not convenient. The apology and the coda are well paced.

The pace stalled nowhere. Nothing had to be re-read to be understood.

## 3. Acceptance questions by movement

**I. The Ridge (1–5)**
- **Who acted and what they wanted:** the demon wants to eat the monk; the monk wants food.
- **Choice and why:** Wukong flies for peaches. The monk accepts the girl's basket because it holds no meat.
- **Who was affected:** the monk, who is nearly eaten.
- **What the protagonist believes:** Wukong believes his eyes, and that he is the guard.
- **Why the next scene:** the staff is falling.
- **Result:** passes on the page alone.

**II. Three Faces (6–19)**
- **Who acted and what they wanted:** Wukong wants to protect the monk; the monk wants no killing; Bajie wants Wukong taken down.
- **Choice and why:** Wukong strikes three times, the third time against an explicit no. The monk says the words because he trusts what he sees.
- **Who was affected:** Wukong is sent away.
- **What the protagonist believes:** being right was not enough. He is still loyal ("walk at his back").
- **Why the next scene:** he flies home.
- **Result:** passes.

**III. Home (20–22)**
- **What he wanted:** freedom.
- **What he chose:** to keep the robe.
- **What he believes:** he says he is happy, but looks west.
- **Why the next scene:** the monk has nobody at the front.
- **Result:** passes.

**IV. The Yellow Robe (23–34)**
- **Who acted and what they wanted:** the monk wants supper; the demon wants proof that everyone is a beast.
- **Choice and why:** the monk sends Wujing off. The tiger refuses the meat. The horse risks itself, then speaks.
- **Who was affected:** all of them.
- **What the protagonist believes:** the monk believes "I sent away the one who could have kept me."
- **Why the next scene:** the pig has to go back.
- **Result:** passes.

**V. Going Back (35–40)**
- **What Bajie wanted:** to be the favourite. He chooses the truth, which is the only thing that will move Wukong.
- **What Wukong chose and why:** to go back, because "a pig came all the way back for a monkey."
- **Why the next scene:** two clouds are heading west.
- **Result:** passes. The slow flight is explained on the page (p39, p40).

**VI. The Star in the Pagoda (41–49)**
- **What Wukong chose:** to expose the demon rather than kill it, and not to chase it.
- **What the monk chose:** to apologise and promise trust.
- **What Bajie chose:** to confess unasked.
- **What the protagonist believes:** Wukong is believed. He shows this by not running ahead and not reaching for the staff.
- **Result:** passes.

No movement depends on information outside the pages.

## 5. Nonblocking observations (none would change how a reader follows the story, and none justifies replacing a promoted page)

- **Bajie's design shifts.** He alternates between two looks:
  - a smooth, pink, human-faced pig with no tusks, sometimes in a cap, on pp 1, 3, 5, 9, 10, 11, 18 and 46;
  - a hairier boar with tusks on pp 4, 7, 16, 26, 27, 33–39 and 48.

  He is the only pig and is never mistaken for anyone, so this is drift, not confusion.
- **Wujing looks less like an ogre in some small background figures.** On p8 panel 3 and p12 he could pass for a man in plain clothes. He is unmistakable everywhere else.
- **The cage changes shape.** It is rectangular on pp 30 and 45 and cylindrical on p32. Small object drift.
- **p41 panel 1:** the horse walks normally, not limping or holding up its right foreleg as scripted. The injury is still carried by p33, p38 and p49's bandage.
- **p4 panel 3:** the incoming streak of cloud in the sky, and the green pinpoints in the girl's eyes, can't be read at proof scale. p5 delivers Wukong's arrival anyway.
- **Gold band on Wukong:** present and clear on every page where he is shown at readable size. On the small or distant figures (p1 panel 1, p12, p20, p42, and arguably p44) it can't be confirmed at 600 × 900. I am not reporting it as missing.
- **Red blood:** none anywhere. The horse's foreleg gash on p33 is drawn dark.
- **Skulls on Wujing:** none anywhere.
- **Typos, wrong letters, punctuation slips:** none found. All lettering matches the script, including the British spellings on p35 and p38, which are as written.
