## Page intent

## PAGE 10

Dramatic page. The turn: the bone hands reach for the monk, Wukong strikes a second time, the mist escapes again, and the monk's hands come together. The old woman, cane dropped, reaches up for the monk on his horse, and the reader sees faint bone fingers behind her hands closing on his robe; the monk sees only an old woman's hands. The dominant beat is the staff coming down and the old woman falling face-down and lying still, while inside Wukong's sight, for him and the reader only, the white mist streams away toward the crag. Wukong shouts after it and asks if the monk saw the mist. The monk, climbing down, white-faced, says he saw an old woman looking for her daughter, and his palms are already closing. No blood.

with both hands for the monk on the horse; and in the reader's view, behind

white-faced, his palms coming together. Bajie on the right behind him,
nodding hard.

> **SANZANG:** "I saw an old woman looking for her daughter."

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 10 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All three lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order: the old woman's "come down", Wukong's "did you see the mist?", the monk's "I saw an old woman…", each owned by its speaker's mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, and prop counts; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The second blow does not land
One panel owns the page: the staff down, the old woman fallen face-down and still, Wukong shouting after the mist. If the eye cannot find what happened, that is blocking.

### C3 — The seeing rule breaks
The bone fingers are faint behind her hands and the monk does not react to them; the mist appears only inside Wukong's view. A mist or skull plain to the monk, or the monk acting on what he did not see, is blocking.

### C4 — No blood
Any blood, wound or gore on the fallen old woman, or anywhere, is blocking. Her face is hidden.

### C5 — Band on, attribution, identity
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in (band on); missing is blocking. The monk's closing line hangs from his own mouth, not Bajie's. The old woman, the monk, Wukong and Bajie each read as themselves; a swapped identity is blocking.
