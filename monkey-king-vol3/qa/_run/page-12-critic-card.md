## Page intent

## PAGE 12

Illustrated-prose page. The turn: the silent afternoon, the monk riding in front without looking back, Bajie whistling, and Wukong walking behind for the first time. One wide amber image of the ridge road carries it: the monk on the white horse leading, Bajie and Wujing with the luggage pole, and far at the back, small, Wukong with his head down, watching the rocks. A clear gap separates him from the others. The prose field sits in the open sky and tells the silence, the order of march, what this costs a monkey who always ran ahead, and that on her crag the demon has one more face. The quiet distance between Wukong and the company is the page; the reader turns knowing the third face is coming.

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The long amber afternoon. The road along the
ridge. The monk on the white horse in front, not looking back; Bajie and
Wujing with the luggage pole; and at the very back, small, Wukong walking with

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the ridge)*
> "When the monk stopped at last, nobody said anything for a long time.
>
> They went on along the ridge in the long afternoon. The monk rode in front and did not look back. Bajie whistled. Wujing walked at the monk's back and said nothing at all.
>
> And Wukong, who had run ahead of them every day since the monk let him out from under the mountain, walked behind, with his head down, watching every rock and every shadow on the road.
>
> On her crag, the demon was very hungry now, and very angry, and she had one more face."

**Why you turn the page:** the demon has one more face, and the monk has made his last warning.

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, as one prose field, with no other text (no balloons, no lettered whistle). A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, and prop counts; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The mode or the distance fails
This is an illustrated-prose page: one wide amber image of the ridge road, the prose field over open sky, never over figures. The monk leads on the horse, Bajie and Wujing follow with the pole, and Wukong walks far behind, alone, head down, with a clear gap. If Wukong is not visibly behind and apart, the page's turn is lost; that is blocking.

### C3 — Band on
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in (band on), reading as a gold line even at this size; a missing band is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Each traveller reads as himself at this distance: the shaven monk in saffron and red on the white horse, the pig-headed Bajie in black, the gaunt red-bearded Wujing, the small golden monkey. A skull on Wujing is blocking; fine finish is not.
