# Exact script — pages 41–49

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 41 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the gate of the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong landing in the clearing on the right; the white
horse limping out of the pines toward him on the left, holding up its right
foreleg. Bajie's cloud collapsing behind them.

> **WUKONG:** "I hear you talked, horse. Good. Now stay back."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong at the black-iron gate, the red staff
out, hammering on it; the gate shuddering.

> **WUKONG:** "Yellow Robe! Come out and meet the monk's first disciple!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The gate open, the Yellow Robe filling it on the left,
grinning, scimitar on his shoulder. Wukong on the right, grinning back.

> **YELLOW ROBE:** "The monkey! I heard your monk sent you away."
>
> **WUKONG:** "He did. So I came back."

**Why you turn the page:** the fight nobody else could win is starting.

## PAGE 42 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the gate of the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The fight: the clearing torn up, pines bending;
Wukong in mid-air, the red staff spinning; the Yellow Robe's silver scimitar
meeting it in a burst of sparks. Bajie and the limping white horse watching
from the trees.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "The pig could not beat him. The ogre could not. The dragon could not."
>
> **SOUND** *(where staff meets blade)*
> "KRANG"

**Why you turn the page:** the monkey has not lost yet.

## PAGE 43 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the gate of the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Staff and scimitar locked, the two faces close: the
Yellow Robe on the left, Wukong on the right, the red rings of his eyes
flaring.

> **YELLOW ROBE:** "Nobody beats me, monkey. Nobody in the world."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong's view: silver starlight shining out
through cracks in the demon's indigo face and his robe. Wukong on the right,
grinning.

> **WUKONG:** "Not in the world. But I lived in Heaven once, and I know a star when I see one."
>
> **WUKONG:** "You're a star that ran away from the sky."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The Yellow Robe on the left, staggering back, the
scimitar dropping, silver light pouring from his face. Wukong on the right,
staff ready.

> **YELLOW ROBE:** "Who told you? Nobody can see that!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "My eyes can. Heaven counts its stars every night, and one is missing. Shall I tell them where you're hiding?"

**Why you turn the page:** the demon has been seen.

## PAGE 44 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the gate of the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Dusk. The Yellow Robe rising into the evening
sky as a streak of silver light, becoming one bright star among the first
stars. On the ground below, Wukong with the staff on his shoulder, not
chasing, already turning toward the pagoda door. Bajie on the left, pointing
up.

> **BAJIE:** "He's getting away! Chase him!"
>
> **WUKONG:** "He's not getting away. He's going home to the sky he was hiding from, before Heaven counts tonight."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I've got a monk to get."

**Why you turn the page:** the star is gone, and the monk is inside.

## PAGE 45 · *dramatic*

**Location:** inside the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong on the right snapping Wujing's ropes at the black
pillar with his bare hands. Wujing on the left, head bowed.

> **WUJING:** "Wukong, I didn't keep him. I left him alone to look for the pig."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Nobody could have kept him from that demon. That's why the horse sent for me."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The iron cage, lit by the iron lamp; high
above, the hall's one window boarded shut again. The
tiger shrinking back into the far corner, ears flat, the torn red kasaya at
its throat, the monk's dark eyes on Wukong. The untouched meat on the cage
floor. Wukong on the right, at the bars, the red rings of his eyes flaring: inside
the tiger, for him and the reader, the pale ghost of the monk's own face.

> **WUKONG:** "I'd know your face under any face, Master. Don't be afraid of me."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Wukong, looking down at the meat, then up at the
tiger.

> **WUKONG:** "Claws, teeth, and three days hungry, and you didn't touch the meat. You kept your rule, even like this."

**Why you turn the page:** he has found his monk, and his monk is still a tiger.

## PAGE 46 · *dramatic*

**Location:** inside the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong on the right, the cage door open, lifting a water
jar. Bajie on the left, in the doorway, gaping.

> **BAJIE:** "How do you undo a tiger?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Changing shapes is my trick, Bajie. I know how to take one off."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong spraying a mouthful of water over the
tiger; in the silver spray the stripes fading, the monk's shape rising out
of the tiger's, the red kasaya whole on his shoulder again.

> **SOUND** *(in the spray)*
> "PFFFT"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Sanzang, a man again, on his knees on the floor of the
open cage, dripping, looking up. Wukong on the right, standing over him.

> **SANZANG:** "Wukong. You came back for me."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Somebody had to. You can't even be a tiger properly."

**Why you turn the page:** the monk is a man again, and he owes the monkey something.

## PAGE 47 · *dramatic*

**Location:** inside the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Sanzang on the left, still on his knees. Wukong on the
right, standing.

> **SANZANG:** "In that cage I was a beast outside and myself inside, and nobody could see it but you. That's what I did to you."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Close on Sanzang, palms together, bowing his
head to the monkey.

> **SANZANG:** "I should have believed you. I'm sorry, Wukong. I sent you away for saving my life."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Wukong on the right, hit by it: his eyes wet,
looking away, one hand gone to the mended patch on his shoulder, the gold
band on his brow. Sanzang's bowed head at the left edge.

> **WUKONG:** "Get up, Master, before the pig sees you bowing to a monkey."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I heard you. I heard you the first time."

**Why you turn the page:** the monk has said sorry, and the monkey has heard it.

## PAGE 48 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the clearing outside the golden pagoda.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Morning in the clearing. Bajie on the left, kneeling in
front of the monk, ears down, nobody asking him to. Sanzang on the right, his
hand coming to rest on Bajie's big head.

> **BAJIE:** "Master, it was me. I knew they were her bones. I said it was a trick because I wanted you to like me best."
>
> **SANZANG:** "You told me yourself, Bajie, and nobody made you. You can carry the whole luggage pole today."
>
> **BAJIE:** "That's fair, Master."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Sanzang on the left and Wukong on the right,
face to face. The white horse and Wujing behind them.

> **SANZANG:** "From now on, if you tell me a face is a demon's, I'll believe you, even when I can't see it."
>
> **WUKONG:** "And what if I'm wrong?"
>
> **SANZANG:** "Then we'll be wrong together. I'd rather that than walk without you."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Bajie on the left, nose to nose with the white horse,
pleading. The horse on the right, looking away, mouth shut. Wujing behind,
almost smiling.

> **BAJIE:** "Go on, horse, say something! Tell them you can talk!"

**Why you turn the page:** the horse will not say a word, and the road is waiting.

## PAGE 49 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the road west.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Morning. A long road going west through green
hills into far blue mountains. The monk walking, leading the white horse,
whose right foreleg is bandaged; Wukong walking beside the monk, not ahead,
the gold band on his brow; Bajie behind, carrying the whole luggage pole
alone, both bundles swinging, grumbling; Wujing walking beside him with only
his crescent spade.
Coming down the road toward them, small, a woman with a basket on her arm.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the mountains)*
> "In the morning they went on west. The horse's leg was bandaged, so the monk walked and led him, and nobody minded how slow it was. Bajie carried the whole luggage pole and complained about it, and nobody minded that either.
>
> Wukong did not run ahead to the top of the next hill. He walked beside the monk and complained about the pace.
>
> Toward noon a woman came down the road toward them with a basket on her arm. The monk stopped, and looked first at Wukong.
>
> Wukong looked at her for a long moment. 'Just a lady with lunch, Master,' he said. 'You can say thank you.'"

**Why you turn the page:** there is no next page. The monk looked at Wukong first, and Wukong did not reach for his staff.
