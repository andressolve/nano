## Page intent

## PAGE 25

Dramatic. The Yellow Robe's huge hand closes on the monk's shoulder, and he enjoys himself: everybody on the road wants to eat this monk, and this one walked into the one house where nobody comes looking. Then the monk is tied to a thick black pillar, frightened, chin up, and he speaks the bravest sentence he has: he has never eaten meat or hurt anyone; the demon may eat him, but may not hurt his friends. The demon, sprawled on his stone bed tossing his scimitar, throws back his head and laughs at the word "may". The dominant turn: caught, the monk forbids the demon to hurt his friends, and the demon laughs. The monk's small defiant face at the pillar owns the page; the grab and the laugh frame it. The reader turns because the demon thinks the monk is a joke.

### C2 — The monk's defiance owns the page
The dominant image is the monk tied to the black pillar, frightened, chin up, speaking; the reader must see a small brave man, not a cowering one or a smiling one. If the monk's panel does not own the page, or the laugh panel swallows it, blocking. Dramatic mode.

### C3 — Attribution
Both opening lines hang from the demon's mouth as he grips the monk; the monk is silent there. "I've never eaten meat..." hangs from the monk's mouth, not the demon's. "May not!..." hangs from the laughing demon's open mouth. A tail to the wrong mouth or a balloon on a silent figure is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and separation
The Yellow Robe (indigo, tusks, black topknot, mustard-ochre with silver stars over black armour, scimitar) is half again the monk's height; the monk is shaven, in saffron under a red kasaya. If they blur or swap colours, blocking. **Any monkey on the page is blocking.** The monk holding his ringed staff is blocking (it was dropped at the door).

### C5 — Integrity
