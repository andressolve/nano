## Page intent

## PAGE 31

Spectacle. Night in the pine wood. The white horse, tied to his pine, has seen everything: the pig run, the ogre dragged inside. Then the reader learns his secret with him. In the dominant image the horse uncoils into a slim white dragon, sea-green eyes and pale-blue mane, the empty bridle falling away. In the last beat the dragon tears the shutter off the pagoda's high window and slides in with the moonlight behind him. The dominant turn is the horse becoming the dragon and going in. The reader turns because the dragon has gone in after the monk. Wukong is absent. The pagoda's storeys, the pines and the gate stay subordinate to the transformation; two short captions carry the words and the pictures carry the rest.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 31 — card

### C1 — Caption transcription fails
Either scripted caption is missing, altered, duplicated, out of order, or does not read from the 600 × 900 proof, or any other text appears (no balloons are scripted); a caption that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word; the words and their order are what must match. Also nonblocking: the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, and incidental prop counts. No object carries scripted words.

### C2 — The transformation does not own the page
The eye does not find the white horse becoming the white dragon as the page's dominant image, or the sequence does not read in order: horse tied in the pines, horse becoming dragon, dragon tearing the shutter off and going in. The page must read as spectacle, its pictures carrying the turn.

### C3 — Horse and dragon are not the same creature
The dragon lacks the sea-green eyes or pale-blue mane the horse has, or reads as a different beast (antlers, whiskers, blue-green scales, gold eyes, a human body), so the reader cannot see that the horse became him.

### C4 — Wounds or shutter in the wrong state
The horse or the dragon shows a wound, bandage or blood on this page; or the shutter is already gone before the last panel, or the last panel does not show the dragon tearing it off and entering. The number of pagoda storeys or pines is nonblocking.

### C5 — Wrong figures or generation failure
Wukong, the monk, Bajie, Wujing or the Yellow Robe appears; the horse and the dragon are drawn as two separate animals side by side in the dominant panel rather than one becoming the other; gross focal anatomy on either form.
