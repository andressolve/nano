## Page intent

Dramatic. The old man reaches both hands up to the monk, smiling, inviting him down to pray; the reader alone glimpses the green pinpoints in his eyes and the bone fingers behind his hands. The monk starts to slide off the horse toward him. ### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the 3 strings is missing, altered, duplicated, reordered, unreadable from the 600 × 900 proof, or any extra text appears. A string that reads is not measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; words and order must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Wukong's choice does not land
One panel owns the page: Wukong, face set, the staff growing in his fist. If the old man's invitation or the monk's descent dominates, or the page is not dramatic, it blocks.

### C3 — Attribution
"Come down, holy master…" from the old man's mouth; "Then send me away…" from Wukong's; "Wukong, no!" from Sanzang's, his mouth open. Each tail to its own speaker; a line hung on the wrong figure blocks.

### C4 — Band on; identity
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in (band on) is blocking. The old man never reads as the monk.

### C5 — Nobody acts on what he did not see
The bone fingers are a faint glimpse for the reader. Solid skeleton hands that the monk smiles at or slides toward block; their absence is nonblocking if the green-eyed smile carries the threat. No blow lands, no bones on the road, no blood.

### C6 — Focal integrity
Wukong's two hands on the staff and the speakers' faces are whole; the staff is a straight red rod with gold caps.
