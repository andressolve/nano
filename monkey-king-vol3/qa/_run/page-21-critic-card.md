## Page intent

## PAGE 21

Dramatic. Inside the Water Curtain Cave, behind the falling water, Wukong is crowned again and tries to undo the whole journey in one afternoon. The monkeys set a crown of flowers and leaves on his head, on top of the gold band, while he lounges on the stone throne and announces that there will be no monk, no magic words, no walking, ever again. Then the page's one dominant turn, which the middle panel owns: he hauls at the band with both hands and a chain of monkeys hauls at him like a tug-of-war, and the band will not move. Last, a small monkey tugs at his hemp robe and he snatches it back, fierce, a hand over the patch his monk sewed. He is the only speaker; the monkeys are silent. The reader turns because he is home and free, and he will not take off the robe.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 21 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave behind the waterfall.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Inside the cave behind the falling water: young brown
monkeys putting a crown of flowers and leaves on Wukong's head, on top of the
gold band. He sits on a stone throne, one foot up.

> **WUKONG:** "That's right, your king is home! No monk, no magic words, and no walking. Not ever again!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong straining with both hands to drag the
gold band up off his head; it will not move. A chain of monkeys pulling on
him from behind, like a tug-of-war.

> **WUKONG:** "And this thing comes off too. Pull! Pull harder! It won't come off."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** A small monkey on the left tugging at the sleeve of
Wukong's hemp robe to take it off him. Wukong on the right, snatching the
sleeve back, fierce, a hand over the mended patch on his shoulder.

> **WUKONG:** "Leave the robe alone. My monk sewed that patch himself."

**Why you turn the page:** he is home and free, and he will not take off the robe.

### C1 — Transcription
All three of Wukong's lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in script order, one balloon per panel, no extra text (no chatter, caption, or label). A line that does not read, is missing, doubled, or out of order is blocking; a line that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and an exclamation mark doubled or dropped; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly beside Wukong in its panel is owned even if its tail wanders. The run length of a repeated letter in any sound cue is nonblocking (none is scripted), and the count of background props or crowd figures is never a transcription matter.

### C2 — The band will not come off
Dramatic mode; the middle panel owns the page by eye: Wukong hauling at the band with both hands, the monkeys pulling him like a tug-of-war, and the band plainly staying on. Blocking if the band appears lifted, loosened, or off, or if the reader cannot tell he is failing to remove it.

### C3 — Attribution
Wukong is the only speaker. Each balloon hangs by its tail from Wukong's mouth in its own panel, his mouth visible; in the last panel he stands on the right with the balloon on his side. Blocking if a balloon points at a monkey or reads before the action it answers.

### C4 — Wukong, band, crown, robe
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in is blocking: band on, still showing under the flower crown. He wears the hemp robe in every panel and keeps it; in the last panel one hand snatches the sleeve back and one covers the shoulder patch. A missing band or the robe coming off is blocking; the crown's exact flowers or tilt are not.

### C5 — Monkeys and integrity
Every other monkey is brown, bare, young: no clothing, no gold, no band, no balloon. Wukong drawn once per panel; the focal hands on the band are exactly two. The number of monkeys in the chain or at the crowning is nonblocking. No blood.
