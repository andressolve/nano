## Page intent

## PAGE 39

Dramatic. Wukong stands up off the throne, the flower crown slipping, and asks the hard question: the monk sent him away, said the words three times in one day; why should he go back? The dominant turn is Bajie's answer, on his feet and shouting, honest for once: he does not know, he hates flying, he hates being bossed, and he came all the way back anyway. That is what decides it. In the close that follows, Wukong lays his hand on the mended patch at the shoulder of his robe as the flower crown falls away behind him and the gold band shines on his brow; a pig came back for a monkey, on a cloud like that. He asks where the pagoda is, and Bajie, in frame, says he will show him, slowly. The reader turns because Wukong is going back.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 39 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All five lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A line that does not read, is missing, doubled, reworded or hangs from the wrong mouth is blocking; a line that reads is never measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly or straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, and the number of background monkeys or props. The words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement, directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders. No object carries scripted words.

### C2 — Bajie's shout owns the page
Dramatic. The dominant image is Bajie on his feet, shouting, honest, with Wukong taking it in. Blocking if the shout reads as anger at the monk or as a gag, or if the close on Wukong takes the page from it.

### C3 — Who says what
"He sent me away…" is Wukong's; "I don't know!…" is Bajie's; "A pig came all the way back…" and "Fine. Where is this pagoda…" are Wukong's; "I'll show you…" is Bajie's, and Bajie must be visibly in frame with the tail to his mouth when he says it. A reply with no speaker in frame, or any line tailed to the wrong mouth, is blocking.

### C4 — Band on, crown off
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in; under the flower crown the band still shows. A missing or hidden band is blocking. The crown slips and then falls away behind him; a crown still seated on his head in the close is blocking because it undoes the decision.

### C5 — The patch
In the close, one hand of Wukong's rests on the mended patch at the shoulder of his hemp robe. Blocking if the gesture does not read or the hand is malformed. Which shoulder is nonblocking.

### C6 — Identities
Wukong reads as the small golden monkey with the ruff, robe and tail; Bajie as the pig. No eye flare. Background monkeys, if any, are brown and bare.
