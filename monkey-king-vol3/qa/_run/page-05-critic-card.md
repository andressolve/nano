Spectacle. Wukong drops out of the sky onto the road, the peaches tumbling from his arms, his red staff already swinging down, the red rings of his gold eyes flaring. ## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 5 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The one caption must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, tail-free, with no other text on the page; if it does not read, or any balloon, sound, or extra text appears, it is blocking, and a caption that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word. The peaches are four; four or five passes, and their count never blocks.

### C2 — The seeing rule
The skull is shown only as Wukong's view: a faint, translucent ghost over the still-visible smiling girl, his eyes flaring. If the girl is drawn openly as a solid skull or monster for all to see, or if the monk or the pig point at or recoil from a skull, the reader loses why the monk will be horrified; blocking.

### C3 — The turn does not land
One spectacle image owns the page: Wukong dropping from the sky, staff coming down over the girl, the monk and the pig too late. If the staff has already struck, or if the strike reads as a wound or blood, it is blocking.

### C4 — Band on
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in. A bare brow, a crown, or a jeweled or wide band is blocking; its finish is nonblocking.

### C5 — Identity and separation
Wukong the small golden monkey in hemp with the red-and-gold iron staff; the girl in peach-pink with her plait and basket; the monk shaven in saffron and red; the pig in black; the ogre gaunt and blue-gray with red beard. A skull, necklace, or neck cord on Wujing is blocking (his sash of river reeds at the waist is correct).

### C6 — Generation integrity
A second Wukong, the eyes as pure red or beams, malformed hands on the staff, or the monk's ringed staff drawn as Wukong's staff is blocking. Background anatomy and texture are not.
