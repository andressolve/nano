## Page intent

## PAGE 9

Dramatic page. The turn: a weeping old woman comes looking for her daughter, Bajie cries "her mother!", and Wukong sees the same skull under the new face. First, on the crag, the White Bone Lady draws an old woman's kind wrinkled face down over her skull. Then the dominant beat on the road: the old woman hobbling up on her cane, crying for her daughter, the monk stricken on his white horse, Bajie pointing and crowing that the monkey is in trouble now. Last, close on Wukong's flaring eyes, and through the old woman's face beyond him the faint ghost of the skull, which only he and the reader see. He warns the monk; the monk tells him not to dare. The reader turns on that order.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Up on the crag: the White Bone Lady drawing a second face
down over her skull, an old woman's, wrinkled and kind, a cane in her bone
hand.

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** On the road, the old woman hobbling toward them
on her bamboo cane, weeping. Sanzang on the left on the white horse,
stricken. Bajie on the right beside the horse, pointing at her.

> **OLD WOMAN:** "My daughter! Has anyone seen my daughter? She went to take her father his lunch!"
>
> **BAJIE:** "That's the girl's mother! Now you're in trouble, monkey."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Wukong on the right, the red rings of his eyes
flaring; through the old woman's wrinkled face beyond him, the pale ghost of
the same skull. Sanzang on the left at the edge of the panel, on the horse.

> **WUKONG:** "It's her again, Master. Same skull, under a different face."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Wukong, don't you dare."

**Why you turn the page:** Wukong can see the skull, and the monk has told him not to dare.

## Page 9 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order: the old woman's "My daughter!", Bajie's "That's the girl's mother!", Wukong's "Same skull", the monk's "don't you dare", each owned by its speaker's mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, and prop counts; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The old woman's arrival does not land
One panel owns the page: the weeping old woman on her cane, the stricken monk on the horse, Bajie pointing. If the eye cannot find her, that is blocking.

### C3 — The seeing rule breaks
The skull shows through the old woman's face only in the panel framed as Wukong's view, faint or tinted by his sight. In the arrival panel she is plainly an old woman. A skull visible to the monk, or the monk or Bajie reacting to it, is blocking.

### C4 — Attribution
The old woman's cry hangs from her mouth; Bajie's reply from his and after hers; "It's her again, Master…" from Wukong's; "Wukong, don't you dare." from the monk's, read last. A balloon tailed to the wrong mouth is blocking.

### C5 — Band on, and the disguise reads
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in (band on); missing is blocking. The crag panel shows the bone lady putting on an old woman's face; the old woman (white bun, brown jacket, cane) must not read as the girl. A swapped identity is blocking.
