## Page intent

## PAGE 36

Dramatic. Bajie lands on Flower-Fruit Mountain and a swarm of young brown monkeys leaps on him, grabbing his ears and his rake. Inside the cave behind the waterfall they drag him before the stone throne, where Wukong lounges, the flower crown on top of the gold band, one foot up, eating a peach, not even looking: "I don't know any pigs. Throw him in the waterfall." The dominant turn is that refusal, and the comedy of it holding until the last line, when Bajie, flattened under sitting monkeys, pleads "It's me, Bajie!" and Wukong, still not looking, answers that he once knew a Bajie who talked a monk into sending him away. The monkeys are noise and weight, never the focus. The reader turns because Wukong knows exactly who he is.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 36 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All four lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A line that does not read, is missing, doubled, reworded or hangs from the wrong mouth is blocking; a line that reads is never measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly or straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, and the number of monkeys or background props. The words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement, directly under its speaker's previous balloon or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders. No object carries scripted words.

### C2 — The refusal owns the page
Dramatic. The dominant image is Wukong at ease on his throne, eating a peach, not looking at the pig. Blocking if the monkey swarm takes over the page so the refusal does not land, or if Wukong is visibly looking at Bajie in the panel where he says "I don't know any pigs".

### C3 — Who says what
"Ow!" and "Big brother!" hang from Bajie's mouth; "Who's this?" and "I knew a Bajie once" from Wukong's. In the last panel Bajie's line reads before Wukong's reply. A Bajie line tailed to Wukong, or the other way round, is blocking.

### C4 — Band on
The thin gold band on Wukong's brow in every panel he is in; under the flower crown the band still shows. A missing or hidden band is blocking. A crown with a different mix of flowers is nonblocking.

### C5 — Identities
Wukong reads as the small golden monkey with the ruff, the hemp robe and the tail; Bajie as the pig. The other monkeys are brown, bare and young, with no clothing, no gold and no band; a second golden or robed monkey is blocking. Repeated monkey faces are nonblocking.
