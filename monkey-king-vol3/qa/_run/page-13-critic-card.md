## Page intent

## PAGE 13

Dramatic. Late amber afternoon on the ridge. A gentle white-bearded old man with prayer beads comes up the road asking after his lost wife and daughter, and the monk's face softens. The page belongs to Wukong at the horse's side: for the first time he does not swing first. Low, urgent, not joking, he asks permission to stop the third face, and the monk refuses him. The dominant turn is that exchange: Wukong asks first, and the monk says no. The last beat is Wukong silent, the gold band on his brow, looking from the monk to the old man and back while his hand drifts toward the needle behind his ear; the reader turns to see what that hand will do. The old man's softness, the beads, and the road stay subordinate to the ask and the refusal.

## Exact owner-controlled script

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** An old man coming along the road toward them,
white-bearded, in a gray pilgrim's gown, brown prayer beads clicking through
his fingers. Sanzang on the left on the horse, his face softening.

> **OLD MAN:** "Has a holy master seen my wife? Or my daughter? They went out this morning and never came home."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong on the right at the horse's side, low and
urgent, not joking, the red rings of his eyes bright. Sanzang on the left in
the saddle, looking down at him.

> **WUKONG:** "Master, it's her again, the third face. I'm asking you first this time. Let me stop her."
>
> **SANZANG:** "No. He's an old man saying his prayers."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Wukong, the gold band on his brow, silent,
looking from the monk to the old man and back, his hand going up toward the
needle behind his ear.

**Why you turn the page:** Wukong has asked, and the monk has said no.

### C2 — The ask and the refusal do not land
One panel must plainly own the page: Wukong asking, urgent and unjoking, and the monk refusing. If the eye finds the old man or scenery first, or the page is not dramatic, it blocks.

### C3 — Attribution of the exchange
The old man's line hangs from the old man; "Master, it's her again…" hangs from Wukong's mouth by tail; "No. He's an old man…" hangs from Sanzang's and reads after Wukong's. A reply readable first or tailed to the wrong mouth blocks.
