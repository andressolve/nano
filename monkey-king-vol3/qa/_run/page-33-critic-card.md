## Page intent

## PAGE 33

Dramatic. Dawn at the stream by the pine wood. Bajie, who hid in the reeds all night, crawls out muddy and calling, then finds the white horse lying in the shallow water with a clean dark gash on his right foreleg and his bridle broken. A caption tells the reader the dragon could not hold his shape. Bajie kneels and asks who did this. Then the page's one turn: the horse lifts his head, sea-green eyes steady, and speaks for the first time in the book. The reader turns because the horse has spoken. Bajie owns the comedy of the first two beats, the horse owns the last. Wukong is absent. The reeds, the water and the far pagoda stay subordinate.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or

focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 33 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the four strings is missing, altered, duplicated, reordered, or does not read from the 600 × 900 proof, or extra text appears; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word; the words and their order are what must match. Also nonblocking: the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, and incidental prop counts. No object carries scripted words. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Attribution of the three spoken lines fails
Both of Bajie's lines must hang by their tails from Bajie's mouth, and "Bajie. Listen to me." must hang from the horse's mouth with the horse in frame. A horse balloon whose tail points at Bajie, or a Bajie line whose tail points at the horse, is blocking. The caption is tail-free.

### C3 — The horse speaking does not land
The last panel does not read by eye as the horse lifting his head to speak, sea-green eyes steady, so the reader misses that the horse has spoken; or the dominant panel does not show the horse lying hurt in the stream with Bajie beside him.

### C4 — Identity or wound state fails
Bajie loses the pig head, floppy ears, belly or black tunic; the horse loses the pale-blue mane or sea-green eyes; the right-foreleg wound is missing, bandaged, or bloody, or the water runs red.

### C5 — Wrong figures or generation failure
Wukong, the monk, Wujing, the dragon or the Yellow Robe appears; a duplicated Bajie or horse within one panel; gross focal anatomy on either face. Reed and stone counts are nonblocking.
