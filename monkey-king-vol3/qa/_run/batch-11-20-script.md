# Exact script — pages 11–20

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 11 · *spectacle*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Wukong rolling over and over in the white dust,
both hands clamped on the gold band, tail thrashing, mouth open in a howl.
The monk standing over him, palms together, eyes shut, saying the words and
not stopping. Bajie and Wujing behind the horse; Wujing looking away. At the
edge of the road, the old woman lying still, and her cane.

> **SANZANG:** "Gold, hold fast. Gold, hold fast. Gold, hold fast."
>
> **SANZANG:** "If you strike one more person, you are not my disciple."

**Why you turn the page:** the monk has made his last warning.

## PAGE 12 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The long amber afternoon. The road along the
ridge. The monk on the white horse in front, not looking back; Bajie and
Wujing with the luggage pole; and at the very back, small, Wukong walking with
his head down, behind them all for the first time.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the ridge)*
> "When the monk stopped at last, nobody said anything for a long time.
>
> They went on along the ridge in the long afternoon. The monk rode in front and did not look back. Bajie whistled. Wujing walked at the monk's back and said nothing at all.
>
> And Wukong, who had run ahead of them every day since the monk let him out from under the mountain, walked behind, with his head down, watching every rock and every shadow on the road.
>
> On her crag, the demon was very hungry now, and very angry, and she had one more face."

**Why you turn the page:** the demon has one more face, and the monk has made his last warning.

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** An old man coming along the road toward them,
white-bearded, in a gray pilgrim's gown, brown prayer beads clicking through
his fingers. Sanzang on the left on the horse, his face softening.

> **OLD MAN:** "Has a holy master seen my wife? Or my daughter? They went out this morning and never came home."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong on the right at the horse's side, low and
urgent, not joking, the red rings of his eyes bright. Sanzang on the left in
the saddle, looking down at him.

> **WUKONG:** "Master, it's her again, the third face. I'm asking you first this time. Let me stop her."
>
> **SANZANG:** "No. He's an old man saying his prayers."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Wukong, the gold band on his brow, silent,
looking from the monk to the old man and back, his hand going up toward the
needle behind his ear.

**Why you turn the page:** Wukong has asked, and the monk has said no.

## PAGE 14 · *dramatic*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The old man on the left at the horse's other side,
reaching both hands up to the monk, smiling; green pinpoints in his eyes; and
behind his hands, for the reader, bone fingers.

> **OLD MAN:** "Come down, holy master. Pray with me a while."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Wukong on the right, the needle out from behind
his ear and already growing into the red staff in his fist, his face set.
Sanzang on the left, starting to slide down off the horse toward the old man.

> **WUKONG:** "Then send me away, Master. You'll be alive to do it."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Sanzang on the left, half off the horse, turning, seeing
the staff go up, his mouth wide.

> **SANZANG:** "Wukong, no!"

**Why you turn the page:** Wukong has chosen.

## PAGE 15 · *spectacle*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Seen as Wukong sees it, through the flare of
his red-ringed eyes: the red staff coming down; the old man's shape bursting
apart, and in the middle of the burst, for Wukong and the reader only, the
White Bone Lady's skull face shrieking, caught, the mist with nowhere to go;
white bones falling and scattering onto the road, brown prayer beads flying.
Wukong in mid-air above it, both hands on the staff. Sanzang at the side,
his face buried in the white horse's neck, eyes shut tight: he sees none of
it.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "This time he was faster than the mist."

**Why you turn the page:** there is a heap of bones on the road where the old man was.

---

## PAGE 16 · *dramatic*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** A neat heap of white bones on the road, the skull on top.
Wukong on the right, crouched beside it, pointing. Sanzang on the left,
staring.

> **WUKONG:** "There she is. That's what was under all three faces. Look at the skull, Master. A demon's name, on a demon's skull. Read it!"
>
> **SANZANG:** "I saw you strike an old man, and then there were bones."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Close on the skull: cut deep across its brow,
in big letters, a name.

> **PRINTED SKULL** *(carved across the brow)*
> "WHITE BONE LADY"

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Bajie on the left, at the monk's elbow, whispering.
Sanzang on the right, trembling, his palms coming together.

> **BAJIE:** "He scratched that on with his fingernail, Master. He turned an old man into bones so you'd believe him."
>
> **SANZANG:** "Gold, hold fast."

**Why you turn the page:** the proof is on the road, and the monk has said the words again.

---

## PAGE 17 · *dramatic*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Wukong on the left, getting up out of the dust, holding
his head. Sanzang on the right, standing very straight, his face wet.

> **SANZANG:** "Three people came to us kindly today, and three times you raised your staff. The last time, I told you no."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Close on Sanzang, saying the hardest thing he
has ever said.

> **SANZANG:** "Go back to your mountain, Wukong. You are not my disciple anymore."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong on the left, small. Sanzang on the right, turned
half away.

> **WUKONG:** "You'd be dead three times today without me. If you're sending me away, then take the band off."
>
> **SANZANG:** "I don't know any words for that."

**Why you turn the page:** he has been sent away, and the band is staying on.

---

## PAGE 18 · *dramatic*

**Location:** White Tiger Ridge.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong on the left, kneeling to bow. Sanzang on the
right, turning his back.

> **WUKONG:** "Then let me bow to you before I go, Master. Once, for the robe you gave me."
>
> **SANZANG:** "I won't look at you."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Four Wukongs, identical, each with the gold
band, kneeling and bowing on four sides of the monk; whichever way the monk
turns, a monkey is bowing to him. Bajie gaping.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "He pulled out three hairs and blew on them. Wherever the monk turned, a monkey was bowing to him."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** One Wukong again, on the right, standing close to Wujing
on the left, speaking low.

> **WUKONG:** "Walk at his back, Wujing. And don't let the pig talk him into anything."
>
> **WUJING:** "I'll walk at his back."

**Why you turn the page:** he has said goodbye.

---

## PAGE 19 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the sky east of the ridge.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Wukong on his cloud streaking east over hazy
valleys, tears flying back from his face, the hemp robe flapping, the thin
gold band on his brow. Far behind and below, the tiny white ridge with three
small figures and a white horse on it.

> **CAPTION** *(lower right)*
> "He could cross the world in one somersault. He cried the whole way home."

**Why you turn the page:** he is going home.

## PAGE 20 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A green peak on the eastern sea, peach trees,
a waterfall roaring white down its face. Hundreds of young brown monkeys
pouring out from behind the waterfall and up the rocks toward a small golden
figure landing on the peak, beside a split stone and an old flagpole with a
rag of faded pink banner on it.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(in the open sky above the peak)*
> "Flower-Fruit Mountain stood on the eastern sea where it always had, green and loud, with the waterfall roaring down in front of the cave where he had once been king.
>
> The monkeys saw him from the trees. They screamed. They came pouring out from behind the waterfall, hundreds of them, the children and grandchildren of the monkeys he had left, who had heard stories about him every night of their lives.
>
> The Monkey King had come home."

**Why you turn the page:** the king is home, and his monkeys want to crown him.
