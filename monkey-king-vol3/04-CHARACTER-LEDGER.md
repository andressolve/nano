# Character Ledger — 7 locks of a hard ceiling of 8

Identity hierarchy, in order of priority:
**1.** face shape and apparent age · **2.** hair and facial-hair mass ·
**3.** body silhouette and habitual posture · **4.** costume value pattern ·
**5.** colour accent, prop, repeated gesture.

> Costume and colour may reinforce a character; they may never be the only
> cues. Separation is built through skull, jaw, nose, eye set, hairline,
> apparent age, body mass and posture — the traits that survive grayscale and
> thumbnail reduction. **Every page prompt names both the positive lock and the
> nearest forbidden lookalike.**

The cast divides into four silhouettes that must never blur: **the monkey**
(small, gold, never still); **the monk** (young, shaven, robed, upright — and,
for a third of the book, a tiger wearing the rags of his red kasaya); **the
company's beasts** (a big-bellied pig in black, a gaunt blue-gray ogre with a
red beard, a slim white horse who becomes a slim white dragon); and **the two
demons** (a tall lady of white bone in gray-violet burial silk, with three
human disguises; a huge indigo-faced demon in mustard-ochre armour-robes).
The danger pairs are Wujing and the Yellow Robe (both blue-skinned
monsters), the Yellow Robe and the monk (yellow robes), the White Bone Lady
and Guanyin, whom the reader remembers as "the lady in white" (a pale lady),
and the three disguises against each other and against the monk.

## 1 · SUN WUKONG, the Monkey King — apparent age: a youth, ageless

**Anchor:** Volume II, `refs/parents/10-wukong.png` (the banded pilgrim,
locked identity). Same monkey as Volume II's last page. **Do not try to make
the ears rounder** (Volume II, six candidates; method/07 watch).

**Wants (volume):** to be believed. Failing that, to be home, a king, and
never told what to do again. **Wants (secretly):** for the monk to see him.

**Moral position:** believes the monk would be dead three times without
him, and is right. Is also the one who swings first and explains later, and
learns on the third face to ask first — and that asking does not make the
answer right.

**Arc:** runs ahead → flies for peaches → sees the skull, swings without a
word → the words → the second face, swings again → the words, longer → walks
behind → the third face: asks, is refused, chooses → the bones → sent away →
the four bows → flies home crying → king again, happy, he says → looks west
every evening → sees through the pig's lie → hears the truth → goes back →
beats the demon nobody could → lets the star go → takes the tiger's shape off
the monk → is believed.

**Structural lock (inherited from Volume II verbatim):** a monkey, not a man.
Short heart-shaped face, pale hairless muzzle and cheek patches against a
golden-tawny fur; a wide mouth; large round eyes set wide, **gold with a thin
red ring of fire around each iris** (from the furnace, Volume I); low brow;
two small upright monkey ears at the sides of the head, cupped, their rims
rounded or turning through a soft peak, the top at or a little above the brow
line; never long, never narrow and sharp-tipped like an elf's or a fox's,
never floppy (Bajie's ears hang; Wukong's stand); a golden face ruff
continuous with a pronounced swept-back crown and rear ruff of separated
golden fur tufts, longest behind the ears and held as the same silhouette in
every view. The swept mass is monkey fur, not human hair, and the pale
heart-shaped face stays clear inside it. Small and wiry, a head shorter than
any human in the book, long arms, a long tail always in frame. Never stands
square: one foot up on something, crouched, mid-leap, or sitting on a thing
meant for sitting under. Habitual gesture: a flat hand shading his eyes to
look far away.

**The eyes (story device, this volume).** When he sees through a disguise,
the red rings flare bright and the reader is shown what he sees: the true
shape inside the false one, drawn as a pale ghost-image of the White Bone
Lady's skull face and bone hands showing through the disguise (or the star's
silver light inside the Yellow Robe). The flare is the only change; the eyes
never become pure red, never glow white, never shoot beams.

**Costume (one state, the whole book): Pilgrim, banded** — the monk's spare
robe, a plain undyed hemp travelling robe, knee length, belted with a cord,
sleeves rolled, worn over the tiger-skin kilt, with a mended patch at the
left shoulder; bare feet; a thin plain **gold band** around the brow, just
above the eyes, flush to the fur, no ornament. **The band is on, on every page
he appears, in every panel. Band on is blocking on every card.**
At home (pp 20–22, 36–39) he may wear a crown of flowers and leaves on top of
the band; the band shows beneath it. He never takes the robe off.
The staff (unchanged): a straight constant-diameter red-lacquered iron
cylinder, blunt gold end caps, tight flush gold banding only beside the caps;
a tiny red-and-gold needle behind his right ear when not in use, lying close
and parallel to the skull, with one capped tip allowed to show just beyond the
rear ruff as a single red-and-gold tick.
**The copies (p18):** three more Wukongs, identical to him in every lock,
band on each; made from his own hairs. Four Wukongs on one page is the one
place the same principal is drawn more than once, and only there.

**Never:** gray or silver fur, a stooped back, a stick. A human face. A
monk's shaven head, a monk's kasaya. A cap. The band never jeweled, never
wide, never a crown, never a different colour from the staff's gold, never
absent. Never tall, narrow, sharp-tipped, or elf-like ears; never smooth
human coiffure or long hanging hair. The stored needle is never doubled,
oversized, or shaped like a horn. The staff never has mid-shaft collars,
rings, a tapered point, a blade, the monk's ringed staff head, or the pig's
rake teeth. Never armour; never the tiger-skin kilt without the robe over it.

**Voice:** says exactly what he thinks to exactly the person he should not,
in short sentences, and laughs in the middle of them — except in this book,
three times, when he does not laugh. Calls the monk "Master," sometimes as a
joke, and on p13 not as a joke.

## 2 · TANG SANZANG, the monk — apparent age: about twenty-five

**Anchor:** Volume II, `refs/parents/02-sanzang.png`.

**Wants (volume):** to reach the west without anyone being killed for his
sake; and food. **Wants (secretly):** to be sure he is a good man.

**Moral position:** believes nothing may be killed, even to save him, and
that what he sees with his own eyes is true. Is right about the first and
wrong about the second, and pays for it as a tiger.

**Arc:** hungry → polite to a girl with a basket → horrified → says the words
→ forgives once → says them again, longer → forbids the third blow → says
them → sends his monkey away → walks alone into a pagoda → tells a demon he
may not hurt anyone → is made a tiger → will not eat the meat → is turned
back → kneels to the monkey → promises to believe him.

**Structural lock (inherited verbatim):** a young man, slender, upright, of
middle height (Wukong comes to his shoulder). Long oval face, smooth and
beardless; a high round fully shaven head with no topknot and no hair at all;
straight dark brows; calm dark almond eyes that widen when frightened, which
is often; a straight nose; a small closed mouth. Fair skin. Stands straight
with his weight even and his hands together at his chest; walks with a staff.
Habitual gesture: palms pressed together under his chin, praying or
panicking, or both. **Saying the words:** palms pressed hard together, eyes
shut, lips moving.

**Costume:** a saffron-yellow travelling robe to the ankles, under a red
kasaya with a gold-thread grid over the left shoulder; straw sandals; a
broad round straw hat on his back on the road. The monk's staff: a dark wooden
staff as tall as he is, topped with a pewter loop that carries six small
rings. Rides the white horse on the road.

**State (b) — the tiger (pp 29 panel 2, 30, 32, 45, 46 panel 2):** a lean, long-legged tiger,
orange with black stripes, thin from not eating; the **torn red kasaya with
its gold-thread grid still hanging over his left shoulder and knotted at his
throat**, the only scrap of the monk left on him; **calm dark almond eyes**,
the monk's, not a tiger's yellow; he sits upright with his front paws
together, the closest a tiger can come to palms pressed together. Never
snarling at anyone; never eating; never bloody; never a human face on a
tiger's body.

**Never:** a beard, any hair, a topknot. White robes, a crown, a halo, a
feminine face. Never armed. Never smiling while frightened. His staff never
reads as Wukong's staff. Never in the Yellow Robe's mustard-ochre.

**Voice:** polite, plain, and stubborn; frightened sentences that end in a
firm full stop. Never preaches; asks. Says "the words" as three words only:
"Gold, hold fast."

## 3 · ZHU BAJIE, the pig — apparent age: thirties, a big man gone soft

**Anchor:** Volume II, `refs/parents/07-bajie.png`.

**Wants (volume):** dinner, a nap, and to be the monk's favourite instead of
being bossed by a monkey. **Wants (secretly):** to be brave and honest once,
where someone sees.

**Moral position:** believes the monkey is a bully who thinks he is better
than everyone, and is partly right. Tells a lie that sends him away and is
the only one who can bring him back.

**Arc:** grumbles at being bossed → in love with the basket → "he fooled me
with a face once" → "her mother!" → "he made those bones with a trick" →
whistles on the road → fights and runs → hides in the
reeds all night → finds the horse; the horse speaks → flies east → lies →
is seen through → tells the truth → comes back with Wukong → tells the monk
the truth himself.

**Structural lock (inherited verbatim):** a pig-headed man, big and round. A
pig's head: long snout with a wide pink-gray nose disc, two small lower tusks,
enormous floppy ears that hang to his jaw, small black eyes, bristly black
hair on a pink-gray hide. A huge round belly, short thick legs, broad sloping
shoulders; taller than the monk by a head and twice as wide. Slouches with
his belly forward. Habitual gesture: patting his belly, or fanning himself
with one ear.

**Costume:** a black cotton tunic open over the belly, a blue sash, black
trousers. His weapon: a nine-toothed iron rake on a long wooden shaft. The
luggage on a carrying pole, one end of it, the other end Wujing's.
**Flying (p35, p40):** on a small, lumpy, gray-white cloud that sags under
him, holding his rake across his knees.

**Never:** fur, a tail in frame, a muzzle like Wukong's, gold anything. A
blue-gray face, a red beard, skulls, a crescent spade (Wujing). Never thin,
never upright.

**Voice:** loud, whining, flattering, and funny; talks about food at the
worst moments. In Movement V, for one page, he stops being funny.

## 4 · SHA WUJING, the river monster — apparent age: forties, gaunt

**Anchor:** Volume II, `refs/parents/08-wujing.png`.

**Wants (volume):** to keep the monk safe, as he promised. **Wants
(secretly):** for someone to say his failure was not his fault.

**Moral position:** believes Wukong was right about the demon, and says
nothing, because he was a monster too and the monk forgave him. Fails to keep
the monk and says so first.

**Arc:** walks at the monk's back → watches → is told "walk at his back" →
goes to find the pig → fights and is beaten → tied → "I didn't keep him" →
cut free by Wukong.

**Structural lock (inherited verbatim):** a tall gaunt ogre, bony and
long-limbed, a head taller than the pig and half as wide. A long
hollow-cheeked face, slate blue-gray skin, deep-set round red-brown eyes under
a heavy brow ridge, a broad flat nose; a wild bristling red beard and wild red
hair; a wide grim mouth. Stands hunched forward from the shoulders, long arms.
Habitual gesture: a hand closed at his chest where the skulls used to hang, as
if counting.

**Costume (changed by Volume II's ending):** a dark sand-brown sleeveless
tunic, a sash of river reeds, bare arms and feet. **No skull necklace** — the
nine skulls became the raft on Volume II p47 and are gone. His weapon: a long
staff with a flat crescent-moon blade at one end and a small spade at the
other. One end of the luggage pole.

**Never:** skulls (they are gone; a skull on Wujing is blocking). A pig's
head, ears, a belly, a rake, black clothes (Bajie). Never the Yellow Robe's
bulk, black hair, mustard-ochre robe or scimitar. Never a monk's shaven head
or robes.

**Voice:** few words, low and flat, no jokes. Says the worst thing about
himself first.

## 5 · THE THIRD PRINCE, the white horse / the white dragon — young

**Anchor:** Volume II, `refs/parents/06-white-horse.png` (horse) and
`refs/parents/05-dragon.png` (dragon).

**Wants (volume):** to carry the monk west and be useful. **Wants
(secretly):** to be heard, once.

**Moral position:** believes a horse should not talk. Breaks that once,
because nobody else will say it.

**Arc:** carries the monk → watches → tethered in the pines → becomes the
dragon → fights the Yellow Robe → is cut → lies in the stream → speaks:
"go and get our big brother" → is asked to talk again, and does not.

**Structural lock — horse form (inherited):** a slim white horse with a
pale-blue mane and tail, sea-green eyes, carrying the monk's saddle (dark red
leather, brass fittings). Unmistakably the dragon: the eyes and the blue mane
are the carriers.
**Structural lock — dragon form (inherited, p31–32 only):** a slim
serpentine dragon, white as river foam, long and lithe; a compact young
lizard head *(head-structure sentence superseded by the relock ruling below: the locked Volume II head, a narrow scaled lizard head with a moderate snout, governs)* with a blunt wedge silhouette, almost no projecting muzzle, and
one broad shield-shaped nasal scale carrying a small side slit nostril; no
beard and no fins at the jaw; small pale-gold horns, two short unbranched
spikes, never antlers; large round sea-green eyes; a pale-blue mane along the
spine; four small clawed legs. The shield nose and blunt wedge head, not
colour or horns alone, separate the dragon face from the horse.

**Relock ruling (2026-10-16, after six candidates):** the dragon locks as Volume II's approved `05-dragon` sheet, unchanged (it passed Volume II's sheet critic and whole-cast gate as this same character). No wound view is required on the sheet: the cut on the right foreleg (pp 31–32) is drawn from words, and `06-white-horse` carries the wound lock. Shared sea-green eyes, pale-blue mane and white value with the horse are the intended cross-form identity, not a collision; the dragon is separated from the horse by the serpent body, four small clawed legs, the two short horns and no tack.

**Wound continuity — both forms:** the injury is always on the anatomical
right foreleg. The horse sheet locks the injured state through a right-flank
view, lowered head and only the right hoof lifted; it carries no wound graphic.
On the fight and stream pages the fresh clean dark gash is drawn from words in
that location. On the last page it is covered by one plain pale bandage. The
dragon's injured foreleg is likewise drawn from words.

**Costume / tack:** the horse carries the monk's dark-red leather saddle with
brass fittings. On the last page only, one plain pale bandage wraps the injured
right foreleg. The dragon wears nothing and carries no tack.

**Never** *(rostrum/muzzle items superseded by the relock ruling; judge the dragon by serpent body, four clawed legs, two horns, no tack)*: a long horse-like rostrum, rounded fleshy muzzle, terminal horse
nostril, antlers, whiskers, a beard of fins, blue-green scales, gold eyes,
robes, a crown, a human body. Never brown, never dappled. Never speaks except
on pp 32–33. Before treatment: no bandage, wrap, ring, red, blood, open flesh,
or dangling material on the wounded leg. On the last page: one pale bandage
only, with no visible second wound mark. When the horse speaks, his mouth is
closed or barely open; the balloon tail goes to his head.

**Voice:** young, quiet, plain. Speaks only on pp 32–33, a handful of sentences, and never again.

## 6 · THE WHITE BONE LADY — apparent age: ancient

**Anchor:** new. One character, four sheets: the true shape and three
disguises. **The reader is shown the true shape before every disguise and
under it.**

**Wants (volume):** one bite of the monk, and never to die. **Wants
(secretly):** to be something other than bones.

**Moral position:** believes everything is food. Is right that the monk
cannot see her.

**Arc:** watches from the crag → the girl → slips out of the skin → the old
woman → slips out again → the old man → too slow → bones.

**Structural lock — true shape:** a tall, gaunt lady made of bone. Her face
is a smooth white skull with a lady's high cheekbones and long jaw, deep dark
eye-hollows with two pinpoints of cold green light in them, and no lips; long
straight black hair hanging loose to her waist from the white skull; a long
neck. Long white bone hands with fingers like knitting needles. Wrapped in
tattered burial silks of **ash-gray and faded violet**, trailing like smoke at
the hem, so she seems to float a little above the rock. Habitual gesture:
one bone finger drawn slowly across her teeth.
**The mist (her escape):** a thin cold white mist with her skull face just
visible in it, streaming away from a fallen disguise. Seen only by Wukong and
the reader.

**Disguise 1 — the girl (pp 4–6):** a pretty young village woman, about
eighteen, round soft face, rosy cheeks, black hair in a single long plait over
one shoulder with a red string at the end, a **peach-pink cotton jacket**
and dark blue trousers, cloth shoes; carries a **round lidded bamboo basket**
on her arm (inside: white rice and noodles; later, maggots and green frogs).
Smiles a great deal. **Her tell, for the reader:** the pinpoints of green in
her eyes.
**Disguise 2 — the old woman (pp 9–10):** a small bent old woman of about
eighty, white hair in a tight bun with a wooden pin, a short broad deeply
wrinkled kind face with wide cheeks, a small low rounded nose, a blunt rounded
chin, narrow dark eyes with the same small green pinpoints, and a small
toothless mouth. She wears a **dark brown padded jacket** to the hips, darker
trousers and cloth shoes, and leans on a shoulder-high **bamboo cane**, weeping
and wiping her cheek with her bare fingers. **Wukong's sight-reveal is a
graphic shadow, not anatomical transparency:** the old woman remains wholly
living and opaque, while one flat pale-gray head-and-shoulders shadow sits
immediately behind her; its head alone has the true shape's long-jawed skull
silhouette. The shadow has no green lights, skeletal body or bone hands.
**Disguise 3 — the old man (pp 13–15):** a thin old man of about eighty,
a long wispy white beard, a bald brown head with a white fringe, a **gray
lay-pilgrim's gown**, a string of brown wooden **prayer beads** in his hands,
murmuring prayers. Same green pinpoints.
**The fallen disguises (pp 6, 10):** each disguise left face-down and hollow,
like the dry skin a cicada leaves, flattened and wrong; never a wound, never
blood, never a face shown.
**The bones (pp 15–17):** a neat heap of clean white bones on the road, the
skull on top, and cut deep into the skull's brow, large enough to read in a
close panel, her name: **WHITE BONE LADY**.

**Relock ruling (2026-10-16, after six candidates):** the lock for `13-bone-old-woman` is views (a)–(c), the living disguise. View (d), the reveal, is out of scope and is never a reference for any page: pages draw Wukong's view of the skull from `11-bone-lady` and words. A fifth figure or a reveal artifact confined to view (d) is nonblocking on this sheet.

**Never:** white robes, a white hood-veil, a willow sprig, a vase, a cloud,
gold, a red dot between the brows (Guanyin — "the lady in white"). Never
blood, guts or rotting flesh. Never the monk's saffron or red. The disguises
never wear gold, saffron, red kasaya, or the gold band; the girl's jacket is
peach-pink, never the monk's saffron. The old woman's sight-reveal never puts a
skull inside or over her living face, never shows a skeletal body or bone
hands, and never adds a separate Bone Lady figure or extra green lights; she
never carries a handkerchief.

**Voice:** in her true shape, slow and hungry, purring. In disguise, sweet,
plain, and a little too eager.

## 7 · THE YELLOW ROBE — apparent age: a huge man in his prime

**Anchor:** new. One sheet; the star is an effect, not a state.

**Wants (volume):** to be amused, and to eat the monk once the monk breaks
his own rule. **Wants (secretly):** never to be found and sent home.

**Moral position:** believes gentleness is a joke and everyone is a beast
underneath. Is proved wrong by a tiger.

**Arc:** asleep in his pagoda → catches the monk → laughs → beats the pig and
the ogre → makes the monk a tiger → wounds the dragon → is beaten by the
monkey → is seen → flies home to the sky as a star.

**Structural lock:** a huge demon, half again as tall as the monk and broad as
a door, heavy-shouldered and thick-necked. A wide square **deep indigo-blue**
face, heavy jaw, two short white tusks rising from the lower lip, a broad
flat nose, thick black brows; **pale silver eyes** with no iris colour, like
starlight; long **black** hair pulled up into a high topknot bound with a
black cord, and a short square black beard. Big hands. Stands with his feet
planted wide, chin up. Habitual gesture: tossing his curved scimitar from hand
to hand.

**Costume:** a long robe of **mustard-ochre** over black lamellar armour, the
robe's edges stitched with one repeated pattern of small silver stars; their
point count is not an identity carrier and never blocks. A wide black belt;
black boots. His weapon: a broad curved scimitar with a silver blade and a
black or dark-iron hilt; a small dark-bronze guard is allowed, but never a
bright-gold fitting.
**The spell (p28):** he fills his mouth from a cup, says a word, and sprays a
fine silver mist of water over the monk.
**The star (pp 43–44):** when Wukong names him, silver light shines through
the cracks of his face and robe; he rises into the night sky as a streak of
silver and becomes one bright star among the others.

**Never:** red hair or a red beard, slate blue-gray skin, gauntness, a
crescent spade, a sand-brown tunic (Wujing). Never saffron-yellow or a red
kasaya, never a shaven head (the monk). Never gold eyes or bright-gold costume
ornaments (Wukong). Scimitar fittings are black, dark iron, or at most a small
dark-bronze guard, never bright gold. Never white robes.

**Voice:** big, lazy, delighted, cruel; laughs at his own jokes.

## Reserved design space

No background or unnamed figure may inherit a complete identity stack:

- golden fur + gold band + hemp robe → **Wukong** (and his three copies, p18 only). No other monkey wears anything.
- shaven head + saffron robe + red kasaya + ringed staff → **Sanzang**. No other monk appears.
- tiger + torn red kasaya with gold grid + dark almond eyes → **Sanzang as the tiger**. No other tiger appears.
- pig head + belly + black tunic + rake → **Bajie**
- blue-gray skin + red beard + crescent spade, no skulls → **Wujing**
- white horse + pale-blue mane and tail + sea-green eyes + dark-red brass-fitted saddle → **the Third Prince**; white serpent + pale-blue mane + sea-green eyes → **the Third Prince as the dragon**
- white skull face + long black hair + ash-gray/violet burial silk → **the White Bone Lady**
- peach-pink jacket + single plait with red string + lidded bamboo basket → **the girl**
- white bun + brown padded jacket + bamboo cane → **the old woman**
- white beard + gray pilgrim gown + brown prayer beads → **the old man**
- indigo face + tusks + black topknot + mustard-ochre robe with silver stars + scimitar → **the Yellow Robe**
