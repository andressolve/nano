# Ratio: The Seeker — Settings (Vol 1)

All locations Vol 1 visits, in page order. Croton's **Brotherhood hall** and **beach** are Vol 2. The bean field, the fire, and Hippasus's room are Vol 2.

---

## Samos (pages 1–3, and 11–13)

Ionian Greek island off the Anatolian coast. Mountainous, cypress and olive, white-walled villages along the coast. Home of the tyrant **Polycrates** (~538 BC and after — dramatic license; we place him in the distance during Pythagoras's youth).

### The coastal village (page 1, establishing)
A small Ionian village on the Samos coast. White-walled houses with terracotta or flat roofs, cypress trees, a fishing boat being hauled up a beach. A low cliff path overlooks the bay. The sea is pale blue-green. Dawn or early morning.

**Palette:** dawn gold on water, cool slate-blue in shadow, cream buildings, cypress dark green.

### The forge (page 2)
An open-fronted village blacksmith's workshop in the same Ionian village. Thatched roof supported by rough wood posts. Glowing coals in a brick hearth at the center. Hammers of different sizes hung on pegs on the back wall — small, medium, large, and extra-large. An anvil. A water barrel. Dust, soot, heat haze. The forge opens onto the street, so outside light contrasts with the amber interior. A 10-year-old Pythagoras stands at the threshold in light from outside, listening.

**Palette:** warm amber coals, red-orange iron being struck, sooty dark walls, cream street-light from outside.

### Pherecydes's terrace (page 3)
A stone terrace on a hillside on Samos. Olive trees dapple the light. A low stone bench. The sea shimmers in the distance. In the further distance, on a high cliff above the harbor, the flat-roofed palace of **Polycrates** is visible with guards on its walls. Pherecydes sits on the bench with a papyrus scroll. Young teen Pythagoras sits cross-legged at his feet.

**Palette:** dappled olive-green, pale blue sea, distant white palace, warm cream Pherecydes.

### The harbor at departure (page 4)
The main harbor of Samos town. Stone jetty, a Greek merchant ship (trireme or larger sailing vessel) tied up, crew loading amphorae. Young-man Pythagoras, now age 20, stands at the edge of the jetty with a travel staff and a slim bundle. He looks back once up the hillside. The ship will take him south to Egypt.

**Palette:** bright Aegean blue, pale cream stone, cypress on hillsides behind, sail canvas tan.

### The return harbor (page 11)
Same harbor, two decades later. The stone jetty is unchanged. A different ship — this one arriving. Mature Pythagoras, now ~40, bearded with grey at the temples, steps off. He looks older than when he left. Polycrates's palace still on the cliff (slightly different light — older, or repaired, so the reader registers time passing even though the place is the same).

**Palette:** same as page 4, slightly more gold (returning at a different season).

### The cave-school on the hillside (page 12)
A modest limestone cave high on a Samos hillside. The mouth of the cave frames a view of the Aegean below. Inside, a stone bench. A few students (3–4 in number) sit on the ground. Mature Pythagoras stands in profile, silhouetted against the light from the cave mouth. The cave is plainly too small for the scale of what he wants to build.

**Palette:** cool limestone grey interior, warm cream sea-light from the cave mouth, student tunics pale.

### The coast again, deciding (page 13)
A clifftop path on Samos at dusk. Mature Pythagoras stands alone, looking west across the Aegean. The sunset is gold-orange. A wooden ship's mast is distantly visible in the harbor below. He has decided.

**Palette:** gold-orange sunset, deep cobalt sea, silhouetted cypress and Pythagoras.

## Egypt — Thebes or Memphis (pages 5–7)

Pythagoras studies with Egyptian priests in one of the great temple cities. We keep it generic between Memphis and Thebes — name it just "Egypt" in captions.

### Arrival (page 5)
The approach to a great temple complex. Massive sandstone pylon gates. Hieroglyphic-painted exterior walls in ochre, red, and deep lapis. A broad processional way lined with stone ram- or sphinx-statues leading up to the gates. Young-adult Pythagoras, dusty from travel, walking up the processional way with his staff. Hot high sun.

**Palette:** hot ochre sand, warm red-brown sandstone, deep lapis sky, cream linen-clad figures, bright gold highlights on the temple walls.

### The temple courtyard (page 6)
A sun-drenched sandstone courtyard inside the temple precinct. Thick columns with painted lotus capitals (red, ochre, blue). A pyramid rises in the far distance beyond the temple walls. On the sand of the courtyard, a **rope laid in a 3-4-5 triangle** — 12 knots at equal intervals — with the Egyptian priest crouched beside it demonstrating. Pythagoras crouches opposite, watching.

**Palette:** hot ochre, warm red-brown stone, cream kilts, pops of lapis and gold from the priest's jewelry.

### Egypt falls (page 7)
Same temple precinct, but weeks or months later. Smoke rising over sandstone walls. Persian soldiers (distant, silhouetted in the background, bronze armor, spears) have invaded. Foreground: Pythagoras, now with his wrists bound with rough rope, being led away with other captives. The priest from page 6 is visible in the far background, kneeling or fallen — a small detail, not melodramatic.

**Palette:** ochre with a smoky grey-orange overlay; spears as dark verticals; Persian armor warm-dull bronze.

**Note:** Show the invasion through its CONSEQUENCES, not violence on panel. Pythagoras being marched away is the dominant image; the soldiers and the fallen priest are context.

## Babylon (pages 8–10)

The great Mesopotamian city after Persia's conquest of Babylonia and Egypt. Ziggurats, flat-roofed palaces, astronomical towers.

### The rooftop under stars (page 8)
A broad flat rooftop of a Babylonian palace or temple. Low parapet. The **ziggurat of Marduk** rises in silhouette against a deep cobalt night sky full of stars. A bronze brazier in one corner gives warm amber light to the two figures on the rooftop. The Babylonian Magus stands at the parapet with a clay tablet covered in cuneiform numbers. Young-adult Pythagoras, bearded now, stands with him, looking up.

**Palette:** deep cobalt and indigo sky, star-white points, brick-red ziggurat silhouette, warm amber on Pythagoras and the Magus.

### The scholar's room (page 9)
An interior scholar's room in Babylon. A low wooden table with several clay tablets. A bronze star-chart disc. Oil lamps. Scrolls and writing instruments. Young-adult Pythagoras seated at the table, a tablet in his hand, studying. The Magus stands behind him, pointing at a line of cuneiform.

**Palette:** warm amber lamp-light, dark brick walls, clay-grey tablets, bronze accents.

### Years passing (page 10)
A montage page — same room, or similar room, but Pythagoras has clearly aged. Subtle greying in his beard, more weathered face. Scrolls and tablets stacked higher. He bends over his work. Alternatively, one panel interior, one panel exterior (a Babylonian market street showing the years passing through the sight of a now-older Pythagoras walking through it).

**Palette:** warm amber interior, cobalt/ochre exterior.

## The crossing west (pages 14–15)

Pythagoras's second voyage, from Samos to Magna Graecia (southern Italy), age ~40.

### The ship at sea (page 14)
A Greek merchant ship on the open Mediterranean at dusk. Single square sail, a dozen oarsmen at the benches, crew on deck. Mature Pythagoras stands at the prow, wind in his beard, looking west. A pale gold-pink sunset. The sea is deep cobalt below, pink-gold above.

**Palette:** cobalt sea, pink-gold sky, tan sail, cream Pythagoras.

### Italian coast (page 15)
First sight of Italy. The coastline of southern Italy rising on the horizon — low green hills, a distant hint of a white coastal city. Mature Pythagoras leans on the ship's railing. Dawn or morning — this is a NEW day, a new continent.

**Palette:** pale dawn pink and gold, cobalt sea, green-dusted hills, white coastal city in distance.

## Croton (pages 16–17)

A wealthy Greek colony on the Gulf of Taranto in Calabria (Magna Graecia). Famous for athletes (Milo of Croton) and doctors. White-walled coastal city, Ionic temples, olive groves, blue-green Ionian Sea. Golden hour.

### Establishing Croton (page 16)
Wide shot of the city from the approach road. White-walled houses, a few Ionic temples, olive groves behind, the Ionian Sea in front. Mature Pythagoras, travel-worn, walks into the city. Crotonian citizens go about their business — market stalls, a runner in training (Croton was famous for its athletes), an old philosopher reading on a bench. Warm late-afternoon light.

**Palette:** cream and ivory stone, ochre dust, olive-green, Ionian Sea blue, warm gold sunlight.

### The assembly step (page 17)
A semi-circular outdoor stone amphitheater or a public portico with a raised speaking step. Mature Pythagoras, now cleaned up and in a fresh ivory himation, stands one step above a gathered crowd of Crotonian citizens. The citizens are attentive. Some young, some old, several women (Croton was known to be open-minded about women in public life). Golden-hour light through the columns behind him.

This is the final page of Vol 1. The city falls silent. He is about to speak. The caption: *"He had crossed three lands to find his question. Now he would build a place to answer it."*

**Palette:** ivory stone, cream fabrics, ochre dust, golden-hour sunlight, warm shadows. This is the warmest single image in Vol 1.

## Consistency rules across Vol 1

- **Time of day by section:** Samos is morning and noon; Egypt is hot mid-day; Babylon is night; the return is mid-day; the cave is afternoon; the crossing is dusk-into-dawn; Croton is golden hour.
- **No palm trees on Samos.** Samos is cypress and olive.
- **No Romans.** Pre-Roman period. No Roman architecture, no togas.
- **No pointed beards.** Classical Greek beards are full and rounded or trimmed square.
- **Greek clothing is chiton + himation.** Reference classical Greek statuary for drape.
- **Writing surfaces are context-specific:** wax tablet + stylus for Pythagoras's notes (page 2), papyrus scrolls for Egyptian temple scenes (page 5), knotted rope for the 3-4-5 demonstration (page 6), clay tablets with cuneiform in Babylon (pages 8–10).
- **No anachronistic mathematical notation in this volume.** The only numerals are "12 · 9 · 8 · 6" (hammer weights, page 2) and "3", "4", "5" labels on the Egyptian rope (page 6). Save "a² + b² = c²" for Vol 2.
