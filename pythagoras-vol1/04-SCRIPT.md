## PAGE 02 — THE HAMMERS

**Movement:** A
**Setting:** The village blacksmith's forge, late morning.

**Composition:** 3 panels.

- Panel 1 (wide, top half): The forge from Pythagoras's POV at the threshold. The blacksmith strikes iron with a large hammer. On the back peg-wall, hammers of different sizes. Hot coals. Pythagoras at the threshold, hand cupped near his ear, listening.

- Panel 2 (small, lower left): Close-up of Pythagoras's face, eyes wide, listening. A small chalk-style label floats in the panel: **HARMONY**.

- Panel 3 (lower right): Pythagoras crouched beside three hammers laid on the ground, weighing them with a simple wooden-arm balance. A wax tablet beside him shows: **12 · 9 · 8 · 6**. The blacksmith watches, amused but patient.

**Captions:**
- Panel 1: *He passes the blacksmith's forge every day. Today, for the first time, he hears it.*
- Panel 2: *Different hammers. Different weights. Different notes. Together — a chord.*
- Panel 3: *He weighs them. The hammers that sound well together have weights in simple ratios. Twelve. Nine. Eight. Six.*

**Dialogue:**
- Pythagoras (small bubble, panel 3, to himself): "The sound is the number."

**Note:** Render "12 · 9 · 8 · 6" exactly on the wax tablet. These are real musical ratios (12:6 = octave, 12:8 = fifth, 12:9 = fourth). The legend may not be literally true, but it captures Pythagoras's foundational insight — harmony is ratio.

**Ref:** `pythagoras-boy.png`.

## PAGE 03 — PHERECYDES AND THE TYRANT

**Movement:** A

- Panel 1 (second): *But Samos has grown small. The tyrant Polycrates watches every free man. A seeker cannot breathe here.*
- Panel 2: *At twenty, he prepares to leave. Egypt first.*

**Dialogue:**
- Pherecydes (panel 1): "The Egyptians knew shapes before we did. Go and learn from them."

## PAGE 06 — THE ROPE-STRETCHERS

**Movement:** B
**Setting:** A sunlit sandstone temple courtyard, mid-afternoon.

**Composition:** 3 panels.

- Panel 1 (top, wide): The **Egyptian priest / rope-stretcher** (shaved head, white linen kilt, lapis-and-gold collar) crouched in the sand beside a knotted rope. Pythagoras crouches opposite, watching. The rope is laid out in a triangle with 3 knots along one side, 4 along another, 5 along the hypotenuse. Painted temple columns in the background. A pyramid visible far behind.

- Panel 2 (lower left, close-up from above): Bird's-eye view of the rope triangle. 12 knots at regular intervals. Chalk-style labels at each side: **3**, **4**, **5**. A small right-angle indicator (a square) at the corner where the 3-side meets the 4-side.

- Panel 3 (lower right): Pythagoras looking at the priest, question in his eyes.

**Captions:**
- Panel 1: *In Egypt, the priests know geometry the way farmers know the flood. They use it to build temples.*
- Panel 1 (second): *They call themselves harpedonaptai — the rope-stretchers. With twelve knots and a patient hand, they make a perfect right angle.*
- Panel 2: *Three and four and five. The corner is always square.*

**Dialogue:**
- Priest (panel 1): "Three. Four. Five. A right corner every time. This is how we set our temples straight."
- Pythagoras (panel 3): "But why does it work?"
- Priest (off-panel, last caption of panel 3): "We are builders. You are the question-asker."

**Note:** THIS IS THE SEED PAGE. The 3-4-5 triangle handed to Pythagoras without explanation is the question he will spend twenty years carrying. Volume 2 answers it.

Render "3", "4", "5" as small chalk-style numerals on the rope — not as floating equations, but as tick-labels on the sides. No "a² + b² = c²" — that is Vol 2.

**Ref:** `pythagoras-young-man.png` (+ `egyptian-priest.png` if made; otherwise prompt in-page). `compose_images` if both refs available, else `edit_image`.

## PAGE 07 — EGYPT FALLS

**Movement:** B
**Setting:** The temple precinct, days or weeks later, late afternoon.

**Composition:** 2 panels.

- Panel 1 (top, wide): Smoke rising over the sandstone walls. Persian soldiers (distant silhouettes, bronze armor, spears) have entered the temple precinct. The Egyptian priest from page 6 kneels near a column in the far middle-ground, head bowed — NOT bloody, NOT on the ground, just kneeling as a captive too. Do not render violence.

- Panel 2 (bottom, small): Close on Pythagoras's face as he is led away. He looks back once. The priest lifts his head and meets his eyes — a last look across the courtyard.

**Captions:**
- Panel 1: *Then Egypt fell.*

## PAGE 09 — THE NUMBERS OF THE SKY

**Movement:** B
**Setting:** A scholar's room in Babylon, interior, evening.

**Composition:** 3 panels.

- Panel 1 (top, wide): Interior of a scholar's chamber. A low wooden table covered with **clay tablets** bearing cuneiform script and number-tables. A bronze star-chart disc. Oil lamps. Pythagoras seated at the table examining a tablet; the Magus stands behind him, pointing at a row of cuneiform.

- Panel 2 (lower left): Extreme close-up of a clay tablet. Rows of cuneiform wedges representing numbers. In the corner of the tablet, small Greek-style annotations in Pythagoras's hand (wax-scratch marks).

- Panel 3 (lower right): Pythagoras's face in lamp-light, absorbed, eyes moving across the tablet. He has aged slightly — a few threads of grey in his beard.

**Captions:**
- Panel 1: *They showed him how they recorded the sky. Numbers in rows. Ratios of time. The return of the moon. The appearance of Venus.*
- Panel 2: *They had been doing this for a thousand years before Greece remembered it had a name.*
- Panel 3: *He wrote it all down.*

**Dialogue:**
- Magus (panel 1, quietly): "The sky repeats. Learn its table, and you can read its next page."

## PAGE 10 — YEARS PASS

**Movement:** B
**Setting:** Babylon, across time. This is a time-passage page.

**Composition:** 3 panels arranged as a visual montage.

- Panel 1 (top): Same scholar's room. Tablets stacked higher. Pythagoras at the table, now late 30s, beard fuller, hair beginning to grey at the temples. Scrolls on the wall now too.

- Panel 2 (middle, wide): A Babylonian street at dusk. Pythagoras walks alone through a crowd of Babylonians. He fits now — the foreigner has become the resident scholar. He carries a scroll under his arm.

- Panel 3 (bottom): Same rooftop as page 8. Pythagoras alone this time under the stars. The Magus no longer there. He is older. He looks up at the same sky.

**Captions:**
- Panel 1: *Years passed.*
- Panel 2: *The exile became a life.*
- Panel 3: *Then one night, under a sky he had almost memorized, he remembered there was an island called Samos. And he went home.*

**Dialogue:** None.

**Ref:** `pythagoras-young-man.png` with age modifier "slightly older, more grey in beard". Alternatively begin using `pythagoras-mature.png` from panel 3 onward to bridge the age.

## PAGE 17 — THE FIRST WORD

**Movement:** C (closing)
**Setting:** A Croton assembly space — a semi-circular stone step, or a colonnaded portico, golden hour.

**Composition:** 2 panels.

- Panel 1 (top, wide): Mature Pythagoras, in a fresh ivory himation, stands one step above a gathered crowd of Crotonian citizens. The crowd is attentive — men and women, young and old, a few obviously educated, a few obviously skeptical. Golden light through columns. He has not yet spoken.

## QUIZ (5 questions, shown at the end of the reader)

## Historical notes and acknowledged compressions

- Pythagoras's exact travels (Egypt, Babylon) are legendary rather than firmly historical. We present them as tradition records them.
- The hammer-ratios anecdote is almost certainly legend. Kept for thematic weight.
- Dates are compressed for narrative clarity. The Persian conquest of Egypt was 525 BC; Pythagoras would have been older than we imply. This is OK — the book is a biography, not a chronicle.
- Polycrates's rule on Samos began ~538 BC, which technically overlaps with Pythagoras's young adulthood more than his boyhood. Graphic-novel compression.
