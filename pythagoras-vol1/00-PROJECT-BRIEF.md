# Ratio: The Seeker — Project Brief (Vol 1 of 2)

## Series
**Ratio: The Life of Pythagoras** — two-volume biographical graphic novel. This is **Volume 1: The Seeker**. Volume 2 is **The Brotherhood** (separate folder: `pythagoras-vol2/`).

## Format
Biographical graphic novel. Same format as *Cogito* (Descartes) and *Relativity* (Einstein). Dark-themed HTML reader. ~18 pages (1 cover + 17 story). 5-question quiz at the end.

## Audience
Francisco (9) and Sebastian (7). Sebastian preferred Descartes over Einstein because the Cartesian plane was something he could DO on paper AND he was studying it in the Lyceum. **Pythagoras hits the same two marks even harder: the Pythagorean Theorem is a dedicated Lyceum lesson, and a² + b² = c² is the most DRAWABLE theorem in math.** This volume plants the seed. Volume 2 delivers the theorem itself.

## The emotional arc of Volume 1
A boy hears the world speaking in numbers. He leaves home to find out how deep that goes. He spends twenty years in foreign lands gathering pieces of an answer that none of his teachers can complete. He comes home to an island that cannot hold him. He leaves again. The book ends with him standing before an unfamiliar city, preparing to speak — about to build the place where he will finally answer his own question.

**The one emotional beat:** *seeking*. The volume's satisfaction is the arrival — finding the place where the work can begin. The reader does not yet know what he will discover there. They feel only that the long journey has found its landing.

## The one central idea (Vol 1)
**"The world is made of ratios — and the question is how deep the pattern goes."** This is the hypothesis the young Pythagoras carries with him from the forge at age 10 to the steps of the Croton assembly at ~40. The volume is the hypothesis being tested against three civilizations. The Egyptian rope-stretchers hand him a working truth (3-4-5 triangles make right angles) but cannot explain WHY. Volume 1 ends with the question unanswered. Volume 2 answers it.

## Why split here?
The natural pivot of Pythagoras's life is his arrival at Croton. Before Croton, he is a seeker receiving; after Croton, he is a teacher giving. The geography, the register, and the energy all change at that moment. Volume 1 ends as he steps to the assembly. Volume 2 opens the next morning as the Brotherhood begins to form.

## Length rationale
~18 pages lets each of his three foreign teachers (Pherecydes on Samos, the Egyptian rope-stretchers, the Babylonian Magi) have real weight rather than being a single panel each. The return to Samos, the cave that is too small, and the crossing to Italy each earn their own page. The arrival at Croton gets a proper three-page landing. None of this was possible in the original 22-page single-volume plan.

## Tone
Serious, contemplative. Same register as Descartes and Einstein. NOT a children's book. Painterly realism. The volume is quiet — more about absorption than action. The kid's "agency moment" in this volume is small: a boy weighs hammers, records ratios, and discovers that the world has a grammar. The big agency moment (a² + b² = c²) is saved for Vol 2.

## Volume 1 structure (3 movements)

**Movement A — Samos (boyhood to departure, pages 1–4):** The forge. The mentor Pherecydes. Polycrates's tyranny. Departure at 20.

**Movement B — Foreign Lands (travels, pages 5–11):** Egypt and the rope-stretchers. The fall of Egypt to Persia. Babylon and the Magi. Cuneiform tablets, astronomy, the number-sky. Years pass.

**Movement C — Return and Re-Departure (pages 12–17):** Ship returning to Samos. The cave-school too small. The decision to leave again. The crossing to Italy. First sight of Croton. The assembly step. The first word.

## Volume 1 does NOT include
- The founding of the Brotherhood
- The Pythagorean Theorem
- Theano, Hippasus, or Cylon
- √2 or the irrationality crisis
- The fire and the bean field
- The theorem's afterlife

All of the above are in **Volume 2: The Brotherhood**.

## What Volume 1 plants that Volume 2 pays off
- **The hammers (page 2)** → the monochord and music-of-the-spheres teaching (Vol 2)
- **The Egyptian 3-4-5 triangle (page 6)** → the theorem's discovery (Vol 2)
- **"Why?" that the priest can't answer (page 6)** → the moment on the beach where Pythagoras answers his own 20-year-old question (Vol 2)
- **The tetractys-adjacent sky-numbers of Babylon (page 9)** → the Brotherhood's tetractys teaching (Vol 2)
- **The cave that is too small (page 12)** → the Brotherhood hall (Vol 2)
