# Source Map and Cut List

Source: Alexandre Dumas, *Le Comte de Monte-Cristo* (1844–46) — the
Villefort thread, scattered across roughly chapters 40–48, 51–52, 57–63,
72–80, 93–104, 108–112, pulled out of the Paris braid and run as one arc.

**A source map with an empty cut list is not finished.** This one is mostly
cut list.

## The cut list — named, unapologetic, famous material included

### Whole sequences cut

- **The Cavalcanti fraud** — the false father, the Major, the forged
  letters, the stipend, the Lucca nobility. Benedetto is "Prince Cavalcanti"
  for one dinner and one handshake; that is all the disguise has to do.
- **Benedetto and Eugénie Danglars** — the engagement, the contract
  signing, the arrest at the Danglars house, Eugénie's flight with Louise
  d'Armilly. → Volume IV, if anywhere.
- **Caderousse's Paris career** — the blackmail, the burglary of the Count's
  house, his murder by Benedetto, the Count's deathbed reckoning with him.
- **Bertuccio's confession** — the Corsican vendetta, his brother, the
  stabbing of Villefort in the garden, Assunta, the inn at the Pont du Gard.
  One line of the Count's covers it.
- **Franz d'Épinay and the marriage** — the arranged match, Noirtier's
  revelation that he killed Franz's father, the broken contract.
- **Noirtier's will** and the notary scene with the dictionary.
- **The Saint-Mérans** as two people with scenes; the Marquis's death on the
  road.
- **Barrois' death.**
- **Madame Danglars as Benedetto's mother** and the Villefort–Danglars
  affair. Benedetto's mother is "a woman who was not his wife", unnamed.
- **The telegraph fraud, the Spanish bonds, Debray** → Volume IV.
- **Haydée's love declared, Mercédès, Albert** → Volume IV.
- **The island, "wait and hope"**, the Count's last letter → Volume IV.

### Characters cut

Bertuccio · Caderousse · Ali · Baptistin · Doctor d'Avrigny · Barrois · the
Marquis de Saint-Méran · Franz d'Épinay · Major Cavalcanti · Eugénie
Danglars · Louise d'Armilly · Madame Danglars · Danglars (named once, never
drawn) · Lucien Debray · Beauchamp · Albert · Mercédès · Julie and Emmanuel
Herbault (mentioned, never drawn) · the Abbé Busoni (named once, as a past
disguise) · Lord Wilmore · the President of the Assizes (Villefort conducts
the questioning himself).

### Characters merged

- **Bertuccio + the Busoni confession + Caderousse → the Count's own
  knowledge.** One face, one line.
- **D'Avrigny → Villefort.** The prosecutor diagnoses his own house.
- **The Saint-Mérans + the will + the notary → the grandmother, one page,
  no lines.**
- **The President of the Assizes → Villefort.** He asks the prisoner his
  father's name with his own mouth.

### Why the biggest cut pays for the volume

Cutting the Cavalcanti fraud and the Danglars engagement deletes roughly
half of the Paris material, the largest unnamed cast in the novel, and every
page that would have been a contract, a ball or a forged letter. What it buys
is the one thing Dumas made circuitous: **Villefort meets his son.** Here the
Count walks the boy Villefort buried into Villefort's dinner, under the tree
where he was buried, and tells the table a ghost story about the garden. The
fraud's only plot function was to get Benedetto into a courtroom with his
father; the Count's own letter to the police does that in a paragraph, and
Villefort's vanity — insisting on prosecuting the impostor who sat at his
table — does the rest.

## Deliberate departures from Dumas

Stated so no later gate mistakes them for errors.

**1. The Count arms the poisoner deliberately, on the page.** In Dumas he
talks chemistry and lends a recipe with ambiguous intent. Here he reads her,
recognises a woman who would kill for her son, and puts the vial in her hand.
This is the engine: judgment that cannot choose its victims has to be his
choice, not his accident.

**2. Noirtier is the Count's confessor.** The Count tells the one man in Paris
who cannot repeat it who he is, and promises him Valentine has nothing to
fear. That promise is the volume's moral hinge.

**3. The runaway horses are the Count's doing.** He sold Madame de Villefort
the horses. He endangered a child to get through a door; at the end of the
volume the same child dies by the weapon he handed over that same afternoon.

**4. Villefort conducts the questioning at the trial.** He asks his own son,
contemptuously, for his father's name.

**5. Valentine's return is withheld.** The reader sees the Count lift her from
the tomb and watches her sleep; Maximilien is asked to wait a month. Volume IV
pays it.

## Protected claims

- **Distinct moral responsibility.** Danglars wrote the letter, Fernand
  posted it, **Villefort read it, knew Edmond was innocent, saw his father's
  name, burned it and buried Edmond for fourteen years.** He is the only one
  of the three who knew. Never blurred.
- **Villefort believed the newborn dead and did not stop to look.** He is
  culpable of not looking, and of burying it to hide the scandal; he is not a
  man who knowingly buried a crying child. Benedetto's version ("he buried
  me alive") and Villefort's ("it was not breathing") both stand; the reader
  judges.
- **Valentine and the boy did nothing.** The price lands on them, Valentine
  above all.
- **The women are active choosers.** Valentine chooses to trust the Count and
  swallow the draught; Madame de Villefort chooses everything she does, for
  her son, and is never a puppet of the Count's.
- **Noirtier has agency.** He saves Valentine's life twice — by dosing her
  with his own medicine, and by naming the poisoner — with nothing but his
  eyes.
- The Count's crime is the vial. He is never let off it by the story.

## Sensitive material and its treatment

- **A child's death.** Villefort's son dies by his mother's hand at the end
  of Movement V. Decision: kept, because it is the bill the whole volume has
  been running up and the hinge to Volume IV's renunciation; told, never
  drawn — a closed door, a tin hussar on the floor, a mother's last line, and
  the faces of the two men who come too late. No body.
- **Poison.** Never a recipe. A red liquid, a glass of water, a bitter taste.
- **The buried newborn.** Told in words only. Villefort's own line ("It was
  not breathing. I thought it was dead.") is always given beside the
  accusation.
- **Suicide.** Madame de Villefort takes the poison after her husband's
  ultimatum; shown fallen, eyes closed, with dignity.
