## The health metric — why a page failed, not how often

Count REVISE verdicts by category:

- **Craft** — wrong for a reason a reader would feel: a dropped line, a
  mirrored room, a tail on the wrong mouth. The gate working. Cheap.
- **Gate** — wrong against something the critic *measured* or against a
  criterion that is not on the card. The gate eating the run. **A page that
  costs more than two generations for gate reasons is not a hard page; it is a
  broken gate.** Stop and look at the criterion.

## 2026-10-15 — learnings

- A sequel's engine survives when the protagonist's crime is on the page in his own hand (the vial on p8) and the volume keeps billing him for it; every critic named that as the book's spine.
- The confidant earns her pages by asking the plain question at each hinge (pp 9, 17, 26); two of the script rounds were won by giving Haydée scenes rather than narration.
- A mute character needs one rule taught once and kept literally (the asker speaks the answer); three critics flagged every place the convention slipped.
- Run the page loop in parallel with a reference regate when the regate's findings touch only critic-only boards.
- Two men in the same costume value in one wide panel: give the non-protagonist a head marker that holds at a distance and a different depth, in the packet, before generation.
