## Page intent

## PAGE 51

Dramatic page. We stay in the corridor: the Count has gone through the doorway, out of sight, and Villefort slumps against the wall beside it, staring at the tin hussar on the floor. The Count comes slowly back out, his face emptied, the empty vial held up in his open hand: one drop brought the boy back once; full, it would still be too late; the boy is dead. Villefort laughs a terrible small laugh and says he will dig this son up too. Last, the Count's gloved hand picks the hussar off the floor. The dominant turn: the Count comes out with the empty vial, the boy is dead, and Villefort's mind breaks. The Count in the doorway owns the page. The boy is never drawn; the room stays out of sight.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The Count coming out of the doorway with the empty vial held up in his open hand must own this dramatic page, and Villefort's breaking laugh must follow it. Blocking if the vial in his hand is not findable or the reader cannot tell the boy is dead.

### C3 — The Count and Villefort collide
Villefort in black robe, white bands and grey hair against the Count's loose black and swept-back black hair. Blocking if they read as one man or swap.

### C4 — The boy and the objects
The boy is never drawn; nothing is seen through the doorway. Exactly one vial, empty; exactly one tin hussar, on the floor and then in the Count's hand. A second vial, a full vial, or any sight of the boy is blocking.
