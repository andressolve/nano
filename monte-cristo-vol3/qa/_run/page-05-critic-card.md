## Page intent

## PAGE 5

Dramatic. The cellar, continuous. Benedetto, interested at last, demands his father's name; the Count refuses and offers him a better name of his own. On the stained table the Count's gloved hand sets down a fat purse, a gold signet ring and an engraved white visiting card that reads Prince Andrea Cavalcanti: from tonight the convict is an Italian prince. Benedetto slides the ring on, delighted, and asks the price; the Count says the prince will dine where he is told, next week with the King's Attorney, and Benedetto laughs at the joke. The dominant turn: Benedetto becomes Prince Andrea Cavalcanti, bound for the King's Attorney's table. The reader turns because only the Count knows the prince is Villefort's son. The purse and ring stay secondary to the card.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 5 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All seven balloon lines and the printed card read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. "Prince Andrea Cavalcanti" is engraved object text on the white visiting card on the table in the large panel, not a balloon; the same card legible again in another panel is continuity, not a doubled string. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The new name does not land
The visiting card with its name must be the image the eye finds, set down by the Count's gloved hand. If the card is lost among the other props or unreadable, it is blocking.

### C3 — Exchange misowned
"Rich how rich?…", "And what does a prince do…" and "A prosecutor?…" hang from Benedetto's mouth; the Count's four lines from his. A reversed owner is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Benedetto young and chestnut-haired in a stained mustard-brown jacket, never green velvet; the Count pale in black. A costume change, or a background figure in unrelieved black, is blocking.
