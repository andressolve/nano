## Page intent

## PAGE 26

A dramatic page in the Count's black-and-gold study at night, one candle. Haydée stands across the desk, hands flat on the wood, and lays the dead grandmother in front of him: an old woman drank his bottle in a cup of milk; did he see the milk? The Count keeps writing and answers with Villefort, who knew the face and chose not to ask. The dominant turn: Haydée confronts him with the dead woman; he bets the poisoner will wait and writes to the police. Haydée's straight question owns the page; his cold reasons and the folded letter are his defence. The reader turns because he is wagering Valentine's life on a murderer's patience, and the letter is for the police.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 26 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's study, Champs-Élysées, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The black-and-gold study, one candle. The Count
at his desk, writing a letter in a plain hand. Haydée stands across the desk
from him in crimson and gold, very straight, her hands flat on the wood.

> **HAYDÉE:** "An old woman who never knew your name drank your bottle in a cup of milk."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "You said nothing would happen in that house that you didn't see. Did you see the milk?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The Count, not looking up, the pen still moving.

> **COUNT:** "Villefort buried her in two days and never sent for a policeman. He knows that face. He chose not to ask."
>
> **COUNT:** "That is the man who sent me to If. I wanted to watch him do it once more, with his eyes open."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Haydée leaning over the desk; the Count looking up at last,
folding the letter.

> **HAYDÉE:** "And his wife told you today who is next."
>
> **COUNT:** "She won't dare again while the house is in mourning. By then his own son will be in the dock."

**Why you turn the page:** he is betting Valentine's life that the poisoner
will wait — and he is writing to the police.
