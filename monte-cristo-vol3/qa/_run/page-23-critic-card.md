An illustrated-prose page in a dim guest room of Villefort's house, one candle. Under the white covers of a curtained bed lies a small still shape with white hair on the pillow, her face never shown. Valentine kneels at the foot of the bed with her face in the coverlet; in the doorway stands Madame de Villefort in violet, perfectly composed. covers a small still shape with white hair on the pillow; one candle. At the

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper band)*
> "Valentine's grandmother arrived on Tuesday, with a great deal of luggage and a list of young men. She was old, and tired from the road, and very fond of her granddaughter.
>
> On the third night Madame de Villefort carried her a cup of warm milk herself. On the way she stopped in Noirtier's room, where nobody ever looked, and tipped something red into the cup in front of the one man in the house who could not tell. The old lady drank it and did not wake.
>
> By the next afternoon the whole house knew two things: that the old lady was dead, and that everything she had owned, which was a great deal, now belonged to Valentine, who was suddenly very rich. The young men on the list went home."

**Why you turn the page:** Valentine is suddenly rich, and the reader knows
what was in the milk.
