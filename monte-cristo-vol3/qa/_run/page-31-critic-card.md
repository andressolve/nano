## Page intent

## PAGE 31

An illustrated-prose page in a bare, dusty upper room of the empty house next door to Villefort's, at night. The Count, alone, holds a lantern beside a narrow doorway newly cut through the wall; through it, past the back boards of a tall wardrobe left open a crack, lies a sliver of a white room. The prose tells how he bought the house, had the door cut by workmen paid to keep quiet, put out his lantern and sat down in the dark to wait, why he did not warn Valentine, and that he could wait three nights in a wall. Dominant turn: the Count cuts into the wall and waits in the dark. The reader turns because he is inside the wall and someone is coming. Subordinate: the dust, the tools, the room's emptiness.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the bare wall)*
> "The house next to Villefort's had stood empty for a year. By the next evening it was the Count's.
>
> That night, workmen who were paid not to talk cut a narrow door through the wall, into the back of the tall wardrobe in Valentine's room.
>
> Then the Count sent them home, put out his lantern, and sat down in the dark to wait.
>
> He had bet that she would wait; she had not waited a week. She would not try the chocolate twice; next time she would come at night, to the glass by the bed. He did not warn Valentine: she would never believe it of her stepmother until she saw it with her own eyes. He had waited fourteen years in the Château d'If; he could wait three nights in a wall."

**Why you turn the page:** he is inside the wall, and someone is coming.
