## Page intent

## PAGE 20

A dramatic page in the candlelit dining room at Auteuil, the dark garden and the pale plane tree beyond the glass doors. The Count, delighted host, presents "Prince Andrea Cavalcanti" — Benedetto in green velvet — and the young man bows over Villefort's hand, two long narrow faces close together, one young and one old. The dominant turn: Villefort shakes his own son's hand and calls his father a fool. Benedetto's charm, Villefort's cold courtesy, and the Count's toast "To fathers" carry the irony the reader holds and the guests do not. Madame de Villefort is radiant and subordinate. The reader turns because the King's Attorney has just insulted the man who buried his son: himself.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 20 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the dining room of the house at Auteuil, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** A long candlelit table, glass doors open on the
dark garden and the pale trunk of the plane tree. The Count at the head of the
table. Madame de Villefort radiant in violet. And BENEDETTO, transformed — a
bottle-green velvet coat, cream embroidered waistcoat, a diamond pin, gloves —
bowing over Villefort's offered hand. The two long narrow faces, the young one
and the old one, are inches apart.

> **VILLEFORT:** "I did not know you had bought this house, monsieur."
>
> **COUNT:** "It was very cheap. Nobody wanted it. May I present Prince Andrea Cavalcanti, just arrived from Italy?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Benedetto straightening, charming, the crooked grin.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Prince. You are very young to travel alone."
>
> **BENEDETTO:** "I've always been alone, monsieur. My father left me when I was very small."
>
> **VILLEFORT:** "Then your father was a fool."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The Count at the head of the table, raising his glass,
delighted.

> **COUNT:** "To fathers."

**Why you turn the page:** Villefort has just shaken his son's hand and called
his father a fool.

focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
All 6 scripted strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each hung by tail and placement from the right mouth, with no extra text; a missing, altered, doubled, reordered or unreadable string is blocking. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. A string that reads is never measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The prince presented, the handshake of a young and an old long narrow face, "My father left me", "Then your father was a fool", and the toast. One image owns the page by eye; a dramatic page.

### C3 — Attribution
Villefort then the Count; then Villefort, Benedetto, Villefort; "To fathers." is the Count's. Blocking if Benedetto's line or the insult can be read as the other man's.

### C4 — Identity and separation
Villefort (rigid black frock coat, high white stock, iron-grey side part) never reads as the Count (loose unrelieved black, swept-back black hair); Benedetto in bottle-green velvet is young and plainly not Villefort. Reserved black on an extra that changes who seems to act is blocking.

### C5 — Continuity and integrity
No vial on the page. The handshake is two hands, intact; gross failure of a speaker's face is blocking, table detail is not.
