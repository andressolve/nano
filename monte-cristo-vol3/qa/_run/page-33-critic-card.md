## Page intent

## PAGE 33

A dramatic page in Valentine's room minutes later. The Count steps out of the wardrobe and quiets her with a hand over her mouth, then holds her glass up to the candle so she can see the red threads drifting in it. He tells her who poured them; she cannot believe it of her stepmother, then lifts her chin and says she will tell her father, and the Count answers that two people have been poisoned in her father's house and he has not called the police. Dominant turn: the Count shows Valentine the poisoned glass and that her father will not save her. The glass held to the candle owns the page; the reader turns because Valentine now knows, and even her father will not save her. Subordinate: the bed, the wardrobe, the room.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 33 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Valentine's room, minutes later.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The wardrobe door open; the Count bending over the bed with
one hand over Valentine's mouth as she wakes in fright.

> **COUNT:** "Don't cry out, Valentine. It's the Count of Monte Cristo. Maximilien's friend."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** The Count holding the glass of water up to the
candle; thin red threads drift through it.

> **COUNT:** "Look at your glass. Somebody poured red drops into it five minutes ago — the same drops that killed your grandmother."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "Who would do that?"

**Panel 3 — 18%.** Valentine sitting up, staring at him.

> **COUNT:** "Your stepmother. I was standing in your wardrobe, and I watched her do it."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "No. She's been so kind to me —"
>
> **COUNT:** "She will be kind to you every night until you are dead."

**Panel 4 — 17%.** Valentine alone, gripping the bedclothes, chin up; then
the Count, quiet and hard.

> **VALENTINE:** "Then I'll tell my father in the morning. He will have to look at me then."
>
> **COUNT:** "Two people have been poisoned in your father's house, and he hasn't called the police. Ask yourself why."

**Why you turn the page:** Valentine knows, and even her father will not save
her.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the 8 strings is missing, altered, duplicated, out of order, or does not read comfortably from the 600 × 900 proof; any other text appears. A string that reads is never measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Attribution across the exchanges
"Who would do that?", "No. She's been so kind to me —" and "Then I'll tell my father…" hang from Valentine's mouth; the stepmother line, "She will be kind…" and the closing line about the police from the Count's. A swapped owner is blocking.

### C3 — The glass does not own the page
The glass held to the candle with red threads in it is not the page's centre, or the page does not read as waking, the glass, the name, her refusal, his hard answer.

### C4 — Identity and state
Valentine is not in the white nightdress with loose hair, or cannot be told from her stepmother; the Count is not recognisable. More than one glass, or a vial drawn, is blocking.

### C5 — Generation failure
Duplicated or extra figure, gross anatomy on the Count's hands or either face.
