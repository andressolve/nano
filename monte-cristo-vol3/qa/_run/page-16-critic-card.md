## Page intent

## PAGE 16

Dramatic. The Morrel house, by the fire. Maximilien paces: her father would sooner see her dead than married to a shipowner's son; they meet at an iron gate at the bottom of his garden and talk through the bars. The Count asks who her father is. Maximilien turns, glowing, and gives the name like a gift: the King's Attorney's daughter, Valentine de Villefort. Then the Count's face, and for the first time in the book the pleasure goes out of it. Of every house in Paris, that one. Maximilien sees it and asks whether he has met her; once, the Count says, standing by a window, and nobody was looking at her. The dominant turn: the girl Maximilien loves is Valentine de Villefort, and the pleasure leaves the Count's face. Maximilien's glowing name owns the page; the Count's changed face answers it. The reader turns because she lives where the Count left a poison.
