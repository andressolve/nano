## Page intent

## PAGE 13

Dramatic. Noirtier's room. The old man's eyes burn at the Count, not with horror but with fierce interest. The Count tells him his son let him think the letter was lost, and that he means to take that son apart, piece by piece; does he want him to stop? Many quick blinks: no. The Count speaks the no. Then Noirtier's eyes move deliberately to the door Valentine went out by and stay there, and the Count understands the question and asks it aloud; one long slow close: yes. The Count's poised hand goes still, and he gives his word that she has nothing to fear from him. The dominant turn: Noirtier does not want him to stop, and the Count gives his word Valentine is safe. Noirtier's eyes own the page, and every word is the Count's. The reader turns knowing what is in Madame de Villefort's reticule.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Noirtier's eyes, huge and fixed on the Count —
not horror: a fierce, burning interest.

> **COUNT:** "You never knew he burned it. He let you think your letter was lost."
>
> **COUNT:** "I am going to take your son apart, monsieur, piece by piece. Do you want me to stop?"

**Panel 2 — 15%.** Noirtier's eyes blinking, quick and many.

> **COUNT:** "No."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Noirtier's eyes move, deliberately, to the door Valentine
went out by, and stay there.

> **COUNT:** "Are you asking me about your granddaughter?"

**Panel 4 — 20%.** Noirtier's eyes close, slowly, once; and the Count's poised
right hand goes very still on his knee. A long moment.

> **COUNT:** "Yes."

> **COUNT:** "She has done nothing to me. She has nothing to fear from me. You have my word."

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what is in Madame de Villefort's
reticule, and the Count has just given his word.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 13 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All six strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The eyes do not answer
The reader must see three eye-states in order: quick many blinks (no), a deliberate look at the door, one slow close (yes). If the "No." panel shows a single calm close, or the "Yes." panel shows open eyes, the answers contradict the words and it is blocking. The opening panel's eyes read as fierce interest, not horror.

### C3 — Noirtier never speaks
Every balloon is the Count's, beside the Count's mouth. "No." or "Yes." with a tail to Noirtier, or any balloon from him, is blocking.

### C4 — The promise
The Count's hand goes still on his knee as he gives his word. Identity: the Count clean-shaven, pale, black hair, black dress; Noirtier white mane and moustache, green coat, armchair. Valentine and any vial absent; room detail nonblocking.
