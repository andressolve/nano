## Page intent

## PAGE 9

Dramatic. Evening in the long dark dining room at Auteuil. The Count stands at the glass doors watching a carriage's lamps go away down the lane; Haydée rises from the table behind him and puts it to him plainly: the runaway horses were his, sold half broken to Villefort, and the hill road past his gate was his suggestion. He does not deny it. The boy was never in danger, he says, because he was standing in the road, and now the family owes him its door. Then she asks about the little bottle, and he answers that its use is Villefort's problem. The dominant turn: the Count admits he rigged the horses and armed the mother on purpose. The two of them own the page in the dark; the departing lamps stay tiny and far beyond the glass. The reader turns because on Thursday he walks into her house.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the dining room of the house at Auteuil, evening.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The long dark room; the Count at the glass doors
watching the lamps of a carriage go away down the lane. Behind him Haydée
stands up from the table.

> **HAYDÉE:** "Those horses came from your stables. You sold them to her husband last week."
>
> **COUNT:** "Half broken. And I told him the hill road past my gate was the prettiest drive in Auteuil."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** Haydée, very straight.

> **HAYDÉE:** "There was a child in that carriage."
>
> **COUNT:** "The boy was never in danger, Haydée. I was standing in the road."
>
> **COUNT:** "And now his mother owes me her son, and her husband owes me his door. It opens on Thursday."

**Panel 3 — 28%.** The two of them in the dark, the carriage lamps tiny
beyond the glass.

> **HAYDÉE:** "And the little bottle? I saw her face when you gave it to her."
>
> **COUNT:** "So did I. She'll do whatever she was always going to do."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "To whom?"
>
> **COUNT:** "That is Villefort's problem."

**Why you turn the page:** he has admitted he armed her on purpose, and on
Thursday he walks into her house.
