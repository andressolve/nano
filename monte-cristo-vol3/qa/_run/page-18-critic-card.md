## Page intent

## PAGE 18

Illustrated prose. The Count's study, late at night, black and gold, one candle. The Count sits alone at his desk, writing; three plain cream invitation cards lie in a row before him, seen edge-on; through the tall window, the lamps of Paris. The prose carries his private belief: that God kept Villefort's buried child breathing for him; that the boy in the carriage lived because he stood in the road, and that he will stand in every road of Villefort's house. Then he writes three invitations to Auteuil, for Villefort, his wife, and Prince Andrea Cavalcanti, and Danglars can wait. The dominant turn: the Count believes he will see everything, and invites Villefort and his son to Auteuil. His solitary figure at the desk owns the page; the cards stay small and wordless. The reader turns because Villefort is coming to dinner where he buried a child.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 18 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All four prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text and no balloons; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The solitary belief does not land
One image must own the page: the Count alone at his desk by one candle, writing, the lamps of Paris beyond the window. A second person in the room fails the turn.

### C3 — The invitations
Exactly three plain cream cards on the desk, countable, because the prose names three guests; any readable writing on them is blocking, since the words belong to the prose. A different number is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
The Count: clean-shaven, pale, swept-back black hair with first grey, high cheekbones, unrelieved black. The writing hand must be whole; desk clutter and candle detail are nonblocking.
