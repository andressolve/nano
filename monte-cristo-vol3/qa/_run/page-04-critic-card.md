## Page intent

## PAGE 4

Dramatic. A low, smoky cellar tavern in Paris. Benedetto, young, insolent, in a stained mustard-brown jacket, lays down a winning hand while two rough card players glare; the Count sits down across from him, black and still. Benedetto mocks him; the Count names him, then names his crimes: the escape from the galleys at Toulon and the guard who did not fall by himself. Leaning into the candlelight, the Count says he knows where Benedetto was born, in a garden, in a box, and that his story has a rich father in it. ## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a cellar tavern in Paris, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A low vaulted cellar, smoke, candle stubs stuck
in bottles. At a card table BENEDETTO — twenty-one, long narrow face, glossy
dark chestnut hair in a careless wave, a crooked grin higher on the right, a
stained mustard-brown jacket and grey neckerchief — lays down a winning hand
while two rough card players in brown and grey glare at him. The Count has sat
down in the empty chair across from him, black and still.

> **BENEDETTO:** "That chair's taken, my lord. Unless you've come down here to lose money too."
>
> **COUNT:** "I've come to make you some. You're Benedetto."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** Benedetto's grin stays on but his eyes go flat; under the
table his hand finds the knife in his boot.

> **BENEDETTO:** "Who's asking?"
>
> **COUNT:** "A man who knows you broke out of the galleys at Toulon in April."
>
> **COUNT:** "And that the guard on the wall didn't fall by himself."

**Panel 3 — 28%.** The Count leans into the candlelight.

> **COUNT:** "I also know where you were born: in a garden, in a box, on your very first night."
>
> **BENEDETTO:** "Everybody down here has a sad story, my lord."
>
> **COUNT:** "Yours has a rich father in it."

**Why you turn the page:** the convict wants his father's name, and the Count
is not giving it yet.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 4 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All eight lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The bait does not land
The page must end on the Count leaning into the candlelight with the rich father dangled, Benedetto caught. A card game with no confrontation is blocking.

### C3 — Exchange misowned
Benedetto's three lines ("That chair's taken…", "Who's asking?", "Everybody down here…") hang from his mouth; the Count's five from his. A card player's mouth owning any line, or Benedetto owning a line about his own crimes, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and extras
Benedetto young, long-faced, chestnut-haired, in a stained mustard-brown jacket, never green velvet or a white open shirt. The Count pale in unrelieved black. The card players two heavy bearded men in brown and grey; an extra in unrelieved black, changing who the reader thinks is acting, is blocking.

### C5 — The knife
The knife stays half-hidden under the table in Benedetto's hand. A drawn blade raised or used is blocking; its exact visibility is not.
