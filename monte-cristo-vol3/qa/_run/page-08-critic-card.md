## Page intent

## PAGE 8

Dramatic. On the gravel inside the Count's gate, he kneels with the limp boy across his knee while Madame de Villefort, hair fallen, begs. One red drop from a small crystal vial falls on the boy's lips; his eyes fly open and he flings his arms round his mother, tin hussar still in his fist. She asks what the Count gave him. Then, face to face over the boy's head, the vial held up between them, her eyes are on the vial, wanting it, and his are on her, reading her: she asks what more than one drop would do, he tells her ten would stop a heart undetectably, and gives it to her. The dominant turn: the Count revives the boy and hands the vial to a mother he knows will use it. The reader turns because he knew. The gravel and the gate stay background to the two faces and the one vial.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "And if someone gave more than one drop?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Ten drops would stop a heart, and no doctor in Paris could say why."
>
> **COUNT:** "Keep it, madame. You may need it one day."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just put a poison into the hand of a woman
who wanted it — and he knew.

### C1 — Transcription fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The handover does not land
One image owns the page: the two faces over the boy's head, the vial between them, her eyes on the vial and his on her. If her wanting and his reading do not show, the page's turn is lost and it is blocking.

### C3 — Exchange misowned
Madame de Villefort's three lines hang from her mouth; the Count's four, including both closing lines, from his. "Ten drops would stop a heart…" read as hers is blocking. The boy has no balloon or tail; one is blocking.

### C4 — The vial
Exactly one small cut-crystal vial of red liquid with a gold stopper in any panel; two vials in one panel is blocking. The same vial seen in two panels is continuity.

### C5 — Identity
Madame de Villefort with honey-gold coils fallen loose and violet silk; the boy awake in the third panel, hussar in fist; the Count pale in black. A misread identity is blocking.
