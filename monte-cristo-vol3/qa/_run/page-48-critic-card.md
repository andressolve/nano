## Page intent

## PAGE 48

Dramatic page. Villefort bursts into his silent hall shouting for his wife and his son, and finds Madame de Villefort at the top of the white marble stair in her violet silk, swaying, the empty crystal vial slipping from her hand and falling down the steps. She tells him, gently, that she did what he told her. Then she sinks onto the top stair, eyes closing, and answers the question he cannot finish asking: she could not leave the boy behind. The dominant turn: Héloïse has taken the poison and the boy with her. The dominant image is her on the stair with the vial falling; Villefort's entry and her sinking are subordinate. The reader turns to find the boy. She is not drawn dying in agony; the falling vial and her quiet sentence carry the horror, not any struggle.

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the 4 strings is missing, altered, duplicated, reordered, unreadable from the 600 × 900 proof (a string that reads is never measured), or not owned by the right mouth by tail and placement; extra text appears. Villefort owns the shout and "Where is my son?"; Madame de Villefort owns "I did what you told me" and "I couldn't leave him behind". Nonblocking, the words and their order being what must match: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter. A line directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as: she is on the white stair, the empty vial falling from her hand, and she has taken the boy with her. Blocking if she and the falling vial do not plainly own this dramatic page.

### C3 — Identity or separation fails
Madame de Villefort must read as herself (honey-gold coils, violet silk, oval face); Villefort as himself in a black robe with white bands and grey hair. Blocking if either reads as another principal.

### C4 — The vial count or state fails
Exactly one vial, empty. Two vials, or a vial full of red liquid, is blocking. The same vial seen lying lower on the steps in a later panel is continuity, not a second vial.

### C5 — Generation integrity
A duplicated figure, gross focal anatomy on her face or the falling hand, or a balloon collision that hides a line is blocking. Background texture and stair hardware are not.
