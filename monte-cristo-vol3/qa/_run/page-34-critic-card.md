## Page intent

## PAGE 34

A dramatic page in Valentine's room by candlelight, minutes after she has seen the poisoned glass. She asks to be taken away through the wardrobe; the Count refuses, because a missing girl is hunted and a dead one is not. The dominant turn: Valentine chooses to swallow the Count's draught and die. The page belongs to the Count's open palm, a small gold box holding one white pastille, with his face above it and the corner of his mouth lifting very slightly, because he is enjoying the plan. Then the choice passes to her: she asks about Maximilien, he asks whether she trusts him, and she takes the pastille with her eyes on his. The reader turns because she has swallowed it and in the morning she will be dead. The wardrobe and the room stay subordinate.

**Location:** Valentine's room.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Valentine clutching the bedclothes, white, glancing at the
open wardrobe.

> **VALENTINE:** "Then take me away now, through your wardrobe."
>
> **COUNT:** "If you vanish, your father will turn Paris upside down and bring you home to her. Nobody hunts for a girl who is dead."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The Count's open palm: a small gold box, open,
holding one white pastille. Above it his face in the candlelight — and the

> **COUNT:** "So you will die. Swallow this, and by morning the whole house will believe it."
>
> **COUNT:** "Your father will bury you, the way he buries everything. And I will bring you out."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "And Maximilien? Will you tell him I'm alive?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Maximilien trusts me. Do you?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Valentine puts the pastille in her mouth, her eyes on his.

> **VALENTINE:** "He does. So I will."

be dead.

### C2 — The choice does not land
The Count's open palm with the box, his face above it, does not own the page, or the page does not end on Valentine taking the pastille. Dramatic mode.

### C3 — The exchange is misattributed

### C4 — Identity
The Count is not recognizable (pale, clean-shaven, swept-back black hair, black dress), or Valentine is not the young fair girl in a white nightdress.

### C5 — The draught is wrong

### C6 — Focal generation
Gross error in either focal hand or face; a duplicated figure.
