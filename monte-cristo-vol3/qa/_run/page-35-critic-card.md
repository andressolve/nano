## Page intent

## PAGE 35

An illustrated-prose page in grey morning light. The dominant turn: the house believes Valentine is dead. She lies white and still on her bed, hands folded, a white sheet drawn up; her father stands rigid at the foot with a hand over his eyes and does not weep; her stepmother bows her head prettily in the doorway; and wheeled to the bedside, her grandfather fixes his wide wet eyes on her face. The page belongs to the stillness of the bed and to Noirtier's eyes, which are the only living thing in the room and are saying something nobody there can read. The prose tells what the house believed; the picture lets the reader see the three different faces of grief. The reader turns to learn what the old man's eyes are saying. The furniture stays subordinate.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 35 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** Valentine's room, morning.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Grey morning light. Valentine lies white and still
on the bed, hands folded, a white sheet drawn to her breast. At the foot,
Villefort stands rigid with one hand over his eyes. In the doorway, Madame de
Villefort bows her head. And wheeled to the bedside, Noirtier, his eyes wide
and wet and fixed on Valentine's face.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper band, over the grey wall)*
> "In the morning they found Valentine cold in her bed, and the house believed what it saw.
>
> Villefort stood at the foot of the bed and did not weep. Madame de Villefort wept very prettily in the doorway.
>
> Noirtier made them carry him up to her side, and would not let them carry him away. He did not take his eyes off her face, and nobody in that house could tell what they were saying."

**Why you turn the page:** everyone believes Valentine is dead — her
grandfather too — and his eyes are saying something.

### C2 — The house's belief does not land
The page does not read as one illustrated-prose page in which Valentine lies apparently dead and her father, stepmother and grandfather react; or the field covers a face or hands.

### C4 — Identity and separation
