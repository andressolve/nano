## Page intent

## PAGE 25

A dramatic page in Villefort's cold grey-blue drawing room. Madame de Villefort sits on the sofa in violet, her son asleep in her lap with his tin hussar, stroking his curls, while the Count stands before her in black. Sweetly she thanks him for his "little medicine" and talks of Valentine's million and her poor boy's nothing — unless something happened to Valentine. The dominant turn: Héloïse as good as tells the Count Valentine is next, and he does not stop her; his still hand and his pleasant question are the whole of his answer. Then the boy, half awake, presses his tin soldier into the Count's gloved hand, and the Count gives it back. The reader turns because the Count has been told who is next and let it stand.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 25 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the drawing room of Villefort's house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Madame de Villefort on the sofa in violet, her son
asleep with his head in her lap, the tin hussar in his hand; she strokes his
gold curls. The Count stands before her, black.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "I never thanked you properly for your little medicine, Count. It has been such a comfort to me. We've had such a sad week."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Her face, sweet and soft, her hand on the sleeping boy's
curls.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "Valentine's grandmother. So sudden. And now Valentine is worth a million, and my poor boy is worth nothing at all."
>
> **HÉLOÏSE:** "Unless something happened to her, of course. Then it would all come home to her papa, and one day to my poor boy."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** The Count says nothing. He holds her look, and his poised
right hand is very still. Then, pleasantly:

> **COUNT:** "You are careful with it, I hope?"

**Panel 4 — 20%.** The boy, half awake, sits up and solemnly presses his tin
hussar into the Count's black-gloved hand. The Count looks down at it for a
long moment — and gives it back.

**Why you turn the page:** she has as good as told him who is next, he did not
stop her, and her son has just tried to give him his soldier.

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
All 4 scripted strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each hung by tail and placement from the right mouth, with no extra text; a missing, altered, doubled, reordered or unreadable string is blocking. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. A string that reads is never measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
Her sweet thanks for the medicine, Valentine's million and "unless something happened to her", the Count's still hand and pleasant question, and the boy pressing his hussar into the Count's hand. The opening image owns the page by eye; a dramatic page.

### C3 — Attribution
"You are careful with it, I hope?" is the Count's; the three sweet lines are hers. The boy has no balloon, no tail, and no lettered name; any is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Madame de Villefort in violet with honey-gold coils; the Count tall in unrelieved black; the boy small in violet velvet with gold curls. Blocking if confused.

### C5 — Objects and integrity
Exactly one tin hussar, passing into the Count's gloved hand. Zero vials: a drawn vial is blocking. Faces and the hands on the hussar intact.
