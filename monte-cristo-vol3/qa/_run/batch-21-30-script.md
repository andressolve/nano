# Exact script — pages 21–30

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 21 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the garden at Auteuil, night, after dinner.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The guests strolling out with candles under the tree; the
Count stops on the bare ground.

> **COUNT:** "This house has a story. They say a child was born here one night in 1817, and buried under this tree before morning."
>
> **HÉLOÏSE:** "How horrible! How thrilling! Who would do such a thing?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The tableau under the tree: Villefort white as
paper, one hand gripping the trunk; Madame de Villefort thrilled; and
Benedetto, his grin gone, staring at the bare ground under his own feet — then
up at the Count.

> **COUNT:** "Someone with a great deal to lose, I imagine."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Villefort turning away toward the house, stiff as a
rod.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Servants' gossip. Come, Héloïse, you are cold. Good night, monsieur."

**Why you turn the page:** the King's Attorney is walking away from a tree as
fast as he can, and the false prince has understood whose garden this is.

## PAGE 22 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the garden at Auteuil, the same night, very late.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Under the plane tree, Villefort alone in his
shirtsleeves, his coat flung on the ground, a lantern on the earth beside him,
a spade. He has dug a deep raw hole and is on his knees at its edge, staring
into it. It is empty.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "At two in the morning, the King's Attorney came back over the wall with a spade. There was nothing in the hole at all."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Up at the black house: in one dark upstairs window, the
Count's pale face, watching.

**Why you turn the page:** Villefort now knows that someone dug the box up —
and the Count watched him find out.

## PAGE 23 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** a guest room in Villefort's house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A dim room, a curtained bed, under the white
covers a small still shape with white hair on the pillow; one candle. At the
foot of the bed Valentine kneels with her face in the coverlet. In the doorway
stands Madame de Villefort in violet, perfectly composed, one hand resting on
her reticule.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper band)*
> "Valentine's grandmother arrived on Tuesday, with a great deal of luggage and a list of young men. She was old, and tired from the road, and very fond of her granddaughter.
>
> On the third night Madame de Villefort carried her a cup of warm milk herself. On the way she stopped in Noirtier's room, where nobody ever looked, and tipped something red into the cup in front of the one man in the house who could not tell. The old lady drank it and did not wake.
>
> By the next afternoon the whole house knew two things: that the old lady was dead, and that everything she had owned, which was a great deal, now belonged to Valentine, who was suddenly very rich. The young men on the list went home."

**Why you turn the page:** Valentine is suddenly rich, and the reader knows
what was in the milk.

## PAGE 24 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Villefort shutting the door behind him, grey-faced, still
in black.

> **VILLEFORT:** "I have prosecuted four poisoners, Father. I know the face I saw on that pillow this morning."
>
> **VILLEFORT:** "Someone in this house did it. Do you know who? Close your eyes once for yes."

**Panel 2 — 13%.** Noirtier's eyes: one long close.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Yes."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Villefort leaning down over the chair, hard.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Is it Valentine? Everything her grandmother had is hers now."

**Panel 4 — 12%.** Noirtier's eyes: furious rapid blinking.

> **VILLEFORT:** "No. Then who —"

**Panel 5 — dominant, 30%.** Villefort straightening and holding up one hand
between himself and his father's eyes, to stop them.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Stop. Don't tell me. If I know, I must prosecute, and the name of Villefort will be in every newspaper in France."

**Why you turn the page:** the King's Attorney has chosen not to know who the
poisoner is.

## PAGE 25 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the drawing room of Villefort's house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Madame de Villefort on the sofa in violet, her son
asleep with his head in her lap, the tin hussar in his hand; she strokes his
gold curls. The Count stands before her, black.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "I never thanked you properly for your little medicine, Count. It has been such a comfort to me. We've had such a sad week."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Her face, sweet and soft, her hand on the sleeping boy's
curls.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "Valentine's grandmother. So sudden. And now Valentine is worth a million, and my poor boy is worth nothing at all."
>
> **HÉLOÏSE:** "Unless something happened to her, of course. Then it would all come home to her papa, and one day to my poor boy."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** The Count says nothing. He holds her look, and his poised
right hand is very still. Then, pleasantly:

> **COUNT:** "You are careful with it, I hope?"

**Panel 4 — 20%.** The boy, half awake, sits up and solemnly presses his tin
hussar into the Count's black-gloved hand. The Count looks down at it for a
long moment — and gives it back.

**Why you turn the page:** she has as good as told him who is next, he did not
stop her, and her son has just tried to give him his soldier.

## PAGE 26 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's study, Champs-Élysées, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The black-and-gold study, one candle. The Count
at his desk, writing a letter in a plain hand. Haydée stands across the desk
from him in crimson and gold, very straight, her hands flat on the wood.

> **HAYDÉE:** "An old woman who never knew your name drank your bottle in a cup of milk."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "You said nothing would happen in that house that you didn't see. Did you see the milk?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The Count, not looking up, the pen still moving.

> **COUNT:** "Villefort buried her in two days and never sent for a policeman. He knows that face. He chose not to ask."
>
> **COUNT:** "That is the man who sent me to If. I wanted to watch him do it once more, with his eyes open."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Haydée leaning over the desk; the Count looking up at last,
folding the letter.

> **HAYDÉE:** "And his wife told you today who is next."
>
> **COUNT:** "She won't dare again while the house is in mourning. By then his own son will be in the dock."

**Why you turn the page:** he is betting Valentine's life that the poisoner
will wait — and he is writing to the police.

---

## PAGE 27 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the steps of the Opéra, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The great lit steps of the Opéra, a fashionable
crowd in furs and greys drawing back; two gendarmes in blue have Benedetto by
both arms. He is in his bottle-green velvet coat, the diamond pin flashing,
and he is smiling.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper band, over the night sky)*
> "The morning after the funeral, the police received a letter with no signature. Prince Andrea Cavalcanti, it said, was Benedetto, a convict who had killed a guard escaping the galleys at Toulon. The Count had written it himself.
>
> They arrested Benedetto on the steps of the Opéra, in his green velvet coat. He was smiling.
>
> The King's Attorney had shaken that man's hand and sat at a table with him. He had dug under the Count's tree in the dark, and never asked the Count one question about it; he did not ask now. Next morning he announced that he would prosecute the false prince himself, and ask for his head."

**Why you turn the page:** Villefort is going to prosecute his own son — and
Valentine, now an heiress, is still living in the poisoner's house.

## PAGE 28 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room, morning.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Valentine on a footstool beside Noirtier's chair, a book on
her knee and a cup of chocolate in her hand.

> **VALENTINE:** "Madame de Villefort sent my chocolate down to me here. She's been so kind since Grandmother died."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** She drinks, and stops, puzzled.

> **VALENTINE:** "It tastes strange today. Bitter, like your medicine —"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** Valentine falling sideways off the stool onto the
carpet, the cup breaking, the book falling open. Above her in the chair
Noirtier is a statue — not a muscle can move — and his eyes are wide with
terror, fixed on her.

> **CAPTION** *(lower right)*
> "Nobody else was in the room, and Noirtier could not even cry out."

**Why you turn the page:** the poison has reached Valentine.

---

## PAGE 29 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's study, Champs-Élysées.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Maximilien bursts in, cloak soaked with rain, hat
crushed in his hand; the Count rises from his desk, and at "should be dead"
his poised right hand closes.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Valentine fell in her grandfather's room this morning. Her maid ran to the gate to tell me."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "She should be dead, and nobody knows why she isn't."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Maximilien seizes the Count by both arms.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Her grandmother died in that house last week. You know everything, monsieur. What is happening there?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Someone in that house is a poisoner."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Maximilien already turning for the door; the Count's hand
on his shoulder stops him.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Then I'm going to the police."
>
> **COUNT:** "In this city, the police answer to the King's Attorney. Go home, Maximilien. Valentine will not die. I give you my word."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count has promised to save the girl his own
poison is killing.

## PAGE 30 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room, evening.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Valentine pale on a daybed beside her grandfather's chair;
the Count kneeling between them.

> **VALENTINE:** "The doctor says I ought to have died. Grandfather keeps looking at me as if he's proud of me."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "He's made me sip his horrid medicine every morning since Grandmother died. I only did it to please him."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** The Count lifting the glass of lemon-water from beside
Noirtier's still hand and breathing it in; then looking up at Noirtier.

> **COUNT:** "Every morning since her grandmother died. Your medicine."

**Panel 3 — 13%.** Noirtier's eyes: one long close.

> **COUNT:** "Yes."

**Panel 4 — dominant, 22%.** The Count close to the old man, low, so Valentine
cannot hear.

> **COUNT:** "It's made from the same thing that is in her stepmother's bottle."
>
> **COUNT:** "A little every day, so that a big dose can't kill her. It won't save her twice."

**Panel 5 — 13%.** Noirtier's eyes fixed on the Count's face, questioning;
the Count meets them and does not look away.

> **COUNT:** "You want to know how I know what's in her bottle. I know because I gave it to her."
>
> **COUNT:** "And I will still have your son."

**Why you turn the page:** the old man saved her, and the next dose will be
bigger.
