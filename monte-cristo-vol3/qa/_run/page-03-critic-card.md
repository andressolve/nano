## Page intent

## PAGE 3

Dramatic. Continuous in the night garden. Haydée turns on him: how can he know? The Count explains that a hidden enemy of Villefort dug the box up alive, and later confessed it to a priest who was the Count in disguise. Then, wide and small under the vast tree with the black house behind and the glow of Paris far off over the wall, he tells her the child lived, is twenty-one, and is a thief, a liar and a murderer; Villefort's son by a woman not his wife. Villefort does not know he has a son, and the Count means to give him back. The dominant turn: the buried baby lived, and the Count means to return him to Villefort. The reader turns because that gift is a murderer. The distant city glow stays a small light, never a scene of its own.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the garden at Auteuil, night, continuous.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Haydée turns on him, sharp, the lantern between them.

> **HAYDÉE:** "If he buried it in the dark, how can you know?"
>
> **COUNT:** "A man who hated Villefort was hiding behind that wall. He dug the box up the moment Villefort left, and the baby was breathing."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The Count, privately amused.

> **COUNT:** "Years later he confessed it to a priest, the Abbé Busoni. I was the Abbé Busoni — one of my disguises."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 55%.** Wide: the two of them small under the vast tree,
the black house behind; far off over the wall, the glow of Paris. The Count
looks toward it.

> **HAYDÉE:** "And the child?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Alive. Twenty-one. A thief, a liar, and since spring, a murderer."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "Villefort's son."
>
> **COUNT:** "His child by a lady who was not his wife. A baby like that would have ruined a young prosecutor."
>
> **COUNT:** "He doesn't know he has a son. I'm going to give him back."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count means to hand Villefort a murderer — his
own son.

### C1 — Transcription fails
All eight lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. ### C2 — The plan does not land
One image owns the page: the two of them small under the vast tree with the dark house behind, the Count looking toward the far glow of the city as he says he will give the son back. If the wide panel reads as incidental scenery, it is blocking.

### C3 — Exchange misowned
"And the child?" and "Villefort's son." must hang from Haydée's mouth; the answers, including the two closing lines, from the Count's. "Villefort's son." read as the Count's line inverts who reaches the truth, and is blocking.

### C4 — Past drawn in

### C5 — Identity
