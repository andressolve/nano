## Page intent

## PAGE 29

A dramatic page in the Count's black-and-gold study. Maximilien bursts in soaked from the rain with the news that Valentine fell this morning and should be dead. The Count's poised hand closes when he hears it. Maximilien seizes him and demands to know what is happening in that house; the Count tells him plainly that someone there is a poisoner, then stops him from running to the police, because the police answer to Valentine's father, and gives his word that she will not die. The page belongs to Maximilien's desperation meeting the Count's stillness, and the turn is the promise. Dominant turn: Maximilien begs for help, and the Count promises Valentine will not die. The reader turns knowing he has promised to save the girl his own poison is killing. Subordinate: the study's furniture, the rain, the crushed hat.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 29 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's study, Champs-Élysées.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Maximilien bursts in, cloak soaked with rain, hat
crushed in his hand; the Count rises from his desk, and at "should be dead"
his poised right hand closes.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Valentine fell in her grandfather's room this morning. Her maid ran to the gate to tell me."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "She should be dead, and nobody knows why she isn't."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Maximilien seizes the Count by both arms.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Her grandmother died in that house last week. You know everything, monsieur. What is happening there?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Someone in that house is a poisoner."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Maximilien already turning for the door; the Count's hand
on his shoulder stops him.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Then I'm going to the police."
>
> **COUNT:** "In this city, the police answer to the King's Attorney. Go home, Maximilien. Valentine will not die. I give you my word."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count has promised to save the girl his own
poison is killing.

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the 6 strings is missing, altered, duplicated, out of order, or does not read comfortably from the 600 × 900 proof; any other text appears. A string that reads is never measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Attribution across the exchanges
"Someone in that house is a poisoner." and the closing promise must hang from the Count's mouth by tail; the news, the question, and "Then I'm going to the police." from Maximilien's. Each reply sits clearly after its prompt. A swapped or ambiguous owner on the promise or the poisoner line is blocking.

### C3 — The turn does not land
The page does not read as Maximilien's desperate entrance meeting the Count's stillness, ending on the hand on his shoulder and the promise. An unclear closing fist is nonblocking.

### C4 — The Count and Maximilien collide
The pale clean-shaven Count in unrelieved black cannot be told from the sun-browned, moustached Maximilien in dark blue and gold. Blocking if who grips and who promises is confused.

### C5 — Generation failure
Duplicated figure, an extra person, gross anatomy on the gripping hands or either face. Desk clutter and rain texture are nonblocking.
