## Page intent

## PAGE 28

A dramatic page in Noirtier's green room in the morning. Valentine sits on a footstool at her grandfather's chair, chatting about how kind her stepmother has been and sipping the chocolate her stepmother sent down. She notices it tastes bitter, and then she falls. The page belongs to the fall and to the old man above it: Valentine sliding off the stool, the cup breaking, the book dropping open, while Noirtier, who cannot move a muscle or make a sound, stares at her in terror. The dominant turn: Valentine drinks the poisoned chocolate and falls while Noirtier can only watch. The reader turns because the poison has reached the girl the Count's plan was supposed to keep safe. Subordinate: the furniture of the room, the book, the medicine glass on his table.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 28 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room, morning.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Valentine on a footstool beside Noirtier's chair, a book on
her knee and a cup of chocolate in her hand.

> **VALENTINE:** "Madame de Villefort sent my chocolate down to me here. She's been so kind since Grandmother died."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** She drinks, and stops, puzzled.

> **VALENTINE:** "It tastes strange today. Bitter, like your medicine —"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 50%.** Valentine falling sideways off the stool onto the
carpet, the cup breaking, the book falling open. Above her in the chair
Noirtier is a statue — not a muscle can move — and his eyes are wide with
terror, fixed on her.

> **CAPTION** *(lower right)*
> "Nobody else was in the room, and Noirtier could not even cry out."

**Why you turn the page:** the poison has reached Valentine.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 28 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the 3 strings is missing, altered, duplicated, out of order, or does not read comfortably from the 600 × 900 proof; a Valentine line not hanging from her mouth, a tailed caption, or any other text is blocking. A string that reads is never measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The fall does not own the page
The eye does not land on Valentine falling, the cup breaking, and Noirtier rigid above her with terrified eyes. Judge by what the eye finds first.

### C3 — Noirtier speaks or moves
Any balloon or tail from Noirtier, or any movement but his eyes, is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Valentine (pale, low chestnut knot, dove-grey) or Noirtier (white mane and moustache, green coat, wheeled chair) is not recognisable.

### C5 — Object state or generation failure
More than one cup, any vial, a duplicated or extra figure, or gross anatomy on Valentine's face or hands. Texture and the medicine glass are nonblocking.
