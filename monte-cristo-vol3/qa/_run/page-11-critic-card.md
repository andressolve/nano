## Page intent

## PAGE 11

Dramatic. Noirtier's dark green room, kept as it was in 1815. In his high-backed wheeled armchair sits Villefort's father, white-maned, utterly still; only his black eyes live, fixed on the Count in the doorway. A caption tells who he is and that young Edmond's letter was addressed to him. Valentine kneels at his chair, her hand on his, and introduces the Count, then explains to him, cheerfully, how her grandfather talks: one slow close for yes, many quick blinks for no. She asks whether he is glad the Count has come, and his eyes close, slowly, once. She speaks the meaning. The dominant turn: Noirtier, who speaks only with his eyes, is glad to see the Count. Noirtier never speaks; every word is Valentine's or the caption's. The reader turns because the Count has come to tell the old man something.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 11 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room, ground floor of Villefort's house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** An old man's room kept as it was in 1815: dark
green walls, an Empire clock, a framed eagle. In a high-backed wheeled armchair
by the window sits NOIRTIER — seventy-six, gaunt leonine face, long swept-back
white hair, white cavalry moustache, a faded forest-green Empire coat, a rug
over his knees — utterly still. Only his eyes are alive, black and glittering,
fixed on the Count in the doorway. Valentine kneels by the chair with her hand
on his.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Noirtier, Villefort's father. In 1815, the secret letter young Edmond carried from Napoleon's island had been addressed to him."

> **VALENTINE:** "Grandfather, this is the Count of Monte Cristo, who saved Madame de Villefort and the boy on the hill."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Valentine turns to the Count, cheerful and matter-of-fact.

> **VALENTINE:** "He can't move or speak, but he hears everything. He closes his eyes once for yes, and blinks lots of times for no."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "Grandfather, are you glad the Count has come?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Noirtier's face: his eyes closing, slowly, once.

> **VALENTINE:** "That's yes. He doesn't say yes to many people. He and Papa haven't agreed about anything since before I was born."

**Why you turn the page:** the old man is glad to see the Count, and the Count
has come to tell him something.

## Page 11 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All five strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. The caption is tail-free.

### C2 — The eyes do not land
Noirtier must own the page: an utterly still old man in a wheeled armchair whose eyes alone are alive, fixed on the Count. The last panel must show his eyes closing once, so the reader sees the yes Valentine speaks. Eyes open in that panel, or a face that moves, fails the turn.

### C3 — Noirtier never speaks
Every balloon belongs to Valentine and hangs from her mouth. Any balloon or tail from Noirtier, or "That's yes…" readable as his, is blocking. The Count has no balloon.

### C4 — Identity
Noirtier: gaunt, long white hair, white cavalry moustache, forest-green coat. Valentine: young, heart-shaped face, light chestnut low knot, dove-grey with a pale-blue ribbon. The Count: tall black figure in the doorway, clean-shaven.
