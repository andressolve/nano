# Exact script — pages 31–40

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 31 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** an empty upper room in the house next door to Villefort's,
night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A bare dusty room; the Count holds a lantern; a
narrow doorway has been cut through the wall. Through it, framed by the back
boards of a tall wardrobe that stands open a crack on the far side, a sliver of
a white room beyond.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the bare wall)*
> "The house next to Villefort's had stood empty for a year. By the next evening it was the Count's.
>
> That night, workmen who were paid not to talk cut a narrow door through the wall, into the back of the tall wardrobe in Valentine's room.
>
> Then the Count sent them home, put out his lantern, and sat down in the dark to wait.
>
> He had bet that she would wait; she had not waited a week. She would not try the chocolate twice; next time she would come at night, to the glass by the bed. He did not warn Valentine: she would never believe it of her stepmother until she saw it with her own eyes. He had waited fourteen years in the Château d'If; he could wait three nights in a wall."

**Why you turn the page:** he is inside the wall, and someone is coming.

## PAGE 32 · *spectacle*

**Location:** Valentine's room, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Seen from inside the dark wardrobe, through the
crack of its door: a white moonlit room, Valentine asleep in the narrow bed,
and Madame de Villefort in a violet dressing-gown, a shaded candle in one hand,
tipping red drops from the small cut-crystal vial into the glass of water on
the night table.

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Inside the wardrobe, in the black: the Count's eye at the
crack, and his hand flat on the door.

**Why you turn the page:** he has caught the poisoner in the act — with his
own vial.

## PAGE 33 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Valentine's room, minutes later.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The wardrobe door open; the Count bending over the bed with
one hand over Valentine's mouth as she wakes in fright.

> **COUNT:** "Don't cry out, Valentine. It's the Count of Monte Cristo. Maximilien's friend."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** The Count holding the glass of water up to the
candle; thin red threads drift through it.

> **COUNT:** "Look at your glass. Somebody poured red drops into it five minutes ago — the same drops that killed your grandmother."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "Who would do that?"

**Panel 3 — 18%.** Valentine sitting up, staring at him.

> **COUNT:** "Your stepmother. I was standing in your wardrobe, and I watched her do it."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "No. She's been so kind to me —"
>
> **COUNT:** "She will be kind to you every night until you are dead."

**Panel 4 — 17%.** Valentine alone, gripping the bedclothes, chin up; then
the Count, quiet and hard.

> **VALENTINE:** "Then I'll tell my father in the morning. He will have to look at me then."
>
> **COUNT:** "Two people have been poisoned in your father's house, and he hasn't called the police. Ask yourself why."

**Why you turn the page:** Valentine knows, and even her father will not save
her.

## PAGE 34 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Valentine's room.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Valentine clutching the bedclothes, white, glancing at the
open wardrobe.

> **VALENTINE:** "Then take me away now, through your wardrobe."
>
> **COUNT:** "If you vanish, your father will turn Paris upside down and bring you home to her. Nobody hunts for a girl who is dead."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The Count's open palm: a small gold box, open,
holding one white pastille. Above it his face in the candlelight — and the
left corner of his mouth lifting, very slightly.

> **COUNT:** "So you will die. Swallow this, and by morning the whole house will believe it."
>
> **COUNT:** "Your father will bury you, the way he buries everything. And I will bring you out."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "And Maximilien? Will you tell him I'm alive?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Maximilien trusts me. Do you?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Valentine puts the pastille in her mouth, her eyes on his.

> **VALENTINE:** "He does. So I will."

**Why you turn the page:** she has swallowed it, and in the morning she will
be dead.

## PAGE 35 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** Valentine's room, morning.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Grey morning light. Valentine lies white and still
on the bed, hands folded, a white sheet drawn to her breast. At the foot,
Villefort stands rigid with one hand over his eyes. In the doorway, Madame de
Villefort bows her head. And wheeled to the bedside, Noirtier, his eyes wide
and wet and fixed on Valentine's face.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper band, over the grey wall)*
> "In the morning they found Valentine cold in her bed, and the house believed what it saw.
>
> Villefort stood at the foot of the bed and did not weep. Madame de Villefort wept very prettily in the doorway.
>
> Noirtier made them carry him up to her side, and would not let them carry him away. He did not take his eyes off her face, and nobody in that house could tell what they were saying."

**Why you turn the page:** everyone believes Valentine is dead — her
grandfather too — and his eyes are saying something.

## PAGE 36 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room, afternoon.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Villefort alone with his father, in black, exhausted.

> **VILLEFORT:** "They tell me you have stared at Valentine's empty stool since they wheeled you back, Father. She is dead. What is there to say?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Noirtier's eyes blazing; Villefort, unwilling, at
a little table with a pencil, reciting the alphabet; Noirtier's eyes closing
once.

> **VILLEFORT:** "You want to spell something. Very well. A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H —"
>
> **VILLEFORT:** "H."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** A scrap of paper under Villefort's pencil, the letters
written large, one by one.

> **CAPTION** *(beside the paper)*
> "Letter by letter, it took a quarter of an hour."

> **PRINTED NOTE** *(on the paper, in Villefort's hand)*
> "H É L O Ï S E"

**Panel 4 — 20%.** Villefort, grey, looking up from the word at his father;
Noirtier's eyes closing, slowly, once.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Héloïse? My wife?"
>
> **VILLEFORT:** "Yes."
>
> **VILLEFORT:** "I think I have known it since the night her grandmother died."

**Why you turn the page:** Villefort can no longer pretend not to know.

## PAGE 37 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Père-Lachaise cemetery, the Villefort tomb, rain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Rain on black trees and white stone; the iron
door of the family vault closing; mourners under umbrellas in dark greys and
browns. Villefort in black at the front. At the very door of the tomb,
bareheaded in the rain in his blue uniform, Maximilien. At the back of the
crowd, the Count.

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Maximilien, turned away from the tomb, facing the Count at
the back of the crowd, rain running down his face.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "You gave me your word. You said she would not die."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The Count, rain on his face, saying none of the things he
could say.

> **COUNT:** "Come away, Maximilien. The whole house is watching your face."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "You promised me."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count is letting Maximilien believe she is
dead.

## PAGE 38 · *spectacle*

**Location:** inside the Villefort tomb, Père-Lachaise, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Night; the rain has stopped. The iron door of the
vault stands open. Inside, by the light of one lantern on the stone, the Count
lifts Valentine — white, limp, in a white dress — out of her open coffin in his
arms.

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The cemetery path between wet black trees: the Count
carrying her toward the small warm lamp of a waiting carriage.

**Why you turn the page:** he has her — but she has not woken.

## PAGE 39 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** an upstairs room in the Count's house, Champs-Élysées.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Candlelight. Valentine asleep in a white bed,
very pale, her light chestnut hair loose on the pillow. Haydée in crimson and
gold sits beside her with her sleeves pushed back, a cloth in her hand. In the
doorway, black, the Count.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper band, over the dark wall)*
> "The draught should have worn off the morning after he carried her out. It did not. Valentine's body had already fought one poison that month, and it had nothing left to wake with.
>
> Haydée sat by her day and night. “Why don't you tell Maximilien she's alive?” she asked on the second night.
>
> “Because she may never wake,” said the Count. “I won't give him a hope I may have to take back.”
>
> On the third night she found him at the window, smiling, and put her hand on his sleeve. He did not move it away. The trial was in the morning."

**Why you turn the page:** Valentine may not wake, and the Count is smiling
about tomorrow.

## PAGE 40 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a prison cell, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A stone cell, a barred window, one lantern.
Benedetto in a grey prison smock lounges on the bench as if it were a sofa,
grinning. The Count stands over him, black.

> **BENEDETTO:** "My lord! Come to watch them cut my head off? The trial's in the morning."
>
> **COUNT:** "I've come to keep my promise. You wanted your father's name."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** The Count bending down to him.

> **COUNT:** "Your father is Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney — the man who will stand up tomorrow and ask for your head."
>
> **COUNT:** "You were born at midnight on the twenty-seventh of September, 1817. Learn it. A good story needs a date."

**Panel 3 — 28%.** Benedetto's grin spreading, slow and enormous.

> **BENEDETTO:** "Him? The one who buried me?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Say my name in that court, and you're one more liar. Say his, and you'll be the most famous man in France."
>
> **BENEDETTO:** "My lord, I wouldn't miss it."

**Why you turn the page:** tomorrow the son will name the father, in front of
everyone.
