## Page intent

## PAGE 12

Dramatic. Noirtier's room. The Count asks Valentine to leave them alone, and she goes out promising to wait outside. He draws a chair knee to knee with the old man and, voice low, tells him about a sailor who carried a letter from Elba addressed to him in 1815, a letter that never came. Noirtier's eyes close once: he remembers. The Count speaks that yes himself. Then, closer still, the left corner of his mouth lifting, he says it: Villefort read the letter, burned it, and buried the sailor in the Château d'If for fourteen years. He was the sailor. The dominant turn: the Count tells Noirtier who burned the letter and that he was the sailor. The confession in the last panel owns the page; Valentine's exit stays small at the top. The reader turns because the one man who cannot repeat it now knows exactly who he is.

>

>
> **COUNT:** "I was the sailor."

## Numbered blocking criteria

### C1 — Transcription fails
All eight strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The confession does not land
The last panel must own the page: the Count's face close to the old man's, the left corner of his mouth lifting, ending on "I was the sailor." A confession that reads as casual talk across a room fails.

### C3 — Noirtier never speaks
"Yes." is the Count's balloon beside the Count's mouth, with Noirtier's eyes closed once beside it. A balloon or tail from Noirtier is blocking. "I'll be just outside, Grandfather." hangs from Valentine at the door.

### C4 — Identity
The Count: clean-shaven, pale, swept-back black hair, high cheekbones, black evening dress. Noirtier: white mane, white moustache, forest-green coat, armchair, still. Valentine: young, dove-grey, light chestnut hair. A letter or paper drawn is nonblocking unless it carries words.
