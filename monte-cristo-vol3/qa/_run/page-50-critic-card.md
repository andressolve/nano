## Page intent

## PAGE 50

Dramatic page. A quiet establishing beat: Madame de Villefort lies fallen with dignity on the top step, eyes closed, while the Count, coming up the stair, stoops and lifts his own empty vial from where it fell, his eyes on her face. Down the corridor Villefort kneels in his son's open doorway and asks who is there. The Count answers that he has come to tell him who he is, and then, face full in the frame with every mask off, says it: Remember the Château d'If; I am Edmond Dantès. Villefort rises, seizes him by the coat and drags him toward the door to see what he has done. The dominant turn: over Héloïse's body and at his son's door, the Count tells Villefort he is Edmond Dantès. That face and that sentence own the page. The boy and the room beyond the door stay unseen.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 50 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the top of the white stair and the upstairs corridor, outside
the boy's door.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The top of the white marble stair. Madame de Villefort lies
fallen on the top step in her violet silk, eyes closed, with dignity, one hand
still on the iron rail and the other hanging open over the edge of the step.
The Count, black, is coming up the stair; a few steps below her he has stooped
and lifted the empty cut-crystal vial from the step where it fell — his own,
come back to him — and his eyes are on her face above him.

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Down the corridor: Villefort on his knees in the open
doorway with his back to us, his head down. The Count at the corridor's end
behind him.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Who's there?"
>
> **COUNT:** "You dug under my tree at two in the morning, monsieur, and never once asked who I was. I've come to tell you."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 40%.** The Count's face, full in the frame, every mask
off.

> **COUNT:** "Remember the Château d'If. Remember a sailor named Edmond Dantès, who carried your father a letter."
>
> **COUNT:** "I am Edmond Dantès."

**Panel 4 — 20%.** Villefort up off his knees, seizing the Count by the coat
with both hands, dragging him toward the open door.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Dantès. Then come and look. Come and see what you have done!"

**Why you turn the page:** Villefort is dragging him in to see.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
Any of the 5 strings is missing, altered, duplicated, reordered, unreadable from the 600 × 900 proof (a string that reads is never measured), or not owned by the right mouth by tail and placement; extra text appears. Villefort owns "Who's there?" and "Dantès. Then come and look…"; the Count owns the other three. Nonblocking, the words and their order being what must match: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, the run length of a repeated letter. A line directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The Count's face and "I am Edmond Dantès." must plainly own the page as a dramatic page; the stair panel is a quiet opening. Blocking if the confession is buried under the action panels.

### C3 — The Count and Villefort collide
Villefort in his black robe with white bands and grey hair must stay distinct from the Count in loose black with swept-back black hair. Blocking if the reader cannot tell who confesses and who drags whom.

### C4 — Death and the boy
Madame de Villefort lies with dignity, eyes closed, and has no balloon. The boy is never drawn; the doorway stays dark. Any glimpse of the boy, or a balloon or tail from her, is blocking.

### C5 — Object counts
Exactly one vial, empty, in the Count's hand; exactly one tin hussar. A second vial or a full red vial is blocking.

### C6 — Generation integrity
A duplicated named figure, broken focal hands in the seizing panel, or a balloon collision hiding a line is blocking.
