## PAGE 2

Dramatic. In the overgrown garden at Auteuil, under the great plane tree, the Count holds his lantern low over a circle of bare earth while Haydée, arms folded against the cold, watches him rather than the ground. A caption tells the reader who Haydée is and why she alone knows his secret. ## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 2 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the garden of the house at Auteuil, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The walled garden, overgrown; the great plane
tree; under it a circle of bare earth where nothing grows. The Count holds the
lantern low over the bare ground. HAYDÉE stands beside him in crimson and
gold, her black braid over her shoulder, arms folded against the cold, looking
at him and not at the ground.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Only Haydée knew who the Count really was. Fernand had betrayed her father to his death and sold her as a slave; the Count freed her and told her everything."

> **HAYDÉE:** "You bought a whole house to stand in its garden in the dark?"
>
> **COUNT:** "I bought it for this tree. Look at the ground."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** Close: the lantern's gold on the bare earth; the Count's
right hand, still, a finger pointing down.

> **COUNT:** "Twenty-one years ago, at midnight, a man dug a hole here and put a box in it."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "What was in the box?"

**Panel 3 — 28%.** The Count's face lit from below by the lantern, the left
corner of his mouth lifting. He is enjoying this.

> **COUNT:** "A baby, born that night in this house. The man thought it was dead. He didn't stop to listen."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "And you're smiling. Who was he?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Gérard de Villefort."

**Why you turn the page:** a baby went into this ground — and the Count is
smiling.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The caption and all seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The smile does not land
The Count must visibly enjoy naming Villefort: face lit from below, the left corner of his mouth lifting. A grim or neutral face loses the turn and is blocking.

### C3 — Questions and answers misowned
Haydée asks three questions; the Count answers each. "What was in the box?" and "And you're smiling. Who was he?" must hang from Haydée's mouth; "Gérard de Villefort." from the Count's. A question tailed to the Count or the name tailed to Haydée is blocking.

### C4 — Ground already dug
The earth under the tree is bare and undisturbed. A hole, a box, a baby or bones drawn on the page is blocking.

### C5 — Identity
The Count pale and clean-shaven with swept-back black hair, in black; Haydée with black braid, olive-gold skin, crimson and gold. Either unrecognisable, or a third person present, is blocking.
