## Page intent

## PAGE 1

Illustrated prose. The book opens at night outside the high garden wall of a dark, shuttered house at Auteuil. The Count of Monte Cristo stands alone at the iron gate, lantern held low, looking up at one huge old plane tree as if it were a face he knew. The title is printed over the night sky; one prose field over the dark of the lane tells the reader who he is, what was done to him, and that the second man on his list is Villefort, the King's Attorney, the one who knew Edmond was innocent and buried him anyway. The dominant turn: the Count has come back to his list, and the second name is Villefort. The reader turns because he has come to this dark house for that man. The house details, the gate's ironwork and the lane stay subordinate to the lone figure and the tree.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 1 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** outside the garden wall of a dark house in Auteuil, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Night-blue. A high old stone wall and an iron
gate; beyond it, black against the sky, the shuttered house and the pale
spreading crown of one huge old plane tree. At the gate, tall and black,
lantern held low, the COUNT OF MONTE CRISTO looks up at the tree as if it were
a face he knew. His shadow runs long across the lane.

> **TITLE CARD** *(top, over the night sky)*
> "THE COUNT OF MONTE CRISTO"
>
> "VOLUME III · THE KING'S ATTORNEY"

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(lower third, over the dark of the lane)*
> "Twenty-three years ago, three men sent a young sailor named Edmond Dantès to the prison of the Château d'If. Fourteen years later he came out as the Count of Monte Cristo, with a fortune and a list.
>
> The first man on the list, Fernand, who had made himself the Count de Morcerf, was already dead. The second was Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney: the only one of the three who had known Edmond was innocent, and had buried him anyway."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count has come to a dark house at night for
the second man on his list.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both title lines and both prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. The title lines are printed title text over the night sky, not a balloon; the paragraphs belong to one prose field. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The arrival does not land
One image owns the page: a lone tall black figure at the gate of a dark house at night, looking up at one huge tree. If he reads as a passer-by, the tree is lost, or the page reads as a dramatic panel page rather than illustrated prose, it is blocking.

### C3 — Prose field over the figure
The prose field sits over plain dark ground or sky. A field covering the Count's face, his hands or the lantern is blocking; a field that crosses the lane or his shadow is not.

### C4 — The Count not himself
A pale, clean-shaven man with swept-back black hair, in black, tall and still. A second figure anywhere, or a face that reads as a grey-haired older man, is blocking.
