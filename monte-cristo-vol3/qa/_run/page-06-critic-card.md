## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Assize court in the Palais de Justice, day.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A dark oak courtroom in grey winter light.
VILLEFORT — fifty-three, long narrow face, iron-grey hair in a hard side part,
black magistrate's robe with white bands — stands at the prosecutor's desk with
one arm out toward the dock, where a thin ragged man in brown hangs his head.
Every face in the court is on Villefort. High in the public gallery, the Count,
black, watches.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Villefort had not lost a case in eleven years."

> **VILLEFORT:** "The law cannot weigh a man's hunger, gentlemen. It weighs what he did. I ask for twenty years in the galleys."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Later. The court empty. Villefort at his desk tying a
bundle of papers with red tape; the Count coming down the gallery stair toward
him.

> **VILLEFORT:** "The Count of Monte Cristo. We met at the Count de Morcerf's — before his unhappy end. You enjoy trials, monsieur?"
>
> **COUNT:** "I enjoy watching a man get what he deserves."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The two of them face to face across the desk, close: the
Count's swept-back black hair and pallor; Villefort's grey side part, high
white bands, thin cold smile.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Then you've come to the right court. I have never once let a guilty man go free."
>
> **COUNT:** "No. I don't imagine you have."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count is close enough to touch the man who
buried him, and Villefort has no idea who he is.

### C1 — Transcription fails
The caption and five lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — Villefort's power does not land
One image owns the page: Villefort performing in the court, every face on him, with the Count watching from above. If the Count cannot be found in the gallery, or the prosecutor is not the centre, it is blocking.

### C3 — Two men confused
This is the book's riskiest pair. Villefort: narrow high-browed face, iron-grey side part, black robe with white bands. The Count: swept-back black hair, pallor, high cheekbones, loose black. If the reader cannot tell which man speaks in the face-to-face panel, it is blocking.

### C4 — Exchange misowned
Villefort's three lines hang from his mouth, including "The Count of Monte Cristo…"; "I enjoy watching…" and "No. I don't imagine you have." from the Count's. A reversed owner is blocking.

### C5 — Extras in reserved colour
Prisoner and crowd in brown, grey, buff, dark green or navy; judges in red. An extra in unrelieved black who could be read as the Count is blocking.
