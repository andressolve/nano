# Visual-continuity gate — pages 51–56

APPROVED

The run remains inside the Velvet Cinema register. Across the six pages, the
matte gouache/opaque-watercolor treatment, visible angular brushwork, selective
facial edges, substantial black masses, and tactile cloth, stone, wood, snow,
and metal all read as one book. Night-blue exteriors, cold grey-blue Villefort
interiors, dark green in Noirtier's room, and the Count's black-and-gold rooms
follow a coherent lighting logic rather than looking like unrelated palettes.

- Page 51 keeps Villefort's long, aged face, iron-grey side part, court robe and
  white bands distinct from the Count's black swept wave, pallor, high
  cheekbones, hollow temples, all-black column and gloves. The cold marble and
  grey-blue room stay on lock. The mounted tin hussar remains recognizable and
  consequential.
- Pages 52–53 preserve the ruined Villefort and the Count through costume
  changes and scale: Villefort's fallen hair and open shirt do not erase his
  long face or age, while the Count remains tall, still, pale, black-haired and
  fully black-clad. The colder snow scenes retain the same black level and
  painted surface as the interiors.
- Page 54 holds Noirtier exactly through the broad leonine face, white mane and
  cavalry moustache, forest-green coat, wheeled chair and tartan rug. The
  Count's profile remains distinct beside him, and the room's dark-green
  register is intact.
- Page 55 keeps Maximilien's square sun-brown face, dark moustache, broad build
  and plain brown civilian state. The dark-wood pistol case with brass corners

  warmer than its plate, but its dark field, gold/brass accents and glossy-black
  furniture still read as the Count's house; recognition and story state are
  unaffected.
- Page 56 keeps Valentine, Haydée and the Count mutually unmistakable:
  Valentine's fair heart-shaped sleeping face and loose chestnut hair,
  Haydée's olive-gold complexion, long black hair and crimson-gold Epirote
  dress, and the Count's black columnar silhouette all hold. White bedding and
  candlelight make the bedroom lighter and creamier than its empty-room plate,
  but the dark furniture and gold trim preserve the house language. The same
  red-and-gold rider on a white rearing horse carries the tin-hussar continuity
  from page 51.

Lettering weight and the warm-white, black-outlined balloon shapes are stable
throughout. The serif narration on pages 53 and 56 is a consistent narration
system, not lettering drift. No page in 51–56 looks as if it belongs to a
different book, and no character, recurring setting, or consequential object
