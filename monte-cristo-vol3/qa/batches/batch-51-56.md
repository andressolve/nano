## Uninterrupted read

The Count brings Villefort back from collapse with a measured drop, tells him
the boy is dead, and picks up the tin hussar after Villefort's mind breaks.
Villefort tears into his frozen garden looking for the son he once buried,
while the Count watches the punishment he has made. Outside the house, the
Count tries and fails to count this victory as “Two”: the hussar and the
memory of the red bottle force him to reckon with the innocent lives caught
inside his revenge.

He returns to Noirtier, reports the destruction in the garden, and reveals
that Valentine is alive but may not wake. Noirtier's answers turn the Count's
old mandate back on him: the man who once told him not to stop now wishes he
had. In the Count's study, Maximilien arrives prepared to die; the Count wins
one month from him. At Valentine's bedside, Haydée keeps vigil while the
Count remains at the threshold with the hussar. On the fourth night,
Valentine's hand closes around Haydée's, giving the run its first fragile
movement back toward life.

## Gate assessment

The material story matches the script across all six pages. Text and speaker
attribution remain exact and readable through the transitions. Villefort,
the Count, Noirtier, Maximilien, Haydée, and Valentine retain clear roles and
focal relationships. The empty vial, discarded robe, tin hussar, pistol case,
and Valentine's hand all preserve their consequential meaning across their
respective turns; the moves from corridor to garden to street, back into the
house, then to the Count's study and upstairs room are legible.

The pacing has sufficient variation: two escalating dramatic pages give way
to a still illustrated-prose reckoning, the Noirtier exchange supplies the
moral aftermath, the Maximilien bargain redirects the danger forward, and
the final quiet tableau lands on physical evidence rather than summary. No
stretch reads as compressed synopsis, no sequence becomes an undifferentiated
wall of dramatic scenes, and no consequential beat is left without readable
aftermath. The page turns preserve the Count's focal line from victory,
through doubt and accountability, to a provisional act of hope.

No material sequence, continuity, attribution, pacing, or page-turn failure
justifies a complete-page redraw.
