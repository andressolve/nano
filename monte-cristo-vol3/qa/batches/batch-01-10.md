# Sequence gate — pages 1–10

## Uninterrupted read

The Count returns at night to the Auteuil house connected to Gérard de
Villefort, the second surviving target of his revenge. In the garden he tells
Haydée that Villefort buried a newborn beneath the plane tree twenty-one years
earlier. The child was rescued alive by a hidden enemy, grew into a thief and
murderer, and is Villefort's illegitimate son. The Count intends to give him
back to his father.

The Count finds the young man, Benedetto, in a cellar tavern. By proving that
he knows both Benedetto's crimes and his secret birth, he gains control of him
and remakes him as Prince Andrea Cavalcanti. Andrea's immediate assignment is
to dine with the King's Attorney, although Benedetto does not yet know that
Villefort is his father.

Villefort is then shown in court demanding twenty years in the galleys for a
hungry man and boasting afterward to the Count that he has never let a guilty
man go free. The exchange establishes his merciless public certainty while the
Count silently holds the knowledge of the innocent Edmond and the buried baby
against him.

Outside the Count's gate, Madame de Villefort and her little son arrive in a
runaway carriage. The Count stops the horses and revives the unconscious boy
with one drop from a red vial. He tells Madame de Villefort that ten drops can
stop a heart without leaving a cause a doctor could identify, then gives her
the vial. Haydée confronts him afterward: the horses came from his stable, he
had engineered their route, and he knowingly armed a woman who wanted the
poison. He says the rescue has bought him entry to Villefort's house and leaves
the victim of her future action as Villefort's problem.

On Thursday the Count is received by the grateful family. Villefort gives his
warmth to his young son while ignoring Valentine, the daughter of his first
wife Renée, whose engagement he was celebrating on the night he condemned
Edmond. When Villefort tries to discourage contact with his paralyzed father,
the Count asks to see him at once.

## Script comparison and batch judgment

The visible pages preserve the script's material story and exact transition
logic. Dialogue and prose remain correctly attributed. Haydée, Benedetto/
Andrea, Villefort, Madame de Villefort, her son, and Valentine are introduced
or carried with stable named identity. The buried box, Andrea card and signet,
toy soldier, red vial, carriage, Auteuil gate, and the two households retain
their consequential meaning across page turns. The Count remains the focal
intelligence throughout, while Haydée supplies the intended moral resistance
without displacing him.

The page turns escalate cleanly: target to buried child, child to living
murderer, murderer to fabricated prince, prince to prosecutor, prosecutor to
engineered rescue, rescue to poison, and poison to household access. No turn
breaks causality or withholds information needed to understand the next page.

The pacing does not read as compressed summary. The prose pages frame the
movement at pages 1 and 10, while garden disclosure, criminal negotiation,
courtroom irony, physical spectacle, intimate revival, and moral aftermath
provide distinct page modes between them. Page 9 gives the carriage spectacle
its necessary aftermath and makes the Count's agency and intent explicit
before the move into Villefort's home.
