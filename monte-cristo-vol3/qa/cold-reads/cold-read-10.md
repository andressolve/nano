## Retell of the story so far

Twenty-three years ago, three men knowingly sent the innocent sailor Edmond Dantès to the Château d'If. After fourteen years he escaped, became the wealthy Count of Monte Cristo, and began taking revenge. Fernand, the first man on his list, is already dead. The Count has now turned to Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney: the one conspirator who knew Edmond was innocent and buried the truth.

At a house in Auteuil, the Count tells Haydée about another secret Villefort buried. On the night a baby was born there, Villefort put the child in a box and buried it, believing it dead. A hidden witness who hated Villefort dug up the box as soon as Villefort left and found the baby alive. Years later that man confessed the story to the Abbé Busoni, who was actually the Count in disguise. The child survived and grew into a twenty-one-year-old thief, liar, and murderer. He is Villefort's illegitimate son, born to a woman who was not his wife, and Villefort does not know he is alive. The Count intends to return the son to his father.

The Count finds the son, Benedetto, in a rough tavern. By revealing knowledge of Benedetto's escape from the Toulon galleys, a guard's suspicious death, and his birth in a garden, the Count proves that he knows exactly who Benedetto is. He offers him a rich father and remakes him as Prince Andrea Cavalcanti, an Italian aristocrat with clothes, money, a carriage, and instructions to dine with the King's Attorney.

Villefort, meanwhile, is shown as a merciless and self-righteous prosecutor who has not lost a case in eleven years. He asks that a starving man be sent to the galleys for twenty years and tells the Count that he has never let a guilty man go free. The Count's response makes the irony unmistakable: Villefort once knowingly imprisoned an innocent man.

The Count then engineers his own entrance into Villefort's household. He acquires the horses used by Villefort's wife, has them left only half broken, and arranges for their runaway carriage to pass his gate while he is waiting in the road. He stops the horses and rescues Madame de Villefort and her young son. The boy appears lifeless, but the Count revives him with one drop from a red bottle. He explains that a larger dose would stop a heart beyond any doctor's ability to explain, then deliberately leaves the bottle with Madame de Villefort after recognizing that she wants it.

Haydée understands that the Count staged the danger while ensuring the boy would survive. The rescue makes Madame de Villefort indebted to him and gets him invited into the family home. The Count also leaves the lethal medicine in her hands and treats whatever she chooses to do with it as Villefort's problem.

On Thursday, Villefort receives the Count and thanks him for saving his wife and son. Madame de Villefort is effusively grateful. Valentine, Villefort's daughter by his dead first wife, Renée, stands neglected while Villefort keeps a hand on his son's head and never looks at her. When Villefort mentions that his own father lives helpless and unable to speak at the back of the house, the Count immediately asks to see him.

## Cast

- **The Count of Monte Cristo / Edmond Dantès / the Abbé Busoni:** A tall, lean, severe middle-aged man with swept-back dark hair, a gaunt face, and black formal clothes, gloves, and cloak. He is the wrongfully imprisoned sailor turned wealthy avenger. Busoni was one of his disguises. He controls the present plot and is using secrets, money, and carefully staged events against Villefort.

- **Haydée:** A young woman with dark hair, olive-toned skin, and a red-and-gold dress. Fernand betrayed her father and sold her into slavery; the Count freed her. She is the only person said to know who the Count really is, and she serves as his trusted confidante and moral questioner.

- **Gérard de Villefort:** An older, pale, sharply featured man with gray hair, dressed first in black prosecutor's robes and later in a formal black suit. He is the King's Attorney, an unyielding prosecutor, one of the men responsible for Edmond's imprisonment, and the secret father of Benedetto.

- **Benedetto / Prince Andrea Cavalcanti:** A slim young man with tousled brown hair, a ragged brown coat, scarf, and a sly, amused manner. He is an escaped galley prisoner and violent criminal, as well as Villefort's presumed-dead illegitimate son. The Count equips him with a false princely identity and sends him toward Villefort.

- **Madame de Villefort:** A fair-skinned blonde woman with curled hair and a purple dress. She is Villefort's current wife and the mother of his young son. The Count saves them, wins her gratitude, and gives her access to a substance that can either revive or kill depending on the dose.

- **Villefort's young son:** A small blond, curly-haired boy dressed in purple and carrying a toy soldier. He is used in the staged rescue but, according to the Count, was never beyond the Count's ability to save. Villefort openly favors him.

- **Valentine:** A young woman with light brown hair and a plain gray dress, standing apart from the rest of the household. She is Villefort's daughter by his first wife, Renée, and is visibly ignored by her father.

- **Renée:** Villefort's dead first wife and Valentine's mother. She is identified in narration but does not appear as a present character.

- **Villefort's father:** An unseen man living at the rear of Villefort's house, unable to move or speak. The Count's urgent wish to see him marks him as important.

- **The baby's rescuer:** An unnamed man who hated Villefort, witnessed the burial from behind a wall, rescued the living baby, and later confessed the event to the Count while the Count was disguised as Busoni.

## Causal chain

2. Edmond escapes, becomes the Count, and targets Villefort for revenge.
3. The Count learns that Villefort buried an illegitimate newborn and that a witness secretly rescued it.
4. That child grows up to be the criminal Benedetto.
5. The Count finds Benedetto, invents the identity of Prince Andrea Cavalcanti for him, and directs him toward Villefort.
6. The Count also stages a runaway-carriage rescue involving Villefort's wife and favored son.
7. Saving them wins the family's gratitude and an invitation into Villefort's home.
8. The rescue lets the Count place a potentially lethal medicine in Madame de Villefort's hands after seeing that she desires it.
9. Inside the house, the Count sees Villefort's favoritism toward his son and neglect of Valentine.
10. Villefort reveals that his immobile, speechless father lives in the house, and the Count asks to see him immediately.

## What I expect next

I expect the Count to meet Villefort's incapacitated father and discover either an ally, a witness, or another piece of the family's buried history. In parallel, Andrea Cavalcanti will enter Villefort's social circle without Villefort recognizing him as the son he tried to kill. Madame de Villefort will likely use the red medicine as a poison, probably against someone whose removal benefits her favored son; Valentine appears especially endangered because she is neglected, belongs to the first marriage, and stands outside the mother-and-son unit. The Count's revenge therefore seems ready to attack Villefort from three directions at once: the resurrected son, the poison inside his household, and the silent father at the back of the house.
