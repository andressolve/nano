# Script-blind cold read — pages 1–40

## Verdict

**APPROVED**

The story lands from the pages alone. I could follow the identities, family relationships, revenge machinery, poison plot, and apparent-death rescue without outside documentation. I did not misidentify any character, and I found no page-level confusion that caused material reader harm.

## Retell of the story so far

Twenty-three years ago, three men knowingly sent the innocent young sailor Edmond Dantès to the Château d'If. Fourteen years later he emerged as the fabulously wealthy Count of Monte Cristo. Fernand, the first betrayer, is already dead. The Count has now come for Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney, while leaving the third man, Danglars, for later.

The Count buys a house in Auteuil because of what happened beneath its great tree. Years ago, Villefort buried a box there containing a newborn child he believed dead. Another man saw him leave, dug up the box, and found the baby breathing. The Count learned the confession while disguised as the Abbé Busoni. The child grew into Benedetto, now twenty-one and a criminal. The Count finds him, gives him money and the invented identity of the Italian prince Andrea Cavalcanti, and prepares to place him directly in Villefort's path.

The Count also engineers a runaway-carriage accident involving Villefort's wife, Héloïse, and their little son. He stops the horses and revives the boy with one drop from a red bottle. He then tells Héloïse that a larger dose would stop a heart without leaving a trace and gives her the bottle. The rescue wins him access and gratitude inside Villefort's home, but it also puts a deadly idea and the means to act on it into Héloïse's hands.

At the Villefort house, the Count meets Valentine, Villefort's gentle daughter by his first wife, and Noirtier, Villefort's paralyzed father. Noirtier recognizes the Count as the sailor ruined by a letter Noirtier once sent from Elba. Communicating with his eyes, he accepts the Count's intention to destroy Villefort but makes him promise that Valentine will be spared.

The Count then visits Maximilien Morrel. Maximilien's father once believed that a mysterious benefactor sent by God had saved his life and business; that benefactor was Edmond, though the Morrels never learned his name. Maximilien confides that he loves Valentine, but the lovers meet secretly through an iron gate because Villefort would reject him and Valentine's maternal grandmother intends to marry her to someone else. This gives the Count a second, deeply personal reason to protect Valentine.

At a dinner in the Auteuil house, the Count introduces Andrea to Villefort and recounts, as if it were an old rumor, the story of the baby born there and buried before morning. Villefort is visibly shaken. He later climbs back over the wall, digs beneath the tree, and finds the grave empty. He now knows the secret he thought buried has survived, though he does not yet know that Andrea is his son.

Meanwhile, Héloïse begins clearing the path for her own son, Édouard, to inherit. Valentine's grandmother arrives with prospective husbands and a fortune. Héloïse poisons the old woman's milk in front of Noirtier, the one witness who cannot speak. The death makes Valentine rich. Villefort realizes someone in the house is poisoning people, and Noirtier tells him with his eyes that Valentine is innocent, but Villefort refuses to expose the murderer because scandal would destroy his family name. Héloïse then speaks openly to the Count about how Valentine's death would eventually send the fortune to her husband and then to her boy. The Count warns her to be careful but lets the machinery continue. Haydée challenges him: an innocent old woman has died, and the Count is watching Villefort suffer with his eyes open.

The Count anonymously exposes Andrea as Benedetto, the escaped convict who killed a guard. Benedetto is arrested, and Villefort declares that he will personally prosecute the false prince and demand his head, still unaware that he is condemning his own abandoned son.

Héloïse next sends poisoned chocolate to Valentine. Valentine collapses but survives because Noirtier has secretly been giving her a small daily dose of the same medicine, building her resistance. Maximilien rushes to the Count. The Count identifies a poisoner in the house and promises that Valentine will not die.

The Count secretly buys the empty house next door, cuts a hidden passage through the wall into Valentine's wardrobe, and waits. He watches Héloïse poison Valentine's nighttime glass. When Valentine wakes, he shows her the red drops and tells her that her stepmother killed her grandmother and now means to kill her. Rather than simply removing Valentine, which would expose Héloïse and provoke a search, he gives Valentine a pill that makes her appear dead. She trusts him because Maximilien does.

The household finds Valentine cold in bed and believes her dead. Noirtier knows more than he can say and stares at her face. Afterward, he slowly spells Héloïse's name for Villefort, confirming that Villefort's wife is the poisoner. At Valentine's tomb, the grieving Maximilien accuses the Count of breaking his promise. The Count cannot yet reveal the deception, because he must let the entire Villefort household believe Valentine is dead. Later he removes her living body from the tomb and carries her to his own house. The draught should wear off by morning, but her already weakened body does not wake. Haydée tends her while the Count waits anxiously.

On the night before Benedetto's trial, the Count visits him in prison. He finally gives Benedetto the name he has always wanted: his father is Gérard de Villefort, the prosecutor who will stand in court the next day and ask for his execution. The Count tells Benedetto to announce that truth publicly, ensuring that Villefort's attempt to condemn his son will instead expose him before all France.

## Cast as read from the pages

- **The Count of Monte Cristo / Edmond Dantès / the Abbé Busoni:** A tall, lean, pale man with swept-back black hair, severe dark features, black gloves, and almost unbroken black formal dress. He is the wealthy former prisoner directing an elaborate revenge against Villefort. He is brilliant and controlled, but Haydée's objections and Valentine's failure to wake reveal growing doubt beneath that control.

- **Haydée:** A young woman with long dark hair and a warm olive complexion, usually dressed in a red robe embroidered with gold. She alone knows the Count's history, acts as his confidante and moral counterweight, and later nurses Valentine.

- **Gérard de Villefort:** A rigid, gray-haired older man with a narrow, stern face, dressed in severe black. He is the King's Attorney, one of Edmond's betrayers, Noirtier's son, Valentine's father, Héloïse's husband, and Benedetto's biological father. His public devotion to law is opposed by his lifelong willingness to bury crimes that threaten his name.

- **Noirtier:** A formidable elderly man with thick white hair and a large white moustache, generally shown in a green coat beneath a tartan blanket in his wheelchair. He cannot move or speak but communicates precisely with his eyes. He is Villefort's father, a former Napoleonic agent, Valentine's protector, and the only witness to Héloïse poisoning the grandmother. He quietly builds Valentine's resistance to the poison.

- **Héloïse, Madame de Villefort:** A beautiful blonde woman with elaborate curls, most often in a rich purple dress. She is Villefort's second wife and Édouard's mother. Fascinated by the Count's drug, she becomes the household poisoner and tries to eliminate those standing between her son and a fortune.

- **Édouard:** Héloïse's small, curly blond son, dressed in purple and frequently carrying a toy soldier. His rescue wins the Count entry into the Villefort household, and his prospective inheritance motivates his mother's murders.

- **Valentine de Villefort:** A fair, red-haired young woman in a modest pale-gray dress, later shown in a white nightgown. She is Villefort's daughter by his first wife, Noirtier's beloved granddaughter, Maximilien's secret love, and Héloïse's principal target. The Count stages her death and removes her from the tomb, but she has not yet awakened.

- **Maximilien Morrel:** A sturdy, brown-haired cavalry officer with a moustache, often in a blue-and-gold uniform. He is the son of the shipowner Edmond once secretly saved, Valentine's devoted lover, and someone the Count sincerely values. He currently believes Valentine is dead and the Count has betrayed him.

- **Benedetto / Prince Andrea Cavalcanti:** A slim young man with unruly brown hair, a sly smile, and a lounging manner, first in shabby brown clothes and later in a green velvet prince's coat. He is the infant Villefort buried, now grown into a thief, liar, murderer, and escaped convict. The Count turns him into a false aristocrat, exposes him, and finally arms him with his father's name for the trial.

- **Valentine's maternal grandmother:** An elderly, wealthy woman who arrives to arrange Valentine's marriage. Héloïse poisons her, causing her fortune to pass to Valentine and the suitors to disappear.

- **Pierre Morrel:** Seen only through the account of Maximilien's family history. He was a Marseilles shipowner whom Edmond secretly saved from bankruptcy and suicide, creating the bond that now connects the Count to Maximilien.

## Causal chain

1. Villefort knowingly sends innocent Edmond to prison and later buries his illegitimate newborn to protect his career.
2. Edmond survives, becomes the Count, learns the buried child's fate, and decides to make Villefort's hidden crimes destroy his public life.
3. The Count buys the Auteuil house, recruits the surviving child as the fake Prince Andrea, and places him socially beside Villefort.
4. The Count engineers the carriage rescue, which gives him influence over Héloïse, then teaches her how his red drug can kill invisibly and leaves it with her.
5. Noirtier recognizes Edmond and permits revenge on Villefort only after the Count promises to spare Valentine.
6. Maximilien's love for Valentine makes that promise personal to the Count as well as binding.
7. The Auteuil dinner forces Villefort to confront the empty grave while placing his unrecognized son at his table.
8. Héloïse uses the poison to kill Valentine's grandmother so the inheritance moves toward Édouard; Villefort suppresses investigation to protect his name.
9. The Count exposes Andrea as Benedetto, prompting Villefort to prosecute his own son without knowing it.
10. Héloïse poisons Valentine, but Noirtier's daily microdoses keep her alive.
11. The Count catches Héloïse's next attempt, stages Valentine's death, and removes her from the tomb so Héloïse and Villefort believe her gone while Maximilien remains unaware.
12. Noirtier identifies Héloïse to Villefort, while the Count reveals Villefort's paternity to Benedetto on the eve of trial.
13. Benedetto is now positioned to expose Villefort publicly at the very moment Villefort asks the court to execute him.

## What I expect next

I expect Benedetto's trial to become Villefort's public ruin. Villefort will confidently prosecute the supposed impostor, Benedetto will name him as the father who buried him alive, and the courtroom will turn against the prosecutor. I also expect Villefort to return home already shattered and confront Héloïse after Noirtier's accusation, with Édouard placed in immediate danger by the poisoner's desperation. The Count will have to reckon with whether his revenge has escaped his control, especially if Valentine does not wake promptly and if Maximilien's grief becomes dangerous. Valentine should eventually wake and be restored to Maximilien, but only after the Count is forced to prove that his promise meant more than his vengeance.
