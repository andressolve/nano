# Whole-book gate: The Count of Monte Cristo, Volume III, *The King's Attorney*, pages 1–56

## 1. Blind account

I read the 600 × 900 proofs once, in order, before opening anything else.

### Retell

At night in Auteuil, the Count of Monte Cristo stands at the gate of a dark house. He has bought it for the plane tree in its garden. He tells Haydée what happened there 21 years ago. Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney, buried his newborn son there in a box, alive, because the baby was born outside his marriage. A man hiding behind the wall dug the baby up. The Count heard the story as the Abbé Busoni. The baby grew up to be Benedetto: a thief, a liar, and an escaped galley convict who killed a guard.

The Count finds Benedetto in a cellar tavern. He makes him "Prince Andrea Cavalcanti" with a purse, a ring and a visiting card, and promises him a rich father. Then he meets Villefort in court and Villefort doesn't recognise him. He bolts Madame de Villefort's carriage past his own gate (the horses were ones he sold her husband), stops it with his own hands, and revives her limp son with one red drop from a vial. He gives her the vial, telling her ten drops would stop a heart. Haydée says it plainly to him on p9: he set all of this up.

Inside Villefort's house there is a cold father, a son he loves, and a daughter nobody looks at, Valentine. Villefort's father, Noirtier, can't move or speak and answers by blinking. The Count tells him that Villefort burned the Elba letter addressed to Noirtier, and that he, the Count, was the sailor. Noirtier does not want him to stop. The Count gives his word that Valentine is safe.

At the Morrels' the red purse sits under glass. Maximilien loves Valentine. Haydée asks the Count to take the bottle back. He refuses and promises Valentine will walk out alive. She answers that he said the same about the boy in the carriage.

At dinner in Auteuil, Villefort shakes his son's hand without knowing it. The Count tells a "ghost story" under the tree. At 2 a.m. Villefort digs and finds the hole empty while the Count watches from a window. Héloïse poisons Valentine's grandmother in front of Noirtier, and Valentine inherits. Villefort chooses not to learn who the poisoner is. Héloïse all but tells the Count that Valentine is next. The Count bets she will wait, and sends an anonymous letter that gets Benedetto arrested on the Opéra steps. Villefort will prosecute him himself.

Valentine drinks poisoned chocolate and survives only because Noirtier had been dosing her with his medicine. The Count admits to Noirtier that the poison is his. He waits inside a wall, watches Héloïse pour the drops, and gives Valentine a death-like draught. She is entombed. Noirtier spells HÉLOÏSE for Villefort. Maximilien accuses the Count at the tomb. The Count carries Valentine out, but she does not wake.

In the cell, the Count gives Benedetto his father's name and his birth date. Villefort orders his wife to poison herself, then kisses his son goodbye. In court, Benedetto names Auteuil, the tree, and "my father is you". Villefort confesses. In his carriage he remembers that the boy is at home with her. Héloïse has drunk the vial and taken the boy with her. Over her body the Count says "I am Edmond Dantès". The empty vial cannot help: "He's dead, Villefort." Villefort's mind breaks and he digs in his own frozen garden.

The Count stands in the snow and cannot say "Two". He tells Noirtier that Valentine is alive. Asked whether he now wishes the Count had stopped, Noirtier answers yes. The Count buys Maximilien one month. On the fourth night, Valentine's hand closes round Haydée's.

### Cast (as identified blind)

All of these were identified correctly on first sight, and every line was assigned correctly.

- **The Count:** black coat, swept-back black hair, gloves.
- **Haydée:** crimson and gold, black hair.
- **Benedetto:** chestnut wave, crooked grin; mustard jacket, then green velvet, then grey smock.
- **Villefort:** grey side part, magistrate's robe and cap.
- **Héloïse:** honey-gold coils, violet.
- **The boy:** gold curls, violet velvet, tin hussar.
- **Valentine:** dove-grey, light auburn hair.
- **Noirtier:** white hair and moustache, green Empire coat, wheeled chair.
- **Maximilien:** moustache; brown coat, or blue hussar uniform with gold frogging.

### Causal chain

Burial (21 years ago) → the Count recruits the son → he arms the mother with the vial → he is let into the house → Noirtier releases him and the Count gives his word → Maximilien's love for Valentine raises the stakes → Haydée's warning is refused → the dinner and the empty hole → the grandmother is poisoned and Villefort chooses not to know → the Count's bet → Benedetto is arrested → Valentine is poisoned and survives → the wardrobe watch → the false death → HÉLOÏSE is spelled → the cell → Villefort's order to his wife → the trial confession → the carriage → the boy is dead → Villefort goes mad → "Two" cannot be said → Noirtier's yes → one month → the hand closes.

Every link is on the page.

### Ending: what it cost and why the final image matters

The Count won. Two of the three men on his list are finished. The price was a boy who had done nothing, and a girl who may never wake. The hussar he carries is the bill for it. The last image (Valentine's hand closing round Haydée's while the Count stays in the doorway holding the soldier) is the one promise that might still be kept. It is also the first thing in the book that the Count did not set up himself.

### Reading-pace record

- **pp 1–8:** fast. The hooks land; the carriage spectacle is a clean turn.
- **pp 9–18:** the Noirtier blink code is taught clearly on p11. The prose pages slow things down where they should.
- **pp 19–27:** the empty-hole spread (p22) and the Opéra arrest land hard.
- **pp 28–39:** the wardrobe pages (pp 31–32) are the best suspense in the book. The tomb pages read clearly.
- **pp 40–47:** the trial runs like a ratchet.
- **pp 48–56:** the boy's death is never shown and is understood completely. Page 53 is the emotional floor of the book, and it is earned.

I hit no stalls or re-reads, and I had no confusion I needed to resolve.

- **Choices:** he recruits Benedetto, arranges the carriage, and gives Héloïse the vial knowingly.
- **Who is affected:** Benedetto, Héloïse, the boy.
- **What the Count believes:** he controls every road.
- **Why we move on:** the poison is in her hand.

All answerable from the pages. **Pass.**

**II. The King's Attorney's House (pp 9–18)**
- **Who acted and what they wanted:** the Count wants entry to the house and wants Noirtier as a witness.
- **Choices:** he tells Noirtier who he is; Noirtier answers "No" to stopping him; the Count gives his word about Valentine; he refuses Haydée.
- **Who is affected:** Noirtier, Valentine, and Maximilien by extension.
- **What the Count believes:** he will see everything, and God kept the child alive for him.

**III. Dinner at Auteuil (pp 19–27)**
- **Who acted:** Villefort, Héloïse, and the Count.
- **Choices:** Villefort chooses not to know who the poisoner is; Héloïse chooses Valentine next; the Count bets on her waiting and betrays Benedetto to the police.
- **Who is affected:** the grandmother, who dies; Valentine; Benedetto.
- **What the Count believes:** the poisoner will wait while the house is in mourning.

- **What happened:** the bet is lost on the page (p28). Noirtier's medicine is why she survived. The Count confesses the vial is his.
- **Choices:** he watches from the wall rather than warn her; Valentine chooses to trust him; Noirtier names Héloïse.
- **Who is affected:** Valentine, Maximilien, Villefort.
- **What the Count believes:** he still believes he controls everything, and smiles before the trial even though she has not woken.
- **Why we move on:** the trial is in the morning.

**Pass.**

**V. The Trial (pp 40–47)**
- **What happened:** the Count arms Benedetto with the name and the date. Villefort orders Héloïse to die and leaves the boy with her. The confession in court is clear. The carriage page gives the reason for the race home.

**VI. The Bill (pp 48–56)**
- **What happened:** the cost is collected. The Count reveals himself as Dantès; the empty vial cannot help; he cannot say "Two"; Noirtier's yes judges him; Maximilien gets one month; Valentine's hand closes.

All answerable from the pages. **Pass.**

## 5. Nonblocking observations

None of these changes how the book reads, and none justifies replacing a promoted page.

**Typos, wrong or missing letters, punctuation slips**

I found no rendered typos, wrong letters or missing letters on any page. One glyph point:
- **p12 panel 4 and p50 panel 3:** "Château d'If" renders exactly as scripted, "Château d'If". In this lettering face the capital I is a plain upright stroke, so at proof scale it can look like "d'lf". That is a property of the font (the same I appears in "I sit" and "I was the sailor"), not a misspelling.

**Small objects and continuity**
- **Season in the window (pp 24 and 28).** Noirtier's room shows snow outside on pp 12, 13 and 24, but autumn foliage outside the same room on p28. Pages 19 and 21 are also autumnal after the snow on p12. This is background season drift only.
- **Blink order (p12 panel 3, p13 panel 2, p24 panel 4).** The spoken answer ("Yes." / "No." / "No. Then who —") sits in the left frame before the blink frames in reading order. The book teaches its convention on p11 and keeps it, so the answer reads as simultaneous with the blink. No harm.
- **Balloon order (p3 panel 3).** On the right, the Count's "His child by a lady…" balloon sits slightly higher than Haydée's "Villefort's son." on the left, so a reader may take them in either order. Both orders make sense.
- **The returned soldier (p25 panel 4).** The boy is shown pressing the hussar into the Count's hand. The giving back is not drawn. The boy has the hussar again on p41, so continuity holds.
- **Villefort at the bedside (p35).** His hand on his brow reads close to weeping, while the prose says he "did not weep". The script stages it as "one hand over his eyes", so the picture and prose tension is the script's own. It reads as restraint.
- **Tomb staging (p38).** The script has the Count lifting Valentine out of the coffin inside the vault. The page shows him carrying her out through the doorway with the open coffin behind him. Same event.
- **Apostrophes (p23, p47).** The prose fields mix straight and curly apostrophes ("Noirtier's", "God's"). Cosmetic.
