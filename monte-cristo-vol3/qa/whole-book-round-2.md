# Whole-book release gate: *The Count of Monte Cristo*, Volume III, *The King's Attorney*, pages 1–56

## 1. Blind account

I wrote this from the 600 × 900 proofs, read once in order with the script and contract closed.

### Retell

Twenty-three years ago, three men sent Edmond Dantès to the Château d'If. He came out as the Count of Monte Cristo. Fernand is already dead, and the second man on his list is Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney. At night in a garden at Auteuil, the Count shows Haydée a patch of bare ground under a huge plane tree. Twenty-one years ago Villefort buried his newborn son there, alive, because a child born outside his marriage would have ruined him. A man hiding behind the wall dug the baby up. That boy is now Benedetto, a thief and murderer, and the Count means to "give him back."

In a cellar tavern the Count finds Benedetto and gives him a purse, a signet ring and a new name, Prince Andrea Cavalcanti, with orders to dine at the King's Attorney's table. In court, the Count meets Villefort face to face, and Villefort has no idea who he is.

The Count sells Villefort a pair of half-broken horses, and they bolt down the hill past his gate. He stops the carriage with his own body, then revives Villefort's little son with one red drop from a crystal vial. He gives the vial to Madame de Villefort (Héloïse), knowing she wants it: ten drops would stop a heart. Haydée calls this out. He admits it and says what she does with it "is Villefort's problem."

Inside Villefort's house the Count sees a cold father who adores his son and ignores his daughter Valentine. He meets Noirtier, Villefort's paralysed father, who answers only with his eyes. The Count tells Noirtier that Villefort burned the letter addressed to him and sent the sailor who carried it to prison, and that he was that sailor. Noirtier does not want him to stop. Asked about his granddaughter, Noirtier gets the Count's word that Valentine has nothing to fear from him.

At the Morrels', the one house where the Count is loved, Maximilien says he is in love with Valentine de Villefort. The pleasure leaves the Count's face. Haydée asks him to take the bottle back. He refuses ("I want to hear it fall"), promises that Valentine will walk out alive, and she answers, "That is what you said about the boy in the carriage."

At dinner in Auteuil, Villefort shakes his unknown son's hand. Under the tree the Count tells a ghost story about a buried baby, and Villefort goes white. At two in the morning Villefort digs and finds the hole empty while the Count watches from a window.

Valentine's grandmother arrives and dies after drinking milk that Héloïse dosed in front of Noirtier. Valentine inherits. Villefort learns from his father that someone in the house is a poisoner, and stops him before he can say who. Héloïse all but tells the Count that Valentine is next. He bets that she will wait and writes the anonymous letter that gets "the prince" arrested. Villefort will prosecute his own son.

The bet fails. Valentine drinks poisoned chocolate and collapses, and she survives only because Noirtier had been building her tolerance with his own medicine. The Count admits to Noirtier that the poison is his. He cuts a doorway through the wall into Valentine's wardrobe and watches Héloïse pour drops into her glass. He then wakes Valentine and offers her a death-like sleep, and she swallows it. The house believes she is dead. Noirtier spells HÉLOÏSE to Villefort. At the tomb Maximilien accuses the Count of breaking his promise. That night the Count carries Valentine out of the vault, but she does not wake. Haydée nurses her.

The Count gives Benedetto his father's name and birth date. Villefort orders his wife to poison herself before he comes home, kisses his son goodbye and leaves the boy with her. In a packed court Benedetto names Auteuil, the tree and finally "my father is you." Villefort confesses ("It wasn't breathing. I thought it was dead—" / "You didn't look"), and the Count, in the gallery, gets the face he waited 23 years for.

In the carriage Villefort remembers that his son is with her. At home Héloïse is dying on the stair: she has taken the poison and the boy with her. The hussar lies outside a closed door. The Count arrives, picks up the empty vial and tells Villefort who he is. The boy is dead, and Villefort's mind breaks. He digs in the wrong garden to find his son.

In the snow the Count cannot say "Two." He tells Noirtier that Valentine lives, and Noirtier, asked whether he wishes the Count had stopped, says yes. Maximilien comes with his father's pistol case to say goodbye, and the Count gets him to wait one month. On the fourth night Valentine's hand closes around Haydée's.

### Cast as I identified them, all confirmed by the script
- **The Count:** black hair, black coat, gloves.
- **Haydée:** crimson and gold, black hair.
- **Benedetto, alias Prince Andrea Cavalcanti:** chestnut hair, crooked grin. Mustard coat, then green velvet, then a grey smock.
- **Villefort:** grey hair, narrow face, magistrate's robe.
- **Héloïse, Madame de Villefort:** honey-gold curls, violet.
- **The son:** gold curls, violet velvet, tin hussar.
- **Valentine:** light chestnut hair, dove-grey.
- **Noirtier:** white hair and moustache, green coat, wheelchair.
- **Maximilien Morrel:** moustache, brown coat or blue Spahi tunic.
- **The grandmother:** seen only as a white head on the pillow.

I made no misidentification and no misattribution on the cold read.

### Causal chain
Buried baby survives → Count makes him a prince → rigged horses → rescue opens Villefort's door and puts the vial in Héloïse's hand → Noirtier and the Count's promise about Valentine → Maximilien's love raises the stakes → dinner and the empty hole → grandmother poisoned for the inheritance → Villefort chooses not to know → Count bets the poisoner will wait and has Benedetto arrested → bet lost, Valentine poisoned and saved by Noirtier's dosing → wardrobe watch → false death → Noirtier names Héloïse → tomb and rescue, but no waking → Benedetto armed with name and date → Villefort orders his wife to die and leaves the boy with her → trial confession → wife and son dead → Dantès revealed → Villefort mad → Count cannot say "Two" → Noirtier's "yes, I wish you had stopped" → Maximilien's month → Valentine's hand closes.

### The ending, what it cost, and why the final image matters
The Count won completely, and it cost a child who had done nothing (the boy whose hussar he now carries) and nearly cost Valentine. The final image puts him in the doorway, not coming in, holding the dead boy's toy, while the girl he nearly killed takes hold of someone else's hand. She is saved, but not by his vengeance, and he is outside the room. The page is earned by p17 ("That is what you said about the boy in the carriage"), p18 ("He believed it"), p53 and p54.

### Reading-pace record
- **pp 1–8:** brisk. The page 7 to 8 turn on the limp boy lands hard.
- **pp 9–18:** steady. The Noirtier blink pages read quickly once page 11 teaches the code. Page 16 panel 4 is a real chill.
- **pp 19–27:** fast. The page 21 to 22 turn (white face, then the empty hole) is the best spectacle beat in the middle of the book.
- **pp 28–39:** the most urgent stretch. Page 32's wardrobe view reads instantly. The prose on pages 35 and 39 slows the pace where grief needs it.
- **pp 40–47:** the trial accelerates page by page. Page 44's "Monsieur, my father is you" and page 46's caption both land.
- **pp 48–56:** dread from page 47 onward. Page 49 is silent and devastating, page 53 gives the exhale, and page 56 closes quietly.

I never stalled or had to reread a page to understand who did what.

## 2. Text, attribution and mode against the script

**Text and attribution.** I checked every balloon, caption, prose field and printed text on pages 1–56 against `08-FULL-SCRIPT.md`. Every string renders with the scripted wording and the scripted speaker. Two conventions work cleanly throughout:
- Noirtier's answers ("Yes." / "No.") are spoken aloud by whoever asked, on pages 12, 13, 24, 30, 36 and 54. Valentine teaches this on page 11.
- Page 3 panel 3 interleaves Haydée's and the Count's lines in two columns. Under either plausible reading order, the attribution and the sense hold.

**Mode.** Every page rendered in its declared mode.

| Mode | Count | Pages |
|---|---|---|
| Illustrated prose | 11 | 1, 10, 14, 18, 23, 27, 31, 35, 39, 47, 53 |
| Spectacle / silence | 7 | 7, 22, 32, 38, 42, 49, 56 |
| Dramatic | 38 | all others |

The balance is healthy. Every movement has at least one prose page and almost every movement has a spectacle page; Movement II (pages 9–18) has none, which suits its talking-house material. Prose pages arrive where the story needs time compressed (23, 27, 31) or a reflective pause (18, 35, 39, 53).

## 3. Acceptance questions by movement

None of these answers depends on information outside the pages.

### Movement I (pp 1–8)
- **Who acted and what they wanted:** the Count wants to hand Villefort his buried son as a weapon.
- **Choice and why:** he makes Benedetto a prince and puts poison in Héloïse's hand, because he wants Villefort's house to fall from inside.
- **Who is affected:** Benedetto, Héloïse and the boy.
- **What the protagonist now believes:** he controls the game.
- **Why the next scene:** Haydée makes him own the rigged horses.

### Movement II (pp 9–18)
- **Who acted:** the Count enters the house, enlists Noirtier and gives his word about Valentine.
- **Choice and why:** when he learns Maximilien loves Valentine and Haydée asks for the bottle back, he refuses, because he wants to hear the house "fall."
- **What the protagonist now believes:** he will stand in every road of that house (p18: "He believed it").
- **Why the next scene:** the invitations to Auteuil.

### Movement III (pp 19–27)
- **Who acted and what they wanted:** Héloïse wants Valentine's inheritance for her son.
- **Choice and why:** Villefort chooses not to know who the poisoner is, to protect his name. The Count chooses to bet that Héloïse will wait and has Benedetto arrested.
- **What the protagonist now believes:** he can afford the wait.
- **Why the next scene:** Valentine is still in the poisoner's house.

### Movement IV (pp 28–39)
- **Who acted:** Héloïse poisons Valentine. Noirtier's dosing saves her once. The Count chooses the wardrobe watch, then the false death.
- **Why:** her father would bring her home to the poisoner.
- **Who is affected:** Maximilien is left grieving, and Noirtier names Héloïse.
- **What the protagonist now believes:** he still means to "have your son," while smiling at the trial even with Valentine not waking.
- **Why the next scene:** the trial is in the morning.

### Movement V (pp 40–47)
- **Who acted:** the Count arms Benedetto with the name and date. Villefort chooses to protect his name by ordering his wife to die, and leaves the boy with her.
- **What happened:** Benedetto exposes him and Villefort confesses.
- **What the protagonist now believes:** he has "that face."
- **Why the next scene:** Villefort realises his son is with Héloïse, and the Count's carriage follows.

### Movement VI (pp 48–56)
- **What it cost:** the wife and son are dead and Villefort is broken.
- **What the protagonist now believes:** he does not know whether God is on his side. Noirtier wishes he had stopped.
- **Choice:** he keeps Maximilien alive for a month.
- **Why the book ends here:** Valentine's hand closes, which is the one undamaged thing, and Volume IV is waiting.

## 5. Nonblocking observations

These are typos, slips and small-object or continuity notes. None changes the reading.

1. **p12, panel 1, Count's balloon.**
   - Rendered: "Mademoiselle, may I sit with your grandfather alone for a moment?", but the second "i" carries a stray diacritic, so it reads close to "Mademoïselle".
   - Scripted: "Mademoiselle, may I sit with your grandfather alone for a moment?"
2. **p53, prose field 1.**
   - Rendered: "On the  night  Fernand died, …" with visibly widened word gaps around "night". The letters are correct.
   - Scripted: "On the night Fernand died, …"
3. **p13 panel 2 and p24 panel 4: Noirtier's "No."**
   - The script calls for quick, many blinks. Both pages render a three-frame open/closed/open strip, which is technically a single blink.
   - The spoken "No." and the consistent use of a single closed frame for "yes" make it read correctly. Noted for drift only.
4. **Season drift.**
   - Snow is visible through the windows on pp 11–13, 24–25 and 41, and frost and snow on pp 52–53.
   - Autumn foliage appears outside Noirtier's window on p28, and autumn leaves at the cemetery on pp 37–38.
   - Cosmetic; it does not affect the sequence of events.
5. **p8, panel 1.** The script has the limp boy across the Count's knee. The render puts him in his mother's arms with the Count beside her. Same beat, no harm.
6. **p25, panel 4.** The script has the boy press the hussar into the Count's hand, which the Count then gives back. The render shows only the handing-over, so the "gives it back" half is implicit. It still reads.
7. **p32, panel 1.** The script asks for a shaded candle; Héloïse holds an open candle. Trivial.
8. **p22, panel 2.** The Count's face in the upstairs window is small at proof scale, but black clothing and a pale face after p21 make him identifiable.
