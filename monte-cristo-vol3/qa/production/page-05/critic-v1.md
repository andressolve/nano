## Blind read
In the cellar, the black-clad man owns the page by converting the younger man’s curiosity about wealth and lineage into a new identity and a command. The turn is the visiting card, purse, and ring placed on the table: the younger man becomes Prince Andrea Cavalcanti and is bound to dine with the King’s Attorney, then answers the danger with amused disbelief.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The 600 × 900 proof contains an eighth balloon in the dominant panel: the black-clad man says “Prince Andrea Cavalcanti” in addition to the required engraved “Prince Andrea Cavalcanti” on the visiting card. C1 requires seven balloon lines and the printed card, once each, with no other text.
- Material reader harm: The added balloon duplicates the new name as dialogue and changes the exact card-led naming beat into a repeated spoken-and-printed line, so the owner-controlled transcription does not pass.
- Redraw justification: Exact text is a release-gate requirement under C1, and the extra balloon cannot be accepted as cosmetic while the full page contains dialogue not present in the seven-line script.
