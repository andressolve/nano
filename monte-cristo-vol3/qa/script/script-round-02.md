# Full Script — The Count of Monte Cristo, Volume III: The King's Attorney

**Owner-controlled.** Production roles never edit this file. No compensation
number, generation note, or critic suggestion is ever written into it.

**How to read this document.** Each page is written as a scene first. The spec
under it is three lines and no more: mode (in the header), location, and why
the reader turns the page. Panel notes are staging, not metadata. **If the
metadata for a page is longer than the page's dramatized content, the story is
not being written.**

**Text discipline.** Every string between straight double quotation marks in a
speaker, `PROSE FIELD`, `CAPTION`, `SOUND`, `TITLE CARD`, or `PRINTED` line
renders exactly as written. Speech quoted *inside* a prose field uses curly
quotes (“ ”) and renders as part of that field. Nothing else in this document
renders. `tools/script_audit.py` and `tools/verify.py` parse this format.

**Noirtier's eyes.** Noirtier never has a balloon. One long, slow blink is yes;
many quick blinks are no. Whoever is asking says the answer aloud, and that
spoken word is the balloon.

# MOVEMENT I — WHAT WAS BURIED · pp 1–8

---

## PAGE 1 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** outside the garden wall of a dark house in Auteuil, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Night-blue. A high old stone wall and an iron
gate; beyond it, black against the sky, the shuttered house and the pale
spreading crown of one huge old plane tree. At the gate, tall and black,
lantern held low, the COUNT OF MONTE CRISTO looks up at the tree as if it were
a face he knew. His shadow runs long across the lane.

> **TITLE CARD** *(top, over the night sky)*
> "THE COUNT OF MONTE CRISTO"
>
> "VOLUME III · THE KING'S ATTORNEY"

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(lower third, over the dark of the lane)*
> "Twenty-three years ago, three men sent a young sailor named Edmond Dantès to the prison of the Château d'If. Fourteen years later he came out as the Count of Monte Cristo, with a fortune and a list.
>
> The first man on the list, Fernand, was already dead. The second was Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney: the only one of the three who had known Edmond was innocent, and had buried him anyway."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count has come to a dark house at night for
the second man on his list.

---

## PAGE 2 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the garden of the house at Auteuil, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The walled garden, overgrown; the great plane
tree; under it a circle of bare earth where nothing grows. The Count holds the
lantern low over the bare ground. HAYDÉE stands beside him in crimson and
gold, her black braid over her shoulder, arms folded against the cold, looking
at him and not at the ground.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Only Haydée lived in the Count's house with him. Fernand had betrayed her father to his death and sold her as a slave; the Count had freed her. He told her everything."

> **HAYDÉE:** "You bought a whole house to stand in its garden in the dark?"
>
> **COUNT:** "I bought it for this tree. Look at the ground under it."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** Close: the lantern's gold on the bare earth; the Count's
right hand, still, a finger pointing down.

> **COUNT:** "Twenty-one years ago, at midnight, a man dug a hole here and put a wooden box in it."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "What was in the box?"

**Panel 3 — 28%.** The Count's face lit from below by the lantern, the left
corner of his mouth lifting. He is enjoying this.

> **COUNT:** "A baby, born that night in this house. The man thought it was dead. He didn't stop to listen."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "Who was he?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Gérard de Villefort."

**Why you turn the page:** a baby went into this ground — and the Count is
smiling.

---

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the garden at Auteuil, night, continuous.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Haydée turns on him, sharp, the lantern between them.

> **HAYDÉE:** "If he buried it in the dark, how can you know?"
>
> **COUNT:** "A man was hiding behind that wall. He dug the box up the moment Villefort left, and the baby inside was breathing."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The Count, privately amused.

> **COUNT:** "Years later he confessed it to a priest, the Abbé Busoni. I was the Abbé Busoni — one of my disguises."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 55%.** Wide: the two of them small under the vast tree,
the black house behind; far off over the wall, the glow of Paris. The Count
looks toward it.

> **HAYDÉE:** "And the child?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Alive. Twenty-one. A thief, a liar, and since the spring, a murderer."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "Villefort's son."
>
> **COUNT:** "His child by a lady who was not his wife. A baby like that would have ruined a young prosecutor."
>
> **COUNT:** "He doesn't know he still has a son. I'm going to give him back."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count means to hand Villefort a murderer — his
own son.

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a cellar tavern in Paris, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A low vaulted cellar, smoke, candle stubs stuck
in bottles. At a card table BENEDETTO — twenty-one, long narrow face, glossy
dark chestnut hair in a careless wave, a crooked grin higher on the right, a
stained mustard-brown jacket and grey neckerchief — lays down a winning hand
while two rough card players in brown and grey glare at him. The Count has sat
down in the empty chair across from him, black and still.

> **BENEDETTO:** "That chair's taken, my lord. Unless you've come down here to lose money too."
>
> **COUNT:** "I've come to make you some. You're Benedetto."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** Benedetto's grin stays on but his eyes go flat; under the
table his hand finds the knife in his boot.

> **BENEDETTO:** "Who's asking?"
>
> **COUNT:** "A man who knows you broke out of the galleys at Toulon in April."
>
> **COUNT:** "And that the guard on the wall didn't fall by himself."

**Panel 3 — 28%.** The Count leans into the candlelight.

> **COUNT:** "I also know where you were born: in a garden, in a box, on your very first night."
>
> **BENEDETTO:** "Everybody down here has a sad story, my lord."
>
> **COUNT:** "Yours has a rich father in it."

**Why you turn the page:** the convict wants his father's name, and the Count
is not giving it yet.

---

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the cellar tavern, continuous.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** Benedetto, interested at last, elbows on the table.

> **BENEDETTO:** "Rich how rich? Tell me his name."
>
> **COUNT:** "Not yet. First you need a better name of your own."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 55%.** On the stained card table between the bottles
the Count's gloved hand sets down a fat purse, a gold signet ring and an
engraved visiting card. The card is large in the panel, clean and white
against the dark wood.

> **PRINTED CARD** *(engraved on the visiting card, centred)*
> "Prince Andrea Cavalcanti"

> **COUNT:** "From tonight you are an Italian prince, of a very old family."
>
> **COUNT:** "You have a carriage, a tailor, and more money than you can spend."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Benedetto sliding the signet ring onto his finger,
admiring it against the candle, delighted.

> **BENEDETTO:** "And what does a prince do for all that, my lord?"
>
> **COUNT:** "He dines where I tell him. Next week, he dines with the King's Attorney."
>
> **BENEDETTO:** "A prosecutor? You do have a sense of humour."

**Why you turn the page:** a murderer is going to sit at the King's
Attorney's table, and only the Count knows he is the man's son.

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Assize court in the Palais de Justice, day.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A dark oak courtroom in grey winter light.
VILLEFORT — fifty-three, long narrow face, iron-grey hair in a hard side part,
black magistrate's robe with white bands — stands at the prosecutor's desk with
one arm out toward the dock, where a thin ragged man in brown hangs his head.
Every face in the court is on Villefort. High in the public gallery, the Count,
black, watches.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Villefort had not lost a case in eleven years."

> **VILLEFORT:** "The law cannot weigh a man's hunger, gentlemen. It weighs what he did. I ask for twenty years in the galleys."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Later. The court empty. Villefort at his desk tying a
bundle of papers with red tape; the Count coming down the gallery stair toward
him.

> **VILLEFORT:** "The Count of Monte Cristo. We met at the Morcerfs'. You enjoy trials, monsieur?"
>
> **COUNT:** "I enjoy watching a man get what he deserves."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The two of them face to face across the desk, close: the
Count's swept-back black hair and pallor; Villefort's grey side part, high
white bands, thin cold smile.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Then you've come to the right court. I have never once let a guilty man go free."
>
> **COUNT:** "No. I don't imagine you have."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count is close enough to touch the man who
buried him, and Villefort has no idea who he is.

## PAGE 7 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the steep lane outside the Count's gate at Auteuil, afternoon.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Top band: two wild-eyed bay horses bolting down the steep
walled lane, no coachman on the box, a light open carriage slewing behind
them. In it MADAME DE VILLEFORT — honey-gold hair in high coils coming loose,
amethyst-violet silk — clutches her small SON, about eight, gold curls, violet
velvet suit, a toy soldier in his fist. Her mouth is open in a scream.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Down the hill toward the Count's gate, out of control, came the King's Attorney's wife and his little son."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 60%.** The Count steps out of his own gateway into the
road, cloak flying, and seizes the lead horse's bridle with both hands, his
whole weight thrown back; the horses rear above him, hooves in the air; a
carriage wheel strikes the stone gatepost in a burst of splinters. The Count
does not move. He is a black post in the road.

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Sudden stillness: the horses standing, heaving, steam off
their backs; the carriage tilted against the gatepost; over its side hangs a
small limp hand still holding a tin hussar on a rearing horse, red and gold.

**Why you turn the page:** the horses have stopped, and the boy is not moving.

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the gravel drive inside the Count's gate at Auteuil.

**Panel 1 — 22%.** The Count kneels on the gravel with the limp boy across
his knee. Madame de Villefort is on her knees beside him, hair fallen, hands
shaking.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "He isn't breathing! My son, my little boy — oh God —"
>
> **COUNT:** "Hold his head, Madame de Villefort. Gently."

**Panel 2 — 18%.** Close: the Count's long fingers tilt a small cut-crystal
vial with a gold stopper, and one red drop falls onto the boy's lips.

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The boy's eyes fly open; he flings his arms round his
mother's neck, the tin hussar still in his fist.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "He's breathing! What is that? What did you give him?"
>
> **COUNT:** "One drop of this gives a life back."

**Panel 4 — dominant, 40%.** The Count and Madame de Villefort face to face
over the boy's head, the little vial held up between them, catching the light.
Her pale-green eyes are on the vial, wanting it. His black eyes are on her,
reading her, and he knows exactly what he is reading.

> **HÉLOÏSE:** "And if someone gave more than one drop?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Ten drops would stop a heart, and no doctor in Paris could say why."
>
> **COUNT:** "Keep it, madame. You may need it one day."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just put a poison into the hand of a woman
who wanted it — and he knew.

# MOVEMENT II — THE KING'S ATTORNEY'S HOUSE · pp 9–17

---

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the dining room of the house at Auteuil, evening.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The long dark room; the Count at the glass doors
watching the lamps of a carriage go away down the lane. Behind him Haydée
stands up from the table.

> **HAYDÉE:** "Those horses came from your stables. You sold them to her husband last week."
>
> **COUNT:** "Half broken. And I told him the hill road past my gate was the prettiest drive in Auteuil."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** Haydée, very straight.

> **HAYDÉE:** "There was a child in that carriage."
>
> **COUNT:** "And now his mother owes me her son, and her husband owes me his door. It opens on Thursday."

**Panel 3 — 28%.** The two of them in the dark, the carriage lamps tiny
beyond the glass.

> **HAYDÉE:** "And the little bottle? I saw her face when you gave it to her."
>
> **COUNT:** "So did I. She'll do whatever she was always going to do."
>
> **HAYDÉE:** "To whom?"
>
> **COUNT:** "That is Villefort's problem."

**Why you turn the page:** he has admitted he armed her on purpose, and on
Thursday he walks into her house.

## PAGE 10 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the drawing room of Villefort's house, Faubourg Saint-Honoré.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A cold grey-blue drawing room, white marble
chimneypiece, frost light. Villefort, in a rigid black frock coat buttoned to a
high white stock, stands by the chimneypiece like a pillar of ice. Madame de
Villefort sits on a sofa in violet, beaming. Her SON stands before the Count
holding his tin hussar up to him with both hands, solemn, for inspection; the
Count, tall and black, has crouched — a beat late — to look at it properly.
Villefort's hand rests on the boy's gold curls: the only warm gesture he makes
in the whole book. By the window, apart from all of them, stands VALENTINE —
nineteen, heart-shaped face, wide grey-blue eyes, light chestnut hair in a
simple low knot, dove-grey dress with a pale-blue ribbon — hands folded,
watching. Nobody is looking at her.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(upper band, over the grey wall)*
> "On Thursday the King's Attorney received the Count in his own house and thanked him for his wife's life and his son's, coldly and correctly. Madame de Villefort thanked him warmly, and often.
>
> By the window stood Valentine, Villefort's daughter by his first wife, Renée — the bride whose engagement he had been celebrating on the night he sent Edmond to prison. Renée had died when Valentine was small. Her father kept his hand on his son's head all afternoon, and did not look at his daughter once."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Villefort's hand gesturing curtly toward a door.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(lower band)*
> "“My father lives at the back of the house,” said Villefort. “He can neither move nor speak. You need not trouble with him.” The Count asked to be taken to him at once."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count wants to meet the one person in the house
Villefort would rather hide.

## PAGE 11 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room, ground floor of Villefort's house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** An old man's room kept as it was in 1815: dark
green walls, an Empire clock, a framed eagle. In a high-backed wheeled armchair
by the window sits NOIRTIER — seventy-six, gaunt leonine face, long swept-back
white hair, white cavalry moustache, a faded forest-green Empire coat, a rug
over his knees — utterly still. Only his eyes are alive, black and glittering,
fixed on the Count in the doorway. Valentine kneels by the chair with her hand
on his.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left)*
> "Noirtier, Villefort's father. In 1815, the secret letter young Edmond carried from Napoleon's island had been addressed to him."

> **VALENTINE:** "Grandfather, this is the Count of Monte Cristo, who saved Madame de Villefort and the boy on the hill."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Valentine turns to the Count, cheerful and matter-of-fact.

> **VALENTINE:** "He can't move or speak, but he hears everything. He closes his eyes once for yes, and blinks lots of times for no."
>
> **VALENTINE:** "Grandfather, are you glad the Count has come?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Noirtier's face: his eyes closing, slowly, once.

> **VALENTINE:** "That's yes. He doesn't say yes to many people. He and Papa haven't agreed about anything since before I was born."

**Why you turn the page:** the old man is glad to see the Count, and the Count
has come to tell him something.

## PAGE 12 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room.

**Panel 1 — 18%.** Valentine at the door, going out; the Count drawing a chair
close to Noirtier's.

> **COUNT:** "Mademoiselle, may I sit with your grandfather alone for a moment?"
>
> **VALENTINE:** "I'll be just outside, Grandfather."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The Count sitting knee to knee with the old man, leaning in,
voice low, face close to Noirtier's still face.

> **COUNT:** "In February 1815, a young sailor brought a letter from Elba to Marseille. It was addressed to you."
>
> **COUNT:** "Do you remember it? It never came."

**Panel 3 — 17%.** Noirtier's eyes: one long, slow close.

> **COUNT:** "Yes."

**Panel 4 — dominant, 35%.** The Count's face nearer still, the left corner of
his mouth lifting, his eyes on Noirtier's.

> **COUNT:** "Your son read it first. He saw your name on it, and burned it."
>
> **COUNT:** "Then he sent the sailor to the Château d'If for fourteen years, so that no one would ever know."
>
> **COUNT:** "I was the sailor."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count has told the one man who cannot repeat it
exactly who he is.

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Noirtier's eyes, huge and fixed on the Count —
not horror: a fierce, burning interest.

> **COUNT:** "You never knew he burned it. He let you think your letter was lost."
>
> **COUNT:** "I am going to take your son apart, monsieur, piece by piece. Do you want me to stop?"

**Panel 2 — 15%.** Noirtier's eyes blinking, quick and many.

> **COUNT:** "No."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Noirtier's eyes move, deliberately, to the door Valentine
went out by, and stay there.

> **COUNT:** "Are you asking me about your granddaughter?"

**Panel 4 — 20%.** Noirtier's eyes close, slowly, once; and the Count's poised
right hand goes very still on his knee. A long moment.

> **COUNT:** "Yes."

> **COUNT:** "She has done nothing to me. She has nothing to fear from me. You have my word."

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows what is in Madame de Villefort's
reticule, and the Count has just given his word.

## PAGE 14 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Morrel house, rue Meslay.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A warm modest room: a fire, books, a worn rug.
MAXIMILIEN MORREL — twenty-eight, square open face, sun-browned, short cropped
dark-brown hair, a neat dark-brown moustache, a plain brown coat over his
uniform shirt — laughs as he takes both the Count's hands. On a side table
behind them, under a glass dome, a small red silk purse.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the warm wall above the fire)*
> "There was one house in Paris where the Count wanted nothing from anyone.
>
> Maximilien Morrel was a captain of cavalry, home from Africa, and the son of Pierre Morrel, the shipowner of Marseille. Nine years ago, on the morning old Morrel meant to shoot himself rather than go bankrupt, a stranger paid every debt he owed, sailed his lost ship back into harbour, and vanished.
>
> The stranger was Edmond Dantès. The Morrels never learned it. They kept the red purse he left behind under glass."

**Why you turn the page:** the Count is in the one house where he is loved,
and nobody in it knows why.

## PAGE 15 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Morrel house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Maximilien lifting the glass dome as carefully as
if it were a crown; the Count beside him, looking down at his own purse.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "My father used to say God sent that man. I think he was just a man who'd once been saved himself."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "If I ever find him, I'll give him my life. I mean it."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** The Count, very still.

> **COUNT:** "He'd want something better done with it than that, Maximilien."

**Panel 3 — 28%.** Maximilien sets the dome down, suddenly shy, and grins.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Then let me do something better with it. May I tell you a secret? I'm in love — properly, hopelessly in love."
>
> **COUNT:** "Why hopelessly?"

**Why you turn the page:** Maximilien is about to tell him who she is.

## PAGE 16 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Morrel house.

**Panel 1 — 28%.** Maximilien pacing before the fire.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Because her father would sooner see her dead than married to a shipowner's son."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "We meet at an iron gate at the bottom of his garden, and talk through the bars."
>
> **COUNT:** "Who is her father?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Maximilien turning, glowing, saying her name like
a gift.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "The King's Attorney. Her name is Valentine. Valentine de Villefort."

**Panel 3 — 27%.** The Count's face: for the first time in the book, the
pleasure goes out of it. His eyes have gone somewhere else entirely.

> **COUNT:** "Of every house in Paris, it had to be that one."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "You look as if I'd told you she was dead. You've met her?"
>
> **COUNT:** "Once. She was standing by a window, and nobody was looking at her."

**Why you turn the page:** the girl Morrel's son loves lives in the house
where the Count left a poison.

## PAGE 17 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Count's study, Champs-Élysées, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The Count alone at his desk in a black-and-gold
room, one candle. His right hand lies flat and still on the bare desk. Through
the tall window, the lamps of Paris; somewhere among them, Villefort's lit
window.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dark of the room)*
> "He could have stopped it that night. He could have gone back to Villefort's house, asked Madame de Villefort for the little red bottle, and carried it home in his pocket.
>
> He did not. Villefort's house was going to fall, and the Count wanted to hear it fall. He would simply make sure that one girl was standing outside it when it did.
>
> As for Danglars, the third man on his list, Danglars could go on counting his money a little longer."

**Why you turn the page:** he has chosen to let the house burn and carry one
girl out of it — and the fire is already lit.

# MOVEMENT III — DINNER AT AUTEUIL · pp 18–25

---

## PAGE 18 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the iron gate at the bottom of Villefort's garden, dusk.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** A tall iron gate set in a high wall heavy with
ivy. Inside, Valentine in dove-grey; outside in the lane, Maximilien in his
dark blue Spahi tunic with gold frogging. Their hands meet through the bars; she is laughing.

> **VALENTINE:** "You're late. I've been standing in the ivy for an hour, pretending to look at snails."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "I galloped across Paris. Forgive me?"
>
> **VALENTINE:** "Always. That's the trouble with you."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Valentine's face falling.

> **VALENTINE:** "My mother's mother arrives on Tuesday, with a list of husbands for me."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "Then I'll go to your father tomorrow and ask for you myself."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** She grips his fingers through the bars.

> **VALENTINE:** "He would throw you down the stairs. I don't want a husband from a list, Maximilien. I want you."

**Why you turn the page:** an old lady is coming to stay in the poisoner's
house.

## PAGE 19 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the dining room of the house at Auteuil, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** A long candlelit table, glass doors open on the
dark garden and the pale trunk of the plane tree. The Count at the head of the
table. Madame de Villefort radiant in violet. And BENEDETTO, transformed — a
bottle-green velvet coat, cream embroidered waistcoat, a diamond pin, gloves —
bowing over Villefort's offered hand. The two long narrow faces, the young one
and the old one, are inches apart.

> **VILLEFORT:** "I did not know you had bought this house, monsieur."
>
> **COUNT:** "It was very cheap. Nobody wanted it. May I present Prince Andrea Cavalcanti, just arrived from Italy?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Benedetto straightening, charming, the crooked grin.

> **VILLEFORT:** "Prince. You are very young to travel alone."
>
> **BENEDETTO:** "I've always been alone, monsieur. My father left me when I was very small."
>
> **VILLEFORT:** "Then your father was a fool."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** The Count at the head of the table, raising his glass,
delighted.

> **COUNT:** "To fathers."

**Why you turn the page:** Villefort has just shaken his son's hand and called
his father a fool.

## PAGE 20 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the garden at Auteuil, night, after dinner.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The guests strolling out with candles under the tree; the
Count stops on the bare ground.

> **COUNT:** "This house has a story. They say a child was born here one night in 1817, and buried under this tree before morning."
>
> **HÉLOÏSE:** "How horrible! How thrilling! Who would do such a thing?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The tableau under the tree: Villefort white as
paper, one hand gripping the trunk; Madame de Villefort thrilled; and
Benedetto, his grin gone, staring at the bare ground under his own feet — then

**Panel 4 — 10%.** Close: the Count's black-gloved hand picking up the tin
hussar from the floor.

**Why you turn the page:** Villefort's mind has broken.

## PAGE 50 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the small walled garden behind Villefort's house, dusk.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** Villefort in his shirtsleeves, robe flung on the
gravel, collar torn open, digging furiously in the frozen flower beds with a
spade, earth flying, hair fallen across his brow; above him the house with all
its windows lit.

> **VILLEFORT:** "He's here. I put him under a tree. I'll find him. I'll find him."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** The Count at the garden door, watching.

> **COUNT:** "Monsieur. This is your own garden. The tree is at Auteuil."
>
> **VILLEFORT:** "Then I'll dig them all."

**Panel 3 — 18%.** Villefort, alone, still digging.

**Why you turn the page:** the Count has destroyed him completely, and is
standing there watching it.

---

## PAGE 51 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the street outside Villefort's house, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The Count alone in a black street under a gas
lamp, snow beginning, looking up at Villefort's lit windows — the same black
figure, the same upward look, as the night he said "One."

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the dark sky above the roofs)*
> "On the night Fernand died, he had stood outside Fernand's house, looked across the roofs at this very window, and counted: “One.”
>
> Now two men on his list were finished, and he stood in the snow and tried to say the next word. It should have been the easiest word in the world.
>
> He could not say it. His hand was closed round a tin soldier in his pocket, and he kept seeing a little red bottle in a woman's hand, where he had put it himself.
>
> For the first time since the Château d'If, the Count of Monte Cristo did not know whether God was on his side."

**Why you turn the page:** he has won, and he cannot say "Two."

## PAGE 52 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Noirtier's room, night.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** The Count kneeling by Noirtier's chair in the dark green
room; one candle; the old man's eyes red-rimmed.

> **COUNT:** "Your son is in the garden, digging. He won't stop. His wife is dead, and the boy."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** The Count close to Noirtier's face, low.

> **COUNT:** "I promised you Valentine had nothing to fear from me. Listen to me. She is alive. She's in my house. Do you understand me?"

**Panel 3 — 15%.** A tear running down Noirtier's still face; his eyes close,
once, slowly.

> **COUNT:** "Yes."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** The Count, his hand on the old man's still hand.

> **COUNT:** "She hasn't woken. I don't know if she will. I'm telling you because you're the one man who can't tell anyone."

**Why you turn the page:** one promise kept, and one still hanging.

---

## PAGE 53 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Count's study, Champs-Élysées, night.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Maximilien in a plain brown coat in the doorway, very calm,
a dark wooden pistol case with brass corners under his arm.

> **MAXIMILIEN:** "I came to say goodbye, monsieur. This was my father's. He nearly used it once, at eleven o'clock in the morning."

**Panel 2 — 22%.** The Count rising from his desk.

> **COUNT:** "And at five minutes to eleven, a stranger came."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "How do you know it was five to eleven?"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 48%.** The Count's hand flat on the pistol case between
them; their faces close.

> **COUNT:** "I know a great many things. Give me one month. If you still want to die then, I'll load it myself."
>
> **MAXIMILIEN:** "One month."

**Why you turn the page:** Maximilien has agreed to wait, and the reader knows
what the month might hold.

---

## PAGE 54 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the upstairs room in the Count's house, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Candlelight. Valentine asleep in the white bed;
Haydée in crimson and gold beside her; the Count in the doorway, black, not
coming in.

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Close: Valentine's pale hand on the coverlet, closing
around Haydée's fingers.

> **HAYDÉE:** "She's holding on."

> **CAPTION** *(lower right)*
> "On the fifth night, Valentine's hand closed."

**Why you turn the page:** the last one — the girl he nearly killed is
coming back, the man who saved her no longer knows what he is, and Volume IV
is waiting.

# The reader-exit line

*Edmond Dantès dug up everything the King's Attorney ever buried, and it
worked. It cost a boy who had done nothing, and a girl who may still wake.
He stood in the snow and could not say the word Two.*

*Volume IV: the last man, and the last choice.*
