## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both title lines and both prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, with no other text. The title lines are printed title text over the night sky, not a balloon; the paragraphs belong to one prose field. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The arrival does not land
One image owns the page: a lone tall black figure at the gate of a dark house at night, looking up at one huge tree. If he reads as a passer-by, the tree is lost, or the page reads as a dramatic panel page rather than illustrated prose, it is blocking.

### C3 — Prose field over the figure
The prose field sits over plain dark ground or sky. A field covering the Count's face, his hands or the lantern is blocking; a field that crosses the lane or his shadow is not.

### C4 — The Count not himself
A pale, clean-shaven man with swept-back black hair, in black, tall and still. A second figure anywhere, or a face that reads as a grey-haired older man, is blocking.
