## PAGE 1 — The second name

### Reader turn
The Count, alone at night at the gate of a dark house, has come for the second man on his list.

### Ordered moments
1. One full-page illustrated-prose image, the dominant image at about 75% of the page. Night-blue. A high old stone wall runs across the page with an iron gate in it; beyond it, black against a deep blue sky, a shuttered unlit house and, rising over everything, the pale crown and trunk of one huge old plane tree. At the gate, right of centre, the Count stands tall and black in his long cloak, lantern held low in his left hand, face turned up to the tree, his pale face lit gold from below. His long shadow runs across the lane toward the lower left.
2. The title card is printed title text across the top of the page over plain night sky, above the tree's crown, two centred lines, the first larger: never over the Count or the tree.
3. One matte parchment prose field across the lower third of the page, over the dark of the lane and his shadow, never over his face, hands or the lantern; two paragraphs.

### Exact strings
TITLE CARD: `THE COUNT OF MONTE CRISTO`
TITLE CARD: `VOLUME III · THE KING'S ATTORNEY`
PROSE FIELD 1: `Twenty-three years ago, three men sent a young sailor named Edmond Dantès to the prison of the Château d'If. Fourteen years later he came out as the Count of Monte Cristo, with a fortune and a list.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `The first man on the list, Fernand, who had made himself the Count de Morcerf, was already dead. The second was Gérard de Villefort, the King's Attorney: the only one of the three who had known Edmond was innocent, and had buried him anyway.`

Render exactly these 4 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page. The two title lines are printed title text over the sky, not a balloon or caption box; the two prose paragraphs are one prose field.

### Character locks
The Count, from the attached sheet's fourth view (outdoors at night, long black cloak): high cheekbones, hollow temples, pallor, clean-shaven, swept-back black wave with first grey; tall columnar stillness. Silent.

### Consequential exclusions
He wears only the fourth view's long black cloak over black evening dress; the other views are never drawn as a second figure. No other person. The ground under the tree, if glimpsed, is bare and undisturbed. No balloons, captions or sound; no readable words on gate, wall or house.

## PAGE 2 — Look at the ground

The caption sits upper left, tail-free. Haydée's balloon upper left below the caption, tail to her mouth; the Count's reply upper right, lower than hers, tail to his mouth.

3. Panel three, about 28%. The Count's face lit from below by the lantern, the left corner of his mouth lifting; he is enjoying this. Haydée in profile at the right edge, mouth visible. A–B–A: the Count's line upper left, tail to his mouth; Haydée's line middle right, tail to her mouth; the Count's last line lower left, clearly third.

### Exact strings
CAPTION: `Only Haydée knew who the Count really was. Fernand had betrayed her father to his death and sold her as a slave; the Count freed her and told her everything.`
HAYDÉE: `You bought a whole house to stand in its garden in the dark?`
COUNT: `I bought it for this tree. Look at the ground.`
COUNT: `Twenty-one years ago, at midnight, a man dug a hole here and put a box in it.`
HAYDÉE: `What was in the box?`
COUNT: `A baby, born that night in this house. The man thought it was dead. He didn't stop to listen.`
HAYDÉE: `And you're smiling. Who was he?`

### Consequential exclusions

## PAGE 3 — I'm going to give him back

### Reader turn
The baby lived, grew into a murderer, and the Count means to give Villefort back his son.

### Ordered moments
1. Panel one, about 25%. Haydée on the LEFT turns on him, sharp, the lantern between them; the Count right. Her balloon upper left, tail to her mouth; his reply upper right and lower, tail to his mouth.
2. Panel two, about 20%. The Count alone, half-turned, privately amused, mouth visible; his balloon beside his mouth.
3. Panel three, the dominant panel, about 55% of the page: wide, the two of them small under the vast plane tree, the black house behind, far off over the wall a faint gold glow of Paris; the Count looks toward it. Haydée stands LEFT, the Count RIGHT. Reserve the open night sky above them as balloon lanes before placing the figures: Haydée's first line upper left; the Count's reply upper right, lower; Haydée's second line middle left, lower again; the Count's last two balloons stacked lower right, each tail to his mouth.

### Exact strings
HAYDÉE: `If he buried it in the dark, how can you know?`
COUNT: `A man who hated Villefort was hiding behind that wall. He dug the box up the moment Villefort left, and the baby was breathing.`
COUNT: `Years later he confessed it to a priest, the Abbé Busoni. I was the Abbé Busoni — one of my disguises.`
HAYDÉE: `And the child?`
COUNT: `Alive. Twenty-one. A thief, a liar, and since spring, a murderer.`
HAYDÉE: `Villefort's son.`
COUNT: `His child by a lady who was not his wife. A baby like that would have ruined a young prosecutor.`
COUNT: `He doesn't know he has a son. I'm going to give him back.`

Render exactly these 8 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
The Count, from the sheet's fourth view: long black cloak over black, pallor, high cheekbones, hollow temples, clean-shaven, swept-back black wave with first grey, the left-mouth lift. Haydée, from her sheet: one heavy black braid, very dark wide-set eyes, olive-gold skin, crimson-and-gold Epirote coat, slight and direct.

### Consequential exclusions
The Count wears only the fourth view's cloak; the other views are never drawn. The ground under the tree is bare and undisturbed: no hole, box, baby or figure from the past. No priest, no Villefort, no Benedetto, no flashback inset. The tree and house are drawn from words (a huge pale-trunked plane tree, a shuttered black house). No other person, no readable words.

## PAGE 4 — Born in a box

### Reader turn

### Ordered moments

Reserve the smoky vault above as balloon lanes: Benedetto's balloon upper left, tail to his mouth; the Count's reply upper right, lower, tail to his mouth.
2. Panel two, about 22%. Benedetto LEFT, close, grin still on, eyes gone flat; under the table his right hand finds the knife in his boot; the Count's pale face at the right edge, mouth visible. A–B–B: Benedetto's question upper left; the Count's two balloons stacked on the right, lower, each tail to his mouth.
3. Panel three, about 28%. The Count LEFT leans into the candlelight; Benedetto right. A–B–A: the Count upper left; Benedetto middle right, lower; the Count lower left, clearly third.

### Exact strings
BENEDETTO: `That chair's taken, my lord. Unless you've come down here to lose money too.`
COUNT: `I've come to make you some. You're Benedetto.`
BENEDETTO: `Who's asking?`
COUNT: `A man who knows you broke out of the galleys at Toulon in April.`
COUNT: `And that the guard on the wall didn't fall by himself.`
COUNT: `I also know where you were born: in a garden, in a box, on your very first night.`
BENEDETTO: `Everybody down here has a sad story, my lord.`
COUNT: `Yours has a rich father in it.`

Render exactly these 8 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

## PAGE 6 — The King's Attorney

### Reader turn
The Count stands face to face with the man who buried him, and Villefort has no idea who he is.

### Ordered moments
1. Panel one, the dominant panel, about 50% of the page. A dark oak courtroom in grey winter light, judges in red on the raised bench behind. Villefort stands LEFT at the prosecutor's desk, one arm out toward the dock at right, where a thin ragged man hangs his head. Every face turned to Villefort. High in the public gallery, small but clear, the Count watches. Caption upper left, tail-free; Villefort's balloon beside his mouth.
2. Panel two, about 25%. Later, the court empty. Villefort LEFT at his desk tying papers with red tape; the Count RIGHT, coming down the gallery stair. Villefort's balloon upper left; the Count's reply right, lower.
3. Panel three, about 25%. Close, face to face across the desk: Villefort LEFT, thin cold smile; the Count RIGHT. Villefort's balloon upper left; the Count's reply right, clearly lower.

### Exact strings
CAPTION: `Villefort had not lost a case in eleven years.`
VILLEFORT: `The law cannot weigh a man's hunger, gentlemen. It weighs what he did. I ask for twenty years in the galleys.`
VILLEFORT: `The Count of Monte Cristo. We met at the Count de Morcerf's — before his unhappy end. You enjoy trials, monsieur?`
COUNT: `I enjoy watching a man get what he deserves.`
VILLEFORT: `Then you've come to the right court. I have never once let a guilty man go free.`
COUNT: `No. I don't imagine you have.`

Render exactly these 6 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.
