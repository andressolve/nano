## The story (presented to the owner, 2026-10-14)

**Engine.** The man Gérard de Villefort buried alive comes back to dig up
everything else the King's Attorney ever buried — and enjoys every spadeful,
until the poison he handed out with his own hand reaches the people who did
nothing.

**Spine.** Villefort is the worst of the three men — the only one who *knew*
Edmond was innocent — and his whole life is one move: when something
threatens his name, he buries it (a sailor in 1815, a newborn in a garden at
Auteuil in 1817, and now a poisoner in his own house). The Count digs each
one up. He saves Valentine by stealing her death, destroys Villefort in open
court through the mouth of the son Villefort buried, and the bill arrives the
same afternoon: Madame de Villefort takes the poison he gave her, and takes
her son with her. That night he tries to say **"Two."** — and cannot.

**Movements and turns (54 pp: 36 dramatic / 11 prose / 7 spectacle).**
1. **What Was Buried (1–8)** — the garden at Auteuil, the child who lived, the
   tavern, Villefort in court, the runaway horses. *Turn:* the Count hands a
   poison to a woman he knows will use it.
2. **The King's Attorney's House (9–17)** — "You bought those horses"; the
   household; Noirtier's eyes; "I was the sailor"; the promise; the red purse;
   Maximilien loves Valentine. *Turn:* he learns his poison is in her house,
   and guards her rather than stop.
3. **Dinner at Auteuil (18–25)** — the garden gate; Prince Cavalcanti shakes
   his father's hand; the ghost story; Villefort's spade and the empty hole;
   the grandmother dies; "Don't tell me"; the arrest. *Turn:* a woman dies of
   the vial and Villefort chooses not to know.
4. **Valentine (26–37)** — she falls; Noirtier's secret medicine; the door in
   the wall; the poisoner caught in the act; the draught; the false death;
   Noirtier spells HÉLOÏSE; the tomb in the rain, "You promised me"; she is
   lifted out and does not wake. *Turn:* only the Count knows she lives.
5. **The Trial (38–45)** — the cell; the ultimatum; Villefort asks the prisoner
   his father's name; "Monsieur, my father is you"; "It is true"; the carriage.
   *Turn:* Villefort confesses in open court.
6. **The Bill (46–54)** — the stair; the tin hussar outside a closed door;
   "I am Edmond Dantès"; the empty vial, "He's dead"; Villefort digging; he cannot say
   "Two"; Noirtier told she lives; Maximilien's pistol case, "one month";
   Valentine's hand closes. *Turn:* he wins, the bill is a child, and he
   cannot count any more.

**Cast (8, hard):** the Count · Villefort · Valentine (new) · Maximilien
Morrel (new) · Noirtier (from Volume I, +23 years) · Madame de Villefort
(new) · Benedetto / "Prince Cavalcanti" (new) · Haydée. Plus one unnamed
sheet, no balloons: Villefort's son, about eight.

**Cuts:** the Cavalcanti fraud and father, Benedetto's engagement to Eugénie
Danglars, Caderousse's blackmail and murder, Bertuccio and the vendetta
(→ one line: "I was the priest"), Franz d'Épinay and the marriage, the will
and notary, d'Avrigny (→ Villefort diagnoses his own house), Barrois, the
Saint-Mérans (→ one grandmother, one page), Danglars (named once, never
drawn), Mercédès, Albert, Beauchamp, every servant. Volume IV keeps:
Danglars' ruin, Haydée's ending, the renunciation, Valentine's return,
"wait and hope".

**Graphical direction:** Velvet Cinema, verbatim from Volume II. Villefort's
house is cold (grey-blue, white marble, frost light); Auteuil is night-blue
and lantern; the court is dark oak and red; the Count is the only unbroken
black vertical in every room.

**The one hard call, made under delegated authority:** the boy's death stays.
It is the bill the whole volume runs up and the hinge to Volume IV's
renunciation; it is told, never drawn (a closed door, a tin hussar, the faces
in the corridor).
