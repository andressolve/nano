# Volume Architecture — 56 pages, six movements

Paris and Auteuil, autumn 1838 into winter. Weeks after the night Volume II
ended, when the Count stood in the street and said "One."

---

## I — WHAT WAS BURIED · pp 1–8

**Emotional entry:** appetite. **Exit:** a vial in a poisoner's hand.

The Count has bought a house in the village of Auteuil, and at night he walks
Haydée into its garden and stops under a tree. Twenty-one years ago a man
buried a box here, at midnight: a newborn child he did not stop to look at.
The man was Gérard de Villefort, now the King's Attorney. Someone hidden in
the garden dug the box up as soon as Villefort was gone; the child was
breathing; years later the digger confessed it to a priest — and the Count
was the priest. The child is alive, twenty-one, and a thief and a murderer.
The Count finds him in a cellar cheating at cards and offers him a prince's
name. Then the Count watches Villefort in court, asking for a man's head, and
meets him in the corridor. And then two runaway horses come down the Auteuil
hill with Madame de Villefort and her small son in the carriage, and the Count
steps into the road. The boy is limp; one red drop from the Count's vial
brings him back. "What is it?" "One drop gives back a life. Ten would take
one." He sees exactly what she is. He gives her the vial.

**The turn:** the Count hands a poison to a woman he knows will use it.
**Page-turn out:** he bought those horses himself.

## II — THE KING'S ATTORNEY'S HOUSE · pp 9–18

**Emotional entry:** cold pleasure. **Exit:** a promise he has already broken.

Haydée knows he sold Madame de Villefort the horses. He does not pretend
otherwise: whatever she does with the vial is Villefort's problem. Grateful,
Villefort opens his house: a cold drawing room, a second wife who adores her
son, a quiet daughter, Valentine, whom her father does not look at, and on
the ground floor Villefort's father, Noirtier — the old Bonapartist to whom
Edmond's letter was addressed in 1815 — paralysed, speaking only with his
eyes. Alone with him, the Count tells the one man in Paris who cannot repeat
it what Villefort did with that letter, and who the sailor was. Noirtier does
not ask him to stop. He asks about Valentine. The Count promises she has
nothing to fear from him. Then he visits the one warm house he knows —
Maximilien Morrel's, where his own red purse sits under a glass dome — and
Maximilien tells him he is in love with a girl whose father would never allow
it: Valentine de Villefort. Haydée tells him to take the bottle back tonight.
He refuses — he wants to hear the house fall — and promises her Valentine will
walk out of it alive.

**The turn:** the Count learns his poison is in the house of the girl
Morrel's son loves — and chooses to guard her rather than stop.
**Page-turn out:** he has decided to let the house burn and carry one girl
out of it.

## III — DINNER AT AUTEUIL · pp 19–27

**Emotional entry:** delight. **Exit:** the first death, buried.

Valentine and Maximilien at the iron gate at the bottom of her father's
garden; her grandmother is coming with a husband for her. The Count gives a
dinner at Auteuil and presents Prince Andrea Cavalcanti to the King's
Attorney, who shakes his son's hand; then, in the garden, under the tree, he
tells the table a ghost story about a child buried there. That night
Villefort comes back alone with a spade and digs, and finds an empty hole,
and the Count watches him from a dark window. The grandmother arrives and
dies on the third night; Valentine inherits. Villefort knows the signs —
he has hanged poisoners — and asks his father whether he knows who it is.
Yes. Is it Valentine? No. Then— "Don't tell me." Madame de Villefort thanks
the Count for his wonderful medicine with her son on her lap, and the Count
says nothing. Haydée confronts him with the dead woman; he answers that he has
waited twenty-three years to watch Villefort choose not to ask, and bets that
the poisoner will wait while the house is in mourning. The Count's anonymous letter reaches the police: Prince
Cavalcanti is Benedetto, a convict who killed a guard escaping Toulon. He is
arrested, and Villefort, humiliated, will prosecute him himself.

**The turn:** a woman dies of the Count's vial and Villefort, who could stop
it, chooses not to know.
**Page-turn out:** Valentine is now worth killing.

## IV — VALENTINE · pp 28–39

**Emotional entry:** dread. **Exit:** a sleeping girl who may not wake.

Valentine drinks from her grandfather's glass and falls, and Noirtier can
only watch. She lives. Maximilien comes to the Count for help, and the Count
works out why she lived: Noirtier has been giving her his own medicine, a
little each day, since the grandmother died. It will not save her twice. The
Count takes the empty house next door and cuts a door through the wall
behind Valentine's room, and at night he watches Madame de Villefort pour
the red drops into the glass by the bed. He wakes Valentine and shows her.
There is only one way out of a house where the poisoner will come every
night: she must die. He gives her a draught that will make her seem dead; she
trusts him because Maximilien does, and swallows it. In the morning the house
believes she is dead, and Noirtier, in his grief, spells the poisoner's name
for his son, letter by letter. Maximilien stands at the tomb in the rain and
says "You promised me." That night the Count opens the tomb and lifts her
out. She does not wake.

**The turn:** Valentine "dies" — and only the Count knows she is alive, and
he cannot promise she will wake.
**Page-turn out:** Villefort knows who the poisoner is, and the trial is in
the morning.

## V — THE TRIAL · pp 40–47

**Emotional entry:** cold purpose. **Exit:** a carriage racing a clock.

In a cell the night before the trial, the Count tells Benedetto who his father
is, and why it pays to say it out loud. In the morning Villefort tells his
wife that when he comes home tonight, if she is still alive, he will arrest
her himself; she has her little bottle. He kisses his son and goes to court.
The court is packed. Villefort, in his robe, questions the prisoner with
contempt — name, birthplace, father — and Benedetto answers: born at Auteuil,
in a garden, at midnight; buried in a box; and his father is the King's
Attorney. "Monsieur, my father is you." Villefort can deny it. He looks at the
faces and cannot. "It is true." The Count, in the gallery, watches the thing
he waited twenty-three years for. In the carriage home Villefort remembers
what he said to his wife this morning, and that his son is in the house.

**The turn:** Villefort admits it in open court.
**Page-turn out:** he told her to die, and the boy was in the house.

## VI — THE BILL · pp 48–56

**Emotional entry:** horror. **Exit:** a hand that moves.

Madame de Villefort is on the stair: she did what he told her, and she did not
go alone. The boy's door; a tin hussar on the floor. The Count, who has come
to finish it, says his name — "I am Edmond Dantès" — and Villefort drags him
to the door: look what you have done. The Count picks his own empty vial up off
the stair, goes in, and comes out: the boy is dead. Villefort goes into the garden with a
spade to find the child he buried, and does not stop digging. The Count walks
out into the street and tries to say "Two." He cannot. He goes to Noirtier and
keeps one promise: she is alive; she has not woken; he does not know if she
will. Maximilien comes to him with his father's pistol case; the Count asks
him for one month. And at the Count's house, where Haydée has sat by the bed
for five nights, Valentine's hand closes.

**The turn:** he wins, and the bill is a child, and he cannot count any
more.
**Page-turn out:** Volume IV — Danglars is still counting his money, a girl
is waking, and a man who has always known what he wanted no longer does.

## The two structural rules this architecture is built to satisfy

**Emotional duration.** The grandmother's death (23) gets the next page for
Villefort's refusal and, on p26, the Count's own reckoning with Haydée. Valentine's collapse (28) is its
own page. Her false death (35) is a prose page alone. The trial's confession
(46) gets the carriage (47). The boy (49) is a silent page. The Count's own
aftermath (53) is a prose page before he does anything else.

**One dominant turn per page.** Every page in `07-PAGE-CONTRACT.md` names the
one thing that changed. A page whose turn cannot be stated in one sentence
comes back here, not to the prompt.
