# Character Ledger — 8 locks + 1 unnamed sheet, hard ceiling

Identity hierarchy, inherited from Volumes I and II, in order of priority:
**1.** face shape and apparent age · **2.** hair and facial-hair mass ·
**3.** body silhouette and habitual posture · **4.** costume value pattern ·
**5.** colour accent, prop, repeated gesture.

> Costume and colour may reinforce a character; they may never be the only
> cues. Separation is built through skull, jaw, nose, eye set, hairline,
> apparent age, body mass and posture — the traits that survive grayscale and
> thumbnail reduction. **Every page prompt names both the positive lock and the
> nearest forbidden lookalike.**

Parent anchors live in `refs/parents/` (copied byte-identical from the parent
volumes). A returning sheet is an identity-continuity pass on its anchor, not
a redesign; this volume is the same autumn as Volume II's last page, so **no
returning face is aged**, except Noirtier (+23 years from Volume I).

## 1 · THE COUNT OF MONTE CRISTO — Edmond Dantès, 42

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/01-count-1838.png` (Volume II approved sheet).

**Wants (volume):** Villefort — not dead, *undone*, in public, by the things
he buried, and to be there for every minute of it. **Wants (secretly):** to
tell someone who he is; he tells the one man who cannot repeat it.
**Moral position:** he believes he is uncovering what Villefort hid. He is
also arming a murderer because it will hurt Villefort, and he knows it the
moment he does it. The volume never lets a character say so; the vial says it.

**Arc:** appetite → the vial → the promise to Noirtier → *Valentine* (he bends,
does not stop) → he steals her death to save her → the trial, triumph → the
boy → he cannot say "Two." Planted, not spent: doubt.

**Structural lock (inherited verbatim):** deep-set black-brown eyes; strong
straight brow; long clean nose; high cheekbones; **slight asymmetry at the left
corner of the mouth**; hollow temples; cultivated pallor; **clean-shaven**;
swept-back black wave with the first grey at the temples; tall columnar
stillness; the poised right hand before a decision.

**Costume:** black, always, unrelieved: black tailcoat, waistcoat and stock,
white linen only at throat and cuff; a black cloak outdoors. He is the only
unbroken black vertical in every room. **Prop:** the vial — a small cut-crystal
flask of red liquid with a gold stopper — appears in his hand on pp 8 and 49.

**Never:** Villefort's narrow pale inverted triangle, iron-grey hard side part
and rigid high collar; Benedetto's chestnut wave, lounging grin and coloured
velvet; Maximilien's moustache, sun-brown skin and blue uniform; and never
young Edmond's loose raven curls, open white shirt or red sash.

**Voice:** dry, exact, amused; short sentences that shut like doors; he lets a
pause do the cruelty; he talks to people as if he already knows their answer.

## 2 · GÉRARD DE VILLEFORT, the King's Attorney — 53

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/08-villefort-1838.png` (Volume II approved sheet).

**Wants (volume):** to keep his name clean — from his father's politics, from
a child, from a poisoner, from the Count. **Wants (secretly):** his small son's
love, the one soft thing in him. **Moral position:** he believes order requires
that some things be buried. He is a man who has never once chosen the truth
over himself, and the volume gives him three chances.

**Arc:** brilliant and cold in court → grateful host → the ghost story, the
spade, the empty hole → "Don't tell me" → his father spells the name → the
ultimatum to his wife → he asks his son for his father's name → "It is true"
→ the stair, the door → he digs for the child he buried.

**Structural lock (inherited verbatim):** long narrow inverted-triangle face;
very high forehead; close-set grey-hazel eyes; thin brows; convex aquiline
nose; pointed chin; cool pale skin; chestnut hair gone iron-grey, still a hard
side part; deep vertical lines beside the mouth; rigid high-necked black
silhouette; clean-shaven.

**Costume:** a rigid black frock coat buttoned to a high white stock — black,
but broken by the white stock and his grey hair; never the Count's loose
columnar line. **Court state:** a black magistrate's robe with white bands at
the throat and a black cylindrical cap (pp 6, 40–44). **Last state:**
shirtsleeves, collar torn open, hair fallen across the brow, earth on his
hands (pp 50).

**Never:** any trait of the Count — no black hair, no swept-back wave, no
high cheekbones, no left-mouth asymmetry, no olive skin; nor Noirtier's broad
leonine face and moustache; nor Benedetto's youth and grin (they share the
long face and the nose, deliberately — 31 years and grey hair separate them).

**Voice:** formal, legal, complete sentences; "monsieur" as a blade; never says
what he feels; when frightened his sentences stop halfway.

## 3 · VALENTINE DE VILLEFORT — 19 · NEW

**Kinship:** the daughter of Villefort and Renée de Saint-Méran (Volume I),
who died when she was small. Volume I's Renée sheet is a likeness note, never
an attached input.

**Wants (volume):** to marry Maximilien; and, underneath, to be looked at once
by her father. **Moral position:** the only person in her house hiding
nothing but her love. **She chooses** — to meet Maximilien at the gate, to
nurse her grandfather, and, shown the poisoned glass, to swallow the Count's
draught on trust.

**Arc:** the quiet daughter → the gate → her grandmother's death and her
fortune → the glass → the choice → "dead" → lifted out → asleep → her hand.

**Structural lock:** heart-shaped face, wide and soft at the cheekbone,
narrowing to a small rounded chin; **wide-set grey-blue eyes**; fine straight
light brows; small straight nose; pale fair skin, the palest face in the
book; **light chestnut hair with a gold cast, centre-parted and worn simply in
a low knot at the nape** with loose strands at the temples — no ringlets, no
high coils; slight, quick, upright; nineteen and looks it.

**Costume:** dove-grey and white, plain, a narrow pale-blue ribbon at the
throat — the plainest dress in her house. **State:** a white nightdress, hair
loose on a pillow, asleep or "dead" (pp 31–37, 54).

**Never:** Madame de Villefort's honey-gold high coils and temple ringlets,
pale-green heavy-lidded eyes, violet silk and thirty-three-year-old face;
Haydée's black unbound hair, olive-gold skin and crimson.

**Voice:** gentle and direct; dry humour with the people she loves; brave in
short complete sentences.

## 4 · MAXIMILIEN MORREL — 28 · NEW

**Kinship:** the son of Pierre Morrel, the Marseille shipowner the Count saved
in secret in Volume I (the red purse, the *Pharaon*, eleven o'clock). Volume
I's Morrel sheet is a likeness note, never an attached input.

**Wants (volume):** Valentine. Then, Valentine alive. Then, to die.
**Moral position:** the most honest man in Paris and the only one the Count
loves. He does not know the Count is his family's benefactor.

**Arc:** warmth → the confession of love → the gate → "Help her" → the
promise → the tomb, "You promised me" → the pistol case → he agrees to wait a
month.

**Structural lock:** his father's **square open face** with a strong straight
jaw; frank brown eyes under level dark brows; **sun-browned skin** from
Africa, the warmest skin value among the men; **short dark-brown hair, cropped
close, not curled**; **a neat dark-brown soldier's moustache — the primary
marker**; broad shoulders, upright cavalry carriage, quick to laugh.

**Costume:** captain of Spahis: a **dark blue tunic with gold braid frogging
across the chest**, dark trousers, a short scarlet-lined dark cloak outdoors;
no sash. Civilian state at home and at the end: a plain brown coat (pp 14–16,
53). **Prop:** his father's pistol case, dark wood with brass corners
(p 53).

**Never:** Benedetto's clean-shaven narrow face, chestnut wave and coloured
velvet; the Count's black columnar pallor; young Edmond's curls, open white
shirt and red sash.

**Voice:** frank, warm, impetuous; says the whole thing at once; swears by his
father's name.

## 5 · NOIRTIER DE VILLEFORT — 76

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/09-leclere-noirtier.png` (Volume I). **The anchor
sheet shows two men; Noirtier is the one with the cavalry moustache and the
forest-green Empire coat.** Aged forward twenty-three years.

**Wants (volume):** Valentine safe. He does not care what happens to his son.
**Moral position:** a conspirator once, and the only person in his house who
sees everything. **He has agency with nothing but his eyes:** he doses
Valentine with his own medicine to make her strong against the poison, and he
names the poisoner.

**Arc:** the eyes → the Count's confession → "Your granddaughter?" → yes, the
poisoner is in this house → he saves her once → he spells HÉLOÏSE → the
Count tells him she is alive.

**Structural lock:** broad leonine face, now gaunt; **long swept-back hair,
pure white**; heavy white brows; **white cavalry moustache**; deep-set dark
eyes, **fierce, glittering, the only living thing in a still body**; sunken
cheeks; seated always in a high-backed wheeled armchair, hands motionless on a
rug, head upright.

**Costume:** the old **forest-green Empire coat** of 1815, faded; a dark
tartan rug over his knees.

**Never:** Villefort's narrow inverted triangle and grey side part; the
grandmother's frailty; any movement but the eyes.

**Voice:** none. Yes is one long blink; no is many quick blinks. Others speak
his answers aloud.

## 6 · HÉLOÏSE DE VILLEFORT, Madame de Villefort — 33 · NEW

**Wants (volume):** everything for her son. Valentine's fortune passes to
Villefort if Valentine dies, and from him to the boy. **Moral position:** she
believes a mother's love excuses anything; she is a patient, careful murderer,
and the most loving mother in the book.

**Arc:** the runaway carriage and the vial → the grateful wife → the
grandmother → the thanks → the glass → the ultimatum → the stair.

**Structural lock:** **long oval face, high rounded forehead, soft full
cheeks**; **pale-green, heavy-lidded eyes**; small rosebud mouth always half
smiling; fine arched brows; pink-white skin; **honey-gold hair dressed high in
coils with long ringlets at the temples** — the most elaborate hair in the
book; medium height, soft rounded figure; her hand always on her son's head.

**Costume:** **amethyst-violet silk**, lace at the wrist, a small gold locket.
The vial lives in a violet reticule.

**Never:** Valentine's heart-shaped face, grey-blue eyes, simple light-chestnut
low knot and dove-grey; Haydée's black hair and crimson.

**Voice:** soft, sweet, a little breathless; every sentence an endearment;
speaks of her son constantly; says the worst things most gently.

## 7 · BENEDETTO, called Prince Andrea Cavalcanti — 21 · NEW

**Kinship:** Villefort's son by a woman who was not his wife, buried in a box
in the garden at Auteuil on the night he was born in 1817, dug up alive. He
has his father's bones, deliberately.

**Wants (volume):** money, a good time, and to be somebody. Then: to destroy
the man who put him in the ground, in front of all Paris. **Moral position:**
none. He is charming, funny, vain and a murderer; he is also, exactly like the
Count, a man Villefort buried who came back.

**Arc:** the cellar → the offer → the prince at Villefort's table → the ghost
story (he understands it is about him) → arrested → the cell, the name → "My
father is you."

**Structural lock:** **his father's long narrow face and convex aquiline
nose**, young and smooth; grey-hazel eyes, lazy and heavy-lidded; **a quick
crooked grin, higher on the right**; **glossy dark chestnut hair worn long,
in a fashionable swept wave**, short side-whiskers; clean-shaven chin; slim,
loose-limbed, lounging — he leans on everything.

**Costume:** three states. **Cellar** (p 4): a stained mustard-brown jacket,
a grey neckerchief, a coarse grey shirt — never a white open shirt. **Prince**
(pp 5, 19–20): a **bottle-green velvet coat**, embroidered cream waistcoat, a
**diamond pin**, gloves. **Prisoner** (pp 38, 41–44): a grey prison smock, the
same grin.

**Never:** Maximilien's moustache, sun-brown skin and blue uniform; the
Count's black, pallor and stillness; Villefort's grey hair, rigidity and age;
young Edmond's raven curls, open white shirt and red sash.

**Voice:** quick, insolent, slangy, delighted with himself; mocks everyone,
including the Count; calls him "my lord".

## 8 · HAYDÉE — 27

**Anchor:** `refs/parents/05-haydee.png` (Volume II approved sheet).

**Wants (volume):** to know what he is doing and to make him say it plainly.
**Wants (secretly):** him — planted, never spent. **Moral position:** she saw
her father betrayed by a man who did not care who else was in the house. She
does not scold. She asks the plain question, and later she sits by Valentine's
bed for five nights.

**Arc:** the garden (she hears the plan) → "You bought those horses" →
the nurse → the hand on his sleeve.

**Structural lock (inherited verbatim):** long unbound black hair or one heavy
braid over the shoulder; large wide-set very dark eyes; straight brows;
olive-gold skin; small straight nose; full mouth; slight build; direct,
unornamented stillness.

**Costume:** Epirote — deep crimson and gold embroidery, a long open coat over
a straight underdress, loose vertical silhouette, never a French waist.

**Never:** Valentine's pale fair face, light chestnut hair and dove-grey;
Madame de Villefort's honey-gold coils and violet.

**Voice:** plain, blunt, no French manners; says exactly what she means; asks
the question nobody else will.

## Unnamed recurring figure — sheet, no balloons

### VILLEFORT'S SON — about 8

On pp 7–8, 10, 24, 39 alive, and the subject of 46–49. **No name, no
balloons.** He must be met: he wakes in the Count's arms clutching a tin
hussar (8); he shows the Count the hussar (10); he sleeps on his mother's lap
while she talks of fortunes (24); his father kisses his hair (39).

**Structural lock:** small for eight; **round face, his mother's pale-green
eyes and her honey-gold hair in a soft curly mop**; pink-white skin; a solemn,
watchful face that breaks into delight.
**Costume:** a dark violet velvet skeleton suit (short jacket buttoned to the
trousers) with a wide white collar — his mother dresses him to match her.
**Prop:** a **tin hussar on a rearing horse, red and gold**, always in his
hand; on p 47 it lies alone on the floor outside a closed door.
**Nearest lookalike:** his mother, Madame de Villefort — he inherits her pale-green eyes, honey-gold hair and violet palette on purpose. Separation is carried by his round child skull, eight-year-old scale, short soft curly mop, wide white collar and solemn child posture against her long oval adult face, high coils with temple ringlets and rounded figure. Secondary: never Villefort's adult face.

**Never:** any adult face; never drawn after p 41 except as the hussar.

## Reserved design space

No background or unnamed figure may inherit a complete identity stack:

- unrelieved black columnar dress with swept-back black hair and pallor →
  **the Count**;
- rigid black frock coat with high white stock and iron-grey hard side part on
  a narrow face → **Villefort**;
- dark blue tunic with gold frogging and a dark moustache on a young man →
  **Maximilien**;
- bottle-green velvet with a diamond pin on a young chestnut-haired man →
  **Benedetto**;
- amethyst-violet silk with honey-gold coils → **Madame de Villefort**;
- dove-grey and white with a pale-blue ribbon on a young fair woman →
  **Valentine**;
- forest-green Empire coat, white mane and moustache, in a wheeled armchair →
  **Noirtier**;
- crimson-and-gold Epirote embroidery with unbound black hair → **Haydée**;
- loose raven curls, open white shirt, red-brown sash → **young Edmond,
  Volume I. Reserved and unused.**

Extras on any page with the Count wear brown, grey, buff, dark green or navy,
**never unrelieved black**. Court officials in black robes appear only behind
Villefort and only on pp 40–44, and never at the Count's height or with his
hair.
