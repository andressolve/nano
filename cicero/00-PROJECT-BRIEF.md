## The one-sentence window
*A small-town nobody with no famous name and no army climbed to the top of Rome on the power of his
voice alone — and discovered that words cannot stop legions; the hands that wrote against a warlord were
nailed up beside his head on the platform he had spoken from.*
Birth at Arpinum (3 January 106 BC) → murder near Formiae (7 December 43 BC), age 63.

## Spine (milestone-first, let-the-subject-be)
**The word as power — and its limits against armies.** Held as real milestones, not a manufactured
emotional arc:
1. **The rise, earned on oratory alone** — Pro Roscio → Verres → consul (a *novus homo* at the youngest legal age).
2. **The honest knot** — crushing Catiline was his finest hour AND a lawless act (executing citizens without
   trial). That exact act destroys him five years later (exile). This tension is the book's engine.
3. **The new milestone** — shut out of public life, he carries Greek philosophy into Latin and *invents the
   vocabulary* (qualitas, humanitas → our "quality," "science," "moral"). The orator becomes the teacher of
   the Western mind. A genuinely new kind of mind for the bio shelf (all prior bios are scientists/inventors).
4. **The limit** — the Philippics against Antony; backing young Octavian; the miscalculation; the proscription;
   the hands and head on the Rostra. The word that beat Catiline and Verres could not out-maneuver legions.

## Visual motif (closing-as-invention seed)
**The orator's hands + the scroll.** Hands gesturing in speech and writing the letters → pays off at the death
(hands nailed to the Rostra) → resolves in the finale (the hands are gone; what they wrote outlives the armies).

## Honest editorial discipline (from research/RESEARCH-NOTES.md — do not violate)
- **No fake ancient quotes.** Only the verbatim quotes vetted in the research notes, each Latin + English helper.
- **Reported quotes hedged** ("by one account"): the last words; *laudandum, ornandum, tollendum*.
- **Rich, not poor:** the *novus homo* story is about lacking a senatorial name, not poverty. No rags-to-riches.
- **Catiline had real grievances** (debt, dispossessed veterans) — dangerous, not a pantomime villain.
- **The execution-without-trial is a moral knot, not a clean triumph.** Hold both sides.
- **The civil-war choice was agonized**, not heroic-clean — show the vacillation.
- **Don't overclaim "saved the Republic."** He stopped Catiline; he could not stop Caesar/Antony/the legions.
- **The death is dignified, not gory** — bared neck, hands+head on the Rostra. NO Fulvia hairpin.
- **Show the vanity honestly** — the self-praising poem ("O fortunatam natam…"), mocked by Romans.

## Deliberate chronology / compression notes
- P3 compresses the lower *cursus honorum* (quaestor 75, aedile 69, praetor 66) into the Verres triumph beat.
- P10 compresses recall (57) + Cilicia (51–50) + the Rubicon (49) into one "the world goes to war" transition.
- No events are reordered out of true sequence; only compressed. The emotional floor (Tullia, P13) sits just
  before the philosophy burst (P14), which is the true chronology (Feb 45 → 45–44 BC).
