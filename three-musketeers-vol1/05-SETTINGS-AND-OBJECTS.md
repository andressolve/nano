# Settings and Objects

**World palette:** brush-ink line with warm gouache washes, lit like a
lantern film: candle and lantern light indoors, rain-grey and river-grey
Paris outdoors, the road in dust and dawn. Two colours own the book and
oppose each other on every page they share: **Musketeer blue** (the cassock,
the King's men) and **the Cardinal's red** (his robe, his guards' cloaks, the
one feather in Rochefort's hat). d'Artagnan's ochre-yellow is the third
colour and belongs to nobody but him and his horse. Each location is a
variation on that, except the two that must look like a different world:
the Cardinal's study (red and black, one candle, no daylight ever) and
Buckingham's palace (white, gold, and mirrors, no shadow anywhere).

Each location names one restrained accent that does dramatic work.

---

## Locations

### 1 · The road out of Gascony · pp 1–2
Ochre hills, a white road, a line of poplars, a hot sky. Morning light from
behind the boy so his shadow goes ahead of him. The place says: everything
he has fits on one horse.
**Accent:** the yellow horse, the same colour as the road.

### 2 · The inn yard at Meung · pp 3–5
A low yard of packed dirt: a well, a trough, a stable door, and a first-floor
window with shutters open where a gentleman leans on the sill. Afternoon
light, hard shadows. The place says: people are watching, and they are
laughing.
**Accent:** the window; the scar is above the boy, looking down, on every
page here.

### 3 · Paris — streets, bridges, the river · pp 6–10, 16, 27, 49
Grey-blue slate roofs stacked to the sky, a brown river, stone bridges with
houses on them, mud, crowds, and blue cloaks and red cloaks passing on the
same bridge without looking at each other. Overcast light, the sun somewhere.
The place says: nobody here knows your name.
**Accent:** blue cloaks and red cloaks in the same street, never touching.

### 4 · The Musketeers' gate · p 7
A great arched carriage door in a stone wall, standing open on a courtyard
full of blue cloaks, noise, and horses, and a porter in the arch who does
not move. The place says: this is the door, and it is not for you.
**Accent:** the door, closing.

### 5 · The field behind the Carmelite convent · pp 12–15, 48
A meadow of long grass behind a high wall of yellow stone, one big tree, a
stone horse trough by the wall, and a gap in the wall the guards come
through. Noon light, flat and bright; on p 48, early morning and dew. The
place says: this is where duels happen, and nobody comes.
**Accent:** the trough. A guard goes in it on p 14; on p 48 the yellow horse
drinks from it.

### 6 · The house in the Rue des Fossoyeurs · pp 17–19, 21, 23, 47
A narrow house on a narrow street: Constance's door at the foot of a steep
wooden stair, the stair itself, and the attic at the top with one window on
the street, a bed, a table, a candle, and a sword on a nail. Warm lamp and
candle light, the street outside blue-dark. The place says: someone lives
downstairs.
**Accent:** the stair. Every scene here is played across it: she looks up,
he looks down.

### 7 · The alley · pp 19–21
The street outside the house at night in rain: a lantern on a bracket, a
puddle, a cart, a wall. Blue-black with one warm lantern, and the rain
catching it. The place says: nobody will hear.
**Accent:** the lantern, which ends up in the puddle.

### 8 · The Cardinal's study · p 22
A red room: red hangings, a black desk, a single candle, maps, one chair with
a thin man in it and a grey cat on his lap, and on the desk a folded letter
with a broken seal. No window, no daylight, ever. The place says: this is
where France is decided, quietly.
**Accent:** the cat. The hand on it never stops.

### 9 · Athos's room · pp 25–26
A bare room: one chair, one table, one bed, whitewashed walls, and a single
sword hung on the wall. Grey window light. The place says: this man keeps
nothing.
**Accent:** the sword on the wall, the only thing in the room, and on p 26
it is in his hand with three others.

### 10 · The road north — Chantilly, Beauvais, Amiens · pp 28–32
Three stops on one road, each with its own light. **Chantilly** (p 28): a
bright inn room at noon, a long table, a stranger with a cup raised. **The
Beauvais bridge** (pp 29–30): a stone bridge over a brown river at dusk, road
workers with picks who are not road workers, a horse down on the planks.
**The Amiens inn** (p 31): a yard in the evening with red cloaks in it, and
inside, a cellar door and stone steps going down into barrels and dark. The
road between (p 32): night, one rider, a sea coming over the hill at dawn.
**Accent:** each stop has a door one friend stands in: the inn door, the
bridge rail, the cellar door.

### 11 · The quay at Calais · pp 33–35
A grey harbour: a stone quay, a boat with a gangplank, ropes, gulls, a grey
sea, a barrier with a sergeant at it and a paper in his hand. Morning, wind,
spray. The place says: this is where the road ends and the water starts,
and only the pass gets you across.
**Accent:** the gangplank, which the duel is fought on.

### 12 · Buckingham's palace, London · pp 36–39
A white palace on a river of masts: a marble stair, a hall of mirrors and
gold, a bedroom with a small table and a velvet casket on it, and a
workroom with a jeweler's bench and a fire. White, gold, and mirror, no
shadow. The place says: this is the richest man in England, and he came
down the stairs himself.
**Accent:** the casket: twelve velvet slots, ten diamonds, two empty.

### 13 · The road home · p 40
Dover, the boat, Calais, and the road south, in a single prose page: five
changes of horse, one red cloak in a post-inn yard looking past him, one
rider, and the sun of Monday going down on Paris.
**Accent:** Monday's sun.

### 14a · The back stair of the Louvre · p 41
A service door at the back of the palace, torchlight, kitchen noise, a stone
stair going up into the light. Warm and cramped, the opposite of the hall it
leads to. The place says: this is how the diamonds actually get in.
**Accent:** the open casket in torchlight, counted.

### 14 · The Louvre ballroom · pp 42–46 (the gallery, p 46)
A hall of gold and a thousand candles: a dais at the far end with two
thrones and a crowned figure on one of them, a gallery above for the
household, long doors along one side, a floor of polished wood, and a court
in silk. Warm gold everywhere, and in it one column of red. The place says:
everyone in France is here, and one man is counting.
**Accent:** the red robe in the gold; on p 45 it turns toward the doors.

## Consequential objects

*Only objects whose state carries reader understanding across pages. Decorative
accuracy is not a continuity obligation.*

| **Athos's sling** | p 8 | black sling, left arm, pp 8–16; gone from p 17; never referred to again |
| **Porthos's baldric** | p 9 | the cloak comes off it on p 9 and the back is plain leather; from p 12 on, worn under a cloak that never comes off again; smoothed by his hand on p 28 and p 47 |
