VERDICT: REVISE

# Script gate — round 03 — *The Three Musketeers, Volume I: The Queen's Diamonds*

*(Saved verbatim by the orchestrating session from the script-critic agent's reply; the critic's toolset is read-only and could not write this file itself.)*

## Cold read

A Gascon farm boy, d'Artagnan, eighteen, rides north on an old yellow horse with his father's too-long sword and a sealed letter to the Captain of the King's Musketeers, and three rules: never sell the horse, never refuse a fight, put the letter in the Captain's hand yourself. At an inn at Meung a man in black with a scar and a red feather laughs at the horse from a window; the boy challenges him, is clubbed from behind by the inn's men, and wakes in the straw with the letter gone. He learns the scarred man rode for Paris and follows. Paris: the Musketeers' gate closes on a boy with no letter; the scar is across the street, smiling; the boy runs after him and in three successive corners collides with three Musketeers (Athos, tall, arm in a sling; Porthos, huge, with a baldric that is only gold in front; Aramis, blond, standing on a lady's handkerchief) and accepts three duels at noon, one, and two. On the field the three turn out to be each other's seconds; they laugh; the scar arrives with five of the Cardinal's red cloaks to arrest them, and the boy draws on the Musketeers' side. Four beat five without a drop of blood; the scar fences the boy, is surprised, and retreats. The four walk home four abreast.

Athos sends him to Constance, who lets an attic, dresses the Queen every morning, and does not let him off the rent. Weeks: dawn lessons, dinners, a girl who looks up the stair. Then four men in the rain seize Constance at her door; the boy goes out the window onto a cart roof and beats them while the scar watches from a doorway and will not fight "for a seamstress. She only carries it." Constance carries the Queen's letters and will not say where. The Cardinal, in his red room with a cat, tells the wet scar to forget the girl: he has read the Queen's letters for a year, the King's twelve diamond studs are on the Duke of Buckingham's shoulder in London, bring two — "Nobody counts to two" — and then the King will give a ball.

Constance comes up the stair white as paper: ball Monday, the Queen must wear twelve, they are in London, the Cardinal smiled. She has the Queen's letter and no right to ask; he says that isn't no and has three friends. In Athos's bare room the three agree without asking why ("You're going. That's the why"), four sword points meet, "All for one." She sews the letter into his coat. On the road the Cardinal's men peel the friends off one by one: Porthos at Chantilly over a toast, Aramis alone on a bridge against six, Athos behind a cellar door at Amiens. The boy rides all night alone and sees the sea. Calais is closed by the Cardinal's order; the scar walks off the England boat with the only pass and two diamonds in a pouch; they duel on a gangplank; the scar goes into the harbour, the pass falls out of his coat, the sergeant reads "the bearer" and decides he can read. From the rail the boy sees something flash twice in the scar's fist and does not know what.

London: Buckingham comes down his own stairs, reads, opens the casket — ten, two holes. A masked man with a scar at his ball three nights ago. The boy reports the flash; the Duke sees the trap: she wears ten, the Cardinal steps forward with two. "Then she wears twelve." An old jeweler makes two in two days and nights; the Duke does not sit; "Tell her," and stops. The boy rides home avoiding his friends' towns, saying their names at milestones. Monday sunset, the Louvre lit. At the back stair Constance counts twelve. In the ballroom the Cardinal, pouch in sleeve, explains to the sling-armed scar how the King will count; the Queen enters; the Cardinal counts — twelve — stands for the only time, sees a muddy boy in the doorway, and reads the boy's name off the father's stolen letter. Constance brings the Queen's ring: "tell him I counted to twelve too." That night three men come home in the order they stopped, each asking "Did you arrive?" Breakfast on the field; he counts what he has; a fair trade. Last page: four abreast down a street, and a man in red at a window who knows the boy's name.

Where I stopped: (1) p28→p30→p31: Beauvais at dusk, Amiens at "Evening," then "rode all night" and dawn at the sea — I counted one day, and the book had told me two, twice. (2) p37, "Three nights ago I gave a ball": I went back to check when the scar had been in the Cardinal's room, and found the script gives no time between p22 and p23. (3) p43: why the Cardinal has the Meung letter at a ball. (4) p11: "from the way the blue cloaks had made way for them" — I reread the sentence. (5) p23: "the Cardinal smiled when the King asked" — half a beat on "asked what." The first two are findings; the rest are observations.

## Is it alive?

Yes. This is the rare script where the want in every room is legible without a ledger, and the voices survive the attribution strip trivially: cover the names and "That was my shoulder. It was hurt before you arrived," "Did anyone see me?", "As the Fathers tell us: no," "I took a letter. I don't remember a boy," "Nobody counts to two," "Double is four livres. Goodnight," and "Ten." each belong to exactly one mouth. No logistics register anywhere; the two places dialogue carries arithmetic (p24 panel 1, p37 panel 3) are the boy's own want counted out loud, and each ends on a turn ("Good. Then nobody can make us tell"; "I don't sleep"). No aphorism: the only line that could be an epigram, "Nobody counts to two," is a villain's tactic and is paid for at p43 and p44.

**I — The Yellow Horse (1–7).** Heat immediately: a boy who wants to be laughed *at* rather than have his horse laughed at, and a man who will not give him even that. The letter theft is enacted with a motive on the page (spite, "let's see how far a Gascon gets on his manners"). p5 is the aftermath done right: the shut window, the empty stall, "Then so do I." p7's door "closing the way doors close on boys" plus the scar smiling across the street is a good stack of humiliations. Appetite at full.

**II — Three Duels at Noon (8–16).** The best comic movement in the script. Each collision is a different wound (pain, vanity, secrecy), each duel is set from a distinct want, and "Bring a friend" / "I don't have any" is the volume's thesis in six words. p12's reveal that the three are each other's seconds is a real turn, and the scar's entrance on the same page keeps it from being only a joke. p13 is the page: "You're not one of us" is what he needs to hear, and Athos asking his name *after* he steps into the line is exactly where the name should be given. p16's walk home is earned. Subtext in Athos throughout: he wants nothing from the boy, which is why the boy gives him everything, and the script never says so.

**III — The Girl Downstairs (17–22).** p17 is a flirtation conducted entirely in rent. Heat: he wants her to notice him, she wants him to pay, and both get something else. p18's stair sentence is the quietest and one of the best in the book. The rain fight is spectacle earned by clarity. p21's "She only carries it" / "Carries what?" is the right way to let the plot in: a villain saying one word too many. p22 is superb — the Cardinal's politics are felt entirely through a cat, a count, and "And then the King will give a ball."

**IV — All for One (23–26).** p23: "I've no right to ask" / "That isn't no" is subtext doing the asking. p24: three men refusing to ask why, and Athos's "Good" when nobody knows what's in the letter. p25 lands because the caption admits nobody had said it before. p26's "did not say come back, because she did not want to hear him promise" — I felt that.

**V — The Road (27–34).** Each friend stops in his own voice: Porthos over a toast and a hat, Aramis with a bridge's geometry, Athos with a barrel and "Ride." p31 is the emotional centre: a boy counting the men who stopped for him, then the sea "bigger than Paris." p32–34: the callback ("I said the third time you'd stay. This is the fourth"), the gangplank, and a sergeant who "decided that he could read" — the volume's scenery rule producing its best line. The flash-twice plant is clean.

**VI — London (35–39).** The thinnest movement, and still alive: "Ten." / "There are two holes." is the right amount of words, "Then I see it" is a man understanding a trap in real time, and "'Tell her,' he said, and stopped" gives Buckingham an ache in one sentence. p39's road home — he avoids the towns because he could not have stopped — is the aftermath the road needed. It is efficient rather than rich, but I did not feel nothing.

**VII — Twelve (40–47).** p40's count with his eyes on her face, not the box; p41's Cardinal relishing a plan the reader now dreads; p43's "Twelve. / Twelve." then the one time he stands, then the name read off the boy's own letter — that is a villain's defeat and a threat in one page and it is the best page in the script. "Tell him I counted to twelve too." Three "Did you arrive?"s and "Good." "A hundred and six. I counted them." The ending counts what he has, and the last image gives the sequel its engine without a word of setup.

Would I read it? Yes. Would Sebastian? The trough, the hat on the wall, the window jump, the gangplank, the barrels. Would Francisco? "Nobody counts to two," and the sergeant. I felt something in six of seven movements and something adequate in the seventh.

The two blockers below are not about life. They are the calendar, in a book whose whole engine is counting.

## Mandatory findings

**1. Page 23 — no elapsed time between the Cardinal's order (p22) and Constance coming up the stair; p37 makes the gap load-bearing.**
p22 is the rain night: Rochefort stands wet from the p19–21 fight and is sent to London. p22's last line, "And then the King will give a ball," is answered by p23's first, "The King gives a ball on Monday," and Constance says "She wrote to him tonight." A first-time reader takes p23 as the same night or the next. Then on p37 Buckingham says "Three nights ago I gave a ball … He had a scar under the mask," and the boy left Paris the morning after p23 and reached London on Thursday — so the London ball was the night of p23, and the scar must have been in London that night while (as the reader has it) he was dripping in the Cardinal's study in Paris. The reader who notices has to reconstruct a week the script never gave them; the reader who does not notice is one question from a parent away from it. Prescribe: open p23 with a caption that fixes the interval concretely and uses it, e.g. *"Five nights, and nobody came for her. On the sixth she came up the stair."* — the writer sets the count so that the scar has time to reach London (two days to the sea, a crossing, the ball). Do not fix this by moving p22; its position after the rain is right.

**2. Page 30 — "Evening" at Amiens reads as the same evening as Beauvais, collapsing the two-day ride to one.**
p28 is "Dusk" at Beauvais; p29 says Aramis "was still holding it when it got dark"; p30 opens "Evening," and p31 has the boy ride "all night" and see the sea "at dawn." Read in order, Chantilly-noon to the sea is eighteen hours. The script has told the reader twice that it is two days ("two days' hard riding to the sea," p23; "Two days to the sea, a day to London," p24) and p37's "It's Thursday" depends on the two. Francisco will count; the book has been teaching him to. Prescribe: put the day into p30's existing caption so it carries orientation as well as the false coin — *"The next evening, at Amiens. The coin was good. The innkeeper had been told to say it was not."* No other page needs to change; p31's "rode all night" then lands on the right dawn.

## Nonblocking observations

- **p11, prose.** "with the three best swordsmen in Paris, from the way the blue cloaks had made way for them" — read aloud the clause stumbles, and the evidence is not on pp 8–10 (the blue cloaks stood *with* Porthos and Aramis; making way is shown later, p16). Consider "the three best swordsmen in Paris, if the way the other blue cloaks looked at them meant anything."
- **p22, the Cardinal.** "Forget the girl. I have read the Queen's letters for a year" sits a little against Rochefort having just been sent for the letter she carries. One clause removes the friction: "I don't need tonight's. I have read the Queen's letters for a year." Not a repair the reader is forced into; the natural reading is already "he doesn't need it."
- **p23, Constance.** "the Cardinal smiled when the King asked" — the object is two balloons up. "when the King asked her to wear them" costs four words and protects the antecedent.
- **p41/p43, the father's letter at the ball.** On p43 the Cardinal takes the cream letter "from the arm of the chair beside him." Nothing has put it there, and a reader may ask why he brought a farm boy's letter to a ball. The p44 payoff (the boy recognising his own letter across the room) is worth keeping the object physical, so plant it: on p41 panel 1 lay the cream letter with its broken red seal on the arm of the red chair, under his hand, as something he was reading while he waited. Then p43 is a man reaching for what he already had.
- **p45 staging.** Panel 1 has Constance "in the doorway" and Porthos's head "coming through the door." Put her a step inside, by the window, so the door is Porthos's.
- **p5.** The boy's "he rode for Paris" rests on the caption's "everyone in the inn had watched him go." It reads; if the writer wants the source audible, the boy can say "The stable boy says he rode for Paris at noon" — the volume rule allows scenery to be quoted.
- **Balance.** 29 dramatic / 8 prose / 10 spectacle is 62 / 17 / 21 against the rough 60 / 20 / 13. Spectacle is high; every spectacle page is earned by clarity on the page before it and they are the pages the seven-year-old will live on. Not a finding.
- **Five-word audit.** All 47 short balloons pass. The three that lean on the picture rather than the preceding line — "They are." (p12, Porthos and Aramis visibly arriving in the next panel), "In theory." (p16, answering "Aramis is paying"), "Ten." (p36, the open casket in the same panel) — are each uniquely determined by what is drawn beside them.
- **Protected claims.** Bloodless holds on every fight page (p14 says it outright; p20, p29, p33–34 injure only pride, hats, and one arm). The Cardinal's politics are felt through a cat, a count, and a chair, and never explained. Scenery speaks nowhere; the sergeant's choice on p34 is the best thing that rule produced.
