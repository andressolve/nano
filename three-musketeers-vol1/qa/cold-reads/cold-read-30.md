# Cold read: canonical pages 1–30

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without documentation. I could follow the protagonist, distinguish the central cast, understand why the four men join forces, and state the mission and danger at page 30. I found no reading-speed failure that caused material reader harm.

## Retell of the story so far

In Gascony in 1625, eighteen-year-old d’Artagnan leaves home on an old yellow horse. His father gives him a sword, a sealed letter of introduction to the Captain of the King’s Musketeers in Paris, and the advice never to refuse a fight.

At an inn in Meung, a black-clad, scarred man with a red feather mocks d’Artagnan’s horse from an upstairs window. D’Artagnan challenges him, but inn workers knock the boy unconscious. The scarred man steals the letter, has d’Artagnan put in the straw, and rides toward Paris. D’Artagnan wakes, learns what happened, and chases him, but the man escapes.

In Paris, d’Artagnan cannot gain admission to the Musketeers’ captain without his stolen letter. He spots the scarred man and runs after him. During the chase he collides with three Musketeers in succession: the injured, grave Athos; the huge, boastful Porthos; and the elegant Aramis. D’Artagnan’s pride turns each collision into a duel appointment—at noon, one, and two—behind the Carmelite convent.

At noon, d’Artagnan meets Athos and discovers that all three proposed opponents are friends. Before the duels can proceed, the Cardinal’s red guards arrive and declare dueling forbidden. When the guards threaten the Musketeers, d’Artagnan chooses their side. The four defeat the guards together. The scarred man, who has followed d’Artagnan to the field, then crosses swords with him and is impressed enough to defer their quarrel rather than finish it.

The three Musketeers accept d’Artagnan as a companion, feed him, and help him find a cheap attic. His landlady, Constance, is a young seamstress who serves the Queen at the Louvre. Over the following weeks Athos trains him and becomes a stern mentor; Porthos entertains him with heroic stories; Aramis studies Latin and talks of entering the Church; and Constance and d’Artagnan grow close.

One rainy night, black-clad men seize Constance in the street. D’Artagnan leaps from his attic window and drives them off. The scarred man watches. When d’Artagnan again demands their postponed duel, the man refuses because Constance carries something more important: the Queen’s letters. Constance explains that she carries secret letters for the Queen and that the Cardinal’s men will keep hunting her.

The scarred man reports to the Cardinal. The Cardinal reveals his scheme: the King gave the Queen twelve diamond studs, but she gave them to the Duke of Buckingham in London. The Cardinal orders the scarred man to steal two studs. Then the King will announce a ball and demand that the Queen wear all twelve, exposing her secret.

Constance tells d’Artagnan that the Queen must have the diamonds back by Monday, but the Cardinal controls the roads and inns. She has a letter for Buckingham and needs someone the Cardinal does not own. D’Artagnan volunteers and brings the problem to Athos, Porthos, and Aramis. They decide all four must ride so that at least one can get through. They seal their new bond with “All for one” and “One for all.” Constance hides the letter in d’Artagnan’s coat lining before the four ride out.

At their first stop, a hostile innkeeper provokes Porthos by toasting the Cardinal. Porthos stays behind to fight while the others continue. At dusk on a narrow bridge, six men disguised as road workers ambush the remaining riders. Aramis sends Athos and d’Artagnan onward and holds the bridge alone as night falls.

## Cast

- **d’Artagnan:** An eighteen-year-old Gascon with a lean build, shaggy black hair, a battered brown hat, patched mustard-brown clothes, tall boots, and a rapier. He begins proud, rash, and poor, but is also brave, loyal, observant, and highly skilled with a sword. He is the protagonist and now the carrier of the Queen’s letter.
- **Athos:** A tall, dark-haired Musketeer with a narrow moustache and beard, black clothes beneath a blue cross-marked cloak, a black hat with a pale feather, and his left arm in a sling at first. He is controlled, serious, and paternal: the group’s strategist and d’Artagnan’s principal teacher.
- **Porthos:** A very large, cheerful Musketeer with curly reddish hair, an enormous curled moustache, blue-and-red clothing, a broad gold baldric, and a black hat with red plumes. He is the loud, comic, boastful powerhouse of the group, generous and fearless in a fight.
- **Aramis:** A slim, fair-haired, clean-shaven Musketeer in a neat blue cloak, lace collar, pale feather, and refined dress. He is elegant, ironic, and intellectually inclined, and says he intends to enter the Church. He is left holding the bridge at page 30.
- **Constance:** A young woman with brown curls, a white cap, grey dress, white apron marked with a small crown, a key ring, and a sewing basket. She rents d’Artagnan his attic, dresses the Queen, carries the Queen’s secret correspondence, and entrusts d’Artagnan with the London mission.
- **The scarred man from Meung:** A tall, severe man with dark hair, moustache, a facial scar, a black cloak and hat, and a red feather. He steals d’Artagnan’s introduction, serves as the Cardinal’s field agent, directs the pursuit of Constance, and is d’Artagnan’s personal enemy. He is also formidable enough to assess rather than merely dismiss the boy.
- **The Cardinal:** An older, thin-faced man in red clerical robes and skullcap, with a pointed grey moustache and beard. He is the political antagonist, calmly designing the diamond-stud trap while holding a grey cat.
- **The King’s Musketeers’ captain:** Not yet seen directly. He was d’Artagnan’s father’s friend and the intended recipient of the stolen introduction.
- **The Queen:** Not yet seen directly. Her gift of the King’s twelve diamond studs to Buckingham creates the urgent mission.
- **The Duke of Buckingham:** Not yet seen directly. He possesses the Queen’s twelve diamond studs in London.
- **The Cardinal’s guards and agents:** Red-cloaked guards openly oppose the Musketeers; black-cloaked men abduct Constance; disguised road workers ambush the riders. They are the Cardinal’s physical reach.
- **d’Artagnan’s father:** An older, grey-haired Gascon seen at the gate. He gives d’Artagnan his horse, sword, letter, and guiding maxim.

## Causal chain

d’Artagnan’s father sends him to Paris with an introduction → the scarred man mocks the horse → d’Artagnan challenges him and is knocked out → the scarred man steals the introduction → d’Artagnan cannot enter the captain’s presence → spotting the thief starts a reckless chase through Paris → three collisions produce three duel appointments → the Cardinal’s guards interrupt the duels → d’Artagnan fights beside Athos, Porthos, and Aramis → shared danger turns the four into friends → that friendship gives d’Artagnan training, lodging, and allies → through his lodging he meets Constance → the Cardinal’s men try to seize her because she carries the Queen’s letters → d’Artagnan rescues her and learns about the Queen’s diamonds → the Cardinal’s plan makes Monday’s ball a deadline → Constance entrusts d’Artagnan with a letter for Buckingham → the Musketeers join him so that one rider may survive the Cardinal-controlled road → planted obstacles begin separating the party, first Porthos at the inn and then Aramis at the bridge.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

- **Page 4:** I briefly checked the panel order to confirm that the scarred man had come downstairs after d’Artagnan was struck, rather than having been beside him before the blow. The action resolves within the page and did not damage the story.
- **Page 9:** “Your baldric. It’s only gold in front” required a beat because I did not initially know the word *baldric*. The picture and Porthos’s embarrassed response supplied the meaning and ownership immediately.
- **Page 12:** I initially read “And the Gascon. I’ll take him myself” as the scarred man claiming d’Artagnan during the confrontation with the red guards; page 15 confirms that reading. No backtracking was needed.
- **Page 16:** Porthos’s claim that he put “him” in the trough momentarily sounded as if it might refer to the scarred man rather than a defeated guard. This is an incidental boast and has no effect on the causal story.
- **Page 19:** The transition from the weeks-long summary on page 18 to the sudden nighttime abduction is abrupt but legible: Constance calls for d’Artagnan, and he reacts at once.
- **Pages 29–30:** I counted the ambushers only because the dialogue specifies six. The exact count is not needed to understand that Aramis deliberately stays behind against overwhelming numbers.

I did not misidentify any continuing character. Athos, Porthos, Aramis, Constance, the scarred man, and the Cardinal remain visually distinct at reading pace.

## What I expect next

The journey will continue as a relay of sacrifices: Porthos and Aramis have already been stripped away, and Athos will probably be forced to remain at the next obstacle so d’Artagnan can reach the coast alone. The scarred man will pursue or overtake him, since their unresolved duel and the stolen introduction tie the personal conflict to the Cardinal’s scheme. D’Artagnan will have to reach Buckingham, recover all twelve studs or discover that two have already been stolen, and race back before Monday’s ball. Constance is likely to remain in danger in Paris while the Cardinal tries to discover who carries the Queen’s letter.
