# Cold read — pages 1–40

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without outside documentation. I understood who d’Artagnan was, why he went to Paris, how he became attached to Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, what the Cardinal’s diamond plot was, why the four riders left for London, why three stayed behind, and what d’Artagnan was carrying back to Paris at the end of page 40.

## Retell of the story so far

In Gascony, eighteen-year-old d’Artagnan leaves home with an old yellow horse, a sword, and his father’s sealed letter of introduction to the Captain of the King’s Musketeers in Paris. His father’s advice is to refuse no fight and to deliver the letter personally.

At an inn in Meung, a well-dressed man in black with a scar and a red feather mocks the horse. D’Artagnan challenges him, but the inn’s men knock the boy unconscious. The scarred man finds and steals the letter, recognizes that d’Artagnan is bound for the Musketeers, and leaves for Paris. D’Artagnan follows.

In Paris, the Musketeers’ gate is closed to d’Artagnan because he has neither his letter nor a name the guards recognize. He spots the scarred thief in the street and gives chase. While running, he collides or quarrels in rapid succession with three blue-cloaked Musketeers: the injured, grave Athos; the huge, boastful Porthos; and the elegant, clever Aramis. His pride earns him duels with all three at noon, one, and two behind the Carmelite convent.

All three Musketeers arrive together for the first duel and discover they have challenged the same boy. Before d’Artagnan and Athos can fight, five of the Cardinal’s guards interrupt and attempt to arrest or disarm them. D’Artagnan chooses the Musketeers’ side. The four defeat the five guards, and Athos accepts him by name. D’Artagnan then catches the scarred man nearby and fights him, but the man escapes a second time.

The three Musketeers take d’Artagnan in. Porthos and Aramis feed him, and Athos directs him to a cheap attic. His landlady, Constance, is a young woman who dresses the Queen at the Louvre. Over the following weeks, Athos trains him, the four become inseparable, and d’Artagnan quietly waits each evening for Constance to return.

One rainy night, men in black try to abduct Constance. D’Artagnan leaps down from his attic and drives them off in the dark. The scarred man is present but refuses d’Artagnan’s demanded third duel and leaves after hinting that Constance carries something important. Constance reveals that she secretly carries the Queen’s letters.

The scarred man reports to the Cardinal. The Cardinal already knows that the Queen gave twelve diamond studs—originally a gift from the King—to the Duke of Buckingham in London. He orders the scarred man to bring back only two of them, then plans for the King to command the Queen to wear all twelve at a Monday ball. Ten studs would expose her secret gift; twelve would save her.

After several quiet nights, Constance tells d’Artagnan about the trap. The Queen has written to Buckingham, but the road to London is controlled by the Cardinal and there is no trustworthy messenger. D’Artagnan volunteers himself and his three friends. The four swear themselves together. Before dawn, Constance stitches the sealed letter into d’Artagnan’s coat, and the four ride north.

The Cardinal’s agents use successive traps to thin the party. At an inn, Porthos stays behind to answer a provocation. On a narrow bridge, six supposed road workers attack, and Aramis holds them while the others ride on. At Amiens, red-cloaked guards surround an inn on a false-coin pretext, and the wounded Athos bars a cellar door so d’Artagnan can escape with the letter. D’Artagnan rides alone through the night.

At Calais, the harbor is closed by order of the Cardinal and no one may sail without his pass. The scarred man arrives with such a pass. D’Artagnan challenges him for the fourth time, defeats him in a sword fight on a plank above the sea, and takes the pass. Because it authorizes “the bearer,” the harbor sergeant lets d’Artagnan cross to England while the wounded scarred man watches.

In London, d’Artagnan delivers the Queen’s letter to the Duke of Buckingham. The Duke opens the diamond case and discovers that two of the twelve are already gone. He realizes a masked woman with a scarred hand stole them at his recent ball as part of the Cardinal’s plan. A jeweler works for two days and two nights to make exact replacements. On Saturday, Buckingham gives d’Artagnan a complete box of twelve for the Queen.

D’Artagnan crosses the rough sea, returns through Calais using the Cardinal’s pass, changes horses five times, and avoids the places where his friends remain because the deadline matters more than stopping the traps. At sunset on Monday, he reaches Paris with the diamond box while the Louvre is already lit for the ball.

## Cast

- **d’Artagnan:** An eighteen-year-old Gascon, slim and wiry, with untidy black hair, a battered broad-brimmed hat, a worn mustard-brown coat, boots, and a rapier. He begins as a proud, poor provincial seeking entry to the King’s Musketeers. He proves brave, quick with a sword, fiercely loyal, and able to finish the Queen’s mission after all three friends are forced to stop.

- **Athos:** A tall, lean, dark-haired Musketeer with a moustache and short beard. He wears a dark blue cloak marked with a white cross, a black hat with a white plume, and carries one arm injured for much of the story. He is the grave, disciplined leader and d’Artagnan’s fencing teacher. He first challenges d’Artagnan, then accepts him, guides the group, and sacrifices his own progress at Amiens so the letter can continue.

- **Porthos:** A very large, broad, red-curled Musketeer with a sweeping moustache, a red-feathered hat, blue tabard, red sleeves, and a conspicuous gold-fronted baldric. He is the loud, proud, comic braggart of the group, but also a formidable fighter. He is the first companion separated from the mission when he stays to answer an insult at an inn.

- **Aramis:** A slim, handsome, fair-haired Musketeer with delicate lace, a blue-and-grey uniform, black hat, and white plume. He is polished, witty, and intellectual, carries a book, and claims he may someday leave soldiering for the Church. He remains on the narrow bridge to hold six attackers and let the others escape.

- **Constance:** A young, curly brown-haired woman in a white cap and grey servant’s dress, with a small crown embroidered on her apron and keys at her waist. She is d’Artagnan’s landlady and the Queen’s dresser at the Louvre. She carries the Queen’s secret letters, explains the diamond trap, prepares the message for London, and waits for d’Artagnan’s return.

- **The scarred man / the Cardinal’s man:** A lean, dark-haired, moustached man dressed almost entirely in black, with a black cloak, black hat, red feather, and a visible scar on his cheek. He is the recurring antagonist: he insults d’Artagnan in Meung, steals the father’s letter, helps pursue Constance, reports to the Cardinal, obtains the harbor pass, and repeatedly escapes d’Artagnan until losing their Calais fight.

- **The Cardinal:** An older, narrow-faced man in red clerical robes and skullcap, wearing a cross and often shown with a grey cat. He directs the conspiracy against the Queen. He knows about her secret gift to Buckingham, arranges for two studs to be stolen, and engineers the King’s demand that she display all twelve.

- **The Duke of Buckingham:** A tall, richly dressed English nobleman with long golden curls and a short golden beard, wearing white and gold with a blue sash and pearls. He possesses the Queen’s twelve diamond studs, understands the Cardinal’s plan once two are missing, commissions the replacements, and entrusts the restored set to d’Artagnan.

- **D’Artagnan’s father:** An older, grey-haired Gascon in rough brown clothes. He equips his son at the gate and gives him the advice that drives both the early duels and d’Artagnan’s determination.

- **The King’s and Cardinal’s armed men:** The King’s Musketeers wear blue with white crosses; the Cardinal’s guards wear red with white crosses. Their rivalry turns d’Artagnan’s interrupted duel into the fight that unites the four heroes. Later red-cloaked guards and disguised agents enforce the Cardinal’s effort to stop the letter and diamond box.

## Causal chain

1. D’Artagnan’s father sends him to Paris with a letter to the Musketeers’ Captain.
2. The scarred man insults him, provoking the proud boy, then has him knocked down and steals that letter.
3. Without the letter, d’Artagnan cannot enter the Musketeers’ headquarters; spotting the thief makes him race through Paris.
4. That chase causes three collisions and insults, which produce three scheduled duels.
5. The shared duel site reveals that Athos, Porthos, and Aramis are companions; the Cardinal’s guards interrupt, so d’Artagnan chooses a side.
6. His courage in their fight earns the three Musketeers’ friendship and gives him lodging, training, and allies.
7. That lodging puts him above Constance, whose position with the Queen draws the Cardinal’s agents to her door.
8. Saving Constance earns her trust, so she tells d’Artagnan that the Queen’s secret correspondence is in danger.
9. The Queen’s gift of twelve studs to Buckingham gives the Cardinal leverage. Stealing two and arranging the Monday ball creates a deadline that can publicly ruin her.
10. Constance needs a courier outside the Cardinal’s control; d’Artagnan volunteers himself and the three friends whose loyalty he has just won.
11. The Cardinal’s road traps successively stop Porthos, Aramis, and Athos, but each stays behind deliberately so the message can keep moving.
12. The harbor closure makes the scarred man’s pass essential. D’Artagnan’s repeated pursuit finally gives him both the pass and passage to England.
13. The missing two studs confirm the plot. Buckingham replaces them so the Queen can appear with twelve.
14. Because the ball is already beginning Monday evening, d’Artagnan leaves his friends behind, changes horses repeatedly, and reaches Paris carrying the completed set just before it may be too late.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

I did not backtrack or reread any page.

The only deliberate unresolved detail was on page 35: something in the wounded scarred man’s closed hand flashes twice after d’Artagnan takes the pass. I registered this as a planted mystery, not missing information needed to follow the journey. Page 15 also brings the scarred man into d’Artagnan’s path immediately after the guard fight, but his visible presence at the fight on page 14 made the transition readable without a guess.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text or speaker attribution blocked the read. The rapid sequence of challenges on pages 8–10 remained attributable through each man’s distinct appearance, and pages 12–13 clearly converted the three separate appointments into one group encounter. The prose pages were readable at the supplied proof size.

## What I expect next

D’Artagnan will race the restored box into the Louvre while the ball is under way. The Queen will be able to wear all twelve studs, defeating the Cardinal’s attempt to expose her, although the timing may force a final handoff or confrontation inside the palace. Afterward, d’Artagnan will have to learn what happened to Athos, Aramis, and Porthos on the road and help recover them. The scarred man remains alive, now personally defeated and still connected to both the stolen introductory letter and the diamond plot, so I expect him to continue as d’Artagnan’s principal enemy.
