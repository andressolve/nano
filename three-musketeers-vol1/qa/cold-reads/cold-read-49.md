The story lands without outside documentation. I could follow who wanted what, why the four young men rode for London, why three of them stayed behind, what the Cardinal's trap was, how d'Artagnan defeated it, and why the ending leaves him in danger.

## Retell of the story so far

In Gascony in 1625, eighteen-year-old d'Artagnan leaves home for Paris on an old yellow horse. His father gives him a sword, a sealed letter to the Captain of the King's Musketeers, and two rules: never refuse a fight, and put the letter into the Captain's own hand.

At an inn in Meung, a scarred man dressed in black, with a red feather in his hat, mocks d'Artagnan's horse. D'Artagnan challenges him, but the inn's men knock the boy unconscious. The scarred man steals the letter and rides toward Paris. D'Artagnan follows.

In Paris, d'Artagnan cannot get through the Musketeers' gate without the letter or a recognized name. He spots the scarred man and runs after him, but in the chase he collides with or offends three blue-cloaked Musketeers. The first, Athos, has an injured shoulder; the second, Porthos, is embarrassed when d'Artagnan exposes the plain back of his magnificent baldrick; the third, Aramis, is embarrassed by a lady's handkerchief. Each demands satisfaction. D'Artagnan agrees to duel Athos at noon, Porthos at one, and Aramis at two.

All three Musketeers arrive together for the noon duel and are amused to discover that the same boy has appointments with all of them. Before the duel begins, the Cardinal's red-cloaked guards arrive, led by the scarred man. Because dueling is forbidden, the guards order the Musketeers to surrender their swords. Though he does not yet belong to them, d'Artagnan chooses to fight beside Athos, Porthos, and Aramis. The four rout the guards. D'Artagnan then confronts the scarred man over the stolen letter and fights him, but the older man breaks away after warning that their repeated encounters cannot continue forever.

The three Musketeers accept d'Artagnan as a friend. Porthos is large, cheerful, boastful, and generous; Aramis is elegant, amused, and supposedly destined for the Church; Athos is grave, observant, and becomes d'Artagnan's fencing teacher. Since d'Artagnan has no money, Athos directs him to a cheap attic. Its keeper is Constance, a young palace servant who dresses the Queen. D'Artagnan falls in love with her while training with his new friends.

One rainy night, men in black try to abduct Constance. D'Artagnan leaps from his attic and drives them off in the dark. The scarred man watches and again declines a duel, saying Constance is only a seamstress—but adds that she carries something for the Queen. Constance then admits that she secretly carries the Queen's letters and that the Cardinal's men will keep coming.

The scarred man reports to the Cardinal. He still has d'Artagnan's father's letter and has read it closely enough to know what sort of son the father raised. The Cardinal reveals his larger trap: the King once gave the Queen twelve diamond studs, but she gave them to the Duke of Buckingham in London. The scarred man has secretly cut two from the set. The Cardinal intends to make the King hold a ball and demand that the Queen wear all twelve. He expects her to appear with only ten, exposing her gift to Buckingham and disgracing her publicly.

Constance brings the emergency to d'Artagnan. The ball is on Monday, the Queen cannot produce the studs, and every inn and road is watched by the Cardinal. Constance asks d'Artagnan to carry the Queen's sealed letter to Buckingham. He recruits Athos, Porthos, and Aramis without telling them its contents. They reason that four riders are necessary because the Cardinal will not let all four pass. They seal their loyalty with “All for one” and “One for all.” Constance sews the letter into d'Artagnan's coat, and the four ride out while a watcher in a white cap observes them.

The Cardinal's obstructions strip the companions away one by one. At the first inn, Porthos deliberately stays to fight a man who insults the Queen and to hold the pursuit. At a narrow bridge, disguised attackers posing as road workers ambush them; Aramis stays behind to hold all six while the others ride on. At Amiens, an innkeeper falsely claims their coin is bad while red guards wait in the yard. Athos barricades a cellar door with a barrel and orders d'Artagnan to ride while he holds them off. D'Artagnan continues alone through the night, counting the three friends who never asked what was in the letter.

At Calais the Cardinal has closed the harbor to anyone without his pass. The scarred man arrives carrying such a pass. D'Artagnan challenges him for the fourth time, fights him on a narrow plank over the sea, knocks him into the water, and takes the pass. The pass says to let its bearer cross, so the harbor sergeant allows d'Artagnan onto the ship. The scarred man survives and comes up clutching two diamonds—the two he stole from Buckingham's set—but d'Artagnan does not see them.

In London, d'Artagnan delivers the Queen's letter to Buckingham. The Duke opens the box he has guarded and discovers that only ten studs remain. D'Artagnan identifies the scarred Cardinal's man as the thief. Buckingham remembers a masked guest with a scar at his recent ball and understands the plot: the Cardinal will present the stolen two when the Queen appears wearing ten. Buckingham summons a jeweler, who works for two days and nights to make perfect replacements. D'Artagnan rides back with twelve studs in the box, avoiding the places where his friends fell behind and changing horses repeatedly.

He reaches Paris at sunset on Monday and finds Constance at the Louvre kitchen door. She has waited there every night since he left. She counts all twelve, recognizes the two new ones, and carries the box to the Queen while d'Artagnan deliberately stands where the Cardinal can see that he has returned.

At the ball, the Cardinal and the scarred man are confident the Queen has only ten. The Queen enters wearing twelve. The Cardinal counts them; the scarred man admits that the two originals are still hidden in his sleeve, so they realize someone made replacements. The King takes the Queen's hand before the whole court, and the trap fails without the stolen diamonds ever leaving the scarred man's sleeve.

The Cardinal notices d'Artagnan and asks who he is. The scarred man identifies him as the Gascon from Meung. The Cardinal opens the stolen paternal letter and finally learns the boy's name. Constance tells d'Artagnan that the Queen saw him, counted to twelve herself, and sent him a ring from her own hand. D'Artagnan sees that the Cardinal now knows his name, but Constance tells him to let the Cardinal know the name of the boy who beat him.

That night Porthos, Aramis, and Athos return in the order they stayed behind. Each answers d'Artagnan's repeated question—“Did you arrive?”—with the same spare answer: “I arrived.” The next morning the four eat together behind the Carmelites. D'Artagnan has lost his original horse, letter, and money, but he has gained three friends, a Queen's ring, and a cause. From a window above the street, the Cardinal watches the four walk away and now knows exactly who d'Artagnan is.

## Cast

- **d'Artagnan:** An eighteen-year-old Gascon with shaggy black hair, a battered broad-brimmed hat, a worn mustard-yellow coat, brown boots, and a rapier. He begins as a poor, proud, impulsive farm boy with an about frontier before him. His courage, speed, loyalty, and refusal to abandon a challenge make him the hero and gradually earn him a place beside the Musketeers.
- **Athos:** A tall, lean, dark-haired and dark-bearded Musketeer, usually in a black hat with a pale feather and a dark blue cloak. His right arm is initially in a sling. He is the grave, disciplined senior figure: d'Artagnan's fencing teacher, strategist, and moral anchor.
- **Porthos:** A huge, broad, red-haired man with an enormous curled moustache, round cheerful face, red sleeves, a blue Musketeer coat, a lavish gold baldric, and a large red-plumed hat. He is the exuberant braggart and comic storyteller, but also a fearless and generous friend.
- **Aramis:** A slim, fair-haired, clean-shaven young Musketeer with refined features, a pale plume, lace at his throat and cuffs, and an elegant blue uniform. He is witty, polished, and slightly vain, keeps a book close, and claims he may enter the Church while behaving very much like a swordsman.
- **Constance:** A young brown-haired woman with curls and freckles, usually wearing a white cap, grey dress, white apron marked with a small crown, and a ring of keys. She keeps the attic where d'Artagnan lodges and serves as one of the Queen's dressers and secret couriers. She trusts d'Artagnan with the mission, waits for him, and becomes his love interest.
- **The scarred man / the Cardinal's agent:** A lean, black-haired, moustached man with a visible scar down his left cheek, dressed in black with a black hat and striking red feather. He steals d'Artagnan's letter at Meung, commands or accompanies the Cardinal's guards, repeatedly duels d'Artagnan, steals two studs in London, and serves as the Cardinal's active field agent. His name has not yet been given.
- **The Cardinal:** An elderly, narrow-faced man with grey hair, a pointed grey beard, red skullcap and robes, and a gold cross; he is frequently accompanied by a grey cat. He is the calculating political antagonist. He engineers the diamond trap, commands the red-cloaked guards and the scarred agent, and ends the volume studying d'Artagnan as a newly identified threat.
- **The Queen:** A poised dark-haired woman in a crown and white-and-gold court dress. She gave the King's diamond studs to Buckingham and is the intended victim of the Cardinal's public trap. Her courage is expressed through Constance and d'Artagnan, and she rewards him with a ring.
- **The Duke of Buckingham:** A very tall, muscular, long-haired blond man with a beard, pearls, ornate white-and-gold clothing, and a blue sash. He is the powerful English duke who loves the Queen, guards her studs in his private room, recognizes the Cardinal's scheme, and has the missing two replaced.
- **The King:** A crowned, bearded man in blue-and-gold robes on the throne at the ball. He is the public authority whose gift and scrutiny make the Cardinal's trap dangerous; taking the Queen's hand completes her escape from humiliation.
- **d'Artagnan's father:** An older grey-haired, bearded Gascon in worn brown clothes. He appears at the beginning to equip his son with the horse, sword, letter, and uncompromising code that drives every later choice.

## Causal chain

1. d'Artagnan's father sends him to the Musketeers with a personal letter.
2. The scarred man mocks him; d'Artagnan obeys his father's injunction never to refuse a fight.
3. The inn men knock him out, letting the scarred man steal and read the letter.
4. Without the letter, d'Artagnan cannot reach the Captain in Paris, so he chases the thief.
5. The chase causes three accidental insults and three scheduled duels, bringing d'Artagnan, Athos, Porthos, and Aramis together in one place.
6. The Cardinal's guards interrupt; d'Artagnan chooses the Musketeers' side, and shared combat turns enemies into friends.
7. Athos finds d'Artagnan a room with Constance, whose palace work connects him to the Queen.
8. The attempted abduction reveals that Constance carries the Queen's letters and that the Cardinal is pursuing them.
9. The Cardinal has arranged for two of the Queen's twelve studs to be stolen from Buckingham, then prompts a ball where the King will demand all twelve.
10. Constance entrusts the Queen's letter to d'Artagnan; friendship gives him three companions willing to help without reading it.
11. The Cardinal controls the inns, roads, bridge, and harbor, so Porthos, Aramis, and Athos successively stay behind to keep the mission moving.
12. Alone, d'Artagnan defeats the scarred man at Calais and uses the man's bearer pass to cross.
13. Buckingham discovers the theft, understands the planned exposure, and commissions two replacement studs.
14. D'Artagnan returns by the Monday deadline; Constance delivers twelve studs to the Queen.
15. The Queen appears wearing twelve while the two stolen originals remain in the agent's sleeve, defeating the Cardinal's proof in front of the King and court.
16. The success earns d'Artagnan the Queen's ring and confirms the four men's bond, but the stolen paternal letter gives the Cardinal his full name and makes him a marked opponent.

## What I expect next

I expect the Cardinal to turn from the failed diamond trap to a personal campaign against d'Artagnan. Because he now has the father's letter, the boy's name, and proof of his loyalty to the Queen's circle, he can target d'Artagnan directly or use Constance as leverage. I expect the scarred agent to return as d'Artagnan's recurring rival, angrier after four confrontations and the defeat at Calais. I also expect d'Artagnan to seek formal entry into the King's Musketeers, with Athos, Porthos, and Aramis supporting him, while the Queen's ring and his connection to Buckingham draw the four into a larger contest between the Queen and Cardinal.
