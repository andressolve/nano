## Retell of the story so far

In Gascony in 1625, eighteen-year-old d'Artagnan leaves home for Paris with an old yellow horse, his father's oversized sword, and a sealed letter of introduction to the Captain of the King's Musketeers. His father tells him never to sell the horse, never to refuse a fight, and to deliver the letter personally.

At an inn on the road, a poised man dressed in black, with a red feather and a scar, mocks d'Artagnan's horse from a window. D'Artagnan challenges him, but the inn workers knock the boy down. While he is helpless, the black-clad man takes the letter, reads its destination, and orders d'Artagnan thrown into the straw. D'Artagnan wakes after the man has ridden for Paris, learns the letter is gone, and immediately follows.

In Paris, d'Artagnan cannot get through the Musketeers' gate without the letter or the name of someone known there. He then spots the thief in the street and chases him. During the chase he collides with three King's Musketeers in succession. First he strikes the injured shoulder of the grave, dark-haired Athos; then he exposes that the large, boastful Porthos wears a baldric gilded only on the visible side; finally he becomes entangled in a dispute over a lace handkerchief with the elegant blond Aramis. His pride and his father's rule leave him committed to duel Athos at noon, Porthos at one, and Aramis at two.

All three Musketeers arrive at the noon meeting and realize the same young stranger has challenged each of them. Before d'Artagnan and Athos can fight, five of the Cardinal's guards arrive to arrest or disarm them because dueling is forbidden. Although Athos says this is not his quarrel, d'Artagnan joins the three Musketeers against the five guards. He fights exceptionally well, and the four defeat them.

Afterward d'Artagnan confronts the black-clad, red-feathered man, now revealed to be aligned with or commanding the Cardinal's guards. The man admits taking a letter but dismisses d'Artagnan as an unmemorable boy. They cross swords briefly; the older man disengages and leaves, warning that d'Artagnan has drawn on him twice in one week. D'Artagnan vows that the third time the man will not get away.

Athos, Porthos, and Aramis accept d'Artagnan into their company. Since he has no money or lodging, they feed him and Athos directs him to a cheap attic. The landlady, Constance, works dressing the Queen at the Louvre. D'Artagnan likes her immediately and promises future fame and double rent. Over the following weeks Athos teaches him, the four friends spend their days together, and d'Artagnan watches for Constance each evening.

One rainy night, four black-clad men seize Constance in the street while the red-feathered antagonist watches. She cries to d'Artagnan. He leaps from his attic window onto a cart, challenges all four abductors, and fights them in the dark. He knocks a lantern into a puddle, leaving his opponents unable to see him while he can still see their silhouettes.

## Cast

- **d'Artagnan:** An eighteen-year-old Gascon with shaggy black hair, a lean build, worn mustard-brown clothes, tall boots, a battered broad-brimmed hat, and an old sword. He is poor, proud, impulsive, brave, highly skilled, and determined to become a King's Musketeer.

- **d'Artagnan's father:** An older, grey-haired Gascon in worn country clothes. He gives his son the horse, sword, letter, and rules that drive the early plot.

- **The red-feathered man / man from Meung:** A tall, mustached man with long dark hair, a facial scar, a black cloak and clothes, a black hat with a conspicuous red feather, and a black horse. He mocks d'Artagnan, steals the letter, commands or accompanies the Cardinal's men, repeatedly avoids a decisive fight, and watches Constance's abduction. He is the continuing antagonist.

- **Athos:** A tall, grave, dark-haired and mustached King's Musketeer in a blue cloak marked with a white cross. One arm is in a sling and his shoulder is injured. He is controlled, perceptive, and authoritative, becomes d'Artagnan's fencing teacher, and appears to lead the friends.

- **Porthos:** A very large, broad, cheerful King's Musketeer with curly reddish hair, a mustache, lavish red sleeves, a blue cloak, a plumed hat, and a showy baldric. He is boastful, sociable, and physically powerful.

- **Aramis:** A slim, elegant, blond King's Musketeer with a refined lace collar, blue cloak, and feathered black hat. He is amused and composed, claims he may leave for the Church, and appears careful about manners and appearances.

- **Constance:** A young, brown-haired woman in a white cap, grey dress, and apron bearing a small crown. She is d'Artagnan's landlady and dresses the Queen at the Louvre. She becomes his romantic interest and the target of an abduction.

- **The Cardinal's guards:** Armed men primarily identified by red uniforms and white crosses. They enforce the ban on dueling and oppose the King's blue-cloaked Musketeers. The four abductors wear black rather than the red uniform, but the red-feathered man links the attack on Constance to the same hostile side.

## Causal chain

D'Artagnan's father gives him a letter to the Musketeers' captain, so he rides toward Paris. The red-feathered man mocks him, provoking a challenge; d'Artagnan is knocked down, allowing the man to steal the letter. Without it, d'Artagnan is refused entry to the Musketeers. Seeing the thief makes him give chase, and that reckless chase causes three collisions and three scheduled duels. The illegal noon duel draws the Cardinal's guards. D'Artagnan chooses to stand with the outnumbered Musketeers, and his courage and skill win their respect and friendship. Their friendship gives the penniless newcomer food, training, and lodging. That lodging introduces him to Constance and lets him witness her abduction. His attachment to her and his established instinct to fight make him intervene immediately, bringing him into the larger conflict represented by the red-feathered man, the Cardinal's forces, and the Queen.
