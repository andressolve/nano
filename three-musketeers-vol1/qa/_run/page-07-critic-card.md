## Page intent

## PAGE 7

Dramatic. A great arched carriage door in a stone wall, open on a courtyard full of blue cloaks, horses, and noise, and in the arch a porter with a halberd who does not move. d'Artagnan on the step, hat in his hands, small, explaining that his father was the Captain's friend and that a man stole the letter. The door closes in his face; behind it, blue cloaks laugh; a caption says the door closed the way doors close on boys. He turns on the step, and across the street, on a black horse, in black with one red feather, is the scar, looking straight at him and smiling. The boy shouts; the man turns the horse into the crowd without hurry, taunting him for being on foot; the boy is already running with a hand on his hilt, and at the edge of the last panel a tall blue-cloaked shoulder comes round the corner into his path. The dominant turn is the gate shutting and the scar across the street. Rochefort owns the taunt; d'Artagnan owns the running. The reader turns because he is running and there is a man in his way.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 7 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Musketeers' gate.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A great arched carriage door in a stone wall, standing open
on a courtyard full of blue cloaks, horses, and noise. In the arch, a porter
with a halberd, not moving. D'ARTAGNAN, left, on the step, hat in his hands,
small against the door.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I've come to see the Captain. My father was his friend. I had a letter, but a man stole it—"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The door closing in his face. Behind it, blue cloaks
laughing.

> **CAPTION** *(over the closing door)*
> "No letter. No name anyone knew. No Captain. The door closed the way doors close on boys."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** D'Artagnan turned round on the step, and across
the street, on a black horse, in black, with one red feather in his hat: the
scar. Looking straight at him. Smiling.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You! The man from Meung! Stay where you are!"

**Panel 4 — small.** Rochefort turning the black horse into the crowd without
hurry. D'Artagnan already running, one hand on the hilt, and at the edge of
the panel a tall blue-cloaked shoulder coming round the corner into his path.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Still chasing, boy? On foot? Then you'll never catch me."

**Why you turn the page:** he is running, and there is a man in his way.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 7 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
The boy's plea, the caption, the boy's shout, and Rochefort's taunt read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the boy's two lines hang from the boy, Rochefort's from the mounted man; the caption is tail-free; the porter and the courtyard carry no balloons. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the boy pleads at the gate, the door closes on him, he turns and sees the scar across the street, he runs. If the door closing and the scar's smile are not the two images the eye finds, or the last panel does not show him running, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Rochefort fails identity or is not mounted
The man across the street must be on a black horse, in black, with one red feather, one scar across a cheek from temple to mouth (which cheek is nonblocking), thin moustache, no beard. A beard, a blue cloak, or a scarless face is blocking.

### C4 — d'Artagnan fails identity
Clean-shaven, black hair in his eyes, ochre doublet, grey hat in his hands in panel one, the too-long sword. A moustache or a cloak is blocking.

### C5 — A background figure wears a lock
Any courtyard Musketeer with a dark pointed beard and shoulder-length hair, red-brown curls and a waxed moustache, or blond curls and a small moustache is blocking; the porter must not have a scar or a red feather.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated boy, two scar-men, gross anatomy on the boy's hands holding the hat, or a balloon crossing a face is blocking. Courtyard horses and crowd repetition are not.
