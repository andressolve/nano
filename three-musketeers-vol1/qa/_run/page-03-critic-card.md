## Page intent

## PAGE 3

Dramatic. The inn yard at Meung: packed dirt, a well, a stone trough, a stable door, and above the yard a first-floor window with the shutters open. d'Artagnan swings down from the yellow horse at the trough. Leaning on the sill above him, a man in black with a red feather and a pale scar from temple to mouth laughs down at the horse, and two of the inn's men laugh with him. The boy turns, hand on hilt, and invites the man to come down and laugh at him; the man does not fight children, or anyone before dinner; the boy draws and heads for the inn door, and behind him the two men pick up a stool and a broom handle. The dominant turn is the scar laughing at the horse and the boy drawing on him. Rochefort owns the height; d'Artagnan owns the page. The reader turns because the man is not coming down, so the boy is going up. The yard is scenery; the window above and the drawn sword below are the page.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the inn yard at Meung.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A low inn yard of packed dirt: a well, a stone trough, a
stable door. D'ARTAGNAN, left, swinging down from the yellow horse at the
trough. Above him, right, a first-floor window with the shutters open, and
leaning on the sill a man in black with a red feather in his hat and a pale
scar from his left temple to the corner of his mouth, laughing down at the
horse. Two of the inn's men in the yard, laughing with him.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Look at that horse. Is it a horse? It's the colour of a buttercup."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Somebody tell the boy. You don't ride a flower to Paris."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** D'Artagnan turned to face the window, hand on
the hilt, hair in his eyes, looking up. The man on the sill looking down,
amused.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You're laughing at my horse, sir. Come down and laugh at me."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "At you? I don't fight children. I don't fight anyone before dinner."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** D'Artagnan with his sword out, already halfway to the inn
door, the two inn men behind him picking up a stool and a broom handle.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then you'll be late for dinner."

**Why you turn the page:** the man with the scar is not coming down, so the boy is going up.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 3 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All five strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each hanging by its tail from the right mouth: Rochefort's two taunts and his refusal from the man at the window, the boy's two lines from the boy in the yard. The inn men carry no balloon. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: a scarred man in a window laughs at the horse, the boy challenges him and is refused, the boy draws and goes for the door. If the eye cannot find the window above and the drawn sword below, or the panels read as equal bands with nothing in charge, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Rochefort does not read as Rochefort
The man at the window must show one scar across a cheek from temple to mouth (which cheek is nonblocking), a thin moustache and no beard, black clothes, and one red feather. A missing scar, a beard, or a blue cloak is blocking. The exact shape of the window is nonblocking.

### C4 — d'Artagnan and Rochefort collide, or d'Artagnan fails
The boy must be clean-shaven, black-haired, in ochre, with the too-long sword; the man must be older, scarred, in black. If the two could be taken for the same man or the boy has a moustache, blocking.

### C5 — Bloodless or state continuity fails
No blood, no wound, no one struck on this page; the horse is yellow and at the trough; the sword is sheathed in panels one and two and drawn in panel three. A stool already landing on the boy here is blocking; that belongs to the next page.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated boy or scar-man, a second red feather in the yard, gross anatomy on the boy's sword hand, or a balloon crossing a face is blocking. Yard texture, well, and background straw are not.
