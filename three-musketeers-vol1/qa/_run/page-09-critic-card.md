## Page intent

## PAGE 9

Dramatic. A second corner. Porthos, enormous, red-brown curls and a waxed moustache, fills the street telling two blue cloaks a story about the Cardinal with his own cloak thrown back to show a baldric embroidered in gold; the boy, ducking past at a run, goes straight into the cloak. On the ground, tangled in it, he sees what the whole street now sees: the back of the baldric is plain buff leather, no gold at all. The boy says so. Porthos, scarlet, fists on hips, will not have the street told about the back of his baldric; the boy offers one o'clock behind the Carmelites since noon is taken; Porthos asks who has him at noon; a tall man with a bad shoulder; Athos! Porthos will take whatever he leaves. The dominant turn is the cloak coming off the baldric. Porthos owns the page; the boy is on the ground for most of it. ## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a Paris street, a second corner.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** D'Artagnan running again. Ahead, filling the middle of the
street, an enormous Musketeer, PORTHOS, red-brown curls and a great waxed
moustache, telling a story to two blue cloaks with his own cloak thrown back
to show a shoulder-baldric embroidered in gold. The boy, ducking past, goes
straight into the cloak.

> **PORTHOS:** "—and I said, if the Cardinal wants my sword, he can come and fetch it himself—"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** D'Artagnan on the ground, tangled in the cloak.
Porthos turning, and the back of the baldric now in plain view of the whole
street: plain buff leather, no gold at all. The two blue cloaks staring at it.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Your baldric. It's only gold in front."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Porthos, scarlet, fists on hips, towering over the boy.

> **PORTHOS:** "Nobody sees the back of my baldric and tells the street about it."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then fight me. One o'clock, behind the Carmelites. I'm taken at noon."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Taken? Who has you at noon?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "A tall man with a bad shoulder."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Athos! Ha! Then I'll take whatever he leaves."

**Why you turn the page:** two duels, an hour apart, and he is still running.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 9 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the Porthos lines hang from the huge red-moustached man and the boy's from the boy on the ground; in the five-line exchange each reply sits after the line it answers. The two listening Musketeers carry no balloon. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the boy runs into the cloak, the cloak comes off and the back of the baldric is plain leather, the huge man demands a duel and gets one at one o'clock. If the plain leather back of the baldric is not plainly the thing the eye finds in the largest image, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Porthos fails identity
Enormous, round ruddy face, red-brown curls, waxed moustache with no beard, blue cassock over crimson, gold-fronted baldric, three red plumes. A beard, a slim build, or dark straight hair is blocking.

### C4 — The baldric state is wrong
In the dominant panel the back of the baldric must be plain buff leather with no gold; gold on the back is blocking. Exact embroidery pattern on the front is nonblocking.

### C5 — d'Artagnan fails identity
Clean-shaven, black-haired, ochre, no cloak of his own; the cloak he is tangled in is Porthos's blue one.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated Porthos, three listening Musketeers where two were staged, gross anatomy on Porthos's fists, or text across a face is blocking. Street texture is not.
