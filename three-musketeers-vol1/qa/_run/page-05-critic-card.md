## Page intent

## PAGE 5

Dramatic. Evening in the stable at Meung: d'Artagnan sits up in the straw with a hand on the back of his head, his hat beside him, his other hand inside his coat with the inside pocket turned out and empty, the yellow horse looking down at him from the next stall. Then the yard at dusk, empty: the boy standing in the middle of it looking up at the window, whose shutters are closed, the stable door open on one empty stall, nobody laughing now, nobody there at all. A caption says what the boy sees and that the inn watched the black horse go; the boy says the stable boy told him the man rode for Paris at noon, with the letter. Then the boy in the saddle at a gallop out of the gate: then so do I. The dominant turn is the letter gone and the boy riding after it. d'Artagnan owns the page alone. The reader turns because the letter that was going to open Paris is in the pocket of the man he most wants to fight.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the inn yard at Meung.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Evening. The stable. D'Artagnan sitting up in the straw,
one hand on the back of his head, his hat beside him; his other hand inside
his coat, and the inside pocket turned out, empty. The yellow horse looking
down at him from the next stall.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "The letter. My father's letter."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The yard at dusk. D'Artagnan standing in the
middle of it, looking up at the window: the shutters are closed. Beside him
the stable door stands open on one empty stall. Nobody is laughing now;
nobody is in the yard at all.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the dark wall)*
> "The window was shut. The black horse was gone, and everyone in the inn had watched it go."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "He took it. The stable boy says he rode for Paris at noon. With my letter."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** D'Artagnan in the saddle, the yellow horse at a gallop out
of the yard gate, hat jammed on, the road north in front of him.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then so do I."

**Why you turn the page:** the letter that was going to open Paris is in the pocket of the man he most wants to fight.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 5 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
The boy's three lines and the caption read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the caption is tail-free and the three balloons hang from the boy. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: he wakes with an empty pocket, he stands in an empty yard under a shut window, he rides out after the man. If the empty yard under the shut window is not the largest image the eye finds, or the pocket's emptiness does not read, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Rochefort appears
Any figure with a scar, a red feather, or a black horse on this page is blocking; the point of the page is that he is gone.

### C4 — d'Artagnan or the horse fails identity
The clean-shaven black-haired boy in ochre and the buttercup-yellow horse must be the same as on the pages before. A moustache, a cloak, or another horse colour is blocking.

### C5 — Bloodless or state fails
No blood, bandage, or wound on the boy's head. The turned-out pocket must read as empty. The stall must read as empty.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated boy, two horses, gross anatomy on the hand at the pocket, or a balloon across the boy's face is blocking. Straw and dusk texture are not.
