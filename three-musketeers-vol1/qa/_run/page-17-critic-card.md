## Page intent

## PAGE 17

Dramatic. A narrow street, a narrow house, a door at the foot of a steep wooden stair. Constance on the doorstep in grey and white, a small gold crown on her apron bib, keys at her waist, a basket on her arm, and the boy on the cobbles with the yellow horse and his hat in his hands. He says Athos sent him for the attic; two livres a week and a steep stair, can he pay it; not this week, next week he will be famous; everyone who comes to Paris says that. The stair, from the bottom: she has gone up ahead with the candle and turned to look down, he is at the foot looking up, and the crown on her apron catches the light; he asks about it; she dresses the Queen every morning and lets attics to Gascons who can't pay. The attic: a window, a bed, a table, a nail; he hangs his father's sword on the nail and promises to pay double; Constance, double is four livres, goodnight. The dominant turn is the attic, and the girl who dresses the Queen, and the boy finished the moment she looked down the stair. The reader turns because he is finished and she has already gone downstairs.

>

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 17 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All eight lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the boy's four hang from the boy and Constance's four from the girl in the cap; in the four-line doorstep exchange each reply sits after the line it answers. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the boy asks for the attic and cannot pay, she looks down the stair at him with a crown on her apron, he hangs his sword on the nail and she says goodnight. If the stair with her looking down and him looking up is not the largest image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Constance fails identity
Small, round-faced, freckled, dark curls under a white cap, grey dress, white apron with a small gold crown, keys, basket. Finery, a crown on her head, jewels, or blonde hair is blocking.

### C4 — d'Artagnan fails identity
Clean-shaven, black-haired, ochre, no cloak, the too-long sword. A moustache or cassock is blocking.

### C5 — Object state fails
The sword must end on the nail in the attic; the apron crown must read as a small emblem; the horse in panel one must be yellow. Exact attic furniture is nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated Constance or boy, a third figure in the house, gross anatomy on the hand with the candle or the hand at the nail, or a balloon across a face is blocking.
