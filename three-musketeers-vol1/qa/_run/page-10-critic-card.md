## Page intent

## PAGE 10

Dramatic. A third corner. Aramis, slim, blond, immaculate, stands with two blue cloaks by a wall with one boot planted on a white lace handkerchief bearing a small embroidered monogram. The boy, slowing at last, sees it, stoops, and pulls it out from under the boot with the observation that it is a lady's. Aramis takes it between two fingers, icy, denying he dropped anything, that it is his, that he has seen it, and that he was standing on it on purpose; the boy asks then why he is taking it. Two fingers at his moustache, perfectly polite, Aramis explains the boy has embarrassed him and will have to be killed at two o'clock; two is free, noon and one are taken; how busy you are. The dominant turn is the handkerchief and the appointment at two. Aramis owns the page's manners; the boy owns its innocence. The reader turns because that is three duels on one field and not one friend in the world. The two listening cloaks are scenery.

**Location:** a Paris street, a third corner.

two blue cloaks looking at the monogram on it, then at Aramis.

>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then why are you taking it?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Aramis, two fingers at his moustache, perfectly polite.

> **ARAMIS:** "Because you have embarrassed me in front of my friends, so I shall have to kill you. Two o'clock, behind the Carmelites?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Two is free. Noon and one are taken."
>
> **ARAMIS:** "How busy you are."

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All six lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; Aramis's three from the slim blond man, the boy's three from the boy; in each exchange the reply sits after the line it answers. The two listening Musketeers carry no balloon. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the boy pulls a lace handkerchief from under a boot, the blond man denies it and takes it, and sets a duel for two o'clock. If the handkerchief passing from boot to fingers is not the image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Aramis fails identity or collides with d'Artagnan
Aramis must be slim, blond, with a small neat moustache, lace collar, blue cassock, book at the belt; the boy must be black-haired and clean-shaven in ochre. If either could be the other, or Aramis has a beard or dark hair, blocking.

### C4 — The handkerchief fails
It must be a white lace handkerchief, first under a boot then between two fingers; a crown emblem or legible letters on it is blocking. The exact monogram shape is nonblocking.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated Aramis, a third listener, gross anatomy on the two fingers holding the handkerchief, or text across a face is blocking.
