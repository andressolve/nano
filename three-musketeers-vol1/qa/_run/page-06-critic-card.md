## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 6 — card

### C1 — Caption transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, tail-free, with no other text anywhere on the page including on signs, bridges, or shop fronts. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The page is not Paris seen from above by a small boy
The eye must find a whole city of stacked roofs, a river, and bridges with houses on them, and then a small boy on a yellow horse looking at it with his hat off. If the boy is large and the city is a backdrop, or the page splits into panels, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — d'Artagnan or the horse fails identity
The small rider must still be a clean-shaven boy in ochre with a grey hat in his hand on a buttercup-yellow horse. A wrong horse colour is blocking; face detail at this size is nonblocking so long as nothing contradicts him.

### C4 — A locked absentee appears
Any figure in the crowd with a red feather and scar, a blue cassock with a white cross, or a red cassock is blocking on this page.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated rider, a corrupted city block that reads as a second image, or text colliding with the rider is blocking. Crowd repetition and roof texture are not.
