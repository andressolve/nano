## Page intent

## PAGE 4

Dramatic. The same yard. d'Artagnan at the foot of the inn stairs with his sword up, shouting up the stairwell, does not see the two inn men behind him, one with a stool raised. The stool comes down across the back of his head with one sound, and he goes forward on his face, the sword skidding. Then the man with the scar has come down after all: he crouches by the boy, who is out cold face down in the dirt, and draws the folded cream letter with its red seal out of the inside of the boy's coat. Standing, he reads the address to the light, says the Musketeers, of course, and opens his own black coat to put it away, telling the men to put the boy in the straw. The dominant turn is the scar reading the address and pocketing the letter. Rochefort owns the page; the boy is on the ground for all of it. The reader turns because the man with the scar has the letter and the boy does not know it yet. The knock is a knock, not a wound: no blood, ever.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
The boy's shout, the sound cue, and Rochefort's three lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the boy's line hangs from the boy, Rochefort's three from Rochefort; the address he reads aloud is a balloon and no words appear on the letter itself. The run length of the K in the sound cue is nonblocking. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the boy shouts up the stair, a stool knocks him flat, the scarred man takes a sealed letter out of the boy's coat, reads it, and puts it in his own. If the eye does not find the letter leaving the boy's coat as the page's event, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Bloodless fails
Any blood, cut, bruise, or wound on the boy is blocking. A stool falling and a boy face down and still is the whole injury.

### C4 — Rochefort fails identity
The one scar across a cheek from temple to mouth (which cheek is nonblocking), thin moustache with no beard, all black with one red feather, on every panel he is in. A missing scar or a beard is blocking.

### C5 — Letter state fails
The letter must be a folded cream paper with an unbroken red wax seal, coming out of the boy's coat and going into Rochefort's. A broken seal, an open sheet, or legible writing is blocking. Exact size is nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Exactly two hands on the letter action; a duplicated boy, a third inn man where two were staged, or gross anatomy on Rochefort's reading hand is blocking. Straw, dirt, and stair texture are not.
