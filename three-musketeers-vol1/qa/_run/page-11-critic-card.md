## Page intent

## PAGE 11

Illustrated prose, two locations because the memory is the turn's other half: a stone bench by the river, and the farm gate remembered. The dominant image is the boy alone on the bench with his sword across his knees and his hat pushed back, the city going about its business behind him and a church clock over the roofs at a quarter to twelve. The smaller image is warm and far: the farm gate, his father's hand on the horse's neck. The prose counts what he has, remembers the rule and that his father never said what to do with three, and sends him to the field at a quarter to twelve because he said he would. The dominant turn is an hour on a bench with three duels and no friend, and standing up anyway. d'Artagnan owns the page alone. The reader turns because he is walking to a field where three men are waiting to fight him, because he said so. The river and the crowd stay subordinate to the boy and the clock.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** D'Artagnan alone on a stone bench by the river,

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the grey river, never over the boy)*
> "He sat on a bench by the river for an hour and counted what he had. A horse, in a stable he could not pay for. A sword. Three duels, at noon, at one, and at two, with the three best swordsmen in Paris, if the way the other blue cloaks looked at them meant anything, none of whom he had wanted to fight. The man with the scar had got away again. The letter was gone. The Captain would never see him.
>
> 'Never refuse a fight,' his father had said, at the gate, with his hand on the horse. He had not said what to do when you had accepted three.
>
> At a quarter to twelve d'Artagnan stood up, put his hat on straight, and went to the field behind the Carmelites, because he had said he would."

**Why you turn the page:** he is walking to a field where three men are waiting to fight him, because he said he would.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 11 — card

### C1 — Prose transcription
The prose field reads exactly, all three paragraphs in order, once, from the 600 × 900 proof, over plain ground and never over the boy's face or the clock, with no other text on the page including on the clock face. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The bench does not own the page
The eye must find a boy alone on a bench by a river with a sword across his knees, and a clock near noon over the roofs, before it finds the small warm memory of the gate. If the memory competes, or the page reads as a comic strip, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — d'Artagnan fails identity
Clean-shaven, black hair, ochre, grey hat pushed back, the too-long sword. A moustache or a cloak is blocking.

### C4 — The memory contradicts page two
The remembered gate must show the grey-haired father and the yellow horse; another horse colour or a bearded Musketeer-looking father is blocking. Softness and distance are expected.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated boy on the quay, a clock with garbled numerals that read as text, or the prose field over a face is blocking. Crowd and river texture are not.
