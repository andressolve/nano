## Page intent

## PAGE 22

Dramatic. A red room with one candle and no window. The Cardinal in a chair: a thin man in a red robe and red skullcap, a short pointed grey beard, a grey cat on his lap, one long hand stroking it; on the black desk before him a folded cream letter with a broken red seal, the letter from Meung. Rochefort standing, wet from the rain, hat in his hands. A caption names the Cardinal and says the scar and every red cloak answer to him. The girl got away, a boy stopped them, the Gascon; the Cardinal has the father's letter, does not need tonight's, has read the Queen's letters for a year and knows what she sent: twelve diamond studs, the King's gift, on the Duke of Buckingham's shoulder; go to London, bring two, only two. Why not all twelve? He would miss twelve; nobody counts to two. And then? And then the King will give a ball. The dominant turn is the order: two diamonds, only two, and a ball. The Cardinal owns the page without raising his voice. The reader turns because he knows what a ball has to do with it and you do not yet. This is the heaviest page in the book: nine strings; lanes before faces.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 22 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
The caption and all eight lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the Cardinal's five hang from the seated man in red and Rochefort's three from the standing man in black; each reply sits after the line it answers; the caption is tail-free; nothing on the desk or maps is legible. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the scar reports failure to a seated man in red, the man says what the Queen sent and orders two diamonds only, and answers the last question with a ball. If the seated red figure with the cat and the wet standing man is not the largest image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — The Cardinal fails identity
Thin, grey pointed beard, red robe and red skullcap, seated with a grey cat under his hand, never standing. A dark beard, hair to the shoulder, standing, or a sword is blocking; the cat missing is blocking.

### C4 — Rochefort fails identity or collides with the Cardinal
One scar across a cheek from temple to mouth (which cheek is nonblocking), thin black moustache, black clothes, hat in hand, standing and wet. If he could be taken for the man in the chair, blocking.

### C5 — The letter state fails
A folded cream letter with a broken red seal must lie plainly on the desk; a sealed letter, a blue-sealed letter, or legible writing is blocking. Exact position on the desk is nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated Cardinal, two cats, a window or daylight in the red room, gross anatomy on the stroking hand, or a balloon across either face is blocking.
