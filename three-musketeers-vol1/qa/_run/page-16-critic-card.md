## Page intent

## PAGE 16

Dramatic. Toward evening, a Paris street: four abreast, filling it, walking back into the city, Porthos with one huge arm round the boy's neck, Aramis dusting his cuffs, Athos on the outside with his good hand on his pommel as he walks, blue cloaks making way. The boy is carried along in the middle, grinning, hair in his eyes. Porthos wants to know if everyone saw him put the guard in the trough; Aramis says all Paris saw it and pride goeth; Athos says pride went into the trough and the boy fought well. A step apart, Athos asks where the boy is sleeping; a horse and no money, so with the horse. Porthos spreads his arms: tonight the boy eats with them and Porthos is paying, well, Aramis is paying; in theory. The dominant turn is four abreast and the boy's name in the three men's mouths. The reader turns because he came to Paris with a letter and nobody to give it to, and is walking home with three friends.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each hanging by its tail from its owner: Porthos's two from the huge red-moustached man, Aramis's two from the slim blond, Athos's two from the tall bearded man in the sling, the boy's one from the boy. Passing Musketeers carry no balloon. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The eye must find four men walking abreast toward the reader with the boy in the middle and an arm round his neck, before anything else. If the four are small, separated, or walking away, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — The three Musketeers collide
Porthos huge and red, Aramis slim and blond, Athos tall and dark with the LEFT sling. Any two merging is blocking.

### C4 — d'Artagnan fails identity
Clean-shaven, black hair in his eyes, ochre, no cloak, grinning in the middle. A moustache or a cassock on him is blocking.

### C5 — Sling or bloodless fails
The sling still on Athos's LEFT arm on this page; no blood or bandage on anyone else.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A fifth companion, a duplicated Musketeer, gross anatomy on Porthos's arm round the boy, or a balloon across a face is blocking. Street crowd is not.
