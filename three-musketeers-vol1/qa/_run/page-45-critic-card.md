## Page intent

## PAGE 45

Dramatic. The Cardinal standing, for the only time in the book, and turning toward the long doors; in the last of them, a boy with mud to the waist, hat in hand, hair in his eyes, looking straight back at him across the whole ballroom. Who is that boy? The Gascon, from Meung. The Cardinal's hand takes the folded cream letter with the broken red seal from the arm of the chair beside him, unfolds it, and reads the name at the bottom: ah, the letter, a father asking a favour for a son; d'Artagnan. The dominant turn is the Cardinal standing, the boy in the doorway, and the name read off the stolen letter. The Cardinal owns the page; the boy owns the doorway. The reader turns because the most powerful man in France has just learned his name and said it quietly. The name spoken is a balloon; nothing on the letter is legible.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 45 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All four lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the Cardinal's three hang from the standing man in red and Rochefort's one from the slung man behind him; the name is a balloon and no legible writing appears on the letter. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the Cardinal stands and turns to see a muddy boy in the far doorway, then reads the boy's name off a letter with a broken seal. If the standing red figure and the small boy in the doorway are not the image the eye finds first, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — The Cardinal fails identity or is seated
Thin, grey pointed beard, red robe and skullcap, STANDING and turned; seated on this page is blocking because this is the only time he stands.

### C4 — The boy in the doorway fails
A clean-shaven black-haired boy in muddy ochre with his hat in his hand must be visible in the last door, looking back. A missing boy or a boy in a cassock is blocking.

### C5 — The letter fails
A folded cream letter with a broken red seal, taken from the chair arm and unfolded; a blue-sealed letter, a sealed letter, or legible words is blocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated Cardinal, a second scar-man, gross anatomy on the hand unfolding the letter, or a balloon across a face is blocking.
