## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 2 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the farm gate, and the road north.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The gate of a small stone farm at dawn. A
grey-haired man in a worn coat with one hand flat on the yellow horse's neck,
looking up at the boy in the saddle. The boy is looking down at him. Between
them, in the father's other hand, a folded cream letter with a red wax seal.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The road, days of it: the yellow horse going up a long hill
in rain, and the same horse coming down the far side in sun, the boy's hat
pulled low.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the pale sky above the gate, never over the faces)*
> "His father gave him three things at the gate. The horse, which was old and yellow and had been in the family longer than the boy had. The sword, which had been his father's, and was too long for him. And a letter, folded and sealed, to the Captain of the King's Musketeers in Paris, who had been his father's friend once, when they were both young and had swords.
>
> 'Never sell the horse,' his father said. 'Never refuse a fight. And put that letter in the Captain's hand yourself.'
>
> The boy's name was d'Artagnan. He was eighteen. He rode north for eleven days, and the whole way he practiced what he would say."

**Why you turn the page:** he has a letter, a sword, a horse, and a rule about fights, and Paris is full of fights.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Prose transcription
The prose field reads exactly, all three paragraphs in order, once, from the 600 × 900 proof, placed over plain ground and never over faces or the letter, with no other text on the page. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The gate does not own the page
The eye must find first a father at a horse's head handing a sealed letter up to a boy in the saddle, and only second the road. If the road strip competes with the gate, or the letter is not plainly a sealed letter changing hands, the turn has not landed. Judge by what the eye finds, never by area.

### C3 — d'Artagnan or the horse fails identity
The boy must be the same clean-shaven black-haired boy in ochre from the page before, on the same buttercup-yellow horse. A bearded or moustached rider, or a horse of another colour, is blocking.

### C4 — The letter is in the wrong state
The letter must be a folded cream paper with a red wax seal, whole. An opened letter, a broken seal, or a letter with legible words is blocking. Its exact size in the hand is nonblocking.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
Duplicated figures, a father with a Musketeer's or cardinal's look, gross hand anatomy on the two focal hands, or text over a face is blocking. Rain texture and hillside detail are not.
