## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 27 — card

### C1 — Prose transcription
The prose field reads exactly, both paragraphs in order, once, from the 600 × 900 proof, over plain ground and never over the riders or the sewing hands, with no other text on the page including on the gate. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The gate does not own the page
The eye must find first four mounted riders departing at sunrise with the boy in the middle, and second the small warm image of a girl sewing a letter into a coat. A visible great arch strengthens the threshold, but missing or indistinct gate stonework is nonblocking when the four riders, sunrise, departure action, and exact prose make the Saint-Denis crossing effortless to understand. If the dominant image no longer reads as departure, the sewing competes, or the page reads as a comic strip, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — The four fail identity
Four riders: the clean-shaven boy in ochre on a bay, the huge red-plumed Porthos, the slim white-plumed Aramis, the tall bearded Athos without a sling. A fifth rider, a missing one, or the yellow horse is blocking.

### C4 — Constance or the letter fails
The girl in the cap sewing a small blue-sealed letter into an ochre coat; a red-sealed letter or a girl in finery is blocking.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
Gross anatomy on the sewing hands, a duplicated rider, or the prose field over a face is blocking. Gate stonework and sky are not.
