## PAGE 11 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — a bench by the river, and the farm gate remembered.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** D'Artagnan alone on a stone bench by the river,
his sword across his knees, hat pushed back, the city going about its business
behind him. A church clock over the roofs shows a quarter to twelve.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Small and warm: the farm gate, his father's hand on the
horse's neck.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the grey river, never over the boy)*
> "He sat on a bench by the river for an hour and counted what he had. A horse, in a stable he could not pay for. A sword. Three duels, at noon, at one, and at two, with the three best swordsmen in Paris, if the way the other blue cloaks looked at them meant anything, none of whom he had wanted to fight. The man with the scar had got away again. The letter was gone. The Captain would never see him.
>
> 'Never refuse a fight,' his father had said, at the gate, with his hand on the horse. He had not said what to do when you had accepted three.
>
> At a quarter to twelve d'Artagnan stood up, put his hat on straight, and went to the field behind the Carmelites, because he had said he would."

**Why you turn the page:** he is walking to a field where three men are waiting to fight him, because he said he would.

## PAGE 12 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the field behind the Carmelite convent.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Noon. A meadow of long grass behind a high wall of yellow
stone, one big tree, a stone trough by the wall. ATHOS, right, under the
tree, arm in the sling, his good hand resting on the pommel of his sword,
point on the ground. D'ARTAGNAN, left, arriving, hat off.

> **ATHOS:** "You came. Most boys don't."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I said I would. Your friends aren't here."
>
> **ATHOS:** "They are."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Porthos and Aramis coming through a gap in the wall, and
stopping dead at the sight of the boy.

> **PORTHOS:** "That's my one o'clock!"
>
> **ARAMIS:** "That is my two o'clock."
>
> **ATHOS:** "He's my noon."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** The three of them looking at one another, and
then laughing: Porthos with his head back, Aramis behind his hand, Athos with
one corner of his mouth. The boy, not laughing. And behind all four of them,
coming through the gap in the wall: five red cloaks with white crosses, and in
front of them, in black, the scar.

> **PORTHOS:** "All three of us? In one afternoon? Athos, I like him."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Gentlemen. Duelling is forbidden. The Cardinal's guards will take your swords."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "And the Gascon. I'll take him myself."

**Why you turn the page:** five red cloaks against three blue, one of them one-armed, and the scar has just claimed the boy.

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the field behind the Carmelite convent.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Athos, Porthos, and Aramis drawing, in a line, facing the
five guards and Rochefort. D'Artagnan off to one side between the two groups,
his sword still in its sheath.

> **ATHOS:** "Three of us, five of them, and one of me is in a sling. It'll do."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Boy, stand back. This isn't your fight. You're not one of us."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** D'Artagnan drawing his father's sword and
stepping into the line beside Athos.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I haven't got the cloak. I've got the sword, and I've got the heart, and I'm standing here."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Four of us."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Athos looking down at him, properly, for the first time.

> **ATHOS:** "What's your name?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "d'Artagnan."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Then, d'Artagnan: the one on the left is yours. Don't get hurt. I'd have to explain it."

**Why you turn the page:** he has picked a side, and it is the side with fewer swords.

## PAGE 14 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the field behind the Carmelite convent.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The whole fight in one picture, top to bottom.
Porthos, huge, lifting a red-cloaked guard bodily off the ground and into the
trough. Aramis's blade pinning a guard's hat to the wall through the brim, the
guard bareheaded and astonished beneath it. Athos, one-handed, stepping aside
from a lunge so neatly that the guard runs straight into the tree. And in the
middle, fast, the boy in ochre, his hair flying, driving a guard backward
across the grass with the guard's own sword already lying behind him. No
blood anywhere. Nobody hurt. Everybody losing.

> **SOUND** *(the trough)*
> "SPLOSH"
>
> **SOUND** *(the hat)*
> "THOK"

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the grass)*
> "It was the best fight of his life."

**Why you turn the page:** four against five, and the five are losing, and the scar has not drawn yet.

## PAGE 15 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the field behind the Carmelite convent.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Rochefort's sword out at last. D'Artagnan turning from his
beaten guard to face him. Left, the boy; right, the scar.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Meung. The inn yard. You laughed at my horse and you took my letter."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "I took a letter. I don't remember a boy."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Steel on steel, the boy fast, the man surprised.
Behind them the guards are leaving: one wet, one hatless, one holding an arm
that has been pulled, all of them going through the gap in the wall while
Porthos waves them off with his hat.

> **SOUND** *(at the blades)*
> "TSANG"
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Where did a farm boy learn that?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "From my father. Where did you learn to run?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Rochefort backing to the gap in the wall, sword up, alone
now, his guards gone through ahead of him.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Twice you've drawn on me, boy. Twice is enough for one week."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "The third time, you stay."

**Why you turn the page:** the scar is gone again, but this time he had to run.

## PAGE 16 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a Paris street, toward evening.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** Four abreast, filling the street, walking back
into the city: Porthos with one huge arm round the boy's neck, Aramis dusting
his cuffs, Athos on the outside, his good hand on his pommel as he walks.
Blue cloaks make way for them. D'Artagnan is carried along in the middle,
grinning, hair in his eyes.

> **PORTHOS:** "Did you see me put him in the trough? Did you see it? He went in like a sack of flour."
>
> **ARAMIS:** "We all saw it, Porthos. Paris saw it. As the Fathers tell us, pride goeth."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Pride went into the trough. The boy fought well."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Athos and d'Artagnan a step apart from the other two.

> **ATHOS:** "Where are you sleeping tonight, d'Artagnan?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I've got a horse and no money. So, with the horse."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Porthos spreading his arms wide; Aramis touching his
moustache.

> **PORTHOS:** "Not tonight. Tonight you eat with us, and Porthos is paying. Well. Aramis is paying."
>
> **ARAMIS:** "In theory."

**Why you turn the page:** he came to Paris with a letter and nobody to give it to, and he is walking home with three friends.

## PAGE 17 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the house in the Rue des Fossoyeurs.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A narrow street, a narrow house, a door at the foot of a
steep wooden stair. CONSTANCE, right, on the doorstep: a grey dress, a white
apron with a small gold crown embroidered on the bib, a white cap with curls
escaping, a ring of keys at her waist, a covered basket on her arm. D'ARTAGNAN,
left, holding the yellow horse, hat in hand.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I'm d'Artagnan. Athos says you have an attic."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "Two livres a week, and the stair is steep. Can you pay it?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Not this week. Next week I'll be famous."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "Everyone who comes to Paris says that."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The stair, seen from the bottom. She has gone up
ahead with the candle and turned to look down at him; he is at the foot,
looking up. The gold crown on her apron catches the light.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "There's a crown on your apron."
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "I dress the Queen. Every morning, at the Louvre. Then I come home and let attics to Gascons who can't pay."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The attic: one window on the street, a bed, a table, a
nail in the beam. He is hanging his father's sword on the nail. She is in the
doorway.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I'll pay. I'll pay double. What's your name?"
>
> **CONSTANCE:** "Constance. Double is four livres. Goodnight."

**Why you turn the page:** he is finished, and she has already gone downstairs.

## PAGE 18 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the field at dawn, and the stair.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Dawn on the Carmelite field. Athos, the sling
gone, teaching the boy: blades crossed, and Athos's boot nudging the boy's
back foot into place. Neither is talking.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** The stair. Constance at the bottom with her basket and
her keys, looking up; the boy at the top, looking down.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the pale field, never over the fencers)*
> "Weeks went by, and the four of them were never apart. Athos taught him at dawn, on the field where they had met, and said almost nothing, and every morning the boy was harder to hit. Porthos told stories at dinner in which Porthos was always the hero, and paid for the dinner, and pretended Aramis had. Aramis read Latin, and said he would leave for the Church any day now, and never left. Athos offered once to walk him through the Musketeers' gate himself. The boy said not without the letter, and Athos said good, and did not offer again.
>
> And every evening, when Constance came home from the Louvre with her basket and her keys, she looked up the stair, and he was there, and neither of them said anything about it."

**Why you turn the page:** every evening she looks up the stair, and this is the last evening on which nothing happens.

## PAGE 19 · *dramatic*

**Location:** two — the attic window, and the street below.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Night, rain. Above: d'Artagnan at the open attic window
in his shirt, cleaning the sword. Below, in the street: a cart, a lantern on
a bracket, and four men in plain cloaks closing on Constance at her own door,
one hand reaching for her mouth, the basket falling.

> **CONSTANCE:** "Let go of me! Help! d'Art—"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** D'Artagnan going out of the window: not the
stair, the window. One hand on the sill, the sword in the other, dropping two
floors through the rain onto the roof of the cart.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Constance!"
>
> **SOUND** *(the cart roof)*
> "WHAM"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** He is on the ground between the men and the door, sword
out, hair in his eyes, the four men turning to him. Across the street, in a
dark doorway, a fifth figure in black with a red feather, arms folded, not
moving.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Four of you for one girl. Let her go, and it's only four of you for me."

**Why you turn the page:** one boy, four men, one lantern, and rain.

## PAGE 20 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the street outside the house.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The fight in the rain, one picture. The boy in
his white shirt a blur in the middle; one man already sitting in the puddle;
one running; one losing his sword into the air; the lantern knocked off its
bracket and falling, still lit; Constance flat against her own door with the
basket held in front of her; and across the street, in the doorway, the black
figure with the red feather, arms folded, watching.

> **SOUND** *(the lantern)*
> "KLANG"

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the wet cobbles)*
> "The lantern went into the puddle. After that nobody could see him, and he could see everyone."

**Why you turn the page:** the lantern is out, and the man in the doorway across the street has not moved.
