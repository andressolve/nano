## Page intent

## PAGE 31

Dramatic. Evening at the inn at Amiens, the next day; a caption says so and says the coin was good and the innkeeper had been told to say it was not. Athos and the boy dismounting in the yard; the innkeeper holding one of Athos's coins up to the light and shaking his head, and behind him, on cue, four red cloaks with white crosses coming into the yard. The boy: he says your coin is false, and there are red cloaks in the yard; Athos: there are always red cloaks in the yard. Inside, chaos: Athos backing down the cellar steps with his sword in one hand and a barrel rolling into the doorway behind him, red cloaks in the inn door, the boy at the back window with one leg over the sill looking back. Ride, d'Artagnan, not for me, for the letter; I'm not leaving you in a cellar. Athos at the cellar door with a barrel across it and guards on the stairs above, his face level: he has the wine and he has the door, they'll be a week getting him out. Ride. The dominant turn is the last friend behind a cellar door and the boy alone with the letter. Athos owns the page with the fewest words. The reader turns because the boy is alone now.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 31 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
The caption and all six lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; Athos's four hang from the tall bearded man and the boy's two from the boy; the single word Ride stands alone in its own balloon below Athos's longer line; the innkeeper and guards carry no balloon. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: a coin is refused as red cloaks enter the yard, Athos backs into the cellar with a barrel while the boy goes out the back window, Athos holds the cellar door and says ride. If Athos backing down the cellar steps with the boy at the window is not the largest image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Athos fails identity
Tall, dark shoulder-length hair, pointed beard, blue cassock, white plume, no sling, level face. A scar, black clothes, or a sling is blocking.

### C4 — d'Artagnan fails identity
Clean-shaven, black-haired, ochre, no cloak, at the window. A moustache is blocking.

### C5 — Guards, bloodless, or state fail
Exactly four red-cassocked guards with faces turned and no feather; no blood; a barrel across the cellar door in the last panel; nobody touched.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated Athos, a fifth guard, gross anatomy on the hand with the sword or the leg over the sill, or a balloon across a face is blocking.
