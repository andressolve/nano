## Page intent

## PAGE 21

Dramatic. The men are gone. Rain. The boy, breathing hard, sword lowered; Rochefort stepping out of the doorway into what light there is, hands empty, scar and red feather. Three times you've drawn on me, Gascon, and I'm still not staying; I said the third time you'd stay, draw; not in the rain and not for a seamstress, she isn't what I was sent for, she only carries it. He walks away up the street, unhurried, and the boy shouts after him: carries what? The doorway: Constance shaking, picking up the basket, the boy beside her wet through. What did he mean, what does she carry? The Queen's letters, she carries them for her, where and to whom she can't tell, it isn't hers to tell. Then don't tell me; I'll be upstairs, tonight and every night, until they stop coming. They won't stop. Then neither will I. The dominant turn is Rochefort's one word too many, the letters, and the boy staying anyway. The reader turns because the men were sent for the Queen's letters and the girl will not say where they go.

## Materiality threshold

blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or

focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All nine lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; Rochefort's two from the scarred man in black, the boy's five from the boy, Constance's two from the girl at the door; in the doorway exchange each reply sits after the line it answers and alternates sides. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the scar refuses to fight and says she only carries it, walks away, and the girl tells the boy it is the Queen's letters while he promises to stay. If the doorway exchange is not the largest image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Rochefort fails identity or draws
One scar across a cheek from temple to mouth (which cheek is nonblocking), thin moustache, black, red feather, hands empty, sword sheathed. A drawn sword, a beard, or a missing scar is blocking.

### C4 — Constance or d'Artagnan fails identity
The girl small in grey with the cap, shaking, with the basket; the boy clean-shaven in a white shirt, no doublet. Blocking if contradicted.

### C5 — Bloodless or continuity fails
No blood on anyone; the attackers gone. An attacker still present is blocking. Whether the fallen lantern is lit or dark, or visible at all, is nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated Rochefort, a third figure in the doorway, gross anatomy on the hands at the basket, or a balloon across a face is blocking.
