# Exact script — pages 1–10

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the road out of Gascony.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A white road between ochre hills, a line of
poplars, a hot morning sky. Riding toward the reader, filling the lower half
of the page: a boy of eighteen on an old buttercup-yellow horse, a grey felt
hat with a broken feather, an ochre doublet, and a sword far too long for
him hanging at his hip. Chin up. Behind him, very small, a farmhouse and a
gate. His shadow goes ahead of him down the road.

> **TITLE CARD** *(upper third, over the sky)*
> "THE THREE MUSKETEERS
>
> The Queen's Diamonds"

> **CAPTION** *(lower left, over the road)*
> "Gascony, in the south of France. April, 1625."

**Why you turn the page:** a boy this pleased with himself on a horse this yellow is going somewhere, and you want to know where.

---

## PAGE 2 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the farm gate, and the road north.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The gate of a small stone farm at dawn. A
grey-haired man in a worn coat with one hand flat on the yellow horse's neck,
looking up at the boy in the saddle. The boy is looking down at him. Between
them, in the father's other hand, a folded cream letter with a red wax seal.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** The road, days of it: the yellow horse going up a long hill
in rain, and the same horse coming down the far side in sun, the boy's hat
pulled low.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the pale sky above the gate, never over the faces)*
> "His father gave him three things at the gate. The horse, which was old and yellow and had been in the family longer than the boy had. The sword, which had been his father's, and was too long for him. And a letter, folded and sealed, to the Captain of the King's Musketeers in Paris, who had been his father's friend once, when they were both young and had swords.
>
> 'Never sell the horse,' his father said. 'Never refuse a fight. And put that letter in the Captain's hand yourself.'
>
> The boy's name was d'Artagnan. He was eighteen. He rode north for eleven days, and the whole way he practiced what he would say."

**Why you turn the page:** he has a letter, a sword, a horse, and a rule about fights, and Paris is full of fights.

---

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the inn yard at Meung.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A low inn yard of packed dirt: a well, a stone trough, a
stable door. D'ARTAGNAN, left, swinging down from the yellow horse at the
trough. Above him, right, a first-floor window with the shutters open, and
leaning on the sill a man in black with a red feather in his hat and a pale
scar from his left temple to the corner of his mouth, laughing down at the
horse. Two of the inn's men in the yard, laughing with him.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Look at that horse. Is it a horse? It's the colour of a buttercup."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Somebody tell the boy. You don't ride a flower to Paris."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** D'Artagnan turned to face the window, hand on
the hilt, hair in his eyes, looking up. The man on the sill looking down,
amused.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You're laughing at my horse, sir. Come down and laugh at me."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "At you? I don't fight children. I don't fight anyone before dinner."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** D'Artagnan with his sword out, already halfway to the inn
door, the two inn men behind him picking up a stool and a broom handle.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then you'll be late for dinner."

**Why you turn the page:** the man with the scar is not coming down, so the boy is going up.

---

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the inn yard at Meung.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** D'Artagnan at the foot of the inn stairs, sword up,
shouting at the stairwell. Behind him, unseen by him, the two inn men, one
with the stool raised.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Come down, or I come up!"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The stool across the back of his head. He goes forward onto
his face in the dirt, the sword skidding.

> **SOUND** *(across the panel)*
> "KRAK"

**Panel 3 — dominant, 40%.** The man with the scar has come down after all.
He crouches by the boy, who is out cold, face down in the dirt, and draws a
folded cream letter with a red seal out of the inside of the boy's coat.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "What does a farm boy carry that's worth all that shouting?"

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Standing, the letter held up to the light, reading the
address; then his other hand already opening his own black coat to put it
away.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "'To the Captain of the King's Musketeers.' The Musketeers. Of course."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "Put him in the straw. Let's see how far a Gascon gets on his manners."

**Why you turn the page:** the man with the scar has the letter, and the boy does not know it yet.

---

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the inn yard at Meung.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Evening. The stable. D'Artagnan sitting up in the straw,
one hand on the back of his head, his hat beside him; his other hand inside
his coat, and the inside pocket turned out, empty. The yellow horse looking
down at him from the next stall.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "The letter. My father's letter."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The yard at dusk. D'Artagnan standing in the
middle of it, looking up at the window: the shutters are closed. Beside him
the stable door stands open on one empty stall. Nobody is laughing now;
nobody is in the yard at all.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the dark wall)*
> "The window was shut. The black horse was gone, and everyone in the inn had watched it go."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "He took it. The stable boy says he rode for Paris at noon. With my letter."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** D'Artagnan in the saddle, the yellow horse at a gallop out
of the yard gate, hat jammed on, the road north in front of him.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then so do I."

**Why you turn the page:** the letter that was going to open Paris is in the pocket of the man he most wants to fight.

---

## PAGE 6 · *spectacle*

**Location:** Paris.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** From the top of the last hill: Paris. Grey-blue
slate roofs stacked to the horizon, a brown river, stone bridges with houses
built on them, spires, smoke, and a road going down into it full of carts
and people. In the foreground, small, the boy on the yellow horse, hat off,
staring.

> **CAPTION** *(lower right, over the road)*
> "Paris. He had never seen more than three hundred people in one place. Here they were all at once, and none of them looked up."

**Why you turn the page:** nobody here knows his name, and he has lost the one thing that would have told them.

---

## PAGE 7 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Musketeers' gate.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A great arched carriage door in a stone wall, standing open
on a courtyard full of blue cloaks, horses, and noise. In the arch, a porter
with a halberd, not moving. D'ARTAGNAN, left, on the step, hat in his hands,
small against the door.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I've come to see the Captain. My father was his friend. I had a letter, but a man stole it—"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The door closing in his face. Behind it, blue cloaks
laughing.

> **CAPTION** *(over the closing door)*
> "No letter. No name anyone knew. No Captain. The door closed the way doors close on boys."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** D'Artagnan turned round on the step, and across
the street, on a black horse, in black, with one red feather in his hat: the
scar. Looking straight at him. Smiling.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You! The man from Meung! Stay where you are!"

**Panel 4 — small.** Rochefort turning the black horse into the crowd without
hurry. D'Artagnan already running, one hand on the hilt, and at the edge of
the panel a tall blue-cloaked shoulder coming round the corner into his path.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "Still chasing, boy? On foot? Then you'll never catch me."

**Why you turn the page:** he is running, and there is a man in his way.

# MOVEMENT II — THREE DUELS AT NOON · pp 8–16

---

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a Paris street.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** D'Artagnan at a full run round the corner into a very tall
Musketeer in a blue cassock with a white cross, whose left arm is in a black
sling. The boy's shoulder goes straight into the sling. The tall man goes
white.

> **SOUND** *(at the collision)*
> "WHUMP"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** ATHOS, right, very tall, very still, grey eyes,
his good hand holding his bad shoulder. D'ARTAGNAN, left, already two steps
past him, looking back over his own shoulder for the vanished scar.

> **ATHOS:** "That was my shoulder. It was hurt before you arrived."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I'm sorry, sir! I'm chasing a man! I can't stop!"
>
> **ATHOS:** "You've stopped."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Face to face. The boy hot, the man level, a head taller.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You want satisfaction? Name the place. I never refuse a fight."
>
> **ATHOS:** "The field behind the Carmelite convent. Noon. Bring a friend to stand with you."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I don't have any."

**Why you turn the page:** he has a duel at noon with the tallest man in Paris, and he has lost the scar.

---

## PAGE 9 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a Paris street, a second corner.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** D'Artagnan running again. Ahead, filling the middle of the
street, an enormous Musketeer, PORTHOS, red-brown curls and a great waxed
moustache, telling a story to two blue cloaks with his own cloak thrown back
to show a shoulder-baldric embroidered in gold. The boy, ducking past, goes
straight into the cloak.

> **PORTHOS:** "—and I said, if the Cardinal wants my sword, he can come and fetch it himself—"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** D'Artagnan on the ground, tangled in the cloak.
Porthos turning, and the back of the baldric now in plain view of the whole
street: plain buff leather, no gold at all. The two blue cloaks staring at it.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Your baldric. It's only gold in front."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Porthos, scarlet, fists on hips, towering over the boy.

> **PORTHOS:** "Nobody sees the back of my baldric and tells the street about it."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then fight me. One o'clock, behind the Carmelites. I'm taken at noon."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Taken? Who has you at noon?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "A tall man with a bad shoulder."
>
> **PORTHOS:** "Athos! Ha! Then I'll take whatever he leaves."

**Why you turn the page:** two duels, an hour apart, and he is still running.

---

## PAGE 10 · *dramatic*

**Location:** a Paris street, a third corner.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** ARAMIS, slim, blond, immaculate, standing with two blue
cloaks by a wall, one boot planted on a white lace handkerchief with a small
embroidered monogram. D'Artagnan, slowing at last, sees it, stoops, and pulls it
out from under the boot.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Sir, you've dropped your handkerchief. It's a lady's, I think."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Aramis taking it between two fingers, icy. The
two blue cloaks looking at the monogram on it, then at Aramis.

> **ARAMIS:** "I dropped nothing. That handkerchief is not mine, I have never seen it, and I was standing on it by accident."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Then why are you taking it?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Aramis, two fingers at his moustache, perfectly polite.

> **ARAMIS:** "Because you have embarrassed me in front of my friends, so I shall have to kill you. Two o'clock, behind the Carmelites?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Two is free. Noon and one are taken."
>
> **ARAMIS:** "How busy you are."

**Why you turn the page:** three duels, one field, and not one friend in the world.
