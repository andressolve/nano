## Page intent

## PAGE 1

Spectacle. A white road between ochre hills under a hot April sky, and riding toward the reader, filling the lower half of the page, a boy of eighteen on an old buttercup-yellow horse: a grey hat with a broken feather, an ochre doublet, and a sword far too long for him. Chin up, entirely pleased with himself. Far behind, small, a farmhouse and a gate. His shadow runs ahead of him down the road. The title card sits over the sky; one caption names Gascony and the year. The dominant turn is simply this: a road out of Gascony, a boy, a yellow horse, a sword too long for him. d'Artagnan owns the page and the horse shares it. The reader turns because a boy this pleased with himself on a horse this yellow is going somewhere. The landscape is scenery; the horse's colour and the boy's chin are the page.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Title card and caption transcription
The two-line title card and the one caption read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, tail-free, in that order top to bottom, with no other text anywhere on the page. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The page is not a boy on a yellow horse coming at you
The eye must find one image that is plainly a boy on a bright yellow horse riding toward the reader on a road out of hills, chin up. If the boy is small in a landscape, or the horse is not unmistakably yellow, or the page reads as several panels, the turn has not landed. Landscape detail and sky are nonblocking.

### C3 — d'Artagnan does not read as d'Artagnan
The rider must be a clean-shaven boy with black hair in his eyes, an ochre doublet, a grey hat with a broken feather, and a sword visibly too long for him. A beard, a moustache, a cloak, or a man's age is blocking. Exact patching and boot detail are nonblocking.

### C4 — The horse is the wrong horse
The horse must be old, calm, and buttercup yellow, the only such animal in the book. A brown, white, grey, or cream horse is blocking. Tack detail is nonblocking.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated rider, a horse with the wrong number of legs, gross anatomy on the boy's face or hands, or text colliding with the figure is blocking. Distant poplars, farmhouse, and road texture are not.
