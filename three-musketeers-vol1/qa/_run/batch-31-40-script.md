## PAGE 31 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the inn yard at Amiens.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Evening. ATHOS and D'ARTAGNAN dismounting in an inn yard.
The innkeeper holding one of Athos's coins up to the light and shaking his
head, and behind the innkeeper, coming into the yard on cue, four red cloaks
with white crosses.

> **CAPTION** *(over the inn wall)*
> "The next evening, at Amiens. The coin was good. The innkeeper had been told to say it was not."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "He says your coin is false! And there are red cloaks in the yard."
>
> **ATHOS:** "There are always red cloaks in the yard."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Inside the inn. Athos backing down the cellar
steps, sword in one hand, a barrel rolling into the doorway behind him. Red
cloaks in the inn door. D'Artagnan at the back window with one leg over the
sill, looking back.

> **ATHOS:** "Ride, d'Artagnan. Not for me. For the letter."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I'm not leaving you in a cellar!"

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Athos at the cellar door, a barrel across it, guards on the
stairs above him, his face perfectly level.

> **ATHOS:** "I have the wine and I have the door. They'll be a week getting me out."
>
> **ATHOS:** "Ride."

**Why you turn the page:** the last friend is behind a cellar door, and the boy is alone with the letter.

## PAGE 32 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the night road, and the sea.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** One rider on a night road under an enormous sky,
bent low, going hard.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** Dawn. The road cresting a long hill, and beyond it the
grey sea, gulls, and a harbour full of masts.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the night sky, never over the rider)*
> "He took the first horse in the back paddock and rode all night. He had never been so far from anyone.
>
> He counted them as he went, because it kept him awake. Porthos at Chantilly, with a stranger's hat. Aramis on the bridge, with six in front of him and his back to the rail. Athos behind a cellar door with a barrel across it, saying ride. Three men who had known him for a month, each of whom had stopped so that he could go on, and none of whom had asked what was in the letter.
>
> At dawn the road went up a long hill, and at the top of it was the sea. He had never seen the sea. It was bigger than Paris."

**Why you turn the page:** he has reached the sea alone, and everything now depends on a boat.

## PAGE 33 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the quay at Calais.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** A stone quay, a boat, a gangplank, gulls. Across the quay a
wooden barrier with a sergeant of guards at it, and nailed to the barrier a
printed notice. D'ARTAGNAN, left, off his horse, reading it.

> **PRINTED SIGN** *(on the barrier)*
> "THE HARBOUR IS CLOSED BY ORDER OF THE CARDINAL. NO ONE SAILS WITHOUT HIS PASS."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Closed. He's closed the sea."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** A second boat, coming in from England, its gangplank down.
Walking down it, in black with a red feather, showing a folded paper to the
sergeant, one hand on a small pouch inside his coat: the scar.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "The Cardinal's pass. I'm coming in, not going out."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Rochefort and d'Artagnan face to face on the
quay, the barrier between them. Rochefort has seen him. The pass is still in
his hand.

> **ROCHEFORT:** "The Gascon. On the sea road. What could a farm boy want in England?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "Your pass. I said the third time you'd stay. This is the fourth. You're staying."
>
> **ROCHEFORT:** "On a quay? In front of the sergeant? Gladly. Nobody stops the Cardinal's man."

**Why you turn the page:** the man with the scar has the only pass in Calais, and the boy has a sword.

## PAGE 34 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the quay at Calais.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The duel on the gangplank: the two of them on a
plank a foot wide over grey water, blades crossed high, the boy's hair flying,
Rochefort's hat already gone into the sea, gulls exploding upward, sailors and
the sergeant frozen along the quay.

> **SOUND** *(at the blades)*
> "TSANG"

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the water)*
> "Steel on a plank a foot wide, over the sea. His father had never taught him that. He learned it."

**Why you turn the page:** one of them is going into the water.

## PAGE 35 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the quay at Calais.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Rochefort in the harbour, streaming, his hat
floating beside him. D'Artagnan on the end of the plank, stooping to pick up
the folded pass from where it fell out of the black coat.

> **SOUND** *(the harbour)*
> "SPLOOSH"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "You stayed. I said you would."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The barrier. The boy holding the pass up to the sergeant,
who reads it, and looks past him at the man shouting in the harbour, and
reads it again, and lifts the bar. Behind them the first boat's sailors
casting off.

> **PRINTED PASS** *(in the sergeant's hand)*
> "Let the bearer cross. — The Cardinal"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "It says the bearer. You can read, and I'm the bearer."
>
> **CAPTION** *(beside the bar)*
> "The sergeant looked at the pass, and at the man in the harbour, and at the pass again, and decided that he could read."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** From the boat's rail, pulling out. On the harbour stairs,
Rochefort climbing out one-handed, soaked, his right arm held against his
chest, and opening the other fist to look at what he still has: two diamonds
catching the light. The boy, small at the rail, sees the glint and does not
know what it is.

> **CAPTION** *(over the grey water)*
> "The man came up the stairs holding one arm the way Athos had held his. The boy didn't know what was in the other hand. It caught the light, twice, and the hand closed."

**Why you turn the page:** the scar had two diamonds in his fist, and the boy is sailing away from them.

## PAGE 36 · *spectacle*

**Location:** London, Buckingham's palace.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A river of masts, and a white palace with a
marble stair running down to the water. At the foot of the stair, a boy with
mud to the waist, hat in one hand, holding up a small letter with a blue
crowned seal to a footman. At the top of the stair, in white satin and pearls,
a golden-haired man already coming down two steps at a time.

> **CAPTION** *(lower edge, over the water)*
> "London. Mud of two countries on him, the Queen's seal in his hand, and the Duke of Buckingham, the richest man in England, came down the stairs himself."

**Why you turn the page:** the Duke is coming down himself, and you want to know what the letter says.

## PAGE 37 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Buckingham's bedroom.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** A white and gold room. BUCKINGHAM, right, reading the
letter by the window, pulling off one glove with his teeth. D'ARTAGNAN, left,
dripping on the carpet.

> **BUCKINGHAM:** "She writes that the King wants the studs on Monday. And that the Cardinal is smiling. Who are you, boy?"
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "d'Artagnan. Nobody. I ride fast."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** A small table by the bed. A velvet casket, open:
twelve slots in a double row, ten diamonds, and two empty places, large and
plain to see. Buckingham's face going white. The boy looking in over his arm.

> **BUCKINGHAM:** "She gave them to me. I keep them here, in the room where I sleep, and nobody comes in."
>
> **BUCKINGHAM:** "Ten."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "There are two holes."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Buckingham straightening, both hands flat on the table.

> **BUCKINGHAM:** "Two are gone. Someone has been in this room."

**Why you turn the page:** someone has been in the Duke's bedroom, and the boy knows who.

## PAGE 38 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Buckingham's bedroom.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Buckingham pacing, the glove in his fist, thinking aloud.
The boy following him with his eyes. A small memory inset in the corner: a
masked man with a scar showing under the mask, bowing on a stair as the Duke
passes.

> **BUCKINGHAM:** "Three nights ago I gave a ball. Every door stood open. A masked man begged my pardon on the stair outside this door."
>
> **BUCKINGHAM:** "A scar under the mask."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "The man with the scar. The Cardinal's man. I fought him at Calais this morning."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "He climbed out of the harbour with something in his fist. It flashed twice."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Buckingham stopping dead, understanding.

> **BUCKINGHAM:** "Then I see it. She wears ten, and the Cardinal steps forward with the two she lacks."
>
> **BUCKINGHAM:** "Then she wears twelve. Send for my jeweler!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Buckingham with both hands on the boy's shoulders.

> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "It's Thursday. The ball is Monday."
>
> **BUCKINGHAM:** "Two days for the diamonds, two for the road. Sleep on the boat."
>
> **D'ARTAGNAN:** "I don't sleep."

**Why you turn the page:** a duke is going to make two diamonds in two days, and the boy has to carry twelve home in two more.

## PAGE 39 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the jeweler's workroom, and the water stair.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** An old jeweler at a bench with a loupe in his
eye and a small fire beside him, two new diamond studs in his tongs beside the
ten. Buckingham standing over him, not sitting. In a chair in the corner, the
boy asleep after all, hat over his face.

**Panel 2 — 45%.** The water stair. Buckingham putting the closed casket into
the boy's hands, and a boat waiting below.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the white wall, never over the bench)*
> "The jeweler was an old man who did not talk. He looked at the ten for a long time through his glass, and then he made two more, and it took him two days and two nights, and the Duke did not sit down for any of it. d'Artagnan, who did not sleep, slept in a chair.
>
> On Saturday night the jeweler laid the two new ones in the two empty places, and even the Duke could not tell which they were.
>
> At the water stair Buckingham put the box into the boy's hands and held on to it a moment longer than he needed to. 'Tell her,' he said, and stopped, and did not finish. 'Ride,' he said instead."

**Why you turn the page:** twelve diamonds in a box, and it is Saturday night, and the ball is Monday.

## PAGE 40 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the road from Calais, and the Paris gate.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The road south: one rider changing horses at a
post inn without dismounting properly, a blown horse behind him, a fresh one
under him, the sun coming up.

**Panel 2 — 40%.** Paris at sunset. The rider coming through the gate, and
beyond the roofs the long front of the Louvre with every window lit.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the road, never over the rider)*
> "The sea was rough and he was sick over the side and held the box the whole time. At Calais the same sergeant looked at the pass, and at the box, and did not ask.
>
> He changed horses five times. At one post inn a red cloak stood in the yard looking hard at every rider who came in, and the boy came in as a boy, muddy and yawning, and the red cloak looked past him for a Musketeer. He did not go through Amiens, or over the bridge, or past Chantilly, because he could not have stopped if his friends were there. He said their names at every milestone instead.
>
> The sun of Monday went down over Paris as he came through the gate, and every window of the Louvre was already lit, for the ball."

**Why you turn the page:** he is in Paris with the box, the ball has started, and the Queen is not yet wearing anything.
