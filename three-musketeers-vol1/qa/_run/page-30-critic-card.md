## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 30 — card

### C1 — Caption transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, tail-free, with no other text on the page. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The page is not a look back at a bridge held by one man
The eye must find two riders' backs in the foreground and, down the road, a small blue figure with his back to a bridge rail against six, and a riderless horse going home. If the bridge is large and the riders absent, or the page reads as panels, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Bloodless or the horse fails
No blood anywhere; the man in the road holds his arm; the horse is up and trotting, not down or hurt.

### C4 — The figures fail identity
The near riders must be the ochre boy and the tall blue Athos; the small figure on the bridge must read as the slim blue Musketeer; Porthos must not be on the page.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated bridge figure, a third near rider, or text colliding with a figure is blocking. Distant crowd and sky are not.
