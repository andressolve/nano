## Page intent

## PAGE 24

Dramatic. The attic. Constance takes a small letter sealed in blue wax with a crown out of her bodice and holds it in both hands, not yet offering it: the Queen wrote to the Duke tonight and told her to find someone to carry it to London, and there isn't anyone. There's me, are you asking? Face to face a step apart with the letter between them, she looks at him properly for the first time: two days' hard riding to the sea and the Cardinal owns every inn, she has no right to ask; that isn't no. He takes his father's sword down off the nail while she holds the letter out to him now: one boy against the Cardinal? one boy and three friends, you haven't met them; the kitchen door at the back of the Louvre, she will be in it every night until he comes. The dominant turn is the ask that is not made and the yes that answers it. Constance owns the letter; the boy owns the sword. The reader turns because she has asked without asking and he has said yes.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 24 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All seven lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; Constance's four hang from the girl holding the letter and the boy's three from the boy; each reply sits after the line it answers. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: she produces a small sealed letter and says there is nobody, he asks if she is asking, she says she has no right, he says that isn't no and takes his sword down while she holds the letter out. If the two of them face to face over the letter is not the largest image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — The letter is the wrong letter
The letter must be small with a BLUE wax seal; a cream letter with a red seal, a large sheet, or legible writing is blocking.

### C4 — Constance or d'Artagnan fails identity
The girl small in grey and white with the cap; the boy clean-shaven and black-haired in ochre. Blocking if contradicted.

### C5 — Object continuity fails
The sword must be in the boy's hands by the last panel; whether it is visible on the nail in the earlier panels is nonblocking. The letter must end the page held out to him.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Exactly two hands on the sword and two on the letter at the moments they are focal; a third figure, a duplicated girl, or a balloon over a face is blocking.
