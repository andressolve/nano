### C1 — Sound cue and caption transcription
The sound cue by the falling lantern and the one caption along the bottom read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, with no other text on the page. The run length of the cue's letters is nonblocking. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The fight is not one boy beating four in the rain
The eye must find one boy in a white shirt in the middle of four men who are falling, running, and losing their swords, a falling lantern, a girl against a door, and a still watcher across the street. If it reads as an even brawl, as panels, or the boy is one of a crowd, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Bloodless fails
Any blood, cut, or wound on anyone is blocking.

### C4 — Rochefort or Constance fails
The watcher in black with the red feather and scar, arms folded, must be present and still; Constance must be at the door with the basket. A watcher who fights, or a missing girl, is blocking.

### C5 — Count or state fails
Exactly four attackers, all losing; the boy in a shirt with no doublet. The lantern must be off its bracket: falling and still lit, or already lying in the puddle, lit or dark, both satisfy the caption; a lantern still hanging on its bracket is blocking. Five attackers is blocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated boy, gross anatomy on the boy's sword arm, or text colliding with a figure is blocking. Rain and cobble texture are not.
