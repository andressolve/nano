## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 15 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
The six lines and the sound cue read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; the boy's three lines hang from the boy and Rochefort's three from the scarred man in black; in each exchange the reply sits after the line it answers. Porthos and the guards carry no balloon. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: the boy names Meung to the scar's face, they fence while the guards run, the scar backs out alone with a count. If the crossed blades of boy and scar are not the largest image the eye finds, or the guards are not visibly leaving, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Rochefort fails identity or collides with the boy
One scar across a cheek from temple to mouth (which cheek is nonblocking), thin moustache, no beard, black, red feather; the boy clean-shaven in ochre. If either could be the other, or the scar is missing, blocking.

### C4 — Bloodless or weather fails
No blood, no wound, no blade in anyone; a wet guard, a hatless guard, and a guard holding his arm are the whole damage. The page is the same bright dry noon as the fight it continues: rain, storm light, puddles, mud, or soaked figures on this page are blocking, because they falsely imply a time jump between two sword strokes.

### C5 — Continuity fails
The guards must be leaving through the gap, not fighting; Porthos must be seeing the guards off rather than fencing (whether he waves his hat or an empty hand is nonblocking); Rochefort ends the page plainly escaping: through the gap in the wall and going, his exit unmistakable, so that no reader has to guess whether he got away. A guard still engaged, Rochefort still surrounded, or Rochefort still fencing in the last panel is blocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A duplicated boy or scar-man, gross anatomy on the two sword hands at the cross, or a balloon over a face is blocking.
