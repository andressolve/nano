## Page intent

## PAGE 41

Dramatic. A service door at the back of the Louvre, torchlight, kitchen noise behind it. Constance in the doorway in her grey with the apron off; the boy coming up out of the dark with mud to the waist and the closed casket under his arm. You're here; every night since you left she has stood in this door; is it—; twelve, count them. The casket open between them in torchlight, her lips moving over the double row, his eyes on her face and not on the box: ten, eleven, twelve, twelve; two of them are new, the scar cut two out at the Duke's ball and the Duke had two made and nobody can tell which, not even the Duke could. The box shut against her chest, she is already turning up the stair and he has sat down on the bottom step: she's dressing, she has ten minutes, go round to the front doors and stand where she can see you; where the Cardinal can see me; yes, that too. The dominant turn is the box changing hands and the count to twelve. Constance owns the count; the boy owns the watching. The reader turns because the box has changed hands and there are ten minutes.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 41 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All eight lines read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order; Constance's four hang from the girl in the cap and the boy's four from the muddy boy; each reply sits after the line it answers. A string that does not read from the 600 × 900 proof is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Any extra text anywhere on the page (a name, a numeral, a label, a stray word on a wall or paper) is blocking. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word (duelling for dueling, colour for color), and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue are nonblocking: the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read in order as: she is in the door and he arrives with the box, she counts twelve while he watches her face, she takes the box up the stair and sends him to the front. If her face over the open box with the boy looking at her is not the largest image the eye finds, the turn has not landed.

### C3 — Constance fails identity or state
The small freckled girl in grey with the white cap, apron off and a white collar, no basket. An apron, a basket, or finery is blocking.

### C4 — d'Artagnan fails identity
Clean-shaven, black-haired, ochre with mud to the waist. A clean or moustached boy is blocking.

### C5 — The casket state fails
The casket must be open in the second panel showing a bright double row of diamonds with no empty slot, partly covered by her hands or foreshortened so that it reads as a cluster rather than a grid, and shut in the third. An empty slot is blocking; a flat fully visible grid that a reader can count to more than twelve is blocking, because this page invites the count.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Exactly two hands on the open box; a third figure in the doorway, a duplicated Constance, or a balloon across a face is blocking.
