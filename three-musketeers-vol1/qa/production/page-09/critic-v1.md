VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
A lean young man steals or pulls away a blue uniform cloak while a broad, red-and-blue-clad swordsman boasts to two companions. The young man exposes that the broad man's ornate baldric is gilded only on its visible front. The broad man turns on him, and their exchange becomes a duel appointment; the young man reveals he already has a noon engagement with a tall man with a bad shoulder, prompting the broad man to recognize Athos and claim whatever remains afterward. The broad swordsman owns the page visually, especially the large central panel; the turn is the young man's exposure of his false finery, which changes public boasting into a private challenge and then links that challenge to Athos.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: In the final panel, the boy’s upper-left balloon, “Then fight me. One o’clock, behind the Carmelites. I’m taken at noon.”, is read before Porthos’s upper-right balloon, “Nobody sees the back of my baldric and tells the street about it.” The boy’s left-center “A tall man with a bad shoulder.” likewise appears before Porthos’s right-side “Taken? Who has you at noon?”
- Material reader harm: Two replies arrive before the lines that prompt them, scrambling the cause-and-response rhythm of the confrontation and making the duel exchange read incoherently.
- Redraw justification: The ordered exchange establishes the one-o’clock duel and reveals the existing noon duel, which is the page’s central turn-page event; reversing both prompt-and-reply pairs substantially damages that event enough to warrant replacing the page.
