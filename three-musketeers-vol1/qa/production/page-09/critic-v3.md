VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
D’Artagnan arrives in Paris and asks to enter the King’s musketeers. A senior musketeer tests him first by mocking his ambition and asking what patronage or money he brings; D’Artagnan answers that he has only his sword, his word, quickness to learn, and courage. The senior musketeer recognizes that spirit and welcomes him through the gate. The large final threshold panel owns the page: the turn is the senior musketeer’s acceptance, changing D’Artagnan from an unproven petitioner into a welcomed entrant whose education is beginning.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: None of the seven required dialogue lines appears. Instead, the page contains unrelated narration and dialogue about D’Artagnan arriving in Paris, asking to enter the musketeers, being questioned about his recommendation or fortune, and being welcomed inside.
- Material reader harm: The required collision, baldric exposure, and five-line duel exchange cannot be read at all, so the scripted causal conversation and its ownership are wholly absent.
- Redraw justification: The complete lettering and depicted exchange belong to a different scene; recovering the required seven-line event requires replacing the full page.

### Finding C2
- Observation: The page reads as a stationary recruitment interview at a gate. D’Artagnan never runs into Porthos’s cloak, is never shown tangled on the ground, and never challenges Porthos to the one-o’clock duel; the largest image shows a welcome through the gate rather than the cloak coming off and exposing the baldric’s plain back.
- Material reader harm: The intended comic humiliation and escalation to a second duel do not occur, so the page’s dominant dramatic turn and reason to turn the page are missing.
- Redraw justification: Every major beat and the dominant image would have to change to stage the required collision, reveal, and duel, making a complete redraw necessary.

### Finding C4
- Observation: The dominant image never shows the back of Porthos’s baldric as plain buff leather with no gold; it shows his gold-decorated front in the preceding portraits and then his body largely from behind at the gate without the required exposed baldric back.
- Material reader harm: The object-state reveal that motivates Porthos’s humiliation and the duel is unavailable to the reader.
- Redraw justification: The dominant composition must be replaced to put the exposed plain leather back at the visual center of the scene.
