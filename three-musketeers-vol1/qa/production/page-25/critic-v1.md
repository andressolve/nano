## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: None of the nine visible lines matches the required nine-line script. The page instead contains an introduction and royal summons, beginning `Pardon our intrusion, Monsieur. We are the King's Musketeers.` and ending `Then I am ready, Messieurs.`
- Material reader harm: The required exchange about the London ride, the unknown letter, and the three Musketeers agreeing to help is entirely absent, so the scripted causal sequence and speaker ownership cannot be read.
- Redraw justification: This is a complete substitution of dialogue and scene content, not a local lettering defect; preserving the page would release the wrong story event.

### Finding C2
- Observation: The eye finds the three men arriving at the young man's room, introducing themselves, and summoning him to the King; it never finds the boy laying out the ride, Athos accepting that nobody knows the letter's contents, the three faces answering, or the boy noticing that nobody asked why.
- Material reader harm: The page's intended turn—three men choosing to ride against the Cardinal for an unknown reason—does not occur.
- Redraw justification: The dominant dramatic relationship and every causal beat belong to a different scene, so the intended turn requires replacement of the complete page.

### Finding C3
- Observation: Although the three men are visually distinct, Athos stands in the doorway rather than sitting, Porthos is not at the window, and Aramis has no book to close.
- Material reader harm: The scripted three-way decision cannot read through each Musketeer's required action and attitude; the figures instead function as a group making an entrance.
- Redraw justification: The focal staging and character actions encode the wrong dramatic event throughout the page and cannot be corrected without replacing its panels.

### Finding C4
- Observation: The clean-shaven boy in ochre never holds or displays a small blue-sealed letter.
- Material reader harm: The consequential object that motivates the London ride and makes the question of its unknown contents readable is absent.
- Redraw justification: The letter is central to the scripted action and must be present in the boy's hand within a differently staged scene, requiring a complete redraw.
