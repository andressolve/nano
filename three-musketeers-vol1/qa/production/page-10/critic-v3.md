## Blind read
A rain-soaked young traveler retrieves a lace handkerchief from beneath a blond guardsman's boot and politely says he believes the man dropped it. The guardsman loftily denies owning it while visibly taking it; when the traveler challenges that contradiction, the guardsman explains that the traveler embarrassed him before his companions and schedules a duel behind the Carmelites. The blond guardsman owns the page through the dominant central panel and his controlled, disdainful presence. The turn is the traveler's simple act of courtesy becoming a second lethal appointment, capped by his dry recognition that his day is filling with duels.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The lower-left panel presents the young traveler's reply, `Two is free. Noon and one are taken.`, before the lower-right panel presents the blond guardsman's prompting duel appointment, `Because you have embarrassed me in front of my friends, so I shall have to kill you. Two o'clock, behind the Carmelites?` The page's left-to-right panel order therefore reverses this exchange.
- Material reader harm: The reply arrives before the reader knows that a two-o'clock duel has been proposed, breaking the required causal dialogue and weakening the reveal that this encounter creates the third appointment.
- Redraw justification: Correct causal reading order is essential to the page's scripted turn; because the blocking exchange is divided between two ordered panels, the harm is structural and substantial enough to warrant replacing the complete page.
