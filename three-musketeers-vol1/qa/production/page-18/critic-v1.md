VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
D’Artagnan arrives at the King’s Arms on a rainy evening, asks a young inn maid for lodging, and is led upstairs to a small room. D’Artagnan owns the page as the arriving traveler followed through every beat; the room reveal closes it. The turn is caused by the maid confirming that a room is available and leading him to it, changing him from an arrival seeking lodging into a guest installed in his room.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The required two-paragraph prose is entirely absent. In its place are a different narrator caption, six dialogue balloons, the inn-sign text `AU ROI`, and the footer numeral `18`; the `Thank you.` balloon also points toward empty wall rather than the depicted speaker’s mouth.
- Material reader harm: The page does not communicate the weeks passing, the four friends’ inseparability, Athos’s offer, the refused gate, or Constance’s repeated evening look, and it adds unrelated text with one visibly unowned line.
- Redraw justification: The owner-controlled prose and its causal reading order are the complete narrative mechanism of this illustrated-prose page; replacing all of that content cannot be corrected without replacing the page.

### Finding C2
- Observation: The page is a five-panel inn-arrival comic sequence dominated by a rainy street and room reveal; it contains no dawn field, crossed-blade lesson, or smaller warm stair tableau of the girl looking up at the boy.
- Material reader harm: The eye cannot encounter the silent lesson first or the repeated evening look second, so the intended transition from the four owning the mornings to Constance owning the evenings never lands.
- Redraw justification: Both required locations, the dominant image, the secondary image, and their dramatic hierarchy would have to be replaced, making a complete redraw necessary.

### Finding C3
- Observation: Athos is absent; there is no tall, dark-haired, pointed-bearded man in a blue cassock teaching with both arms free.
- Material reader harm: The focal silent lesson and Athos’s changed state with the sling gone are missing, breaking named identity and consequential continuity.
- Redraw justification: Restoring the absent focal character and lesson requires replacing the page’s dominant scene.

### Finding C4
- Observation: The grey-and-white girl is presented as an inn maid leading D’Artagnan upstairs and showing him a room, not Constance at the bottom with basket and keys looking up at a clean-shaven black-haired boy at the top; D’Artagnan instead follows behind her and appears in the room below her eye line.
- Material reader harm: The required reciprocal look and Constance’s ownership of the evening are replaced by a transactional lodging exchange, eliminating the dominant dramatic relationship and the page-turn setup.
- Redraw justification: The characters’ locations, actions, relationship, and story event are all contradicted and require a new stair scene rather than a local correction.
