VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Four men bring their swords together above a lit candle and seal a shared oath through a spoken call-and-response. The group oath owns the page, with the large red-plumed man at center as the strongest individual visual presence. The turn is the convergence of four separate blades into one bond: the first declaration prompts the answering declaration, and the narration establishes that this once-new formula thereafter needs no repeating.

## Visible text
- “One for all!” — top-center speech balloon. No name is lettered. The balloon has a short central tail aimed down toward the red-plumed central man’s hat/head rather than clearly to his mouth, plus long tapering points on both sides that terminate in open space rather than at a mouth; attribution to one mouth is therefore unclear.
- “All for one.” — upper-right speech balloon, attributed by its downward tail and placement to the dark-haired, bearded man at right; no name is lettered.
- “Nobody had said it before. After that, nobody had to.” — bottom narration box; narrator/prose source.

No other visible text; no lettered names or `EXTRA TEXT`.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The shared answer “One for all!” appears once, but its top-center balloon does not belong plainly to all four. Its visible points aim toward the three standing men or into open space, with no tail or placement connecting the balloon to the boy at lower left.
- Material reader harm: The boy is excluded from the answer’s visual attribution, so the defining oath reads as an exchange among the standing musketeers rather than a vow spoken by all four together.
- Redraw justification: Equal four-way ownership is the page’s decisive story event; losing the new fourth member from the shared answer materially breaks that event and outweighs the risk of replacing the page.
