## Blind read
Two swordsmen confront each other in a muddy inn yard. The young man in worn brown clothes accuses the older, black-clad man of humiliating him and stealing his letter; the older man dismisses him. The large central clash unmistakably owns the page: the young man meets the older man's blade hard enough to make him question where a farm boy learned to fight, and the young man answers that his father taught him while turning the question into a taunt. Behind them, three guards retreat through the wall opening while a blue-coated man wearing a feathered hat raises an empty hand. In the final panel the older man tries to end the encounter after their second meeting, but the young man keeps his sword leveled and declares that a third encounter will not end in escape.

## Findings

### Finding C5
- Observation: In the central panel, Porthos's feathered hat remains on his head while he waves an empty hand at the departing guards; he is not waving the hat.
- Material reader harm: The specified continuity action that visibly drives the guards' break is absent, so their exit reads as a generic retreat rather than Porthos dismissing them with the established hat gesture.
- Redraw justification: This is the required consequential object state and action in the dominant panel, not optional background polish; leaving the hat on his head removes the scripted cause of the guards' departure and warrants replacing the page.
