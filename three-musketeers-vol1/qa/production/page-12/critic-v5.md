## Blind read
A hatless boy arrives to face a tall, slung blue-cloaked swordsman, who tells him that most boys do not come and that his friends are present. A huge red-moustached man and a slim blond man then each identify the boy as their appointed duel, and the tall man identifies him as his own. The dominant final panel owns the page: the three blue-cloaked men gather around the unsmiling boy, with the huge man laughing and the slim man laughing behind his hand, as five red-cloaked guards and a scarred man in black arrive behind them. The turn is that three separate duels become a shared confrontation when the guards arrive and the scarred man claims the boy.

## Findings

### Finding C5
- Observation: All five guards in red cassocks wear conspicuous red feathers on their headgear; C5 requires that none of the guards have a feather.
- Material reader harm: Repeating the red feather across every rank-and-file guard removes it as Rochefort’s specified identifying feature in the dominant confrontation and breaks the required guard continuity.
- Redraw justification: The violation is repeated across all five guards in the page-owning turn image, so it is not an isolated tiny-prop flaw; it materially weakens the intended visual distinction between Rochefort and the guards enough to prevent release.
