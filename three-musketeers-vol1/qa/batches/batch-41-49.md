## Blind sequence record

D'Artagnan reaches the Louvre back stair with the casket, and Constance counts all twelve diamond studs. Two are replacements, so the Cardinal's theft can no longer expose the Queen. Constance takes the casket upstairs and sends d'Artagnan to a place where both the Queen and the Cardinal will see him.

In the ballroom, the Cardinal waits with Rochefort, the stolen letter, and the two original studs. He expects the Queen to enter wearing only ten. Constance watches from the gallery until the Queen arrives wearing twelve. The Cardinal counts them, learns from Rochefort that the two stolen studs are still in his sleeve, and understands that someone made replacements. His public trap fails.

The Cardinal then spots d'Artagnan, has Rochefort identify him as the Gascon from Meung, and reads the boy's name from his father's stolen letter. Constance finds d'Artagnan in the gallery, gives him a ring from the Queen, and confirms both his victory and the danger of the Cardinal knowing who defeated him.

That night Porthos, Aramis, and Athos return to the attic in the order they stopped on the pursuit. Each asks whether d'Artagnan arrived; each hears that he did. In the morning the four eat together behind the Carmelites. D'Artagnan counts what remains after the journey—horse, sword, the Queen's ring, three friends, and a powerful enemy—and decides that losing the letter was a fair trade. The four walk away together while the Cardinal watches from above.

## Script comparison and sequence judgment

- The material story matches the exact script throughout the batch. Dialogue, captions, speaker attribution, and the naming of d'Artagnan remain legible and correct across transitions.
- Consequential objects retain their function: the closed casket passes to Constance; twelve studs defeat the Cardinal's two-stud trap; the broken-seal letter supplies d'Artagnan's identity; the Queen's ring becomes the durable proof and reward carried into the coda.
- Setting changes are clearly bridged from back stair to ballroom, gallery, attic, Carmelite field, and final Paris street. Character identities remain stable, including Constance's gallery position, Rochefort's sling, the Cardinal's red dress, and the four companions' silhouettes.
- The focal line stays with d'Artagnan's success and its cost even when the viewpoint briefly shifts to the Cardinal. Each page turn either tightens the public test, reveals the failed trap's consequence, or carries the victory into aftermath and future danger.
- The climax is deliberately scene-heavy, but pages 43, 48, and 49 vary the mode with spectacle, illustrated prose, and a closing tableau. Page 47 compresses the companions' ordeals into reports without reading as missing story: its dramatic purpose is reunion and confirmation that d'Artagnan arrived. Page 48 then supplies the necessary emotional accounting before the final threat image.

No material story, continuity, focal, pacing, or page-turn failure justifies a complete-page redraw.
