## Mandatory findings

1. The sheet carries a blocking border system: a dark rectangular perimeter rule and straight cream gutters that frame the three views. Remove every perimeter line and straight horizontal or vertical separator. Carry the common warm laid-paper ground cleanly to all four canvas edges, and let the panorama, narrow street, and gate vignette feather organically into that ground through unmarked negative space.

2. The glowing bracket lantern in the narrow-street view imports the primary marker and warm night-light logic reserved for the separate alley/house location. Remove that lantern completely and relight the entire narrow-street view with the same cool overcast daylight as the Paris panorama, preserving only the tall leaning houses, mud, and corner as its primary markers.

3. The large high dome in the right half of the panorama reads as a later monumental Paris landmark and would lock a consequential skyline in the wrong period. Replace that dome with a lower irregular run of grey-blue slate roofs, chimney smoke, and one narrow church spire; retain the house-lined stone bridge and brown river as the panorama's dominant location markers.

- The blue-cloak/red-cloak dramatic accent is correctly absent from this architecture-only plate because the reference plan explicitly forbids figures on setting plates; it must be supplied at page generation rather than embedded here.
