## Mandatory findings

1. The sealed father's letter and the Queen's sealed letter collide at thumbnail scale and in grayscale: both are cream folded packets with a centered circular wax seal carrying the same crown impression, leaving color and modest relative size to do nearly all the identification. Redesign the father's sealed letter as a distinctly long horizontal tri-fold packet with a plain, unmarked red seal, and carry that same long tri-fold geometry into its opened/broken-seal state; retain the Queen's letter as a compact near-square packet whose blue seal alone carries the crisp crown impression. The two sealed states must remain distinguishable by packet proportion and seal design with all color removed.

## Nonblocking observations

- All four required depictions are present: the father's letter sealed, the same letter opened with its red seal broken, the smaller blue-sealed Queen's letter, and the pass with one short line and a signature.
- The handwriting is indistinct and contains no reliably legible words. It functions as required diegetic object detail rather than a label or name plate.
- There are no figures, borders, watermarks, or extra props. The brush-and-ink contours, matte warm gouache, cream paper, and red/blue wax accents are consistent with the locked register.
- The pass remains distinct through its unsealed single-sheet form and sparse one-line-plus-signature layout.
