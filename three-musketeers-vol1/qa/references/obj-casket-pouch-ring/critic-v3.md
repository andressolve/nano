## Mandatory findings

1. The twenty-four casket pieces and two pouch pieces read as opaque blue/red enamel fleur-de-lis badges, not diamond studs. This changes the identity of the story's causally important object and will teach pages to reproduce heraldic plaques rather than the Queen's diamonds. Redesign every occupied slot and both pouch-mouth pieces as the same compact, bright, clearly faceted diamond jewel in a restrained gold mount, with no enamel field and no fleur-de-lis device. Preserve the exact state geometry already achieved: in the first casket, ten occupied slots and the two adjacent lower-right slots empty; in the second, those identical ten positions unchanged and the two lower-right positions filled; at the pouch mouth, exactly two matching diamond studs and none outside.

2. Soft grey cast-shadow halos are visibly painted beneath both caskets, the pouch, and the ring, contradicting the required shadowless white-and-gold palace light. Remove every ground-plane cast shadow and reconstruct one uninterrupted pale white field beneath all four object groups; retain depth only through brush-and-ink contour, internal velvet value, overlap, and restrained gold hardware highlights.

## Nonblocking observations

- The first casket contains exactly ten occupied slots with two adjacent lower-right slots visibly empty; the second contains all twelve, and the unchanged ten positions are consistent between states.
- The pouch presents exactly two pieces at its mouth with no detached piece outside, and the ring has one dark stone.
- Both caskets use the same orientation and substantially the same construction, so the ten-to-twelve state change reads on re-entry.
- The plate has no figures, lettering, labels, watermark, border, or panel rule, and no extra object is present.
- The oversized adjacent empty slots remain legible at thumbnail scale and in grayscale; the two casket states do not depend on counting tiny pieces.
- The warm laid-paper texture, matte brush-and-ink treatment, dark velvet, pale field, and restrained gold hardware otherwise fit the locked register and Buckingham-palace palette.
