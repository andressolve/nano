## Mandatory findings

1. The location's primary continuity marker is missing. The bedroom includes the required small table, but the table is empty: there is no velvet casket carrying the p. 37-38 state. Redesign the bedroom vignette so an open velvet casket sits prominently on that table, angled enough to show exactly twelve large slots as a clear two-row structure, with ten occupied by bright studs and two unmistakably empty. Keep the two empty slots large and legible rather than asking the reader to count tiny stones; do not scatter any loose studs elsewhere in the room.

2. The lighting breaks the location lock. The canopy interior, bed underside, workbench fronts, fire surround, and rear wall all carry grey-brown recesses and cast shadows, while Buckingham's palace is specified as white, gold, and mirror-lit with no shadow anywhere. Redesign both interior vignettes with broad, even, high-key reflected light: lift the canopy, fireplace recess, floor, bench fronts, and bench undersides to pale white/cream values; remove cast-shadow shapes beneath the bed, curtains, tools, and benches; and reserve darker line only for contours. Add unmistakable tall mirror panels to the bedroom walls so reflected white light, not umber shading, is the primary spatial marker.

## Nonblocking observations

- The candidate is unlettered, unlabeled, unwatermarked, borderless, and contains no figures.
- All three views named by the set manifest are present and correct in kind: water stair with white river front, white-and-gold bedroom, and jeweler's bench with fire. The four-view requirement applies to the character locks; this set manifest enumerates three views.
- The exterior successfully carries the river of masts, monumental water stair, white front, and restrained gold detail.
- The oversized magnifying lens, vise, and tongs are ordinary workroom dressing rather than consequential state, but their scale currently makes them more visually dominant than the absent casket; they should remain subordinate after the primary-marker redesign.
