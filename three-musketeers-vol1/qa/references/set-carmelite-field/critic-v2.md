## Mandatory findings

1. The yellow-stone wall is the wrong structural height in both views. It reads as a low field boundary that could be stepped or vaulted over, not the high convent wall that makes the single gap the guards' necessary entrance and preserves the field's isolation. Redesign the wall in both views as a continuous barrier at least 2.5 times the visible height of the trough, built from at least seven clearly readable stone courses; make the opening a full-height break through that barrier, keep the trough directly against the wall immediately left of the opening, and preserve this same wall–trough–gap geometry in the noon and early-morning views.

## Nonblocking observations

- The required noon and early-morning states are both present: the upper view is flat and bright, while the lower view carries low sunrise light, mist, and dew.
- The restrained location palette is correct: yellow stone, dry ochre grass, muted green foliage, blue-grey sky, and warm laid paper.
- The trough repeats as the primary marker in the same relationship to the wall and to the left of the gap, and its basin reads large enough for the page-14 action and page-48 horse use.
- The plate has two views as specified for this setting; four-view character-sheet tests for face crop, strict profile, and reaction expression do not apply to an architecture-only plate.
- There are no figures, labels, watermarks, or legible lettering. The two views feather into common paper without a straight divider or perimeter frame.
- The small carved inlet above the trough is unlisted decoration, but it does not create a consequential alternate state and this location appears on only five specified pages; it is not independently blocking.
