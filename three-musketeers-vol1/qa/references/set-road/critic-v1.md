## Mandatory findings

1. The sheet carries a distressed perimeter frame and hard, rectilinear seams between its three views. Regenerate it as one unframed 1536 x 1024 landscape canvas: carry the warm laid-paper ground cleanly to every edge, remove all perimeter edging, and let each view feather organically into the common paper instead of meeting at straight horizontal or vertical divisions.

2. The white roadside milestone is an unlisted primary marker and is repeated prominently in all three road states, which would turn it into a false recurring landmark. Remove the milestone completely from every view; the daytime view must be carried only by the white road, ochre hills, and poplar line, the night view by the empty road and large sky, and the dawn view by the hill road and sea horizon.

3. The dawn hill introduces a harbor, clustered buildings, and a fleet of masts, collapsing this road state into the separately planned Calais-quay location and giving consequential geography the wrong state. Remove the harbor, buildings, vessels, and masts; beyond the empty hill road, show open sea and dawn only, reserving all quay and shipping markers for `set-calais-quay`.

## Nonblocking observations

- All three planned road states are present: the white road through ochre hills and poplars, the night road under a large sky, and the hill road above the sea at dawn.
- The road plate broadly carries the required warm laid-paper, brush-and-ink, matte-gouache register. The daytime road reads white against ochre, while the night and dawn variants remain recognizably related.
- There are no figures, labels, watermarks, or other lettering.
- Rochefort's all-black silhouette and d'Artagnan's ochre torso are the two most vulnerable same-panel combinations at thumbnail; page lighting and placement must preserve the separations named in the matrix. Reference approval would not by itself approve those generated pages.
