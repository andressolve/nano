## 1. Blind account (written before the script)

### Retell

Gascony, April 1625. A boy leaves the family gate with three things: an old buttercup-yellow horse, his father's too-long sword, and a sealed letter to the Captain of the King's Musketeers, plus one rule — never sell the horse, never refuse a fight, put the letter in the Captain's hand yourself. At an inn at Meung a scarred man in black with a red plume laughs at the horse from a window; the boy challenges him; the inn's men club him from behind (KRAK); the scarred man lifts the letter from his coat, reads the address, and pockets it. The boy wakes in the straw, learns the man rode for Paris at noon, and rides after him.

Paris. Without the letter the Musketeers' gate shuts in his face. The scarred man sits on a black horse across the street, smiling; the boy runs after him and in three successive collisions earns three duels behind the Carmelite convent: noon with a tall Musketeer whose shoulder is in a sling (Athos), one o'clock with a huge red-haired one whose gold baldric is only gold in front (Porthos), two o'clock with a blond, immaculate one who was standing on a lady's handkerchief (Aramis). On a bench by the river he counts what he has — three duels, no friends, no letter — and at a quarter to twelve goes anyway, because he said he would.

At the field the three discover they share one boy and laugh. The Cardinal's guards in red arrive to arrest them, led by the scarred man, who claims "the Gascon" for himself. Porthos tells the boy to stand back — he's not one of them. The boy draws: "Four of us." Athos asks his name: d'Artagnan. The fight is a rout (trough, hat pinned to a wall, a guard into a tree, d'Artagnan disarming his man). d'Artagnan finally crosses steel with the scar, who breaks off and leaves — "Twice is enough for one week." "The third time, you stay." The four walk home four abreast; Athos sends the boy to a landlady with an attic.

Constance: grey dress, white cap, a gold crown on her apron, keys and a basket. She dresses the Queen every morning at the Louvre. He can't pay; she lets him the attic anyway. Weeks pass — Athos trains him at dawn, Porthos pays and pretends Aramis did, Aramis reads Latin — and every evening she looks up the stair. One rainy night four men seize her at her door while the scarred man watches from a doorway across the street. d'Artagnan goes out the window onto a cart roof, kicks the lantern into the puddle and beats four men in the dark. The scar declines to draw — "She isn't what I was sent for. She only carries it." Constance: she carries the Queen's letters and can't say where. He'll be upstairs every night.

The Cardinal, in a red room with a grey cat, with the father's stolen letter on his desk. He has read the Queen's letters for a year: she gave the King's gift, twelve diamond studs, to the Duke of Buckingham in London. He sends the scarred man to bring back two — "Nobody counts to two" — and then the King will give a ball.

Six nights later Constance climbs the stair: the King gives a ball Monday, the Queen is to wear the twelve studs, she gave them away, the Cardinal smiled. Someone must ride to London. She has the Queen's letter (blue seal) and no one to send. "Are you asking?" — "I've no right to ask." — "That isn't no." He'll go; "one boy and three friends." She'll wait at the Louvre's kitchen door every night. In Athos's bare room the three agree without asking why — "You're going. That's the why." Four swords over a candle: All for one, one for all. Constance sews the letter into his coat lining. Four riders through the Saint-Denis gate at dawn; the yellow horse stays home.

The road eats them one at a time. Chantilly: a stranger toasts the Cardinal; Porthos drinks to the Queen and stays to fight. Beauvais: "road workers" with picks at dusk; Aramis's horse goes down; he holds the narrow bridge against six. Amiens: the innkeeper calls Athos's good coin false, red cloaks fill the yard; Athos takes the cellar with the wine and a barrel across the door: "Ride." d'Artagnan rides alone all night, naming his friends to stay awake, and sees the sea. Calais: the harbour is closed by the Cardinal's order. The scarred man comes down a gangplank from England with the Cardinal's pass. They fight on a plank over the water; the scar goes in; d'Artagnan picks up the pass — "Let the bearer cross. — The Cardinal" — and the sergeant "decides that he can read." From the boat's rail the boy sees the man climb out one-armed with something in his fist that flashes twice.

London. The Duke of Buckingham comes down his own marble stair. In his bedroom the casket holds ten: two are gone. A masked man with a scar begged his pardon on the stair during a ball three nights ago. Buckingham sees the trap — she wears ten, the Cardinal steps forward with the two — and sends for his jeweler. Thursday; the ball is Monday. Two days for the diamonds, two for the road. The jeweler makes two nobody can tell apart; Saturday night the Duke hands over the box: "Tell her—" "Ride." Seasick, five horses, a red cloak at a post inn looks past a muddy boy for a Musketeer; the sun of Monday goes down as he enters Paris.

Louvre kitchen door: Constance counts to twelve, twice. She tells him to stand at the front doors where the Queen — and the Cardinal — can see him. In the ballroom the Cardinal, seated, letter on the chair arm, the scar with his arm in a sling behind him, fingers a pouch in his sleeve: "Ten is ten." The Queen enters; everyone looks at her shoulder. The Cardinal counts: twelve. "I cut two, Eminence." "Then someone made two." The King takes her hand; the two diamonds in the red sleeve stay where they are. The Cardinal stands — the only time — and asks who the boy in the doorway is. "The Gascon. From Meung." He unfolds the father's letter and reads the name: d'Artagnan.

In the gallery Constance gives him a ring from the Queen's own hand — "She said: tell him I counted to twelve too." He has seen the Cardinal read his name off the letter he's been chasing since Meung. That night the three come home in the order they stopped — "Did you arrive?" "I arrived." — Athos smelling of a hundred and six barrels. Breakfast on the grass behind the Carmelites; the yellow horse drinks from the trough; he counts what he has: the horse, the sword, a queen's ring, three friends, and a man in red somewhere in Paris who knows his name. He came with a letter; a fair trade. Last page: four walking away four abreast, and above them at a window, a man in red with a grey cat, watching.

### Cast as recognised on the pages

- d'Artagnan — ochre doublet, grey hat with a broken feather, black hair in his eyes. Never lost across 49 pages.
- His father — grey-haired, one page and one memory inset.
- The man with the scar — never named on the pages; black, red plume, pale scar on the left cheek; "the man from Meung," "the Cardinal's man." Right arm in a sling from the ballroom on, consistent with how he climbed the harbour stairs.
- Athos — tallest, dark, goatee, black under the blue cassock, left arm in a black sling through the duel week, sling gone from the "weeks went by" page onward.
- Porthos — enormous, red-brown curls, red doublet, red plumes, gold baldric.
- Aramis — slim, blond, lace collar, fingers at his moustache, "as the Fathers tell us."
- Constance — grey dress, white cap, gold crown on the apron, keys, basket; apron off at the Louvre.
- The Cardinal — gaunt, red robe and skullcap, grey beard, grey cat; seated in every scene but one.
- Buckingham — golden-haired, white and gold, blue sash, pearls.
- The Queen (from behind), the King (on the throne), the harbour sergeant, the jeweler, innkeepers, the Cardinal's red-cloaked guards.

### Causal chain

Rule "never refuse a fight" → challenge at Meung → clubbed → letter stolen → no letter, gate shuts → chasing the thief causes three collisions → three duels → the choice to go anyway → picking the side with fewer swords → a name asked and given → three friends → a bed under a woman who dresses the Queen → the Queen's letters draw the attack → the scar spares her ("she only carries it") → the Cardinal, holding the father's letter, orders two studs and a ball → Constance's news and the Queen's letter → "That isn't no" → three friends agree without why → All for one → each friend stops so one arrives → the pass taken from the scar → London: ten, the masked scar, the trap understood → two made → back by Monday sunset → Constance counts twelve → the Queen wears twelve; the Cardinal's two stay in the sleeve → the Cardinal stands and reads the name → the ring; the friends come home; the trade counted → the Cardinal at the window.

### The ending — what it cost and why the last image matters

He never gets the letter back and never enters the Captain's gate; the introduction his father gave him is now in the hands of the most powerful man in France, who has read the name off it. He traded the safe door for three friends, a queen's ring he can neither sell nor explain, and an enemy who knows who he is. The final page is that trade seen from the enemy's side: the four walking away as one, small in the street, under the still, seated red figure with the cat — the book's victory framed inside its next threat. It lands.

## 3. Movement acceptance

Each answered from the pages alone.

**I — The Yellow Horse (1–7).** d'Artagnan acts on his father's rule; wants to deliver the letter and to be taken seriously; chooses to challenge the man laughing at his horse and, after the theft, to chase him; the thief and the inn men act against him; he now believes the letter is the only key to Paris and the scar has it; we go to Paris because that is where the thief rode. Passes.

**II — Three Duels at Noon (8–16).** He collides with three Musketeers while chasing the scar; each wants his own honour back; he chooses to go to the field with no friend because he said he would, then chooses the side with fewer swords; the Cardinal's guards and the scar act against all four; he now believes he belongs with them without the cloak; we go home with them because they walk him there and he has nowhere to sleep. Passes.

**III — The Girl Downstairs (17–22).** Constance lets an attic to a boy who can't pay; weeks pass; men sent by the scar seize her for what she carries; he chooses the window over the stair; the scar declines the fight and names her role; she keeps the Queen's secret and he chooses to guard her without knowing it; then the Cardinal, holding the father's letter, tells us what she carries and what he intends. The reader knows more than the boy from here on, which is the engine of IV–VII. Passes.

**IV — All for One (23–27).** Constance brings the trap and the letter; she wants a rider and won't ask; he chooses to go on "that isn't no"; the three choose to ride without the why; the oath; she sews and does not say come back. Passes.

**V — The Road (28–35).** Three times a friend chooses to stop so the letter goes on, each in his own idiom (the toast, the bridge, the cellar); the boy chooses to obey "Ride"; at Calais he takes the pass from the scar by beating him on the plank; the sergeant chooses to read; the boy sees two flashes he can't name. Passes.

**VI — London (36–40).** Buckingham chooses to come down himself, sees ten, remembers the masked scar, understands the trap, chooses to have two made; the boy chooses not to sleep and not to ride past his friends' towns. Calendar (Thursday → Saturday night → Monday sunset) is consistent on the pages. Passes.

**VII — Twelve (41–49).** Constance counts; the boy chooses to stand where the Cardinal can see him; the Cardinal counts to twelve, keeps the two in his sleeve, stands, and reads the name; the Queen sends the ring; the three come home; the boy counts the trade; the Cardinal watches. Passes.

No movement depends on information outside the pages. The one thing never given on the pages is the scarred man's name; the book never needs it and the audience never asks for it.

## 5. Nonblocking observations

Recorded for the owner; none changes reading, none justifies replacing a promoted page.

1. **p31, panels 2–3 — the letter in hand.** d'Artagnan holds a cream envelope with a red wax seal while climbing out the window and looking back. Four pages earlier (p27) the Queen's letter was sewn into his coat lining "so that it could not be found," and the Queen's letter is blue-sealed everywhere else (24, 25, 27, 36, 37); cream-with-red-seal is the father's letter, which is on the Cardinal's desk (22, 42, 45). Athos's "For the letter" still reads correctly and the story is unaffected, but an attentive ten-year-old may pause. Closest call in the book; below the threshold.
2. **p40 prose — "Muskeeer."** One-letter typo for "Musketeer" in the second paragraph. Meaning intact; a reader sounds it out. If the page is ever regenerated for another reason, fix it.
3. **p36 — the footman.** Powdered wig and mid-18th-century livery in a 1625 scene. Background figure; cosmetic.
4. **p37 panel 2 at 600 px** — the ten-with-two-holes is hard to count at the review reduction; unambiguous at 768 and will be at print size.
5. **p47** — Porthos's captured second hat and Aramis's limp/bandaged hand (scripted) are not legible in the drawing; the dialogue carries both.
6. **p4 panel 4** — the scar appears to hold a second paper at his belt in addition to the raised letter. Cosmetic.
7. **p45 panel 1** — Rochefort's sling is not visible, though it is on p44 and p46 either side. Cosmetic.
8. **pp 9–10** — one of the two anonymous blue cloaks beside Porthos/Aramis is dark and goateed and glances like Athos; I did not take him for Athos (no sling), and anonymous crowd repetition is nonblocking by rule.

## 6. Summary

The run reads as one book, not 49 approved pages. The cast is identifiable at a glance on every page; every line lands on its speaker; the prose/dramatic/spectacle rhythm matches the contract and breathes; the calendar and the count (two, ten, twelve) hold across the whole arc; and the ending pays for itself with the letter the boy never gets back. Approved for release.
