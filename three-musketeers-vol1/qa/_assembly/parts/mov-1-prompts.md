## PAGE 1 — the yellow horse

### Reader turn
A boy on a ridiculous yellow horse rides straight at the reader with his chin up, and the reader wants to know where he thinks he is going.

### Ordered moments
1. One image, the whole page, about 100%: a white road between ochre hills, a line of poplars, a hot morning sky filling the upper third. The boy and the yellow horse ride toward the viewer in the lower half, large, the horse's buttercup coat and the boy's ochre doublet the warmest things in the frame, the too-long sword hanging past his knee, the broken feather in the grey hat, his chin up and his hair in his eyes. Very small behind them on the road, a stone farmhouse and a gate. The boy's shadow stretches ahead of the horse toward the reader.
2. The title card sits in the open sky at the top, two lines, large and calm, no box. The caption sits lower left over the road, a small matte field, tail-free. Nothing else is lettered.

### Exact strings
TITLE CARD: `THE THREE MUSKETEERS`
TITLE CARD: `The Queen's Diamonds`
CAPTION: `Gascony, in the south of France. April, 1625.`

Render exactly these 3 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
d'Artagnan, keyed to his sheet: eighteen, long narrow face, sharp chin, long straight nose, close-set quick dark eyes, clean-shaven with no moustache, black unruly hair in his eyes, lean and narrow-shouldered, weight forward, one hand on a plain swept-hilt rapier too long for him; faded ochre-yellow doublet, brown breeches and boots, grey felt hat with one broken feather, no cloak (default state, pp 1–32). The yellow horse, keyed to its plate: an old, calm buttercup-yellow gelding with worn tack; never cream, palomino, dun, or chestnut. No other figures on the page.

### Consequential exclusions
Rochefort, the three Musketeers, and Constance are absent. The father is not on this page. No blood, no wound, no drawn sword: the sword stays sheathed at his hip. The farmhouse stays tiny and unfocal. No name plates, no page number, no lettering on the horse's tack, no signpost with readable words. The title card and the caption are the only text.

## PAGE 2 — three gifts at the gate

### Reader turn
A father hands a boy a letter, a rule, and a horse at a farm gate, and the reader learns the boy's name and that he has been practising what to say for eleven days.

### Ordered moments
1. Panel one, the dominant image, about 60% of the page, close and tall: a stone farm gate at dawn. The father on the LEFT, grey-haired, in a worn coat, standing at the horse's head with one hand flat on the yellow horse's neck, looking up. The boy on the RIGHT in the saddle, hat on, looking down at him. In the father's other hand, raised toward the boy, a folded cream letter with a red wax seal, plainly a sealed letter. Exactly two hands doing the action: one on the horse, one holding the letter. Warm low light from behind the boy.
2. Panel two, about 40%, a wide strip: the same yellow horse and rider small on a long white road, going up a hill in grey rain and, further along the same strip, coming down the far side in sun, the hat pulled low. No other travellers.
3. The prose field sits over the pale dawn sky above the gate in panel one, a matte parchment field across most of the page width, never over the two faces, the letter, or the horse's head. Three paragraphs. No other lettering anywhere.

### Exact strings
PROSE FIELD 1: `His father gave him three things at the gate. The horse, which was old and yellow and had been in the family longer than the boy had. The sword, which had been his father's, and was too long for him. And a letter, folded and sealed, to the Captain of the King's Musketeers in Paris, who had been his father's friend once, when they were both young and had swords.`
PROSE FIELD 1: `'Never sell the horse,' his father said. 'Never refuse a fight. And put that letter in the Captain's hand yourself.'`
PROSE FIELD 1: `The boy's name was d'Artagnan. He was eighteen. He rode north for eleven days, and the whole way he practiced what he would say.`

Render exactly these 3 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

## PAGE 3 — the man in the window

### Reader turn
A man with a scar laughs at the yellow horse from a window and refuses to come down, so the boy draws his sword and starts up.

### Ordered moments
1. Panel one, about 30%, a wide strip: the inn yard, low horizon. d'Artagnan on the LEFT swinging down from the yellow horse at the stone trough. Above and to the RIGHT, the first-floor window with shutters open and Rochefort leaning on the sill, hat with the red feather, scar on the left side of his face, laughing down. Two inn men in aprons in the yard on the right, laughing. Rochefort's two balloons sit beside the window, stacked, the upper one first, both tails to his mouth at the sill.
2. Panel two, the dominant panel, about 45%, close and tall: d'Artagnan seen from slightly above, facing the window, hand on the hilt, hair in his eyes, looking up on the LEFT; Rochefort's head and shoulders at the sill on the upper RIGHT, amused. d'Artagnan's balloon upper left with its tail to his mouth; Rochefort's reply lower right, clearly after it, tail to the mouth at the sill.
3. Panel three, about 25%: d'Artagnan with the long sword out, already halfway to the inn door on the RIGHT, seen from behind the two inn men on the LEFT, one lifting a wooden stool and one a broom handle. The boy's single balloon beside his head, tail to his mouth. The inn men are silent and get no balloons.

### Exact strings
ROCHEFORT: `Look at that horse. Is it a horse? It's the colour of a buttercup.`
ROCHEFORT: `Somebody tell the boy. You don't ride a flower to Paris.`
D'ARTAGNAN: `You're laughing at my horse, sir. Come down and laugh at me.`
ROCHEFORT: `At you? I don't fight children. I don't fight anyone before dinner.`
D'ARTAGNAN: `Then you'll be late for dinner.`

Render exactly these 5 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

Rochefort stays at the window on every panel; he does not come down here. The horse stays unfocal after panel one. No lettering on the inn sign, the shutters, or the trough; no name plates; no sound cue. Only the five spoken strings.

## PAGE 6 — Paris

### Ordered moments

In the near foreground, lower centre, small against all that, the boy on the yellow horse, hat in his hand, staring.

His single balloon beside his head, tail to his mouth.
2. Panel two, about 25%, a strip: the door closing in the boy's face, seen from his side, a slice of laughing blue cloaks visible through the narrowing gap. The caption sits over the closing door, tail-free. The boy is silent here.

Panel three, the dominant panel, about 45%, close and tall: the boy turned round on the step in the lower LEFT foreground, and across the street on the RIGHT, on a black horse, Rochefort in black with the red feather, scar toward the viewer, looking straight at the boy and smiling. The boy's balloon upper left, tail to his mouth.

D'ARTAGNAN: `You! The man from Meung! Stay where you are!`
