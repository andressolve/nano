# Visual continuity — pages 31–40

## Verdict: APPROVED

Pages 31–40 remain in the locked **Lantern and Steel** book. Brush-and-ink contour, warm laid-paper ground, matte/chalky paint, restrained ochre–blue–vermilion palette, and the established lettering system remain coherent across the sequence. No page has drifted far enough in palette, contrast, black level, paint treatment, lighting, lettering, or finish to feel as though it belongs to another book.

Character recognition and story state hold. D'Artagnan retains the narrow young face, rough black hair, ochre doublet, grey broken-feather hat, and long sword; his increasingly wet and muddy travel state reads through Calais and London. Athos on p. 31 retains his tall, still blue-and-black silhouette and pointed beard. Rochefort on pp. 33–35 retains the scar, black costume, red feather, moustache, and backward-weighted posture, then makes the approved transition to hatless, soaked, and clutching his right arm on p. 35. Buckingham on pp. 36–39 remains broad, gold-curled and bearded in white-and-gold satin, pearls, and blue sash, clearly distinct from Aramis.

Recurring settings and consequential objects also remain readable. The Amiens yard and cellar preserve the approved lantern-lit yard, well, stone descent, and barrels. Calais preserves the wet grey quay, boat, gangplank, timber barrier, rough sea, and diffuse morning light across pp. 33–35. Buckingham's water stair, bedchamber, and workroom retain their white-and-gold architectural identity and high-key register. The Queen's blue-sealed letter and the Cardinal's narrow pass remain distinct. The casket on p. 37 clearly carries ten filled positions and two adjacent empty positions; p. 39 preserves those ten while the two replacement studs are worked, then closes the same blue-and-gold casket for the return journey.

The palest values on p. 36 and the warmer candle contrast on pp. 37–38 are location-motivated variations rather than continuity breaks: shared paper texture, line treatment, costume accents, and the unchanged character designs bridge them. The full-page narrative layouts on pp. 32, 36, 39, and 40 use the established serif narration treatment, while dialogue pages retain the same hand-lettered weight and rounded cream balloons. These variations do not impair recognition, story state, or reading.

No revision findings. No redraw is justified.
