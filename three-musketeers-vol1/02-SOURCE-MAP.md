## The cut list — named, unapologetic, famous material included

### Whole sequences cut

- **Tréville's antechamber and the King's audience** (ch. 2–3, 6). Famous.
  Cut. Every page of it is men explaining to other men who is angry with whom.
  d'Artagnan meets the three in the street, chasing the man with the scar; the
  Musketeers' gate is shut in his face and never opened. The King's forty
  pistoles are cut: the boy is poor and stays poor.
- **The Bonacieux marriage and the informer plot** (ch. 8–10, 13–14). Cut.
  Constance is unmarried, the house is hers, and nobody in the book betrays
  her from inside it. The threat comes down the street in the dark, where a
  seven-year-old can see it.
- **The Bastille, the Cardinal's interrogation of Bonacieux, and the
  mousetrap** (ch. 13–14). Cut. The Cardinal appears on four pages, each of
  which ends with a decision, and none of which is an interrogation.
- **Milady in London and Buckingham's masked ball** (ch. 16, 20–21). Cut as a
  scene; Rochefort does the theft, off the page, and Buckingham reports it in
  two lines. Milady does not exist in this volume. See "Characters merged."
- **d'Artagnan's first meeting with Buckingham in Paris** (ch. 12), where he
  escorts the Duke to the Queen at night. Cut. The Duke is met once, in
  London, where he counts.
- **The Queen on the page** (ch. 12, 16, 22 as scenes). Cut to a figure. She
  is seen once, across the ballroom, wearing twelve diamonds; she is never
  heard and her face is never close. Everything she does in this book, she did
  before it began, or she does through Constance and a ring. See the
  protected treatment below.
- **The three separate returns for the three friends** (ch. 25–27, which are
  outside the arc anyway). Cut to one page: the three come home to the attic
  on the night of the ball, one after another, and each says what he held.
- **The horse sold for three écus, Planchet and the three servants, the
  King's money, Athos's wine-cellar fortnight as a drinking joke, the
  Musketeers' quarrel with the guards at the Luxembourg, the second duel with
  Bernajoux, d'Artagnan's enlistment in the Guards, the exact protocol of the
  Merlaison ballet.** All cut.

### Characters cut

Milady de Winter, M. de Tréville, King Louis XIII (scenery: a crowned figure
on a dais), Queen Anne (scenery: a figure in white across the ballroom),
M. Bonacieux, Planchet, Grimaud, Mousqueton, Bazin, the Comte de Wardes,
Jussac, Cahusac, Biscarat, Bernajoux, Lord de Winter, La Porte, Madame de
Chevreuse, the jeweler O'Reilly (scenery at his bench, no balloon), the
innkeepers of Meung, Chantilly, and Amiens (scenery, no names), the Cardinal's
guards (scenery: red cloaks, no faces, no balloons).

### Characters merged

- **Milady + de Wardes + Jussac → Rochefort.** One face does five jobs: mocks
  the horse and steals the letter at Meung; leads the guards at the
  Carmelites; comes for Constance in the dark; cuts the two diamonds in
  London and carries them home through Calais with the Cardinal's pass; and
  stands with his arm in a sling behind the Cardinal at the ball. Every time
  d'Artagnan meets the Cardinal's sword, it is the same sword, and the reader
  never has to learn a second enemy.
- **Tréville → a shut gate.** The letter is addressed to "the Captain of the
  King's Musketeers." The gate is shut. The Captain is never seen.
- **The King → the Cardinal's arrangement.** The demand that the Queen wear
  the studs is the Cardinal's, made in the King's name, and the reader hears
  it from Constance, who was in the room.

### Why the biggest cut pays for the volume

Cutting Milady out of Volume I and giving her theft to Rochefort deletes the
largest set of production problems at the root: a second antagonist with her
own face, wardrobe, and London set, who would need an introduction, a motive,
a scene of seduction that these readers do not want, and a collision board
against Constance. It also fixes the story's shape. With one hand for the
Cardinal, every threat in the book comes from a face the reader met on page
three, the chase from Meung to Calais is one line drawn straight through the
volume, and the boy's personal fight and the Queen's danger are the same
fight. Milady enters Volume II as a new thing, which is what she should be.

## Protected claims

Two, both from the owner (2026-09-07):

1. **Bloodless on the page.** No blood, no wound, no death drawn or stated.
   Swordplay ends in a disarm, a fall, a trough, a cellar, a harbor, a
   pinned hat. Athos's bandaged shoulder at his first appearance is from "a
   disagreement with the Cardinal's guards," already healing, never shown.
2. **The Cardinal's politics are felt, not explained.** No character and no
   narration explains France, England, the King's marriage, the Church, or
   the Cardinal's office. The reader sees a calm man in red who is always
   already in the room, learns that the Queen is the one person he does not
   command, and watches him try to fix that.

Proposed by the session, pending the owner: the book treats the Queen as a
presence, not a character. She is a title, never a name, a figure in white
seen at a distance, and the only thing the reader ever hears from her is a
ring sent through Constance. This is what makes room for Buckingham inside
the ceiling of eight, and it keeps the boy's motive where it belongs: on the
girl downstairs.

## Sensitive material and its treatment

- **Violence.** Constant and bloodless. The Carmelite fight is four against
  five and ends with red cloaks in the grass and one in the horse trough.
  The alley fight is a boy against four men in the rain and ends with a
  lantern in a puddle. The quay at Calais ends with Rochefort in the harbor.
  Nobody is cut. A page that draws blood or a wound fails the page gate.
- **Wine.** Porthos toasts the Queen at Chantilly with a cup in his hand.
  Athos barricades himself in a cellar full of barrels and comes home
  smelling of a hundred of them. Nobody is drunk on the page.
- **Romance.** Constance and d'Artagnan: a hand on the stair, a window, a
  handkerchief, a ring passed on. No kiss. The Queen and Buckingham: one line
  from the Duke, "She gave them to me and I have never taken them off," and
  the casket kept like a shrine. Nothing else, ever.
- **The Queen's gift.** A seven-year-old must understand the danger without a
  lecture. It is stated once, by Constance: the diamonds were the King's
  present to the Queen, she gave them to the Duke, and if she cannot wear
  them Monday the whole court will know she gave them away. That is the
  entire mechanism.
- **The Church.** Aramis wants to be a priest, he says, and never leaves. The
  Cardinal is a churchman in red who runs a country. No doctrine, no sermon,
  nobody prays on the page.
- **Rochefort's defeat.** He goes into the harbor and climbs out. His arm is
  in a sling at the ball, from the fall. He is humiliated, not hurt.
