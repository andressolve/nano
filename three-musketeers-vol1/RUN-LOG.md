## The health metric — why a page failed, not how often

Count REVISE verdicts by category:

- **Craft** — wrong for a reason a reader would feel: a dropped line, a
  mirrored room, a tail on the wrong mouth. The gate working. Cheap.
- **Gate** — wrong against something the critic *measured* or against a
  criterion that is not on the card. The gate eating the run. **A page that
  costs more than two generations for gate reasons is not a hard page; it is a
  broken gate.** Stop and look at the criterion.

- **2026-09-08 — ship record.** 49 pages (46 at greenlight; the script gate added the handoff of the casket and split the ask from the news and the count from the name). Script gate: six rounds, twenty findings, judged alive in every round. References: 33 locks from 87 candidates, three whole-cast gate rounds, ten locks superseded (Rochefort re-locked for scar laterality; boards rebuilt costume-free; the quay and the palace reset or split). Pages: 120 candidates for 49 pages, 2.4 per page; 69 REVISE routes, 53 craft and 16 gate; seven promotions by advisor ruling on a candidate the critic had rejected for a finding without material reader harm; ten pages regenerated after a batch gate (28 lookalike stranger, 35 caption typo, 15 the escape and then the weather, 12–13 Athos scarred, 41 the countable casket, 43 register drift). Every batch gate cleared; whole-book gate APPROVED with no findings. Cost: the ChatGPT subscription for every image, one Claude session for the orchestration and critics, about a day and a half of wall clock.

- **2026-09-08 — what the gates did not catch until a later, fresher gate did.** (1) Athos drawn with Rochefort's scar on pages 12–13 passed two page critics and the first continuity pass; a second continuity critic caught it. (2) "that" for "what" in page 35's caption passed the page critic; the cold read caught it. (3) The escape on page 15 passed two cold reads; a third wanted it enacted. (4) Page 43's photographic drift passed the first continuity pass. Non-transitive gates with fresh critics are what found these; each rerun of a batch is worth its cost.

- **2026-09-08 — the generator's four habits on this run.** (a) **The invented first candidate**: on roughly half the pages the first candidate ignored the packet and drew its own scene with its own dialogue, twice quoting the source novel itself; the targeted second candidate almost always landed. (b) **The reference montage**: with three or more sheets attached the generator sometimes returned a collage of the sheets with no scene and no text; the cure that worked was attaching only the focal sheets (two, at most three) and describing the rest in words, after which pages 30–34 and 36–39 landed first time. (c) **The five-input cap**: the in-app image tool accepts five reference images; a six-input page fails silently three times before the builder says why. (d) **Photographic drift** on bare interiors and gold ballrooms, on plates and on page 43.

- **2026-09-08 — the critic's habit.** Codex critics block on punctuation, spelling variants, prop counts, and tail geometry when the card lets them. Sixteen of sixty-nine REVISEs were of that kind. The card's transcription criterion now names spacing, quotes, commas, regional spelling, and sound-cue run length as nonblocking, and counted props are gated only where the story invites the count. Write those tolerances into the first card, not the fifteenth.

- **2026-09-08 — tool notes.** `refbrief.py` hardcoded Volume I's priority pairs in the gate brief; it now reads the ledger's collision matrix. `boards.py` drew grayscale crops, not silhouettes; it now thresholds against the paper. `pagerun.py` misread a verdict written on a heading line; the parser is wider. A hand promotion skips the driver's batch gates; run `--gates END` yourself. A full prompt reset written by the builder can drift far from the packet; when a clean earlier candidate exists, promote it rather than reset again.
