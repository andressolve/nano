# The Machine That Learned to Breathe - Style Guide

## Style Block

Copy this into every page prompt:

```text
Art Style: late-1960s and early-1970s Japanese industrial oil-painting realism - factory floors, test benches, engineering offices, race circuits, city streets, and compact cars rendered with grounded historical detail.
Color palette: Honda red as an accent, workshop amber, drafting-paper cream, engine steel, asphalt gray, civic blue, smog yellow, clean-sky blue, late-1960s office green, fluorescent factory white.
Effects: natural light through factory windows, fluorescent test-lab glow, rain on race asphalt, blue press-conference panels, oil sheen on machined metal, breathable air as a visual contrast between smog and clear sky.
```

## Register Block

```text
Oil-painting realism. NOT a comic. NO halftones, NO cel shading, NO ink linework. Painted brushwork, cinematic lighting, muted period palette.
```

## Anti-Drift Directive

```text
NOT a children's book. Serious mature graphic novel, realistic proportions, natural lighting, cinematic composition.
```

## Visual Identity

Part One was forge, oil, dust, and motorcycles. Part Two is bigger, brighter, and more crowded: factories, proving grounds, offices, clean rooms, test cells, and the first small cars. It should still feel tactile and serious. Avoid glossy corporate advertising and showroom fantasy.

Honda red appears as a precise accent: a prototype sports car, logo detail, race stripe, or Civic taillight. It should not flood the page.

## Lettering

Use the Honda formula:

- Off-white caption boxes with dark serif text, readable.
- Full-width top/bottom bands for dense pages.
- Short speech bubbles only when they sharpen the scene.
- Open every page lettering block with: `LETTERING - verbatim, render exactly:`
- End every prompt with: `All words spelled correctly. Do not duplicate text. Do not invent extra captions. NO modern logos, NO watermarks, NO spurious signage.`

## Machine Rules

- T360/S500/S360: small, early-1960s, not modern sports cars.
- RA272: white 1965 Formula One car, open wheel, low, compact, 1960s race engineering.
- N360: tiny two-door kei passenger car, boxy and practical.
- CB750 Four: large 1969 motorcycle, air-cooled four-cylinder, strong but not futuristic.
- Honda 1300: late-1960s compact sedan/coupe, air-cooled engine emphasis, not a race car.
- Civic/CVCC: first-generation early-1970s compact, short two-box body, small but grown-up.
- CVCC engine visuals: show a main combustion chamber plus a small prechamber. Keep labels sparse and large.

## Tone

No founder worship. The hard lesson of this volume is that Honda's strength becomes dangerous when it turns into certainty. The mature payoff is that Honda and Fujisawa built something that could disagree with them and continue.
