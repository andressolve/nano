The Boy of Gont

Duny found power before he understood its cost. He saved Ten Alders, received his true name from Ogion, and learned enough of silence to love his teacher without yet being able to follow him. The ship waiting at the quay carries both his ambition and its warning in one name: Shadow.

What Held Together?

1. Why did Duny remain at the forge when many villagers fled?

Yes. His loyalty, pride, and need to be seen as capable all meet at the forge.

The decision belongs to Duny. His aunt leaves; he stays beside his father and asks what work must be done.

2. Why did the fog give Ten Alders an advantage?

Yes. Duny's spell matters because it works with the villagers' knowledge of their own ground.

The fog conceals rather than strengthens. Familiar paths become the villagers' weapon.

3. What did saving the village cost Duny?

Yes. The victory establishes both his gift and the danger of using power without sufficient knowledge.

No blade wounds him, but the working empties him so completely that ordinary village magic cannot reach him.

4. What was Ogion trying to teach through the seed and the rain?

Yes. Ogion teaches attention and restraint as the foundation beneath power.

Ogion could move the rain. His refusal is the lesson: ability alone does not justify action.

5. Why did Ged choose Roke even though he loved Ogion?

Yes. The choice is emotionally difficult, but it is still driven by impatience and ambition.

Ogion offers both roads freely. Ged leaves because the promise of faster mastery answers the hunger he cannot quiet.
