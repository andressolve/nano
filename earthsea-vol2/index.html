The Light She Cannot Unsee

The Tombs of Atuan, Part One — after Ursula K. Le Guin

A girl's name was fed to the dark before she was old enough to remember it. She grew up obedient, proud, and utterly alone — until a stranger's light bloomed under the hill she thought she owned completely, and showed her that the "house of death" she served was beautiful. She trapped him. She could have let him die a dozen different ways her duty demanded. Instead she brought him water. It is the first thing Tenar has ever done that Arha did not choose for her. The door is bolted. He is alive inside. She has not lit her lamp. One quiz stands between you and the end — it tests the WHY, not the what.

Test Your Understanding

1. Why do the priestesses say Tenar "is eaten"?
Right — "eaten" is not a figure of speech in the Place. Her name and self are given back to the Nameless Ones; the girl who ran in the orchard is supposed to be gone entirely, leaving only Arha, the Priestess Ever Reborn.
Not quite. It isn't a graduation title, a joke about food, or a fasting punishment. Her name and self are given to the Nameless Ones — Tenar is meant to be gone, leaving only Arha.
2. Why does Arha order the three prisoners' deaths — and why does she then dream of carrying water?
Right — sentencing the prisoners is simply the office she was raised to fill, with Kossil watching to see that she fills it. The water dreams are the part of her that the Tombs never quite ate — grieving, in her sleep, what she did awake.
Not quite. No knifepoint, no direct Godking order, no personal hatred. It's the duty she was raised for, performed under Kossil's watching eye — and the dreams are the buried part of her mourning it.
3. Why has no one ever seen the Undertomb, and what does the wizard's light reveal about it?
Right — light itself is the forbidden thing in the holiest dark, so no one, in all the generations of priestesses, had ever seen what lay under the hill. The werelight reveals it is not a house of death at all, but a palace of crystal — glory, where Arha expected only silence.
Not quite. It isn't unreachable, empty, hidden treasure, or a shifting maze. Light itself is forbidden there — and the werelight reveals the "house of death" is a palace of crystal, changing everything Arha thought she knew about it.
4. Why did the wizard come to the Tombs?
Right — he came, like generations of wizards before him whose bones lie under the hill, to find the lost half of the ring of Erreth-Akbe. The crescent already on his chain is the other half; the whole ring is a peace long broken and never mended.
Not quite. No shipwreck, no spying, no prisoner rescue. He came seeking the lost half of the ring of Erreth-Akbe — the treasure that has cost wizards their lives for centuries, and the reason he already carries its other half.
5. Why does Arha keep the wizard alive, when her whole duty says to kill him?
Right — nothing in her office asked for this. It is the first act in her whole life that is truly her own, not Arha's: mercy, curiosity, and the memory of a light more beautiful than anything the dark ever gave her, outweighing every rule she was raised to obey.
Not quite. It isn't a hostage plan, Kossil's order, or fear of a curse — Kossil in fact wants him left to die. It is Arha's first truly free choice: mercy and wonder outweighing her whole upbringing.
