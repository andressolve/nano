# Style Guide - Expanded Earthsea Part Four

## Style block

> Bold ink-line graphic-novel art with clean, confident black linework and flat muted color. Fine etched crosshatching in copper and black skin, pale wind-burned skin, rough wool, tarred rope, wet wood, dark water, sleet, snow, moor grass, and worn stone, matching the finished `../earthsea-wizard-part3/pages/` artwork exactly. Serious mythic realism, cinematic staging, realistic heroic proportions. Cold migration palette: sea-grey, slate blue, fog-white, wet brown wood, iron-black, frost-silver, dead heather, snow-grey, and pale stone. Low saturation throughout. Warm light appears only in Low Torning's thanksgiving fires, ordinary lamps, the yew staff's defensive burn, and the final open gate. Weather and negative space become harsher as Ged travels north.

## Part Four palette arc

- **Low Torning:** cold sea-grey held back by small communal fire-gold.
- **Roke-wind:** whitecaps, hard slate cloud, rain, and green-black sea; no magical color.
- **Serd:** dim umber inn light surrounded by wet black streets and sleepless shadow.
- **Orrimy:** crowded muted port colors with one unnaturally dead-grey figure.
- **North ship:** iron sea, raw wood, dirty sailcloth, bruised sky, little warmth.
- **Osskil:** near-monochrome frost, pale skin, black wool, dead heather, early snow.
- **Gebbeth chase:** black, grey, and one desperate white-gold staff burn.
- **Gate:** a single steady warm-white rectangle that reads as rescue while remaining visually uncanny.

## Locked character rules

- Scarred Ged has deep red-brown copper skin, straight rough black hair, sharp hawk-like features, and four pale healed claw marks on the left cheek. He is eighteen, lean, sleep-deprived, and increasingly cold.
- Ged's yew staff is plain dark wood without orb, gemstone, fork, metal cage, decoration, or runes. It burns only during the gebbeth defense and is visibly damaged afterward.
- Hoeg is a tiny brown-black otak, no larger than a large rat or tiny cat.
- Pechvarry and Low Torning retain their locked Part Three identities and dark Archipelagan complexions.
- The grey stranger is a narrow middle-aged man in a weather-stained grey cloak. His face is real but kept mostly in shadow; he must not visually merge with Skiorh or the gebbeth.
- Skiorh is a gaunt pale Osskilian man with hollow cheeks, light stubble, a long dark winter coat, and a peaked black hood. His normal face remains fully human.
- The gebbeth is Skiorh's clothing animated around a complete absence. The hood opening contains pure matte black and no anatomy at all.
- Other Osskilians are pale, wind-chapped, and practical. Their difference from Ged is human and cultural, not monstrous.

## Magic and horror

- The Roke-wind is shown through repeated physical opposition: sail pressed backward, rain driven into faces, waves shouldering the bow away, Ged's weatherworking gesture failing without colored effects.
- The shadow may appear indirectly before Osskil as an impossible matte-black absence in dream, water, doorway, or crowd spacing. Never give it eyes or a human expression.
- Skiorh's first wrongness aboard ship is a momentary loss of facial coherence, like identity slipping out of alignment—not melting flesh or mutation.
- At the full reveal, the hood is empty. The horror is the missing face.
- The gebbeth moves like empty cloth in wind even when the air is still. It does not crawl on all fours, grow claws, bare teeth, or become smoke.
- When the gebbeth speaks Ged's true name, show the consequence on Ged and the deadening of magic; do not depict visible letters, runes, shockwaves, chains, or beams.
- The staff burn is a short white-gold defensive flare along the wood at contact, not a projectile attack.
- The gate is architectural and physical. Its light does not form wings, hands, angels, or a portal vortex.

## Page construction

- 3:2 landscape, exactly 1536x1024.
- Usually three or four panels; use one wide panel for environmental isolation and pursuit geography.
- Give the Roke reversal, empty hood, and final gate full-width or splash emphasis.
- Solid black borders and clean off-white gutters.
- Reading order must be unambiguous left-to-right, top-to-bottom.
- Caption boxes use weathered parchment and dark serif text.
- Speech bubbles use off-white fill and dark serif text.
- All lettering is baked into the generated image.
- No page should require zoom at a 1200-pixel display width.

## Lettering restrictions

Render every supplied line exactly. Spell all words correctly. Do not duplicate, omit, paraphrase, or invent text. No quotation marks inside speech bubbles. No labels, signs, page numbers, watermarks, decorative pseudo-writing, fake runes, modern objects, visible sound-effect lettering, or extra balloons unless specified.

## Dialogue blocking

- Treat attribution as scene blocking. A page must remain readable even if a tail stops slightly short.
- For A-B dialogue, stage A left and B right whenever possible.
- Define every balloon by ordinal, speaker, exact text, balloon position, character position, and tail endpoint.
- State exact balloon counts and explicitly identify silent characters.
- Use one visible speaker and one balloon per panel for the grey stranger's persuasion and the high-risk Skiorh exchanges.
- Keep the gebbeth alone in any panel where it says Ged's name. Ged must never appear to speak his own true name.
- Inspect full resolution for wrong tails, torso-pointing tails, orphan fragments, duplicate balloons, silent-character violations, and canvas drift.
- If attribution fails, regenerate the full page with revised staging. Never crop-patch a tail.
