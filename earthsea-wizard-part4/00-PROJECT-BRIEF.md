# A Wizard of Earthsea - Part Four: Hunted

## Purpose

This is the direct continuation of `../earthsea-wizard-part3/`. It begins on Low Torning after Yevaud's oath and follows Ged through Chapter 6, "Hunted," to the threshold of the Court of the Terrenon.

## Scope

- Source coverage: Chapter 6, "Hunted," only.
- Length: cover + 30 landscape pages.
- Arc: the people of Low Torning celebrate Ged while Pechvarry's grief makes the cost of that victory impossible to forget; Ged tries to return to Roke, but Roke's own warding wind rejects the shadow bound to his trail; stripped of counsel and ruled by fear, he accepts a stranger's suggestion, rows north to Osskil, and follows Skiorh into a winter wasteland where the shadow finally takes a man's shape and speaks Ged's true name.
- Ending: powerless and nearly caught, Ged crosses a gate of light and collapses as it closes against the gebbeth. The apparent refuge remains unexplained.

Chapter 7, "The Hawk's Flight," is reserved for Part Five. Serret's welcome, the Stone of Terrenon, Benderesk, Hoeg's death, the bird-flight escape, and Ged's return to Ogion do not belong in this volume.

## One-sentence pitch

After saving the Ninety Isles, Ged runs toward the safest place he knows—only to find every refuge closed and the thing hunting him waiting inside a human hood with his true name in its mouth.

## Editorial center

This volume is about fear narrowing choice.

Part Three proved that Ged can act bravely when another community needs him. Part Four removes that clarity. He is praised by people he must leave, rejected by the school he trusted, isolated among strangers, and offered one direction at the exact moment he can no longer judge directions well. The shadow does not defeat his strength first. It starves him of rest, companionship, language, and confidence until a dangerous road feels like the only road.

The story must preserve two linked ideas:

1. Ged refuses to endanger Low Torning or Roke, even while terrified.
2. Once alone, he mistakes motion for decision and a planted suggestion for chance.

Hoeg is therefore essential. The otak is the last ordinary living bond Ged carries into Osskil. Its unease is the warning Ged's exhausted mind can barely hear.

## Pacing commitments

- Low Torning receives two pages: one for public celebration and one for the private wound between Ged and Pechvarry.
- The failed return to Roke receives five pages. The Roke-wind must feel like a place of safety recognizing Ged as a danger, not generic bad weather.
- Serd and Orrimy receive five pages. Ged's sleepless fear and the grey stranger's planted direction must be dramatized rather than summarized.
- The north ship receives seven pages. Rowing, coercion, racial estrangement, Hoeg's vulnerability, and Skiorh's first visible wrongness all need room.
- Neshum and the moor receive five pages. Language isolation, cold, fatigue, and the distant keep gradually close the trap.
- The gebbeth reveal and chase receive six pages. The empty hood, spoken true name, failed magic, burning staff, pursuit, and false refuge must each read clearly.

## Canon and visual laws

1. Ged is the locked scarred identity from Parts Two and Three: eighteen, lean, deep red-brown copper skin, straight black hair, four pale healed scars on the left cheek, dark cloak, plain tunic, plain yew staff.
2. Hoeg remains a brown-black otak at large-rat/tiny-cat scale. It never speaks or behaves like a magical familiar.
3. Low Torning and most southern Archipelagan sailors remain brown- and black-skinned. Do not let the opening or ship crowds drift white.
4. Osskilians are the marked pale-skinned northern population in this sequence. Their clothing is severe, practical, and cold-weather appropriate; they are not elves, Vikings, or a fantasy master race.
5. Skiorh begins as a gaunt pale Osskilian oarsman in a peaked dark hood. As a gebbeth, the same clothes move around an absolute, faceless black absence. Do not turn him into a skeleton, corpse, demon, smoke creature, or glowing-eyed monster.
6. The shadow is absolute flat matte black, without face, eyes, texture, reflected light, colored rim, or smoke detail.
7. The Roke-wind is ordinary-looking weather with uncanny purpose. No visible barrier, rune dome, beam, or giant magical hand.
8. The Court of the Terrenon is only an exterior promise at this boundary: a solitary pale keep on a snow-dark moor, with one open gate of steady warm-white light. Do not show the Stone, Serret, Benderesk, or interior luxury.
9. Ged cannot transform or summon after the gebbeth speaks his true name. His defense is a desperate warding stroke with the yew staff, which burns and is damaged.
10. Horror comes from isolation, missing identity, and pursuit. No gore, exposed corpse, possession body horror, or explicit whipping.
