# Settings - Part Four

## Low Torning thanksgiving

The same linked tidal township as Part Three, now after dark. Small fires burn on low docks and islets; fish stew, drums, rough singing, and narrow boats tie the community together across black water. The mood is grateful rather than royal. No throne, banners, parade armor, fireworks, or grand feast hall.

Route value: `low-torning`.

## The Roke passage

Open sea east of the Ninety Isles. Roke begins as a distant green-black line beneath broken cloud. The closer the ship sails, the more the weather physically opposes it: headwind, steep short waves, rain, sail pressed backward. The phenomenon remains meteorological, not a visible magical wall.

Route value: `roke-wind`.

## Serd

A modest wet island port with low dark roofs, timber quay, net sheds, and one cramped inn. Ged's room is small: narrow pallet, shuttered window, one smoking oil lamp, damp cloak, staff within reach, Hoeg curled close. It should feel populated just beyond the door but emotionally unreachable.

Route value: `serd`.

## Orrimy

A busier southern port with covered arcades, steep wet lanes, warehouses, rope markets, awnings, and mixed brown- and black-skinned Archipelagan crowds. Rain makes every doorway dark enough to hide a watcher. The grey stranger chooses a narrow lane within sight of the harbor but outside the crowd's attention.

Route value: `orrimy`.

## The north ship

New object/environment reference required: `ref_north_ship.png`.

A long, narrow trading galley built for northern water. One square dark sail assists when wind permits, but paired oars drive it. Open rowing benches, low cargo deck, high curved prow without carved monster head, raw dark timber, patched rigging, no cannon, no shields, no Viking ornament. Hired rowers and bondsmen share benches under hard officers. Coercion is conveyed through posture, unequal rest, and an officer's presence, not explicit violence.

## Neshum, Osskil

A cold northern harbor under early snow: black timber piers, pale stone storehouses, low boats, iron-grey water, smoke flattened by wind. The pale Osskilian population wears layered black, grey, and undyed wool. Ged's copper skin and southern cloak mark him immediately as foreign.

## The Osskil moor

Long treeless hills of dead heather, stone, frost, and sparse snow under a low sky. A single road becomes two ruts and then a faint track. No pine forest, mountain peaks, ruins, standing stones, or picturesque village. Human shelter disappears behind them early.

The Court of the Terrenon begins as a pale vertical mark impossibly far ahead. It grows without seeming nearer.

Route value: `osskil`.

## Court of the Terrenon exterior

New environment reference required: `ref_terrenon_exterior.png`.

A solitary pale stone keep rising from the empty moor. One severe central tower, lower enclosing walls, narrow windows, and a gatehouse; ancient but intact, wealthy but undecorated. The walls may carry a faint steady pearl-grey clarity against the storm, never runes, blue shields, or glowing tracery. At night, the open gate is a clean warm-white rectangle in the snow. The source of the light is not visible.
