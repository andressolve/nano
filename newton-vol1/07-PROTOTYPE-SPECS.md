# The Invisible Forces — Prototype Specs

## Purpose

These are the seven pages that must be pressure-tested before full production.

Each prototype page exists to answer a concrete question about the workflow. If a prototype fails, the response is structural revision, not blind persistence.

## Prototype 1 — Page 10: The Beam Through The Shutter

**Why it is risky**
The page needs believable apparatus, readable beam geometry, restrained spectrum color, and a room that still feels materially real.

**What success looks like**
- one narrow beam enters the darkened room
- the prism is physically legible
- the spectral spread is visible but not exaggerated
- Newton reads as investigator, not magician
- the page feels like experiment, not decorative wonder

**Non-negotiables**
- one controlled beam
- one prism
- restrained palette outside the spectrum
- Woolsthorpe room realism

**Allowed simplifications**
- fewer panel divisions
- fewer visible notes or labels
- less facial detail if apparatus clarity improves

**Failure signs**
- psychedelic rainbow image
- impossible light paths
- generic fantasy alchemy atmosphere
- text doing all the explanatory work

## Prototype 2 — Page 11: The Second Prism

**Why it is risky**
This is the page that proves the colors are in the light already. If it fails, the optics story becomes smoke and mirrors.

**What success looks like**
- a clear before/after or stage-one/stage-two structure
- one selected color passes through the second prism
- the result preserves the chosen color rather than exploding into vague rainbow effects
- the causal logic is visible without textbook clutter

**Non-negotiables**
- strong continuity with page 10 apparatus
- visible comparison between first and second stage
- the page must read as proof, not repetition

**Allowed simplifications**
- two large panels instead of multiple small ones
- only one selected color emphasized
- minimal text labels if captions already carry the argument

**Failure signs**
- the second prism behaves like fantasy light
- too many tiny labels are needed
- the viewer cannot tell what changed between stages

---

## Prototype 3 — Page 14: The Moon Falls Around The Earth

**Why it is risky**
This page must make orbit look physical, not mystical.

**What success looks like**
- a falling object and the moon are visibly linked by one idea
- sideways motion plus downward pull are both legible
- the page suggests continual falling without turning into soft cosmic symbolism

**Non-negotiables**
- one geometric logic connecting earth and sky
- Newton is present either physically or through notebook evidence
- no romantic star-scape excess

**Allowed simplifications**
- split page with earth scene and orbital diagram scene
- fewer background details in favor of cleaner geometry

**Failure signs**
- moon page feels like astrology art
- orbit is just a pretty circle with no physical logic
- text must explain everything because image explains nothing

---

## Prototype 4 — Page 15: How Force Weakens With Distance

**Why it is risky**
This is the hardest explanatory page in Book One. It can easily collapse into classroom clutter.

**What success looks like**
- distance comparison is immediately visible
- weakening of pull is visually inferable
- the page stays clean enough to read as a graphic novel page

**Non-negotiables**
- comparison near Earth vs far from Earth
- simple, readable geometric structure
- no dense forest of labels

**Allowed simplifications**
- reduce the mathematical explicitness if the comparison remains honest
- use notebook-sheet framing to simplify the world around the diagram

**Failure signs**
- the image looks like a worksheet
- concept only survives in captions
- visual hierarchy collapses into tiny callouts

---

## Prototype 5 — Page 23: One Law For Apple And Moon

**Why it is risky**
This is the climax. It must unify many phenomena without becoming overloaded.

**What success looks like**
- one underlying geometry visibly governs multiple cases
- apple/falling body, projectile/cannonball, moon, and planetary path feel like one system
- Newton remains the center, even if indirectly through manuscript space

**Non-negotiables**
- the page must feel like synthesis, not collage
- one law unifies all the vignettes
- no bombastic cosmic pageantry

**Allowed simplifications**
- fewer cases if the unity becomes clearer
- stronger paper/manuscript frame around the whole composition

**Failure signs**
- a scrapbook of unrelated scenes
- too many phenomena competing for attention
- Newton disappears and the page becomes generic astronomy art

---

## Prototype 6 — Page 12: A Mathematics Of Change

**Why it is risky**
Fluxions is conceptually hard and can quickly become fake-explanatory.

**What success looks like**
- changing quantity is the visual subject
- motion, curve, and changing magnitude feel linked
- the page suggests why static geometry alone is insufficient

**Non-negotiables**
- one central conceptual problem: how to describe continuous change
- real paper or diagram surface anchoring the idea
- no fake equations for atmosphere only

**Allowed simplifications**
- show fewer examples of change
- let captioning do more of the interpretive work than on page 15
- split later if needed

**Failure signs**
- page looks mathematically impressive but says nothing
- too many symbols with no readable function
- the reader cannot tell why this is different from ordinary geometry

---

## Prototype 7 — Page 17: The Reflecting Telescope

**Why it is risky**
The page needs plausible instrument design and a visible connection to the optics problem.

**What success looks like**
- mirror-based instrument is readable as distinct from lens-based telescope logic
- craftsmanship and experimentation are visible
- the page links optics to making, not only to theory

**Non-negotiables**
- telescope must feel tactile and plausible
- Newton must read as maker, not spectator
- connection to the color problem must remain visible in captioning or composition

**Allowed simplifications**
- one large workshop scene instead of workshop plus public demonstration
- reduced instrument complexity if silhouette and reflective logic remain clear

**Failure signs**
- the device looks generic sci-fi brassware
- instrument detail overwhelms Newton
- the optics connection disappears

## Prototype Stop Conditions

Pause and revise the script if any of the following happen:

- three consecutive failed attempts on the same page with no clearer diagnosis
- page logic depends on adding more and more tiny text
- the best image is visually strong but scientifically false
- Newton continuity collapses when the page requires unusual pose or distance

## Prototype Output Standard

A prototype is considered usable when:

- the page objective is legible on first read
- the image can support final captions without needing redesign
- continuity and period plausibility are acceptable
- the science page reads as an argument, not just an atmosphere image
