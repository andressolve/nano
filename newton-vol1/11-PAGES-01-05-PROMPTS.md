# The Invisible Forces — Pages 1–5 Production Prompts

These are first-pass text-bearing production prompts for pages 1–5.

Text policy for this run:
- sparse caption boxes
- minimal or no speech bubbles unless necessary
- no tiny labels
- high-contrast readable text only

---

## Page 1 — Cover

Use the Young Scholar Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: graphic novel cover
Page objective: announce Newton as a private, severe investigator of hidden laws; establish prism light and geometry as the visual engines of the book
Composition structure: single full-page cover image
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton young-scholar reference into a dramatic graphic novel cover. Newton stands in a severe 17th-century study beside a wooden desk with papers, compass, and prism. A narrow beam of window light passes through the prism and throws a restrained spectrum across manuscript pages showing clean orbital and geometric diagrams. The room is dark, tactile, and materially exact: rough plaster, oak table, leaded window, candle, books. Newton looks inward and intense, not theatrical. This is a serious historical graphic novel cover.
Subject: Isaac Newton young scholar, prism, manuscripts, geometry
Setting: austere study combining Woolsthorpe and Cambridge visual language
Text (verbatim): "THE INVISIBLE FORCES" and below it "Isaac Newton, Book One"
Constraints: title large and clean at top, subtitle smaller and legible below; no other text; serious mature graphic novel; realistic 17th-century materials; restrained spectrum; no wig; no children's-book softness
Avoid: psychedelic rainbow, fantasy alchemy, ornate decorative borders, clutter, tiny illegible title text
```

## Page 2 — Born Into Winter

No character reference required for first pass.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: graphic novel story page
Page objective: begin with absence and fragility rather than greatness
Composition structure: two-panel page. Top large panel, bottom smaller panel
Primary request: create a graphic novel page set at Woolsthorpe Manor in winter, 1642. Top panel: a dim rural English bedchamber lit by cold daylight and weak hearth glow. A newborn infant lies swaddled while worried women attend him. The mood is severe, poor, and intimate, not sentimental. Bottom panel: close view of the small infant in blankets, tiny and fragile, the room dark around him, winter pressing at the leaded window.
Subject: newborn Isaac Newton, worried household women, winter room
Setting: Woolsthorpe bedchamber, 1642
Text (verbatim): "Woolsthorpe, Lincolnshire. Winter, 1642." "His father was dead before he was born." "He was so small no one expected him to live."
Constraints: three short caption boxes only; no speech bubbles; realistic 17th-century English domestic interior; restrained palette; no saintly birth imagery; no children's-book softness
Avoid: glowing holy-child symbolism, melodrama, ornate aristocratic room, excessive warmth, cute baby illustration
```

## Page 3 — His Mother Leaves

Use the Child Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: graphic novel story page
Page objective: show the emotional root of Newton's inwardness through a hard, unsentimental departure
Composition structure: two-panel page. Top wide panel, bottom wide panel
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton child reference into a graphic novel page showing the moment his mother leaves him behind after remarrying. Top panel: outside Woolsthorpe, a cart or horse waits in gray daylight as Hannah prepares to leave, composed and restrained. The small boy Isaac stands back with his grandmother, watching. Bottom panel: interior threshold or window view of the child left behind in shadow, the departing figure now farther away. The scene must feel emotionally hard but quiet, not melodramatic.
Subject: young Isaac Newton, Hannah Ayscough Newton, grandmother Margery
Setting: Woolsthorpe yard and threshold, 1646
Text (verbatim): "When his mother remarried, she left him here with his grandmother." "He would remember the fact all his life."
Constraints: two short caption boxes only; no speech bubbles; child must match reference closely; Hannah and grandmother should fit their period looks; serious mature historical graphic novel
Avoid: sentimental embrace, weeping melodrama, children's-book softness, theatrical gestures, bright cheerful weather
```

## Page 4 — Walls Full Of Drawings

Use the Child Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: graphic novel story page
Page objective: show Newton's peculiarity as exact attention, not magical genius aura
Composition structure: three-panel page stacked vertically
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton child reference into a three-panel graphic novel page. Panel 1: the boy studies moving light and shadow on a wall or ceiling in a rough Woolsthorpe room. Panel 2: he scratches circles, marks, or simple measured figures onto a wall, board, or scrap with concentrated seriousness. Panel 3: he watches a small homemade device, paper contrivance, or wind-driven toy with intense focus near a window or yard. The page should feel quiet, exact, and strange in a real-world way.
Subject: young Isaac Newton alone with shadows, marks, and small handmade devices
Setting: Woolsthorpe interior and immediate yard
Text (verbatim): "He watched shadows, wind, and moving things." "He scratched figures on walls and built small devices with his hands." "The world was full of patterns, if you looked hard enough."
Constraints: three short caption boxes only, one per panel; no speech bubbles; child must remain serious and unsentimental; tactile materials; serious historical graphic novel
Avoid: cute tinkerer montage, fantasy inventions, exaggerated genius glow, cartoon motion lines, bright palette
```

## Page 5 — Grantham

Use the Schoolboy Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: graphic novel story page
Page objective: show Newton's widening world in Grantham and the way his inwardness turns into skill and force
Composition structure: three-panel page. Top half one wide schoolroom panel, bottom left workshop panel, bottom right outdoor experiment panel
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton schoolboy reference into a graphic novel page set in Grantham. Top panel: King’s School, where the schoolboy Isaac sits or stands in a wooden schoolroom, severe and intent among other boys. Bottom left: at the Clarke house or apothecary workspace, he studies tools, jars, paper, and a small mechanical model. Bottom right: outdoors in wind or twilight, he tests a kite, lantern, or other handmade contrivance. The page should show a boy becoming formidable rather than charming.
Subject: Isaac Newton schoolboy in school, workshop, and experiment scenes
Setting: Grantham schoolroom, Clarke house, and windy street or yard
Text (verbatim): "In Grantham, away from home, the strange boy became formidable." "He built models, flew kites, and measured everything."
Constraints: two short caption boxes only; no speech bubbles unless absolutely unavoidable; maintain schoolboy reference appearance; serious mature graphic novel; period-correct spaces and tools
Avoid: playful whimsy, cute prodigy energy, modern school imagery, cluttered page logic, children’s-book softness
```
