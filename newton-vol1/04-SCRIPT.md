# The Invisible Forces — Design Script Outline

## Page Structure

Current design: **24 total pages**

- 1 cover
- 23 story pages

This is a design script, not the final generation prompt set. Each page below is specified for narrative function, exposition function, visual strategy, reference needs, and production risk.

## Pre-Production Locks

- Keep `The Invisible Forces` as the working title during production prep.
- Keep the `24 total page` structure as the baseline until prototypes prove otherwise.
- Keep page 24 as the coda for now.
- Keep Barrow out of the first-wave reference set unless page 7 or 16 later proves too empty without him.
- Treat page 19 as the cleanest compression candidate if a science page later needs to split.

## Narrative Arc

A fatherless winter birth -> a child left behind -> a schoolboy who turns inwardness into exact attention -> a student who teaches himself the new science -> plague isolation becomes discovery -> light is broken open -> change gets its own mathematics -> apple and moon fall under one law -> publication comes only when Halley forces the hidden work outward.

## Emotional Arc

Absence -> watchfulness -> competitiveness -> self-invention -> solitude -> revelation -> recoil -> pressure -> synthesis.

## Science Arc

Observation -> experiment -> proof -> mathematical tool -> physical question -> universal law -> engineered consequence -> public system.

## PAGE 1 — COVER

**Title beat:** Newton alone in his chamber, prism light crossing manuscript diagrams and orbital arcs.

- **Narrative function:** Announce that this is Newton before the monument — private, severe, exact
- **Science function:** Establish light and geometry as the two visual engines of the book
- **Suggested layout:** Single figure, desk, prism light, orbital diagram on paper
- **Reference needs:** Newton Mature Professor or Young Scholar depending on final cover choice; no other characters
- **Generation note:** Keep spectrum restrained. This must not look psychedelic.

---

## PAGE 2 — BORN INTO WINTER (Woolsthorpe, 1642)

**Narrative beat:** Newton is born fatherless, tiny, improbable, in winter.

- **Narrative function:** Begin with absence rather than greatness
- **Science function:** None directly; this is tonal groundwork
- **Visual strategy:** Domestic interior, infant, worried adults, winter pressing at the windows
- **Text load:** Two or three concise caption boxes
- **Reference needs:** Hannah, Grandmother, infant Newton may not need a strict lock
- **Generation risk:** Avoid melodrama or saintly-birth imagery

---

## PAGE 3 — HIS MOTHER LEAVES (Woolsthorpe, 1646)

**Narrative beat:** Hannah remarries and leaves Isaac with his grandmother.

- **Narrative function:** Emotional root of Newton's inwardness
- **Science function:** None directly
- **Visual strategy:** Departure scene split against child left at threshold or window
- **Text load:** Minimal; let the image carry the separation
- **Reference needs:** Newton Child, Hannah, Grandmother
- **Generation risk:** Keep it emotionally hard but unsentimental

---

## PAGE 4 — WALLS FULL OF DRAWINGS (Woolsthorpe, late 1640s–early 1650s)

**Narrative beat:** The child watches wind, shadows, walls, and small mechanisms.

- **Narrative function:** Show peculiarity as attention, not generic genius glow
- **Science function:** Seed the habit of measuring and observing
- **Visual strategy:** Multiple small panels or one page of linked details — scratched circles, paper devices, weather watching
- **Text load:** Light captioning only
- **Reference needs:** Newton Child, Grandmother optional
- **Generation risk:** Do not turn this into cute tinkerer montage; keep it quiet and exact

---

## PAGE 5 — GRANTHAM (1654–1659)

**Narrative beat:** Boarding away from home, school, the Clarke house, practical craft, competitive sharpening.

- **Narrative function:** First social world beyond Woolsthorpe
- **Science function:** Show Newton learning through making: models, kites, measuring, observation
- **Visual strategy:** Three-panel page: schoolroom, apothecary/work table, windy outdoor experiment
- **Text load:** Moderate; one short speech bubble is enough if used
- **Reference needs:** Newton Schoolboy
- **Generation risk:** Too many mini-scenes can blur continuity; keep wardrobe stable

---

## PAGE 6 — THE FARM HE REFUSED (Woolsthorpe, 1659–1660)

**Narrative beat:** His mother pulls him from school to manage the farm. He fails because his mind is elsewhere.

- **Narrative function:** Clarify that Newton's life could have gone another way
- **Science function:** None directly, but reinforces misfit relation to ordinary labor
- **Visual strategy:** Split between farm tasks neglected and Newton reading under hedge or tree
- **Text load:** 2–3 captions
- **Reference needs:** Newton Schoolboy, Hannah
- **Generation risk:** Avoid making Newton seem merely lazy; image and captions should show displacement of attention

---

## PAGE 7 — TRINITY (Cambridge, 1661)

**Narrative beat:** Newton enters a university still teaching old frameworks while the new science is breaking in.

- **Narrative function:** Set up inherited order vs self-built mind
- **Science function:** Introduce the need to read beyond the curriculum
- **Visual strategy:** Formal lecture or disputation above; Newton alone with books below
- **Text load:** Moderate
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, optional Barrow or generic tutor
- **Generation risk:** Keep the contrast clear: public scholastic order vs private modern reading

---

## PAGE 8 — THE SECRET NOTEBOOK (Cambridge, 1664)

**Narrative beat:** Newton fills private pages with the new mathematics and natural philosophy.

- **Narrative function:** Self-education as self-construction
- **Science function:** Name the intellectual inputs without turning the page into a bibliography
- **Visual strategy:** Newton bent over the Waste Book; page fragments visible; headings and diagrams readable at a glance only
- **Text load:** Low to medium
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar
- **Generation risk:** Do not cram too much tiny notebook text into the image

---

## PAGE 9 — PLAGUE AND SILENCE (Woolsthorpe, 1665)

**Narrative beat:** Cambridge closes. Newton returns home. The interruption becomes opening.

- **Narrative function:** Shift from institutional life to solitary discovery
- **Science function:** Set the stage for concentrated work
- **Visual strategy:** Journey home or closed Cambridge -> quiet Woolsthorpe chamber
- **Text load:** Light
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, Woolsthorpe setting
- **Generation risk:** Avoid generic plague imagery; focus on withdrawal and room-scale silence

---

## PAGE 10 — THE BEAM THROUGH THE SHUTTER (Woolsthorpe, 1665–1666)

**Narrative beat:** Newton sends a narrow beam of sunlight through a prism.

- **Narrative function:** First great discovery page
- **Science function:** Show that white light splits into colored rays when refracted
- **Question to answer:** Does the prism stain the light with color, or reveal something already in it?
- **Visual strategy:** One controlled room, one beam, one prism, spectrum striking wall or screen
- **Text load:** Sparse and exact
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, Woolsthorpe experimental chamber
- **Generation risk:** Very high. Needs accurate beam geometry and restrained but legible spectrum.

---

## PAGE 11 — THE SECOND PRISM (Woolsthorpe, 1666)

**Narrative beat:** Newton isolates one color and sends it through another prism.

- **Narrative function:** Move from beautiful effect to proof
- **Science function:** Show that the second prism does not turn red into a new rainbow; each color keeps its identity
- **Question to answer:** Is color created by the glass, or already present in the beam?
- **Visual strategy:** Two-stage experiment, probably two or three panels with clear causal flow
- **Text load:** Moderate but disciplined
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, same chamber and apparatus continuity as page 10
- **Generation risk:** Extremely high. This page earns the scientific honesty of the whole book.

---

## PAGE 12 — A MATHEMATICS OF CHANGE (Woolsthorpe, 1665–1666)

**Narrative beat:** Newton develops fluxions because motion, growth, and falling cannot be handled well enough by static geometry alone.

- **Narrative function:** Expand Newton's discoveries beyond optics
- **Science function:** Explain why changing quantities need a new mathematical language
- **Question to answer:** How do you measure something that is always changing?
- **Visual strategy:** Newton at desk with geometric curve, moving point, falling object, and changing quantities linked on paper
- **Text load:** Moderate; captions must do real explanatory work
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, papers/diagrams
- **Generation risk:** High. Must feel intelligible, not fake.

---

## PAGE 13 — THE ORCHARD QUESTION (Woolsthorpe, 1665–1666)

**Narrative beat:** A falling apple or falling fruit is treated as a sober question, not a mythic revelation.

- **Narrative function:** Bring gravity down to earth without cheap legend-making
- **Science function:** Ask why bodies fall toward the Earth at all
- **Question to answer:** What is this downward tendency, and does it stop at the tops of trees?
- **Visual strategy:** Orchard scene paired with Newton's face or notebook, grounded and unromantic
- **Text load:** Light to medium
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, Woolsthorpe orchard
- **Generation risk:** Moderate. The hardest part is tonal: anti-myth, pro-question.

---

## PAGE 14 — THE MOON FALLS AROUND THE EARTH

**Narrative beat:** Newton realizes the moon is not exempt from falling. It falls continuously while moving sideways.

- **Narrative function:** Turn a local question into a cosmic one
- **Science function:** Explain orbit as perpetual falling, not celestial perfection of a different kind
- **Question to answer:** What if the same pull that drops an apple keeps the moon in its path?
- **Visual strategy:** Split earthly falling body and moon orbit, joined by one geometric argument
- **Text load:** Moderate, very exact
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar; diagrams; moon/earth visual system
- **Generation risk:** Very high. This page must be clear, not mystical.

---

## PAGE 15 — HOW FORCE WEAKENS WITH DISTANCE

**Narrative beat:** Newton pushes from intuition toward quantitative law.

- **Narrative function:** Show that he is not satisfied with a verbal analogy
- **Science function:** Introduce the inverse-square weakening of gravity with distance in a child-respectful but clear way
- **Question to answer:** If the moon is much farther away, how much weaker is the pull there?
- **Visual strategy:** Diagram page anchored to notebook paper, with Earth, concentric distances, and a simple comparison to falling near the surface
- **Text load:** Moderate; probably caption-heavy
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, notebook/diagram surfaces
- **Generation risk:** Very high. This is a likely prototype page.

---

## PAGE 16 — YEARS KEPT PRIVATE (Cambridge, 1667–1671)

**Narrative beat:** Newton returns to Cambridge, becomes Fellow, then Lucasian professor, but keeps huge results mostly to himself.

- **Narrative function:** Convert solitude from childhood fate into mature method
- **Science function:** None new; this is consolidation and character
- **Visual strategy:** Newton in Cambridge rooms surrounded by papers he will not yet release; Barrow may appear briefly if useful
- **Text load:** Light to medium
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, optional Barrow
- **Generation risk:** Moderate. Needs to feel charged, not static.

---

## PAGE 17 — THE REFLECTING TELESCOPE (Cambridge / London, 1668–1672)

**Narrative beat:** Newton builds a reflecting telescope to solve the color problem refracting telescopes suffer from.

- **Narrative function:** Show him as instrument-maker, not only abstract thinker
- **Science function:** Connect optics to engineering consequence
- **Question to answer:** If lenses spread colors, can mirrors avoid that problem?
- **Visual strategy:** Workshop-making scene paired with public demonstration or close-up of the instrument
- **Text load:** Medium
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar, telescope components, possible Royal Society setting
- **Generation risk:** High. Instrument details need to look plausible and tactile.

---

## PAGE 18 — LIGHT AND WARINESS (1672–1675)

**Narrative beat:** Public criticism over optics makes Newton recoil from publication and controversy.

- **Narrative function:** Explain why such a mind hides its work
- **Science function:** None new; this is about the cost of exposure
- **Visual strategy:** Newton reading hostile correspondence; public chamber vs private withdrawal
- **Text load:** Moderate; letters can be partly legible but should not require tiny text
- **Reference needs:** Newton Young Scholar or transitional older Newton depending on final age; Royal Society setting optional
- **Generation risk:** Moderate. Avoid crowded talking-head page.

---

## PAGE 19 — A PROBLEM RETURNS (1679–1684)

**Narrative beat:** The orbital question returns through correspondence and lingering unfinished thought.

- **Narrative function:** Bridge the long silent middle stretch to Halley's arrival
- **Science function:** Reawaken the gravity problem as unfinished quantitative work
- **Visual strategy:** Newton with old papers reopened; letter on desk; orbit sketches returning
- **Text load:** Light to medium
- **Reference needs:** Newton Mature Professor
- **Generation risk:** Moderate. This page is mostly structural and must not feel vague.

---

## PAGE 20 — HALLEY COMES TO CAMBRIDGE (August 1684)

**Narrative beat:** Halley asks the right question in person.

- **Narrative function:** External catalyst finally matches Newton's hidden internal work
- **Science function:** Put the planetary-motion problem into crisp verbal form
- **Question to answer:** What curve would a planet follow if pulled by an inverse-square force toward the sun?
- **Visual strategy:** Two-man scene at desk or walking court; intellectual electricity, not melodrama
- **Text load:** A few precise speech bubbles matter here
- **Reference needs:** Newton Mature Professor, Halley
- **Generation risk:** Moderate. Character contrast must be strong.

---

## PAGE 21 — DE MOTU

**Narrative beat:** Newton produces the short tract that proves he has the answer.

- **Narrative function:** First outward crystallization of the hidden work
- **Science function:** Show conic sections and motion becoming formal argument
- **Question to answer:** How do mathematical proof and physical motion lock together?
- **Visual strategy:** Manuscript page, Newton writing, geometric diagrams large and clean enough to read as structure
- **Text load:** Medium
- **Reference needs:** Newton Mature Professor
- **Generation risk:** High. Needs precision without looking like a textbook scan.

---

## PAGE 22 — WRITING THE PRINCIPIA (1685–1686)

**Narrative beat:** Newton writes obsessively while Halley supports, edits, and sustains the project.

- **Narrative function:** Show the compression of a world into argument
- **Science function:** Convey that the work is a system, not a single insight
- **Visual strategy:** Montage or dense single room: piles of manuscript, diagrams, compasses, candle stubs, Halley in one secondary panel if needed
- **Text load:** Moderate
- **Reference needs:** Newton Mature Professor, Halley optional secondary
- **Generation risk:** High. Must feel intense and orderly, not cluttered.

---

## PAGE 23 — ONE LAW FOR APPLE AND MOON (1687)

**Narrative beat:** The *Principia* makes one universe out of many scattered phenomena.

- **Narrative function:** Climax page
- **Science function:** State clearly that the same laws govern falling bodies, projectiles, moons, and planets
- **Question to answer:** What changes when heaven and earth are shown to obey the same mathematics?
- **Visual strategy:** Newton's manuscript world opening into linked vignettes: falling body, cannonball arc, moon orbit, planetary path — all controlled by one underlying geometry
- **Text load:** Moderate but exact
- **Reference needs:** Newton Mature Professor; diagrams; possibly Halley absent so Newton remains the center
- **Generation risk:** Extremely high. This is the main concept page of the book.

---

## PAGE 24 — CODA: PRIVATE MAN, PUBLIC UNIVERSE

**Narrative beat:** Newton remains inward, but the world is now larger and more ordered because of what he brought into it.

- **Narrative function:** End with grandeur that stays human and slightly severe
- **Science function:** Echo the system without repeating page 23 exactly
- **Visual strategy:** Newton alone after publication; manuscript closed or bound; the orchard, moon, prism, and geometry held in quiet relation
- **Text load:** Light
- **Reference needs:** Newton Mature Professor
- **Generation risk:** Moderate. Resist overblown victory imagery.

## Generation Principles For This Script

- Keep one scientific burden per science page
- Split pages rather than overload them
- Use objects and rooms as anchors for explanation
- Never rely on the image model to invent accurate science exposition on its own
- Treat every page as a composition problem first, a prose problem second
