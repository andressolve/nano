# The Invisible Forces — Prototype Page Prompts

These are first-pass prototype prompts for the highest-risk pages.

Rule for this pass: **no in-image text**. We are testing composition, scientific legibility, period plausibility, and Newton continuity first.

---

## Page 10 — The Beam Through The Shutter

Use the Young Scholar Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: explanatory graphic novel page prototype
Page objective: show Newton's prism experiment as a real physical event in a real room, not as decorative magic
Question to show: does the prism create color, or reveal something already present in white light?
Composition structure: a single strong panel or two very clear vertical panels within one page image; the apparatus must dominate the reading
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton young-scholar reference into a full graphic-novel page set in a darkened Woolsthorpe chamber in 1666. Newton stands or leans beside a table in a small shuttered room. A single narrow beam of sunlight enters through a tiny hole in the shutter and strikes a glass prism. The refracted light spreads into a restrained but legible spectrum across a pale wall or screen. Newton is observing the experiment carefully, not posing theatrically. The room is materially real: rough plaster, wood table, papers, leaded window, dust in the beam.
Subject: Isaac Newton young scholar with prism apparatus
Setting: Woolsthorpe experimental chamber
Science burden: one beam, one prism, believable light path, restrained spectrum, real-room realism
Required visible elements: shutter aperture, single white beam, glass prism, spectral spread on wall or paper screen, Newton, plain table, rough room surfaces
Constraints: no text, no labels, no equations, no speech bubbles; serious mature graphic novel; realistic 17th-century materials; spectrum must be visible but not psychedelic; Newton must read as investigator not magician
Avoid: fantasy alchemy, oversized rainbow, mystical glow, floating infographic overlays, modern lab equipment, children’s-book softness
```

## Page 11 — The Second Prism

Use the Young Scholar Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: explanatory graphic novel page prototype
Page objective: show the second-prism proof as a visual argument
Question to show: is color created by the glass, or already present in the light?
Composition structure: two large panels or one split composition with unmistakable stage-one and stage-two logic
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton young-scholar reference into a graphic-novel page in the same Woolsthorpe chamber. The page must show a clear two-stage prism experiment. In the first stage, white light passes through a prism and spreads into a restrained spectrum. In the second stage, Newton isolates one colored ray and sends only that single ray through a second prism. The result should preserve the selected color rather than turning into a full rainbow. Newton is adjusting the apparatus with intense concentration. The apparatus and causal comparison must be more important than facial drama.
Subject: Isaac Newton young scholar performing a two-stage light experiment
Setting: Woolsthorpe experimental chamber matching page 10
Science burden: first prism spreads white light, second prism tests one isolated color, clear visual comparison
Required visible elements: first prism, spectrum, selected single-color ray, second prism, resulting beam, Newton, table, dark room, white wall or screens
Constraints: no text, no labels, no equations; the viewer must understand that the second stage is a test of one selected color; serious painterly historical graphic novel; realistic apparatus; clear comparison without clutter
Avoid: decorative rainbow repetition, impossible light geometry, mystical symbolism, tiny schematic labels, modern optics-lab look
```

## Page 14 — The Moon Falls Around The Earth

Use the Young Scholar Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: explanatory graphic novel page prototype
Page objective: make orbit look physical rather than mystical
Question to show: what if the same pull that drops an apple keeps the moon in its path?
Composition structure: split page with an earthly scene below or to one side and a notebook-or-diagram-based orbital comparison above or opposite; one geometric logic must unify both halves
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton young-scholar reference into a graphic-novel page that links a falling object on Earth to the moon's orbit. Newton is in or near Woolsthorpe, at a table or in the orchard at dusk, studying notes. One part of the page shows a body falling downward near Earth. Another part shows the moon curving around the Earth as if continually falling while moving sideways. The geometry should emerge from Newton's notebook pages, chalked diagrams, or paper on the table, not as a generic floating infographic. The page should feel severe, lucid, and physical.
Subject: Isaac Newton young scholar connecting falling bodies and orbital motion
Setting: Woolsthorpe orchard and study logic combined
Science burden: continual falling plus sideways motion, same pull linking earth and moon
Required visible elements: Newton, notebook or papers, falling body near Earth, Earth-moon relation, one clean orbital arc or tangent logic, dusk or night restraint
Constraints: no text, no labels, no mystical star-pageantry; one geometric logic only; realistic atmosphere; orbit must feel like physical argument, not romantic astronomy art
Avoid: astrology aesthetics, cosmic fantasy, bombastic starscape, too many celestial objects, generic infographic overlay
```

## Page 15 — How Force Weakens With Distance

Use the Young Scholar Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: explanatory graphic novel page prototype
Page objective: show distance comparison clearly without turning the page into a worksheet
Question to show: if the moon is much farther away, how much weaker is the pull there?
Composition structure: a page anchored by a large notebook sheet or manuscript page on a table, with Newton present and a very simple near-Earth vs far-from-Earth comparison
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton young-scholar reference into a graphic-novel page in which Newton studies a clean distance-comparison diagram on paper. The composition should compare pull close to Earth with pull far from Earth in a visually graspable way. Use a notebook or manuscript sheet as the main frame for the argument. Newton is at the desk, intensely focused. The room remains materially real, but the page priority is clarity of the comparison. Keep the geometry spare, large, and readable.
Subject: Isaac Newton young scholar at desk with a gravity-distance comparison diagram
Setting: Woolsthorpe study chamber
Science burden: near versus far, weakening with distance, clean visual hierarchy
Required visible elements: Newton, manuscript sheet, Earth, two distances, a clear comparison of stronger pull near and weaker pull far away, table, papers, candle or window light
Constraints: no text, no labels, no crowded callouts, no pseudo-equations; still read as a graphic novel page rather than a classroom worksheet; serious mature historical realism
Avoid: dense educational clutter, tiny labels, arbitrary symbols, floating infographic boxes, exaggerated cosmic background
```

## Page 23 — One Law For Apple And Moon

Use the Mature Professor Newton reference as the edit base.

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: explanatory graphic novel climax-page prototype
Page objective: show the Principia as a synthesis in which one law governs many cases
Question to show: what changes when the same mathematics governs falling bodies, projectiles, moons, and planets?
Composition structure: one unified page framed by Newton's manuscript world; several linked cases can appear, but they must feel like one system, not a collage
Primary request: transform this Isaac Newton mature-professor reference into a climactic graphic-novel page set around his manuscript table in the 1680s. Newton is present as the anchor of the page, surrounded by papers, compasses, and diagrams. From that manuscript world, several linked phenomena emerge in one coherent composition: a falling object, a projectile or cannonball arc, the moon around Earth, and a planetary path. All of them must be visibly governed by one underlying geometry. The page should feel severe, lucid, and grand without becoming bombastic.
Subject: mature Isaac Newton and the unified law of motion and gravitation
Setting: Cambridge Principia writing chamber
Science burden: synthesis, one law across multiple cases, manuscript-based geometry
Required visible elements: Newton, desk, manuscripts, compasses, one underlying geometric structure, linked vignettes of falling body, projectile arc, moon orbit, planetary path
Constraints: no text, no labels, no title lettering; one-system feeling, not collage; serious painterly historical graphic novel; Newton must remain the center through the manuscript space
Avoid: cosmic spectacle, scrapbook composition, random astronomy montage, ornate allegory, clutter, tiny diagram labels
```
