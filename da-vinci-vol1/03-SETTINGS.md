# The Boy Who Watched Birds — Settings

Recurring locations across the volume. Each setting block is what gets pasted into page prompts when the location appears.

## 1. Vinci hill country (Pages 1–2)

**Era:** 1452–1466. Tuscan countryside ~20 miles west of Florence.

**Description:**
> Vinci, a small Tuscan hill town in the early Quattrocento. Stone and tile-roofed houses cluster on a slope above terraced olive groves and cypress lanes. Below the village: small streams running down to the Arno valley, pools where dragonflies hover, sun-warmed grass, white-and-gray pebbles, kingfishers on willow branches, kites circling above the fields. Distant cypresses on hilltops. Soft warm afternoon light, sun-bleached. Palette: terracotta, olive green, raw umber, bone white, warm shadow.

## 2. Verrocchio's workshop, Florence (Pages 3–6)

**Era:** c. 1466–1481.

**Description:**
> Andrea del Verrocchio's workshop on a Florentine side street, Quattrocento. The bottega is a stone-floored room, beam-ceilinged, larger than it looks from outside. Stations: a sculptor's bench with marble dust and chisels; a goldsmith's bench with small hammers, gravers, and a tiny anvil; an easel area with prepared poplar panels gessoed white; a pigment-grinding table with mortars of lapis lazuli, vermilion, ochre, and lead-tin yellow; a workshop hearth where bronze is melted for casting. Light enters from a single tall north window — clean, even, painter's light. Apprentices and journeymen at work. Drawings pinned to a board. Plaster casts of antique fragments on a high shelf. Smell-language (in image: amber lamp glow, pigment-stone gray, olive-oil-bound varnish gloss, wood shavings).

## 3. Florence streets (Pages 5–6, 21 transition)

**Era:** Quattrocento Florence under the Medici.

**Description:**
> Quattrocento Florence streets — narrow stone-paved ways between four-story tower houses, ground-floor shops with open wooden shutters, the dome of Brunelleschi's cathedral often visible in a high distant view, the Arno river curving past the city. Florentines in colorful giornee, soft caps, occasional cleric in black, occasional cavalryman. Warm yellow stone, terracotta roofs, ochre walls. Smell-language (in image): bread, ink, leather, river damp.

## 4. The road to Milan (Page 7 transition)

**Era:** 1482.

**Description:**
> A late-spring Italian road north — chestnut woods, cypress hills, distant snow-capped Alps. A small party (Leonardo, perhaps two servants, a pack mule with a bundle of canvases and a lyre case) on the road, dust on their boots. Soft cool morning light.

## 5. Castello Sforzesco, Milan (Pages 7–8, 13, 19–20)

**Era:** 1482–1499.

**Description:**
> The Castello Sforzesco in Milan: a vast brick-and-stone fortified palace with corner towers, courtyards within courtyards, terracotta brick walls deepening to crimson at sunset, gray stone arches, dark wooden gates iron-bound. Inside: high-ceilinged court chambers paneled in walnut, painted ceilings, deep red-and-gold velvet, oil-paintings on walls, chains of silver lamps. The great courtyard — wide, flat, brick-paved, large enough to display a 24-foot horse. Cool Lombard light, often overcast.

## 6. Ludovico's audience chamber (Pages 7, 12, 19)

**Era:** Sforza court, 1482–1499.

**Description:**
> A large, formal audience chamber at the Castello Sforzesco. Walnut-paneled walls, painted ceiling beams, a long polished table, a heavy carved chair where Ludovico sits, candles in silver holders, a single high arched window letting in cool north light. The Sforza arms (a coiled serpent swallowing a child) on a banner, but small. Petitioners stand. Ducal advisers in dark gowns flank the duke.

## 7. Leonardo's Milan studio (Pages 9, 10, 11, 17, 18, 22)

**Era:** 1482–1499.

**Description:**
> A working studio in Milan: a beam-ceilinged room with a large north window, a long oak table covered with sheets of paper, sketches pinned everywhere, anatomical drawings, water-flow diagrams, mechanical hinges, bird-wing studies. Two easels: one with the unfinished *Lady with an Ermine* (Pages 8 onward), one with smaller studies. Pigment-grinding station. A bronze model of a horse on a side table. Cages of small birds (ones Leonardo will set free) hung near the window. Open notebooks scattered. A wooden chest with linen wrappings. Cool north light, candle warmth at evening.

## 8. The refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie (Pages 14–16)

**Era:** 1495–1498.

**Description:**
> The refectory of the Dominican convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan: a long high white-plastered hall, vaulted ceiling, two long oak refectory tables running parallel, a small reader's pulpit, narrow clerestory windows letting in cool north light from above. The north wall — the wall Leonardo is painting — is broad and white-prepared, scaffolded with a wooden platform high against it. Buckets of pigment, oil flasks, charcoal. The opposite wall holds a Crucifixion fresco by Donato Montorfano (already there, painted in true fresco). At meal hours, Dominican friars in white-and-black robes eat at the tables; the rest of the time the room is empty and quiet.

## 9. The Last Supper wall — close-up registers (Pages 15–16, 21)

**Era:** 1495–1498 and after.

**Description:**
> Close on the painted wall: white-prepared dry plaster ground, with the Last Supper composition emerging. Christ at the center beneath a triple window opening onto a Lombard landscape, the twelve apostles in four clusters of three, the moment "one of you will betray me" capturing each disciple's reaction. Tempera-and-oil paint, glaze-rich, more luminous than fresco — but visibly *applied to dry plaster*, not absorbed into it. By Page 21 (post-1499 register): the first ghost-flakes already appearing in the lower border, paint not chemically bonded.

## 10. The clay horse model in courtyard (Pages 13, 20)

**Era:** Nov 1493 – late 1499.

**Description:**
> The 24-foot clay model of the Sforza Horse standing on a low wooden plinth in the great brick-paved courtyard of the Castello Sforzesco. Gray-brown unfired clay, dramatic horse anatomy carved in high relief — flared nostrils, taut neck tendons, flowing mane. Wooden armature visible at points where the surface clay has cracked away. In Page 13 (Nov 1493): a wedding crowd in finery applauds, banners hang. In Page 20 (Dec 1499): the courtyard is empty, French archers' arrows stuck in the horse's flanks like quills, fragments of clay on the brick, late-autumn cold light.

## 11. The road south (Page 22)

**Era:** December 1499.

**Description:**
> A late-autumn / early-winter Lombard road heading south — bare poplars, brown fields, low cold sun, the Alps shrinking behind. Two riders: Leonardo and the mathematician Luca Pacioli. Pack mule with two leather saddlebags swollen with notebooks and rolled sheets. Salaì rides a smaller horse beside them. Distant smoke from a farmhouse. The road continues out of frame ahead.
