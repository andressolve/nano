After the story

Test your understanding

Five questions about loyalty, judgment, and the cost of acting alone—not a test of pirate trivia.

1. Why did Tom refuse Silver in the forest?

Yes. Tom understands what Silver is asking and refuses it plainly. His courage is moral before it becomes physical.

Not quite. Tom refuses because joining Silver would mean sharing the mutineers’ betrayal and violence.

2. What did Ben Gunn ask Jim to carry back to the loyal party?

Right. Ben asks for a gentleman’s promise, a reward, and a way home. His “reasons of his own” remain deliberately unresolved.

Not quite. Ben wants Trelawney’s word: one thousand pounds and passage home if his knowledge helps them.

3. What weakness did Smollett expose when he rejected Silver’s terms?

Exactly. Silver sounds powerful, but Smollett names the facts hemming him in: no treasure, no reliable crew for the ship, Gray lost, and a dangerous shore.

Not quite. Smollett’s answer works because it exposes practical failures beneath Silver’s performance of control.

4. What does Jim understand while cleaning the log-house after the attack?

Yes. Jim neither claims all the blame nor escapes responsibility. He begins to measure courage by what it asks of other people.

Not quite. Jim’s reckoning is careful: he did not cause the gunfire, but leaving without permission still had a real cost.

5. Why does Jim decide to cut the Hispaniola loose?

Right. The plan is reckless, but it answers a real strategic danger: the ship can turn the pirates’ numbers into control of the island.

Not quite. Jim sees the schooner as the pirates’ greatest advantage and means to remove it before they can use it.
