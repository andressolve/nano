After the story

Test your understanding

Five questions about choices, trust, and the clues Jim learned to read—not a test of pirate trivia.

1. Why did Mrs. Hawkins return to the inn after Billy Bones died?

Yes. Her courage is joined to a limit: she will claim what is owed, but she refuses to become a thief because thieves are coming.

Not quite. Mrs. Hawkins returns for the inn’s lawful dues and insists on taking no more. That refusal defines her as strongly as her courage.

2. Why did Jim take the oilskin packet from the chest?

Right. Jim takes the sealed packet to square an unfinished count. Only later do Livesey and Trelawney discover what it contains.

Not quite. Jim does not know the packet is a map. He takes it because danger interrupts the coin count and his mother refuses to take more than she is owed.

3. What made Captain Smollett distrust the voyage before it began?

Exactly. Smollett sees that the secret has already escaped. Moving powder, weapons, and loyal berths aft is caution based on evidence.

Not quite. Smollett’s concern is the leaked secret: men he did not choose already know the destination and the treasure.

4. Why was Silver able to win Jim’s trust?

Yes. Silver does not merely tell Jim to trust him. He teaches, works, laughs, and performs innocence convincingly enough that the later revelation hurts.

Not quite. Silver earns trust through real competence and apparent warmth. His performance works because much of the pleasure Jim feels around him is genuine.

5. What is the loyal group’s one advantage when Jim reports the mutiny?

Right. They are badly outnumbered, but knowledge has changed sides. Silver still believes his plan is secret.

Not quite. The loyal party is small. Its advantage is secrecy: the conspirators do not know Jim heard them.
