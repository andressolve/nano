# Treasure Island, Volume I: The Map — Verbatim Production Script

## Production contract

This file governs every visible word on the cover and 33 story pages.

- Canvas: 1024 × 1536, 2:3 portrait.
- All captions, dialogue, sound effects, and necessary signs are native to the finished image.
- Render only the listed text.
- Recurring location-sign exception: whenever the established inn exterior, facade, or hanging sign is visible during Pages 1–14, it may read `ADMIRAL BENBOW` exactly. Repeating that exact location name across chronological views of the same inn is authorized and does not count as invented lettering. No other environmental wording is authorized unless a page lists it explicitly.
- No page titles, page numbers, speaker labels, watermarks, signatures, compass letters, decorative writing, or pseudo-writing.
- A page may change composition during regeneration, but its text remains verbatim unless this script is revised first.
- Every balloon maps reading order, speaker, position, and tail endpoint.
- Silent characters receive no balloons.
- If lettering or attribution fails, regenerate the complete page.

### Density tiers

- **T2:** 20–45 words — atmospheric.
- **T3:** 45–70 words — standard.
- **T4:** 70–100 words — demanding, with protected quiet fields.
- **T5:** 100–130 words — exceptional; avoid unless the scene needs it.

### Language

- Jim narrates in direct first person from memory.
- Sentence structure is modern and immediately readable.
- Selected Stevenson phrases survive where they remain clear.
- Pirate speech has flavor without becoming a decoding exercise.
- The word `Captain` without a name refers to Billy Bones only in the Admiral Benbow sequence. Captain Smollett is introduced by full name.

# COVER — THE MAP

## Narrative job

Promise mystery, voyage, Silver's charisma, and a dangerous island without presenting a generic pirate collage.

## Visual verb

UNFOLD.

## Composition

A folded parchment map opens vertically through the entire page. Its torn lower edge becomes the cliff road above the Admiral Benbow, where small Jim watches Billy Bones and his sea chest arrive. Its central red route becomes the *Hispaniola's* wake across a dark teal sea. At the top, the route curls around the black silhouette of Treasure Island.

Long John Silver stands large along the right edge, balanced naturally on one leg and his wooden crutch, warmly lantern-lit and smiling toward Jim. His shape is inviting, not villain-coded. Captain Flint the green parrot is small on his shoulder.

No skull, crossbones, eye patch, hook, peg leg, skeleton, galleon, glowing treasure, or decorative map writing.

## Lettering — verbatim

Top:

> TREASURE ISLAND

Center:

> VOLUME I
>
> THE MAP

Bottom:

> A GRAPHIC ADVENTURE

**Density:** T2.

# PAGE 1 — THE CHEST COMES UP THE ROAD

## Narrative job

Open in Jim's remembered voice, establish home, and let danger arrive as one unforgettable human silhouette.

## Visual verb

ARRIVE.

## Layout

Three borderless vertical movements joined by one ink line: an older hand begins writing; the pen line becomes the cliff road; the road ends at Billy's enormous sea chest on the inn threshold. Billy and the chest dominate the lower two-thirds. Jim and his parents are small inside the warm doorway.

## Exact lettering

Caption, upper left:

> Years later, people asked me to write down everything about Treasure Island—except where it truly lies.

Caption, beside the road:

> I was Jim Hawkins, and my parents kept the Admiral Benbow inn beside the winter sea. That was where the danger first found me.

Billy, balloon upper right, tail to Billy:

> Rum. Bacon and eggs. And that headland for watching ships.

Billy, smaller balloon below, tail to Billy:

> Call me Captain.

**Density:** T3.

# PAGE 2 — THE CAPTAIN'S BERTH

## Narrative job

Show Billy taking possession of the inn and reveal that the frightening man is himself frightened.

## Visual verb

OCCUPY.

## Layout

Four strictly chronological horizontal tiers, read only from top to bottom. The first shows Billy scanning the road from the headland. The second isolates Jim answering from the inn doorway. The third shows Billy receiving that answer in the common room. The last shows Billy alone beside his locked chest, alert to footsteps outside. Do not use a cutaway or place a later reply above an earlier line. Every balloon must descend in spoken order: Billy's question, Jim's answer, Billy's instruction.

## Exact lettering

Caption, upper left:

> The Captain's gold lasted a week. His stay lasted months.

Billy, first balloon in the upper tier, tail to Billy:

> Did any seafaring men pass the inn today?

Jim, only balloon in the second tier, tail to Jim:

> No, Captain. Not one.

Billy, only balloon in the third tier, tail to Billy:

> Good. You tell me if one does.

Caption, lower right:

> He frightened every traveler who entered. Yet whenever footsteps sounded on the road, he looked more frightened than any of us.

**Density:** T3.

# PAGE 3 — THE ONE-LEGGED MAN

## Narrative job

Give Jim's childhood fear time to form before he meets the real Silver.

## Visual verb

HAUNT.

## Layout

The top quarter is a clearly bordered daylight conversation sequence that must be completed before the storm image begins. Billy places a silver coin in Jim's palm, explains the job, Jim asks his question, and Billy answers. Use separate beats or an unmistakable top-to-bottom balloon path; do not place any storm caption above or beside this conversation.

The lower three-quarters is one large storm-night bedroom. Jim lies awake in a small pool of candlelight. Outside the window, fence posts, blowing laundry, cliff grass, and sea foam suggest one-legged figures without becoming literal monsters. The first storm caption begins only below the completed daylight sequence.

## Exact lettering

Billy, first balloon in the daylight sequence, tail to Billy:

> This silver coin is yours each month. In return, watch for a seafaring man with one leg.

Jim, inset balloon, tail to Jim:

> What does he look like?

Billy, final balloon in the daylight sequence, tail to Billy:

> When you see him, you'll know. Tell me at once.

Caption, below the completed conversation in a protected dark-sky field:

> After that, on stormy nights, I imagined the one-legged sailor everywhere—in a gate swinging loose, a tree bent by wind, a shadow crossing the road.

Caption, near Jim:

> I earned one silver coin each month and paid for it in dreams.

**Density:** T4.

# PAGE 4 — DOCTOR AT THE TABLE

## Narrative job

Introduce Dr. Livesey through calm courage and show Jim that Billy's power has limits.

## Visual verb

STAND.

## Layout

Use five fully bordered chronological panels. Panel 1 introduces Doctor Livesey before the confrontation: he sits beside Jim's visibly ill father at the common-room table, medical case open, while Jim listens. The opening caption appears here. Panel 2 shows Billy interrupting from across the room. Panel 3 shows Livesey turning calmly from Mr. Hawkins to answer. Panel 4 is the cutlass confrontation with Jim watching. The final low panel is Billy's cutlass returning to its sheath. Livesey must never appear already arguing before his identity, purpose, and presence are established.

## Exact lettering

Caption, Panel 1 before any dialogue:

> One evening, Doctor Livesey came to tend my sick father. The Captain interrupted him.

Billy, Panel 2 balloon, tail to Billy:

> Silence there!

Livesey, Panel 3 balloon, tail to Livesey:

> I was speaking.

Billy, balloon right, tail to Billy:

> You tell me what to do, Doctor?

Livesey, balloon left, tail to Livesey:

> I tell you that rum is killing you. Put away that cutlass—or I promise you shall hang.

Caption, beside Jim's reaction:

> I had never seen the Captain obey anyone.

Caption, beside the sheathed blade:

> He obeyed the doctor.

**Density:** T4.

# PAGE 6 — STEEL AND SILENCE

## Narrative job

Let the confrontation erupt, then show that fear and drink have already weakened Billy.

## Visual verb

BREAK.

## Layout

Use six fully bordered chronological panels with an unmistakable top-to-bottom reading order. Panel 1: the private argument. Panel 2: Billy and Black Dog cross blades, then Billy's cutlass visibly nicks Black Dog's upper arm or shoulder. Show the cause and effect in this same panel: Billy's blade at the torn sleeve, a small non-graphic red streak, and Black Dog recoiling from the fresh wound before he runs. Panel 3: the same wounded Black Dog escapes bleeding through the open door while Billy reaches the threshold but stops, suddenly white and unsteady. Panel 4: back inside, show the collapse itself as an action, not merely its aftermath: Billy's cutlass has just struck the floor, his knees visibly buckle, and his heavy body is falling sideways while Jim reaches toward him. One Billy only in this panel. Panel 5: Doctor Livesey arrives visibly through the inn door carrying his medical case; Billy is already prone on the floor and Jim is beside him. Panel 6: Livesey kneels and examines Billy.

Do not jump directly from an upright Billy watching Black Dog flee to Billy already prone under examination. The reader must see, in separate adjacent panels, Black Dog flee, Billy stagger, Billy collapse, Livesey arrive, and Livesey examine him.

Doctor Livesey is the same man established on Page 4: a calm middle-aged physician with the same graying dark hair, green-black coat, cream waistcoat, and medical case. Do not depict a youthful stranger or a second Jim.

## Exact lettering

Billy, first panel balloon, tail to Billy:

> You found me. Speak.

Black Dog, first panel balloon, tail to Black Dog:

> Our old pirate captain Flint's crew wants the packet you stole from him.

Sound effect, small beside crossing blades:

> CLANG

Billy, Panel 2 balloon after the blades cross, tail to Billy. The balloon must sit beside Billy, with one uninterrupted tail whose pointed tip visibly touches the area beside Billy's speaking mouth; never center it beneath the crossed blades or leave it tailless:

> Run, then!

Caption, Panel 3:

> Black Dog escaped bleeding. Billy staggered back from the doorway, white as salt.

Caption, Panel 4:

> His cutlass struck the floor. Then Billy did.

Caption, Panel 5, beside Livesey's visible entrance:

> Doctor Livesey came at once.

Livesey, final panel balloon, tail to Livesey:

> Rum and terror have done half their work. Another drink may finish it.

**Density:** T3.

# PAGE 7 — A HOUSE IN MOURNING

## Narrative job

Honor the loss of Jim's father and allow grief, exhaustion, and Billy's warning to coexist.

## Visual verb

DIM.

## Layout

Three wide descending panels. A covered upstairs candle; Mrs. Hawkins and Jim working quietly below; Billy speaking from a dark doorway while the road beyond gathers fog. The Captain is visually secondary to the family's grief.

Billy must remain the same unmistakable hulking, broad-faced, clean-shaven older sailor established on Pages 1–6: gray-streaked tied-back hair, blue sea coat, and brown waistcoat. Grief and illness may change his expression, but must not make him gaunt, bearded, or into a different man.

## Exact lettering

Caption, upper panel:

> That winter, my father died.

Caption, middle panel:

> Grief made the inn quiet. It did not make the danger wait.

Billy, lower panel balloon, tail to Billy:

> Jim—if they give me the black spot, they're after my chest.

Jim, balloon beside him, tail to Jim:

> What is the black spot?

Billy, final balloon, tail to Billy:

> A summons. Worse men are coming—and the one-legged man worst of all.

**Density:** T3.

# PAGE 8 — THE BLIND MAN'S HAND

## Narrative job

Turn apparent helplessness into physical menace and bring Pew to Billy without horror exaggeration.

## Visual verb

CLAMP.

## Layout

Use six fully bordered chronological panels. Pew is first a small bent figure on the road; Jim points back toward the village; Pew's hand closes around Jim's wrist; the corridor tightens during his threat; a dedicated full-body turnaround panel visibly reverses both figures; the last panel stops at Billy's door. Pew's cane creates one continuous tapping line.

Lock Pew's appearance for Pages 8–9: the same gaunt elderly blind man with pale unseeing eyes, a full gray-white beard, a wide dark hat, long black coat, and long white cane. Every later appearance in this sequence must preserve these features.

Lock the road geography before drawing any panel. In Panels 1–2, the village and the Admiral Benbow are visibly BEHIND Pew, over his shoulder on the left side of the road, while Pew's body, face, cane, and next step point AWAY from them toward the empty coastal road on the right. Jim stands in front of Pew and points clearly backward past Pew toward the visible village. The direction of Pew's body and the direction of Jim's pointing hand must be opposite. Do not place the village ahead of Pew or make Jim point in the same direction Pew is walking.

Panel 5 must show the reversal itself in full-body profile: Jim and Pew have turned 180 degrees and now walk LEFT, back along the same road toward the SAME Admiral Benbow that remains visible on the left. Pew's cane and Jim's feet point left. A caption states the reversal. Panel 6 shows the same inn close-up with its distinctive hanging ADMIRAL BENBOW sign, lit square window, stone wall, and dark wooden door; Jim and Pew enter from the RIGHT and walk LEFT toward that door. Do not replace it with an unrelated cottage, make them continue right, or omit the visible connection to the inn.

## Exact lettering

Pew, Panel 1 balloon, tail to Pew:

> Will any kind young friend guide a poor blind man?

Jim, Panel 2 balloon, tail to Jim:

> The village is behind you.

Pew, Panel 3 balloon, tail to Pew:

> Now, boy. Take me to Captain Bill.

Jim, Panel 3 balloon, tail to Jim:

> You're hurting me!

Pew, Panel 4 balloon, tail to Pew:

> I can break your arm as easily as a pipe stem.

Caption, Panel 5 turnaround:

> He forced me to turn back toward the Admiral Benbow.

Sound effect along the cane path:

> TAP  TAP  TAP

**Density:** T3.

# PAGE 9 — TILL TEN

## Narrative job

Deliver the black spot, Billy's final panic, and his death with clarity and restraint.

## Visual verb

STOP.

## Layout

A large clock face without numerals forms six clearly bordered chronological wedges with an unmistakable clockwise reading order. Wedge 1: Jim is still visibly beside the doorway after Pew marched him there on Page 8. Pew opens the hand that was gripping Jim's wrist and visibly releases him before slapping the paper into Billy's palm; Billy, Pew, and Jim are all present, and Jim does not vanish during the handoff. Wedge 2: close-up of the black spot. Wedge 3: Pew visibly turns his back on Billy and exits through the open inn door into the night, his white cane extended ahead of him; Billy remains inside holding the paper and Jim remains nearby. Wedge 4: the door is now empty and Pew is gone; Billy reads the spot and speaks. Wedge 5: Billy reaches for the stair and collapses while Jim and his mother try to catch him. Wedge 6: the final clock stroke crosses the quiet inn and the death caption.

Do not make Pew vanish between the black-spot handoff and Billy's collapse. His departure must occupy its own complete wedge before Billy reads aloud: show Pew from behind crossing the doorway threshold, then show the doorway empty in the following wedge.

If the wedges are numbered, render ALL SIX number medallions exactly once in order: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The Wedge 3 medallion must be plainly visible beside Pew's exit. Never label the sequence 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 or otherwise imply a missing panel. It is also acceptable to omit all numeric medallions; a partially numbered sequence is forbidden.

Pew must exactly match Page 8: gaunt elderly blind man, pale unseeing eyes, full gray-white beard, wide dark hat, long black coat, and long white cane. Billy must remain the same hulking, broad-faced, clean-shaven older sailor from Pages 1–7, with gray-streaked tied-back hair, blue sea coat, and brown waistcoat. Do not remove Pew's hat or beard, and do not slim or replace Billy.

## Exact lettering

Pew, first wedge balloon, tail to Pew:

> Hold out your hand, Bill.

Black spot, exact visible text:

> YOU HAVE TILL TEN TONIGHT.

Caption, beside Pew's visible exit in Wedge 3:

> Pew tapped back into the night without another word.

Billy, Wedge 4 balloon after Pew's exit, tail to Billy:

> Four hours. I'll beat them yet—

Sound effect, spaced down the page:

> DONG

Caption, lower field:

> He did not. Before our clock finished striking six, Billy Bones was dead.

**Density:** T2.

# PAGE 10 — NO ONE WILL COME

## Narrative job

Give Mrs. Hawkins moral agency and show the community's fear before she returns to the inn.

## Visual verb

REFUSE.

## Layout

Use a strictly chronological doorstep sequence, not an unexplained portrait grid. The householders are anonymous, one-scene functions rather than new cast members. Never give either one a large, memorable face-forward portrait or frame him like a character the reader must remember.

The first tier begins with a narration box, then shows Jim and Mrs. Hawkins visibly standing outside the first neighboring cottage. Jim explicitly asks the neighbor to accompany them back as protection while Mrs. Hawkins retrieves Billy's unpaid debt from their own inn. The frightened first neighbor answers from behind a nearly closed door; show only a partial silhouette, one eye, and/or a hand at the door edge. The doorway, Jim's eyeline, and the reply tail must make his role as the unseen householder instant. Do not show a full new male portrait.

The second tier begins with a second narration box announcing that they have moved to the next house. Show a visibly different cottage—with different materials, roofline, window shape, and door color—and a different neighbor hidden behind a chained, almost fully closed door. His refusal may come from the narrow gap, with only a hand or indistinct sliver of face visible. Jim and Mrs. Hawkins remain visible outside so the relationship is unmistakable. Do not present a second prominent new face.

The third tier isolates Mrs. Hawkins and Jim on the lane between the houses. She explains why she will return. The final bottom strip shows mother and son walking back alone beneath the moon, with Jim's reply placed before the walking image concludes the page.

## Exact lettering

Caption, above the first doorstep:

> We ran to the nearest house and begged a neighbor to come back with us.

Jim, first doorstep balloon, tail to Jim:

> Billy Bones is dead in our inn. The blind man's crew is coming. Please stand watch while Mother takes only what he owes.

First neighbor, balloon from behind the first half-closed door, tail to first neighbor:

> Flint's men? We'll send for the mounted revenue officers, but we won't go near that inn.

Caption, above the second doorstep:

> At the next house, another neighbor barred his door.

Second neighbor, balloon from behind the different chained door, tail to second neighbor:

> Leave the captain's money. Save yourselves.

Mrs. Hawkins, central balloon, tail to Mrs. Hawkins:

> He lived under my roof for months without paying what he owed. I will take our lawful money—not a penny more.

Jim, bottom-strip balloon, tail to Jim:

> Then we go together.

**Density:** T4.

# PAGE 12 — NOT A FARTHING OVER

## Narrative job

Let the coin count become suspense and define Mrs. Hawkins by what she refuses to steal.

## Visual verb

COUNT.

## Layout

Use six fully bordered chronological panels. First, one dominant top-down image of mixed coins spreading like a tide across the floor. Second, Mrs. Hawkins sorts guineas beside a small tied coin pouch. Third, Jim warns her and lifts the oilskin packet; his warning balloon is the single HIGHEST balloon in the panel, and Mrs. Hawkins's reply sits clearly BELOW it so the conversation cannot read in reverse. Fourth, Jim and Mrs. Hawkins leave the inn together while the loose coins are still visibly abandoned on the floor through the open doorway. Fifth, a distinct close-up completes the door-state change: Mrs. Hawkins's hand pulls the front door fully shut from outside and its simple latch visibly catches with CLICK. The doorway must end CLOSED before they run; this directly establishes the closed door that a pirate kicks inward on Page 13. Sixth, they visibly climb off the road into reeds beneath the small bridge while pirate lanterns continue past them toward the inn.

Panel 4 must show BOTH Jim and Mrs. Hawkins leaving the inn together as two separate full human figures. Jim carries the yellow oilskin packet against his chest; Mrs. Hawkins carries the small tied coin pouch. The remaining loose coins are plainly visible on the inn floor behind them through the still-open doorway. Do not show only one departing figure beneath the plural caption "We left."

Panel 5 must be a separate, silent action close-up outside the inn: Mrs. Hawkins's hand pulls the heavy front door fully shut and the simple latch visibly catches with the sound effect CLICK. Show the door closed edge-to-frame by the end of this panel. Do not keep the interior or coins visible, do not leave a gap at the jamb, and do not make the later pirate kick depend on an imagined closing action.

Panel 6 must complete the relocation before Page 13. Jim helps his mother down from the road into tall reeds directly beneath the bridge; both figures are still visible, and Jim still holds the yellow packet. On the road ABOVE and BEHIND them, two or more pirate lanterns pass toward the Admiral Benbow. Jim and his mother are not walking toward a lone lantern-bearer, and no unexplained encounter is deferred to Page 13.

The two containers must be unmistakably different in every appearance. The money pouch is small, soft, rounded, drawstring-tied, and shown beside loose coins. The oilskin packet matches the ochre-yellow folded oilskin visible at the lower right of Page 11: a thin, flexible rectangular envelope with a wrinkled waxed-cloth surface and dark stitching around all four edges. It has no leather cover, spine, pages, drawstring, rounded bulge, or visible coins. In the final inset Jim holds only this yellow oilskin envelope against his chest. Never depict it as a book, ledger, sack, purse, or money bag.

## Exact lettering

Mrs. Hawkins, upper balloon, tail to Mrs. Hawkins:

> Doubloons... pieces of eight... I cannot make this count.

Jim, balloon right, tail to Jim:

> They're coming. We cannot finish the count in time.

Mrs. Hawkins, balloon left, tail to Mrs. Hawkins:

> I will have my dues, and not a farthing over.

Jim, final inset balloon, tail to Jim:

> Then take what you have. I will carry this packet—whatever it is, they must not have it.

Caption, bottom:

> We left the rest shining on the floor.

Sound effect, Panel 5 at the visibly caught latch:

> CLICK

Caption, Panel 6:

> We hid beneath the bridge as pirate lanterns reached the inn.

**Density:** T3.

# PAGE 13 — FLINT'S PACKET

## Narrative job

Show the pirate raid from Jim's hidden point of view and establish that the packet matters more than money.

## Visual verb

RANSACK.

## Layout

Use seven fully bordered chronological panels viewed partly through the grass and bridge timbers where Jim and his faint mother hide. Panel 1 catches the first damage in progress: Pew stands OUTSIDE the still-recognizable Admiral Benbow and commands while one pirate's boot visibly kicks the intact door inward, splintering it at that moment. Panel 2 is one dominant full-width INTERIOR view of Billy's SAME massive sea chest on the floor: the open chest spans at least two adult shoulder-widths and roughly seventy percent of the panel width; its high rounded lid and heavy iron straps are unmistakable. One pirate kneels beside it and must reach both arms down into it rather than lift it. The chest is not brimming with treasure: the established clothes, compass, and pistols remain plainly visible inside with only a modest scatter of silver among them, while most remaining loose coins still shine on the inn floor where Jim and his mother left them on Page 12. Panel 3 returns to Pew at the now-broken doorway. Panel 4 is a closer INTERIOR search of Billy's large body; the anchored left edge, rounded lid, and iron strap of the SAME enormous open chest remain visible behind the pirate, cropped by the panel edge so no second complete container is created. Panel 5 is a distinct destruction beat: pirates visibly overturn furniture, tear open cupboards, smash a window, and break interior or roof timbers while the narration explains that they are searching for the packet. Panel 6 returns to Pew for his order about Jim. Panel 7 shows Jim and his faint mother hidden beneath the bridge, Jim pressing the packet to his chest. Use unmistakable walls, floorboards, windows, and doorway thresholds so every searched object remains INSIDE the Admiral Benbow. Never show the chest as a small repeated inset, portable coffer, or object in a pirate's hands.

The raid itself visibly causes the damage seen on Page 14: pirates kick in the door, smash a window, overturn furniture, tear cupboards open, and break roof/interior timbers while searching. The inn begins intact as on Page 12 and becomes visibly wrecked during this page. Do not begin with an unexplained ruin or postpone all damage until the next page.

Billy's sea chest must remain the exact massive container established on Pages 1–3 and 11–12: very large and floor-bound, with a high rounded lid, heavy dark iron straps, thick lock plate, and broad wooden body. Panel 2 alone shows the kneeling pirate rummaging inside. Whenever the chest or its cropped edge is visible elsewhere, it remains identical, open, and anchored on the inn floor. No pirate lifts, carries, or substitutes a smaller coffer. Do not show a portable chest, inner cash box, second container, flat lid, missing iron straps, or scale reduction.

Billy's body must remain the large, heavy, weathered older sailor established on Pages 1–9: gray-streaked hair, broad face, blue sea coat, and brown waistcoat. He must not resemble slim young Jim, who is simultaneously visible hiding below.

Pew remains outside, commanding through the doorway, and must match Pages 8–9: the same gaunt elderly blind man, dark hat, pale blind eyes, and long white cane. Do not replace him with an unrecognizable sighted man.

In the final hidden-Jim scene, Jim presses the same object established on Pages 11–12 against his chest: a thin, flexible, ochre-yellow rectangular oilskin envelope with a wrinkled waxed-cloth surface and dark stitching along all four edges. It has no leather cover, spine, pages, drawstring, rounded bulge, or visible coins. Do not depict a book, ledger, cloth sack, purse, pouch, or differently shaped parcel.

## Exact lettering

Pew, upper balloon, tail to Pew:

> Search him! Search the chest!

Pirate, interior balloon, tail to pirate:

> The money's here.

Pew, balloon at doorway, tail to Pew:

> Curse the money! Find Flint's packet!

Second pirate, interior balloon, tail to second pirate:

> Bill hasn't got it.

Caption, destruction panel:

> They tore through cupboards, windows, and roof beams searching for the packet.

Pew, final balloon, tail to Pew:

> The boy. Find the boy!

Caption, beside hidden Jim:

> I held the packet against my heart and tried not to breathe.

**Density:** T3.

# PAGE 14 — HOOVES IN THE MOONLIGHT

## Narrative job

End Pew's threat, preserve the shock of the ruined inn, and keep the death non-graphic.

## Visual verb

SCATTER.

## Layout

Use seven fully bordered chronological panels with an unmistakable top-to-bottom reading order. Panel 1 identifies the mounted revenue officers as they arrive from the village and the pirates begin to scatter. Panel 2 shows Pew shouting while Black Dog and the other pirates visibly run away from him. Black Dog must be unmistakably anchored as the same dark-haired adult pirate from Pages 5–6: maroon or dark-red coat and the same freshly torn, lightly bloodied upper sleeve or shoulder caused by Billy's cutlass. Pew shouts toward this visible matching figure, not toward an anonymous crowd. Panel 3 shows blind Pew turn toward the approaching riders instead of away and drop his cane. Panel 4 performs the fatal collision unmistakably but without gore: the lead rider hauls back the reins as the horse rears, yet one front hoof is visibly making contact with Pew's upper body and knocking his dark silhouette down beneath the horse's forequarters. Show clear hoof-to-body contact and Pew falling at that instant; do not stop with empty space between them, a mere near collision, or a horse successfully checking before impact. Panel 5 is the immediate restrained aftermath: Pew lies motionless beside the road with no blood or visible injury, the officers continue past, and his white cane lies clearly separated from his hand. Never skip from approaching riders to an unexplained cane or make the reader infer what killed Pew.

Before the final inn panel, add a complete bordered separation panel at the bridge using an unmistakable left/right fork. The VILLAGE is visibly on the far LEFT as a cluster of several cottages and lights. The Admiral Benbow is visibly on the far RIGHT as the solitary coastal inn with its hanging sign.
