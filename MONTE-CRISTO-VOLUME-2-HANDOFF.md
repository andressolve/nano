## Why the old Volume II plan must be re-budgeted

The early five-volume table described Volume II as investigation, the Morrel
rescue, Rome, and the Paris entrance. The completed expanded Volume I already
contains:

- Busoni's Caderousse interviews;
- the truth about Louis and Mercédès;
- Caderousse's confession;
- knowledge of Danglars's and Fernand's later success;
- Wilmore's archive proof against Danglars and Villefort;
- the complete Morrel & Son rescue and restored *Pharaon*.

Those beats cannot be repeated simply to fill the earlier label “The Return.”
Volumes II–V must be re-budgeted from the actual 55-page endpoint.

The leading Volume II hypothesis is the older four-volume dossier's second
movement:

1. Franz d'Épinay encounters Sinbad and the Count's island legend;
2. Rome, Carnival, Luigi Vampa, Albert's capture, and his release;
3. the Count enters Paris as an event and enters the Morcerf, Danglars, and
   Villefort households;
4. Haydée and Yanina enter as living people and moral history, not merely a
   revenge device;
5. Bertuccio, Auteuil, the buried child, and Benedetto connect the old crimes;
6. the Cavalcantis and the Count's financial/social machinery begin moving;
7. the Auteuil dinner makes the families' buried fault lines physically
   present.

**Provisional ending:** the Auteuil dinner, or an immediately adjacent
revelation, where the audience finally sees that the Count has arranged every
household over its own concealed crime.

That ending is not locked. Test it against a shorter endpoint at the Count's
Paris entrance and a later endpoint after the Cavalcantis are fully installed.
Choose by dramatic arc, scene density, character comprehension, and the space
left for Volumes III–V—not by symmetrical volume labels.

## Volume II's dramatic job

Volume I lets the reader know Edmond intimately and ends with benevolent power.
Volume II should make the transformed man strange again.

The reader should:

- first encounter the Count partly from Franz's, Albert's, and Paris society's
  point of view;
- feel that his wealth changes space, time, access, risk, and social truth;
- thrill to the rescue of Albert while sensing that the rescue may itself be
  staged, leveraged, or incorporated into a larger design;
- understand why Albert admires him;
- see Mercédès recognize danger before most of Paris recognizes Edmond;
- watch the Count enter each household through a different desire;
- understand the first visible connections among Auteuil, Bertuccio,
  Benedetto, Villefort, Danglars, Fernand, and Haydée;
- end with exhilaration and unease, not yet the full moral collapse of later
  volumes.

The Count is fascinating first and frightening by accumulation. Do not make
him a clean superhero, a literal supernatural being, or an all-knowing narrator
who explains his whole mechanism to the reader.

## Exact continuity entering Volume II

Treat the completed Volume I as immutable backstory and visual authority.

Edmond has:

- survived Château d'If and Faria's death;
- found the Spada treasure;
- built the Busoni and Wilmore performances;
- heard Caderousse's confession and obtained documentary proof;
- learned that Mercédès waited for years and married Fernand only after hope
  was exhausted;
- learned that Danglars and Fernand prospered;
- confirmed Villefort's signed detention order;
- used his fortune first to save Morrel & Son;
- restored the *Pharaon* anonymously;
- left Marseille with Jacopo, choosing kindness before vengeance.

The audience knows that Busoni and Wilmore are Edmond. Volume II can create
dramatic uncertainty for new characters, but it must not confuse the reader
about which persona they are watching unless the uncertainty is deliberate,
brief, and fair.

The following emotional facts are locked:

- Faria remains Edmond's teacher and father-figure; memory should guide or
  trouble Edmond, never appear as a convenient talking ghost.
- Mercédès did not betray Edmond. She petitioned, waited, and survived under
  constraint.
- Morrel's rescue proved that wealth can restore as well as punish.
- Caderousse confessed but still chose the diamond; he has received warning
  and grace, not final judgment.
- Edmond has proof, but proof does not give him Providence.

Volume II needs a clean and explicit time bridge from the Morrel rescue to the
Rome/Paris period. Do not make the Count's network, education, languages,
properties, agents, and reputation appear to have formed overnight.

## Locked format and visual inheritance

Unless Andres explicitly changes it, continue the successful expanded-edition
form:

- **1024 × 1536, 2:3 portrait** story pages;
- finished dialogue, captions, letters, and sound cues integrated into the
  flattened image;
- Velvet Cinema matte-gouache / opaque-watercolor finish;
- reader supplies navigation, contents, bookmarks, fullscreen, zoom, and quiz
  only;
- desktop is the primary reading target and tablet the secondary target;
- exact source-pixel typography targets are builder guidance, not independent
  rejection rules when the real proofs are comfortable;
- no HTML/SVG lettering overlay and no crop-patching or relettering of failed
  generated pages.

The visual world should change without abandoning the series:

- **Rome:** carnival scarlet, candle and torch gold, deep catacomb black,
  theatrical masks, public execution, spectacle, and the Count as legend;
- **Paris:** polished black, burgundy, emerald, velvet, mirrors, visiting cards,
  ledgers, seals, conservatories, gaslight, and rooms arranged as traps;
- **Auteuil:** cultivated surface over disturbed earth; elegance made uncanny
  by memory, architecture, and buried evidence.

Do not let “more luxurious” become glossy concept art. Faces, gestures, social
distance, and the dramatic turn retain visual priority.

## Script standard

The script must be a page production authority, not a prose outline. Every
page record should include:

- narrative job;
- reader entering state;
- desire and opposition;
- mode and density;
- visual engine and panel map;
- exact image text;
- numbered speaker/source, reading tier, side, and mouth/source endpoint or
  tail-free designation for every text element;
- silent visible characters;
- identity and anti-lock requirements;
- decisive setting/object continuity;
- explicit exclusions and withheld reveals;
- reader exiting state.

Keep the anti-terse rule from Volume I. A short line is not strong merely
because it sounds cinematic. If a first-time reader can ask “who?”, “what?”,
“why?”, or “when?” after one normal read, rewrite it. Add words, prose, panels,
or pages when they create causal clarity and emotional duration. Never solve
density by shrinking text.

Social scenes need live objectives. A Paris dinner cannot become six people
reciting family trees, bank facts, or historical background. Information must
arrive because someone probes, conceals, seduces, bargains, tests, boasts,
protects, or threatens.

## Preparation critic workflow to reproduce

Use distinct critic assignments. One agent may perform several successive
critic rounds for continuity of judgment, but the critic must remain separate
from the builder or reviser whose artifact it judges.

### Gate 1 — Complete script after architecture approval

The architecture comparison and Volume II endpoint require production-lead
and Andres approval before scripting. Volume I did not use a separate agent
verdict as a substitute for that creative decision. The independent critic
task begins when the complete page script exists.

Give the critic the complete script, not isolated sample pages. Ask for a cold
read first, then an authority-based audit of:

- first-read orientation, motivation, emotional duration, and page turns;
- character knowledge versus reader knowledge;
- natural dialogue driven by wants rather than explanation;
- a dominant turn on every page;
- complete cause and consequence across every transition;
- body, travel, time, money, document, disguise, and household mechanics;
- every line's speaker, side, vertical order, silence, and source endpoint;
- ambiguous or clipped lines and tiny-prop dependencies;
- whether one normal read supports an accurate plain-language paraphrase.

Require each blocking finding to name the page, explain the first-time-reader
failure, and prescribe a concrete repair. Revise from the report and resubmit
the complete script. Approval is withheld until all original blockers and all
changed transitions pass. Script approval does not approve reference art or
rendered pages.

This reproduces the actual Volume I tasks recorded in
[`monte-cristo-expanded/11-SCRIPT-CRITIC-REPORT.md`](monte-cristo-expanded/11-SCRIPT-CRITIC-REPORT.md),
[`monte-cristo-expanded/20-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC-MANDATE.md`](monte-cristo-expanded/20-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC-MANDATE.md),
and
[`monte-cristo-expanded/21-COMPLETE-SCRIPT-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC.md`](monte-cristo-expanded/21-COMPLETE-SCRIPT-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC.md).

### Gate 2 — Character references

Give the critic all proposed and retained character sheets, the identity
ledger, relevant prototype or prior-page appearances, neutral full-cast boards,
grayscale silhouettes, face-only crops, and adversarial near-neighbor boards.
Ask it to judge identity at native size, reduced reader size, and thumbnail;
without costume; in multiple lighting and angles; through age and disguise;
and in the hardest live pairings.

Require a collision matrix, explicit blockers, positive locks, nearest
forbidden lookalikes, and a re-audit from the revised images. Approval means
the casting system is ready to use, not that future pages may drift from it.
This reproduces the actual task in
[`monte-cristo-expanded/12-CHARACTER-DISTINCTNESS-CRITIC-REPORT.md`](monte-cristo-expanded/12-CHARACTER-DISTINCTNESS-CRITIC-REPORT.md).

### Gate 3 — Continuous prototypes

Prototype the hardest continuous causal ranges after the script and relevant
references pass. The minimum recommended Volume II set is:

1. **Sinbad/Count spectacle:** wonder, identity, hospitality, danger, and
   unsettling control without an exposition lecture;
2. **Carnival to Vampa rescue:** public spectacle, action geography, Albert's
   capture, and the Count's leverage;
3. **Paris ensemble:** six or more important people, hidden histories, exact
   attribution, and subtext;
4. **Noirtier communication:** gaze carries agency and information without
   becoming an infographic;
5. **Auteuil/Bertuccio:** layered past and present remain causally legible.

Let the critic cold-read the finished sequence before consulting the prompt,
script, or builder QA. Then require a second fidelity pass against those
authorities. Judge first-read causality, balloon/source attribution, identity,
object geography, page turns, anatomy/generation integrity, and real
desktop/tablet comfort. Preserve and fully redraw failed pages; never patch.

This follows the actual Volume I prototype lesson in
[`monte-cristo-expanded/09-INDEPENDENT-CRITIC-REPORT.md`](monte-cristo-expanded/09-INDEPENDENT-CRITIC-REPORT.md):
the internal prototype QA passed, but the independent cold read still found a
clarity weakness and a balloon assigned to the wrong visible source.

### Prep materials that support but do not replace criticism

Create format proofs, typography tests, page-intelligence contracts, builder
audits, identity boards, and QA records. Treat them as authorities or evidence,
not as self-approving artifacts. Do not say a format, reference library, or
script “passed” merely because its author completed a checklist.

## Production builder/critic workflow to reproduce

After script, references, and prototypes pass, use the exact long-run separation
documented in [`dialogue.md`](dialogue.md) and
[`monte-cristo-expanded/36-BUILDER-CRITIC-RUN-NOTES.md`](monte-cristo-expanded/36-BUILDER-CRITIC-RUN-NOTES.md).

### Builder

The builder prepares the prompt from the approved script and minimum approved
references, generates one candidate, performs one practical essentials audit,
makes desktop/tablet proofs, and submits. It stops at the first plausible
candidate.

It does not privately reroll for nominal typography size, tiny margins, exact
panel percentages, microscopic tail distances, phone-only performance, or
cosmetic finish. Before critic review it may replace only an actual essentials
failure such as missing text, reversed story order, wrong attribution, broken
decisive object, or obvious actor/anatomy failure. Preserve the rejected image
and target only that defect.

### Page critic

The production lead gives the critic:

- candidate path;
- page-generation prompt;
- builder audit;
- desktop and tablet proofs;
- relevant locked authorities;
- an explicit page-specific checklist distilled from the script and prompt.

The repeated Volume I brief was materially:

> Independently review Page NN under the corrected essentials gate: exact
> script/story, clear attribution, obvious generation/anatomy integrity,
> consequential identity/continuity, and actual desktop/tablet comfort.
> Typography/cosmetic/numeric prompt deviations are nonblocking unless they
> materially harm reading or story. Write a concise critic report and return
> APPROVED or REJECTED/REVISE with mandatory findings only. Do not edit or
> promote the production art.

The page-specific checklist is mandatory. It should name the exact action and
balloon order, speaker sides, silent figures, memory separation, decisive
objects, withheld reveals, handoffs, identity changes, and forbidden later
actions that carry this page's story. The critic judges whether the image is
faithful to this page's script and material prompt commitments, not merely
whether it looks good.

### Promotion and sequence control

The production lead alone promotes an unconditional approval byte-for-byte,
records the hash, updates QA/reader/handoff, and releases the next generation.
While Page N is under review, the builder may prepare Page N+1's prompt but may
not generate it. No rejected page becomes a reference.

Run a separate uninterrupted sequence critic after every ten canonical pages
and after the final partial batch. Hold the next batch until it passes story
and emotional continuity, identity/setting/object continuity, attribution
across transitions, obvious integrity, and actual desktop/tablet comfort.

## Adaptation cautions for Volume II

- Preserve the pleasure of spectacle without making execution or bandit
  violence lurid.
- Do not exoticize Rome, Vampa, Ali, or Haydée into decorative danger.
- Haydée must own her memory, evidence, judgment, and eventual choices.
- Mercédès's recognition and restraint should not be treated as weakness.
- Albert must be more than a hostage or doorway into Fernand; his honor,
  admiration, and later conflict need foundations now.
- Eugénie and Louise cannot be erased merely to simplify the Danglars marriage
  machine.
- Noirtier's disability is not helplessness. His gaze, memory, and will should
  command scenes.
- The Count's drugs, theatrical staging, and apparent omniscience require
  age-aware handling without sanitizing away consequence.
- Do not let financial or social machinery become magic. Show visible cause,
  leverage, and choice.
- Do not prematurely spend the later moral climax. Volume II should seed the
  danger of Edmond's certainty while preserving the intoxicating appeal of his
  mastery.
