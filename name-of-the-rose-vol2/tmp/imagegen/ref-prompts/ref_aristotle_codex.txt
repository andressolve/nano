Two views of the same codex, side by side full-page composition.

LEFT half: the codex CLOSED on a small dark oak reading-stand, plain dark brown leather face, brass clasps engaged at the fore-edge, slight wear at the corners. Set at three-quarter angle so the spine and the fore-edge are both visible. Medieval codex, no title on the binding.

RIGHT half: the codex OPEN on the reading-stand, two facing vellum leaves visible, each ruled into two columns of 14th-century Greek minuscule script. The text is small, even, and beautifully copied by a skilled scribe. The corners of both open pages are faintly dark-stained at the fore-edge, a subtle old gum-stain or water-damage mark that a casual reader might miss. Candlelight catches the vellum and the gold-tooled edge of the binding.

THE ARISTOTLE CODEX — a vellum codex of about 80 leaves, bound in dark brown leather over wooden boards, brass clasps at the fore-edge, slightly worn at the corners from centuries of careful handling. When closed: a plain leather face with no title, brass clasps visible. When open: two facing vellum leaves, each ruled into two columns of Greek text written in 14th-century Greek minuscule script. The corners of two facing pages are faintly dark-stained: the contact poison. The stain is subtle. A small reading-stand of dark oak, plain, set on a stone surface in candlelight.

NO text on the binding, no labels in the image, no caption, no border. The Greek script on the open pages is artwork and does not need to be a real readable passage at this reference-sheet stage. NO halftones, NO cel shading, NO ink linework. Painted brushwork.
