# The Name of the Rose — Book Two: The Fire

## One-sentence window

This volume covers **Days Five through Seven** of Umberto Eco's seven-day story plus the **epilogue**: the theological disputation over Christ's poverty, the deaths of Severinus and Malachi, Bernard Gui's interrogation of Remigio, the condemnation of three souls to the stake, Adso's grotesque dream of the *Coena Cypriani*, William's deduction of a secret librarian guiding the abbey for decades, the discovery of the hidden room called the *finis Africae*, the confrontation with **Jorge of Burgos** over the long-lost second book of Aristotle's *Poetics*, the destruction of the great library in a single night's fire, William and Adso's ride away from the ruined abbey at dawn, and **Old Adso's** return to the ash decades later to write the manuscript we have been reading.

## Source

Umberto Eco, *Il nome della rosa* (1980). Continues the adaptation begun in `~/Documents/nano/name-of-the-rose/`. **Faithful to Eco**, not to the 1986 Annaud film. We preserve the theological disputation, the village girl's execution (handled with restraint), Adso's grief, William's *"there is no order in the universe"* speech, the Abbot's death in the passage, and Eco's closing tag *Stat rosa pristina nomine, nomina nuda tenemus*.

## The goal under the goal

Unchanged from Book One. *Constrained intelligence*, not "dark ages" condescension. Book Two delivers the load-bearing volume sentence William seeded in Book One:

> "The monks here are not stupid, Adso. They know more than most kings. They have built the largest library in Christendom. And one fire could end it. That is the world."

That sentence lands twice in Book Two:
1. Implicitly, when the fire arrives and the library is gone (P19–P20).
2. Explicitly, in William's farewell speech as the two ride away from the burning abbey (P22): the line is repeated, and Adso's old-monk narration around it makes its meaning unmistakable.

## Illuminated chapter-break pages — six, up from four

User explicit request: the illuminated chapter-break pages in Book One (P1, P9, P14, P19) were the volume's signature visual move and should be planned generously in Book Two. Six illuminated pages this volume:

- **P1 — *Continuatio Manuscripti*** (Old Adso frame opener, recapping where Book One ended)
- **P2 — *Dies Quintus***
- **P9 — *Dies Sextus***
- **P15 — *Dies Septimus***
- **P21 — *Epilogus*** (a new tag type — Latin epilogue header in the same illuminated register)
- **P24 — *Stat Rosa Pristina Nomine*** (the closing illuminated page — the manuscript revealing itself as the artifact we have been reading)

Marginalia continuity: the **fox-in-monk's-habit** drollery from Book One's four chapter breaks (P1, P9, P14, P19) is locked across all six Book Two illuminated pages. Same scribe's recurring motif. The volume reads as the same manuscript.

P21 and P24 are new illuminated variants:
- **P21 *Epilogus*** drops the central miniature of "a day to come" and replaces it with a miniature of the two riders moving AWAY from the abbey — visual inverse of Book One's cover.
- **P24** is the closing-as-invention page: the whole page reads as the final folio of Adso's manuscript, with the Latin tag *Stat rosa pristina nomine, nomina nuda tenemus* as the central artifact, a small miniature of the burned abbey from a distance, and the historiated initial enclosing Old Adso at his writing desk — closing the frame that opened on P1.

## Hybrid teaching pages — two

Same scaffold as Book One's P5 / P13 / P15 / P22 (half painting + half parchment text panel, ~140 words):
- **P7 — "Why people burn."** Left-half painted scene of the three condemned being led across the snowy courtyard under Dominican guard, right-half ivory parchment panel explaining the Inquisition's mechanics, why the poverty debate became a capital matter, and why the powerful needed the village girl to be a witch and not a hungry girl.
- **P11 — "How the library worked."** Left-half **diagrammatic painted map** of the 56-room labyrinth — room names (LEONES, AEGYPTUS, HIBERNIA, FONS, ANGLIA, IUDAEA, etc.) arranged as a stylized map of the known world, the *finis Africae* sealed at the southern end behind a mirror-door. Right-half parchment panel explaining how the layout secretly maps the world, why the books were shelved by region, why no one but the librarian and his assistant knew the way through, and what the *finis Africae* held. Same diagrammatic-painted-map style validated on Book One P13 INTERLUDE.

## Primary-source pages — one

- **P18 — Aristotle's *Poetics, Book II* as artifact.** Open vellum codex, two columns of Greek with a parallel English helper ribbon (canonical-rule treatment, sibling to Book One P11 cipher and P24 *Practica*). Small inset frame at lower corner: Jorge's gloved hand turning a corner whose vellum is faintly dark-stained with the contact poison. The treatise that the entire seven days of killing has been about — finally rendered as the visible thing.

## Dream-sequence page — new register variant

- **P12 — Adso's *Coena Cypriani* dream.** Same oil-painting realism but **tilted**: slightly skewed perspective, colors more saturated than the volume default, candleflames painted too bright, faces of dream-figures slightly distorted. The dream-content is the medieval *Coena Cypriani* — a real 9th-century parody of the Last Supper as a wedding feast where biblical and patristic figures behave grotesquely — visualized as a swirling vision above the sleeping Adso. This is the dream that cracks William's case (he learns of it from Adso on P13 and recognizes the connection to the forbidden book). One-off register variant; not used elsewhere in the volume. Documented in the style guide delta.

## Quiz (5 questions, written at reader build)

Per project CRITICAL QUIZ RULE: tests **why**, not **what**. Distractors substantive, similar in length to the correct answer, shuffled positions across the five questions. Candidate topics, all driven by the book itself:

1. **Why did Jorge poison the *Poetics* Book II?** (Not "he was crazy" or "he hated the Greeks" — because he believed laughter at sacred things would dissolve the fear of God.)
2. **Why did Bernard Gui condemn the village girl?** (Not because she had committed any crime — because the Inquisition's existence requires that someone be a heretic, and a poor illiterate girl is easier to convict than a powerful monk.)
3. **Why did Remigio falsely confess to the murders?** (Not because he was guilty — because under Bernard Gui's questioning he believed any answer that ended the questioning was better than any answer that prolonged it; that is what an interrogator's manual is for.)
4. **Why did William fail to find the killer before the seventh day?** (Not because he wasn't clever — because the pattern he thought he was reading, the seven trumpets of Revelation, was a false pattern; the world he was reading did not have the order he had assumed.)
5. **Why does Old Adso say, at the very end, "we hold only naked names"?** (Not because he is sad about old age — because the library is gone; the book is gone; the only thing left of the things he loved is the words for them. The names without the things they named.)

The closing question delivers the *Stat rosa pristina nomine* tag to a kid in plain words. The correct answer for each question should be one of three options, with two substantive distractors that a reader skimming for length cannot eliminate. Positions shuffled b/c/a/c/b (or similar — finalize at quiz-write time).

## Anti-patterns specific to this volume

**Carried forward from Book One (apply unchanged):**

- **CAPTION CLARITY RULE — every caption must stand alone for a first-time reader.** Period vocabulary glossed inline on first use (*Matins, Lauds, Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Vespers, Compline* — already introduced in Book One P1; if a fresh reader lands on Book Two, the prayer-hours strip in the reader handles this. *Cellarer, herbarium, lectern, codex* etc. get inline glosses on first use in body captions.) Cryptic teaser captions banned; punchlines never withheld.
- Do **not** frame the medieval period as "dark" or "ignorant." *Constrained intelligence.*
- Do **not** paraphrase Latin tags. Render verbatim with English helper. Canonical rule applied across all six illuminated pages, the *Coena Cypriani* dream caption, the *Poetics* Book II primary-source page, and the closing *Stat rosa pristina nomine*.
- Do **not** name famous historical figures in character lock blocks. Same rule as Book One — the visual description is the lock, not the name. Most acute for Bernard Gui (real-historical) on P3, P5, P6.
- Do **not** generate pages before all 5 new single refs AND all 3 composite refs are locked.
- Do **not** silently compensate for the `edit_image` one-ref limit. The composites exist precisely so you don't have to. Surface explicitly if a page seems to need a fourth named face beyond what a composite holds.

**New for Book Two:**

- **Do NOT render the village girl's burning explicitly.** Her execution must be on-page (it is the book's moral hinge) but treated with restraint. Treatment: P6 ends with her being led under guard toward the cellar where the condemned are held; her execution itself happens between P6 and P7 and is referenced in P7's parchment-panel text ("By the dawn after the disputation, the three had been led down the mountain road, and the wood-pile in the village had been lit by torchlight"). Adso's grief is the emotional anchor — never the burning body itself. **Lead with the cold mechanics of the Inquisition, not with the girl's pain.** (This treatment was decided in Book One brief; preserved here verbatim.)
- **Do NOT render Salvatore's burning or Remigio's burning explicitly either.** Same treatment — the condemnation is the dramatic beat (P6), the executions are summarized in P7's parchment panel.
- **Do NOT render Severinus's death blow explicitly on P4.** Lead with the room — overturned worktable, broken glass, the heavy bronze armillary sphere on the floor near his head, his stained hand fallen open at the side of frame. William crouched, examining. Body half-glimpsed in the middle distance, robe and stained hands visible but face obscured by the angle. Same restraint as Book One P10 (Venantius in the vat) and P17 (Berengar in the tub).
- **Do NOT render Malachi's death moment explicitly on P10.** He has just collapsed at morning prayers; the camera is on the iron key-ring fallen on the stone, his blackened fingertips visible, his face turned away into shadow. The candle on the choir stall behind him still burning. Other monks frozen mid-stand. This is a discovery scene, not a body scene.
- **Do NOT render the Abbot's death.** It happens offscreen — Jorge has sealed him into a hidden passage on Day 7 and he suffocates. Referenced only in old-Adso narration on P22. There is no Abbot body on-page in Book Two.
- **Do NOT show explicit dissection / autopsy** on Severinus's death page. The blackened fingers (already seen on Venantius in Book One P10 and Berengar in Book One P17) carry the poison detail; no need to open the body.
- **Do NOT have Jorge be "evil-looking."** He is severe, ascetic, dignified — *the dogmatic mind defending itself against the future*, not a cartoon villain. The horror of him is that he believes himself right. Render him in candlelight, milky blind eyes catching the flame, expression calm, hands steady. The viewer's discomfort comes from his certainty, not his appearance.
- **Do NOT show Aristotle's text as the model's invented Greek.** P18 primary-source page must use *attested* opening lines of the existing *Poetics* Book I as a stand-in (since Book II is lost), with a parallel English helper ribbon. Script will provide the verbatim Greek string. Do not let gpt-image-2 invent Greek letters.
- **Do NOT alter the cover register from Book One's pattern.** Same Aedificium ref, same establishing-shot framing, same gilt-with-red-inner-shadow title block. The shift is from cold-dawn to night-with-glowing-window. Don't redesign the cover concept.
- **Continuity check on Old Adso ref against Book One P1.** The white-bearded Benedictine at the writing desk inside P1's historiated initial established the visual. The new full-size ref must match. Re-Read Book One P1 before writing the Old Adso ref prompt — RULE 2 from the Book One retrospective applies even when the source is a previously-generated page rather than a ref.

## Operational pre-flight (every session)

Before any image generation in any sub-session, run through:
1. `Glob refs/` — confirm every needed ref is on disk.
2. `Read` every involved character ref PNG and write a one-line verbatim observation per character into the working notes. Not from memory. Not from the lock block. From the eyes.
3. Re-Read the script entry for the page being generated. Copy the lettering block verbatim — do not paraphrase.
4. Confirm the composite ref (if applicable) and the single character ref (if applicable) are the correct paths for this page.
5. Fix one defect per regen if a repair is needed. Do not bundle.

These five steps are the operational form of Book One retrospective RULES 1–3.
