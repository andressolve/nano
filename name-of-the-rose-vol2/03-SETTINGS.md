## New settings — 10 blocks

These are the new locations Book Two introduces. Each block is pasted verbatim into the relevant page prompt's SETTING section.

### I. The chapel during Matins (P10)

> **THE CHAPEL DURING MATINS.** The abbey's Romanesque chapel in the small hours before dawn — Matins, ~2:30 AM. A long narrow nave with a low barrel-vaulted ceiling in heavy weathered stone, thick walls, narrow round-arched windows pitch-black against the night outside. The choir at the east end is divided into two long rows of carved oak choir-stalls facing each other across a narrow aisle; the monks stand here for the hours, ranks of black habits in cold candlelight. Tall iron candle-stands flank the altar at the far end, each holding three thick beeswax candles. Two smaller candles on the lectern between the stalls. The rest of the chapel is in deep cold shadow. The air smells of beeswax and stone-cold incense. Breath visible. The painted wooden crucifix above the altar reads as dark mass against the candle glow. Outside the chapel: nothing — the night silence of a mountain abbey before dawn.

### J. The library catalogue table (P13)

> **THE LIBRARY CATALOGUE TABLE.** Inside the scriptorium (see Book One setting C for the surrounding room), a long oak table near the staircase that connects to the library above. On the table: the **great abbey catalogue** — a massive vellum codex two-feet-square and four inches thick when closed, now open across the centre of the table, two facing leaves visible. The script on the leaves is dense — every book the abbey owns, listed by region, year acquired, and shelf room. A small reading-stand of dark oak holds a smaller catalogue beside the great one. A horn cup of ground pumice and a quill in a brass holder rest on the table. **Two beeswax candles** in iron sticks set at the corners of the open codex provide the only light — late afternoon has gone, this scene is at dusk and the scriptorium windows show pale slate-grey going to dark. The shadows of the catalogue's bindings fall long across the worn oak surface.

---

### K. The Abbot's private chamber (P14)

> **THE ABBOT'S PRIVATE CHAMBER.** A small stone room off the cloister, austere by the standards of the rich abbot — but more comfortably furnished than any other room in the abbey. A heavy oak writing-desk set against one wall, with a single carved oak chair behind it and a low padded stool in front for visitors. A bookshelf of perhaps thirty books — the only books in the abbey outside the library — bound in dark leather, lined neatly on a low shelf opposite the desk. A heavy iron-bound chest at the foot of a narrow cot (the Abbot sleeps in this room). A wooden crucifix above the desk; a worn dark-blue wool tapestry on one wall showing the saints of the Order; a small painted icon of the Virgin on a shelf. **A single tall candle on the desk** is the only direct light — winter dusk has gone outside, the small round-arched window in the wall reads as dark blue-black. The fire in a small stone hearth in one corner is banked low and gives a soft red underlight to the floor. The room is cold despite the fire.

### L. The mirror door (P16)

> **THE MIRROR DOOR — antechamber to the *finis Africae*.** A small stone alcove at the far end of one of the labyrinth's rooms (the room labeled `AFRICA` over its doorway). One wall of the alcove is a **disguised mirror** mounted flush in a heavy stone frame, the mirror's silvered glass slightly fogged and aged — it returns a faint, distorted candlelit reflection. Above the mirror, carved into the lintel in Latin: `Super Thronos Viginti Quatuor` — "Over the Thrones Twenty-Four." Below the mirror and to one side: a small almost-invisible iron lever recessed into the stone, only visible to someone who knows where to look. Floor of dressed limestone, dust thick along the edges of the room. The single candle William carries is the only light source; everything beyond two paces is darkness. The mirror is the door; the lever opens it; behind it is the sealed room that no one but Jorge and the dead librarians has entered in fifty years.

---

### M. The *finis Africae* interior (P17, P18 background, P19)

> **THE *FINIS AFRICAE* INTERIOR.** The sealed hidden room at the south end of the labyrinth library, ~12 feet square, low barrel-vaulted ceiling in heavy weathered stone. Three close walls are **floor-to-ceiling oak shelves** crammed with leather-bound codices, rolled scrolls, and stacked unbound quires — the most dangerous books in the abbey, hidden here for two centuries: Arabic and Hebrew works the Church proscribed, Greek philosophers the Church distrusts, and a small number of texts no one outside this room has read in living memory. A single **lectern of dark oak** in the centre of the room, with the open Aristotle codex (see `ref_aristotle_codex.png`) resting on it. **Two tall iron candle-stands** flank the lectern, each with three thick beeswax candles burning. A small wooden stool to one side. The fourth wall is the mirror-door (entered from). The air is dry, thick with old vellum, slightly smoky from the candles. No window — the room is interior, completely sealed, lit only by the candles. The walls and floor in cold blue-grey shadow beyond the candle-circle. **This is where the fire begins.**

### N. The library burning (P19)

> **THE LIBRARY BURNING — interior.** The *finis Africae* and the adjoining labyrinth rooms in the first minute after Jorge has knocked over Adso's lantern. Flame climbs from the floor up the open shelves at lower-left, vellum codices catching one after another with a sharp dry sound, smoke beginning to fill the upper third of the frame. The lectern is overturned, candles knocked sideways. The two tall iron candle-stands have fallen forward, scattering burning candles across the stone floor. Through the mirror-door, the adjoining labyrinth rooms visible as orange-glowing corridors beyond — the fire has already crossed the threshold and is climbing the shelves of the next room. **The orange of the fire is the only warm light in the frame; the rest of the stone is in cold deep shadow.** Heavy smoke billowing under the low vaulted ceiling. The air is suddenly hot.

### O. The abbey burning at night (P20)

> **THE ABBEY BURNING AT NIGHT — exterior wide shot.** The entire Aedificium engulfed at full night, perhaps four hours after the fire began. Every narrow slit window in the great tower **glowing fierce orange-red** from within; the slate roof now half-collapsed, flames pouring upward through the gap, sending a column of red sparks and acrid grey smoke high into the cold black sky. The four square corner-towers are silhouetted against the inferno, recognizable but burning. The lower abbey buildings — the chapel, the cloister, the refectory — also burning, lower flames climbing the round-arched windows of the chapel. **The snow around the tower has melted into black mud** in a wide ring; beyond that the snow is still clean and white, reflecting the orange of the fire pinkish-red. Small dark figures of lay-brothers and monks running with empty buckets between the buildings, throwing snow uselessly at the flames. The dark pines on the surrounding mountainside silhouetted against the firelight. The mountain shoulder the abbey sits on visible from a low foreground angle. **Cold cold winter night; furious furious fire.** A bell still ringing, faintly, from somewhere in the chapel — and then silent.

### P. The road away from the abbey at dawn (P22)

> **THE ROAD AWAY — dawn, the morning after the fire.** The same switchback stone-paved road that Book One's cover and Book One P2 showed ascending toward the abbey — now descending. Pre-dawn pale grey light, snow fresh on the ground (it has snowed lightly in the small hours), the abbey behind and above the camera **still smoking** — visible in the upper-right corner of the frame as a column of grey smoke against the pale sky, the silhouette of the burned Aedificium half-collapsed. **William** on his grey mare and **Adso** on the smaller bay, riding away from the camera into the foreground, both slumped slightly with exhaustion, both wrapped in their heavy travel cloaks (William's brown, Adso's black) over their habits. Their saddlebags packed. No other riders. The pines lining the road heavy with new snow, breath visible from horses and riders. Cold pale dawn. The sound of horse hooves muffled by fresh snow is implied.

### Q. Old Adso's writing-cell at Melk (P23, P24 central miniature)

> **OLD ADSO'S WRITING-CELL AT MELK — full view.** A small stone monk's cell at the abbey of Melk in Austria, decades after the events. The cell is austere: a narrow cot against one wall with a single dark wool blanket folded neatly; a simple oak writing-desk against the wall under a narrow round-arched window high in the wall (the window shows pale dawn light); the desk holds a stack of vellum sheets, a quill in a brass holder, a small ceramic inkpot of black iron-gall ink, a beeswax candle in an iron stick. A wooden crucifix on the wall above the desk. A wooden cross-staff stool. **A small worn leather satchel on the cot** — and visible peeking from the satchel: charred fragments of vellum, scraps no larger than a hand, edges blackened. These are the fragments Old Adso recovered from the abbey ruins, brought back to Melk to write the manuscript. The cell is lit by the single candle on the desk and the pale grey dawn from the window. Cold but dignified. **OLD ADSO** (see `ref_old_adso.png`) seated at the desk, writing.

---

### R. The abbey ruins (P23 main scene + P24 central miniature)

> **THE ABBEY RUINS — decades after the fire.** The same mountain shoulder, the same surrounding pines, the same low grey winter sky as Book One's cover and approach pages. Where the Aedificium stood: **broken stone walls** rising perhaps one storey from a tangled foundation of charred timbers half-buried in snow. The four square corner-towers are reduced to stumps. The roof and upper storeys long gone — the labyrinth library is open to the sky and snow. The lower abbey buildings around the base also ruined — the chapel's apse half-collapsed, the cloister arcade broken in places. Snow drifts piled inside the broken walls. **No human figure** for P24's central miniature; for P23's main scene **OLD ADSO** stands among the broken walls in his black Benedictine habit and heavy travel cloak, the old leather satchel at his hip, a single charred fragment of vellum held loosely in his gnarled hand. The wind moves his beard slightly. Dark pines silhouetted against the pale sky. The same low grey clouds. The mountain is the only thing unchanged.

## Setting deltas to existing Book One blocks

Two Book One blocks need a small in-prompt delta when reused in Book Two:

### Book One setting E (herbarium) — P4 delta

When pasted into the P4 prompt, append to the COMPOSITION block (NOT the setting block): the room is **wrecked** — the heavy oak worktable overturned at one corner, broken glass vials scattered across the stone floor, dried herbs knocked from their bundles, a small open codex of plant illustrations fallen face-down in the spill, and most importantly the **heavy bronze armillary sphere** that normally sits on a high shelf now lies on the floor near the body, one of its rings bent. The oil lamp on the table is out. Light is the grey winter daylight through the two small round-arched windows. The peg by the door is bare — Severinus's leather apron is on his body.

### Book One setting A (abbey approach) — P22 delta

When pasted into the P22 prompt, append: the road is descending (not ascending — direction reversed from Book One). The abbey is **behind and above** the camera, smoking, visible in the upper-right corner of the frame as a column of grey smoke against the pale dawn sky. The two riders (William grey mare, Adso bay) are in the foreground riding away. Both wrapped in heavy travel cloaks. Pre-dawn pale grey light, fresh snow.

## Setting-block discipline (carry forward from Book One)

- Same painter / same light / same materials across pages that share a setting block — the model needs the same words to render the same place. Do not rewrite a setting between pages.
- Cold blue-grey winter light is the volume default for daytime outdoor scenes. Beeswax-candle warm light is the volume default for interior night scenes. The **fire palette** (Style Guide DELTA 3) is the variant used only on P19, P20, and the cover.
- Period accuracy: round Romanesque arches only; no Gothic pointed arches; no Renaissance refinement; no modern construction; vellum codices only; no glass-paned windows (only narrow slit openings); slate or stone-tile roofs; stone-paved or rush-strewn floors.
- The Aedificium ref `ref_aedificium.png` is the architectural lock for every shot that shows the tower. Re-Read it before generating any page that includes the tower in the frame, the same way character refs are re-Read.
