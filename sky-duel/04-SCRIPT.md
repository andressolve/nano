## PAGE 7 — The Eye — POSTER — T5 — ref: ref_missile (nose crop as anchor)

Composition: ivory poster sheet. Giant cutaway of just the seeker head: glass dome, behind it a gimbal-mounted mirror assembly, the spinning patterned reticle disk rendered like a wagon wheel, the pea-sized detector cell. A faint distant jet rendered upper-left as the "view", with a dashed sight-line into the dome. 5 callouts.

LETTERING:
- Title strip: INSIDE THE SEEKER — HOW A MISSILE SEES HEAT
- Callout 1 (to detector cell): The detector is a chip of LEAD SULFIDE, a material that makes a tiny electric signal when infrared heat-light lands on it. That signal means: I see something hot.
- Callout 2 (to reticle disk): One sensor cannot tell WHERE the heat is — so a patterned disk spins in front of it, like a wagon wheel, chopping the heat-light into flashes. The TIMING of the flashes reveals the target's direction.
- Callout 3 (to gimbal): The whole eye sits on a GIMBAL — a swivel mount — so it can stay locked on the target while the missile and the jet both twist and turn.
- Callout 4 (to dome): Early Sidewinders could only see the white-hot tailpipe — pilots had to sneak behind the enemy. Later models chill the detector with icy gas, making it sensitive enough to feel the warmth of the jet's skin from ANY direction.
- Bottom caption: Seeing the target is half the battle. Now the missile must CATCH a jet that is desperately turning away — and that took a beautiful piece of thinking.

LETTERING:
- Title strip: THE CHASE MATH — PROPORTIONAL NAVIGATION
- Left label: THE DOG'S WAY: aim at where the target IS. You trace a long curve behind it and run out of rocket.
- Right label: THE HUNTER'S WAY: aim at where the target WILL BE — like a quarterback throwing ahead of a running receiver.
- Caption (bottom, full width): Sailors have known the secret for centuries: if another ship stays at the SAME angle from your bow while getting closer, you will collide. The Sidewinder uses that rule in reverse — it steers to keep the target's angle constant, and that locks both machines onto a collision course on purpose. Engineers call it PROPORTIONAL NAVIGATION. The missile never calculates where the jet is going. It doesn't have to.
