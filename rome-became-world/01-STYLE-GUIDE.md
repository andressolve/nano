# 01 — STYLE GUIDE

Sequel to `why-rome-won/`. Same chassis, one shifted accent. The volume must read as the second half of
the same museum exhibit.

## Visual register (two sub-registers, one palette)
All images share ONE palette so the volume reads as a single piece, and harmonizes with book one:
**warm parchment / sun-bleached stone / bronze / iron-grey**, with Mediterranean sky — now joined by
**imperial purple** as the through-color of the *Empire* (the emperors' Tyrian purple). Muted, period,
museum-quality — NOT bright, NOT cartoonish.

**Anti-drift directive — paste into every prompt:**
> NOT a children's book. NOT a comic. Serious, realistic, museum-quality historical reconstruction.
> Realistic proportions, natural light, no cel shading, no halftones, no ink outlines.

### Sub-register A — PAINTED SCENES (cover, P1, P3, P5, P6, P7, P8, P9)
> Painted historical reconstruction in the style of a fine museum / National-Geographic ancient-Rome
> illustration. Oil/gouache brushwork, cinematic natural light, atmospheric depth, accurate period
> Roman detail. Muted warm palette: parchment, stone, bronze, iron, with imperial purple accents.

### Sub-register B — MAPS (P2 Italia-now-Roman, P4 greatest extent)
> Elegant antique cartography / engraved map style on aged parchment. Sepia and iron-ink line work,
> subtle hand-colored fills, compass rose, restrained. Clean and legible like a museum exhibit map.
> VERY FEW words — single-word labels only (see text rule).

## TEXT-IN-IMAGE RULE (critical — keep lettering minimal)
The PROSE in the reader carries every explanation; the image never teaches through labels.
- Maps: at most a few **single-word** labels (`ROMA`, `ITALIA`, `CONSTANTINOPLE`). No sentences, no keys.
- Scenes: NO text at all.
- The four-part synthesis (P9) is HTML/CSS — carries NO in-image text burden.
- If an image seems to need more than ~3 words, redesign it so the reader's caption does the talking.

## Reader theme (same as why-rome-won, re-skinned imperial)
- Light parchment body, serif display (Cormorant Garamond), Inter for labels — same as book one.
- **Accent color: imperial "Byzantium" purple `#702963`** (book one used Republican oxblood `#9a2f2f`;
  the shift marks the move from Republic to Empire and the heart going east).
- Section labels in three parts:
  - Part One · The Machine That Could Not Be Beaten  (muted stone grey)
  - Part Two · The Long Turn Inward  (imperial purple `#702963`)
  - Part Three · What It Became  (bronze `#9c6b2e`)
- Dark cover + dark end page (same as book one).
- Fact boxes = white cards with purple `FACT` / `DID YOU KNOW?` label. Pull-quotes = purple left-border.
- A custom **four-part CSS block** on P9 — the four pillars of book one, each followed to its end (The
  Allies / The Army / The Centre / The Genius for Absorbing) — four equal cards, one line each.
- Progress bar, prev/next nav, keyboard arrows, 5-question quiz, score box — all inherited.

## Tone of the prose
Vivid, concrete, confident; continues the argument of book one. Assume the reader has read book one but
re-explain anything load-bearing (the socii, Cannae's lesson) in a sentence. This is the *actual story*,
told honestly — never "you think X but actually Y." Let the real events (the allies becoming Rome, fifty
years of emperors murdering emperors, the quiet lapse of 476, the East still calling itself Rome in 1453)
carry the feeling. Don't editorialize emotion; don't romanticize the transformation OR catastrophize it —
hold both honestly.
