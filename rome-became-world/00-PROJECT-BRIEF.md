## The point of this book (genesis) — an HONEST FOLLOW-UP, not "myth-busting"
This is the sequel to *Why Rome Kept Winning*, which ended on a machine built to lose a battle and still
win the war (discipline + manpower + resilience + adaptation, working as a system). The natural next
question — and the one a thoughtful reader actually asks — is: **how does a thing like that ever stop?**

The user was explicit: this is NOT a "common misconceptions" / trivia-night book. We are not here to
score points off the "barbarians smashed Rome in 476" cliché. We tell the **actual story**, honestly,
as a continuation of the first reading. The honest answer is the genuinely interesting one: Rome was not
knocked down from outside. **Each of book one's strengths, followed far enough across centuries, became
the thing that transformed it** — and "Rome" did not so much end as change shape and move east, where it
kept calling itself Rome for another thousand years.

## The honest spine (mirrors book one's four pillars, pillar by pillar)
- **Manpower (the socii)** → the allies demand to *be* Rome → Social War → citizenship → they *become*
  Rome.
- **The army** → gradually professionalized across the late Republic (NOT a single "Marian reform" — see
  honesty flags) → loyal to the general who pays it, not the Republic → civil wars → Augustus → Empire.
- **Resilience (the civic centre)** → once power is one throne, the centre is the prize everyone fights
  for → the Third-Century Crisis is Romans destroying Romans → resilience inverts into self-destruction.
- **Adaptation (absorb what works)** → Rome had always absorbed outsiders; now it settles whole armed
  nations inside the borders (foederati) → when absorption fails (Adrianople, the sacks) the newcomers
  inherit the West → it dissolves into the world it had made Roman.

**Through-line / closing:** the same three strengths that made Rome win each, taken far enough, became
what changed it. Book one: *order was where it began.* Book two: *the world it ordered is where it went.*

## Editorial honesty flags (from research/RESEARCH-NOTES.md — DO NOT violate)
1. **No single "Marian reform" of 107 BC.** Professionalization was gradual, completed under Augustus.
2. **Don't swap one myth for another.** Not "Rome never fell, it just gently transformed." Hold the real
   debate: the political title faded quietly in 476, but for ordinary people in the West the 5th century
   brought genuine disruption and decline (Ward-Perkins/Heather) — while the East thrived.
3. **476 is a convention, not a thunderclap** — say so plainly.
4. **Rome ≠ the capital by then** (Ravenna from 402); the sacks were symbolic shocks, not decapitations.
5. **The "barbarians" were peoples Rome had fought, hired, and settled for centuries** — absorption then
   inheritance, not invasion-from-the-void. Odoacer ruled *as* a Roman officer-king.
6. **The East kept the name "Roman"** (*Rhomaioi*); "Byzantine" is a 16th-c. coinage — use it only to
   explain the label.
7. **Keep figures as ranges** ("more than twenty" emperors; "~two-thirds" of the army at Adrianople).
8. **No fake ancient quotes** — closing line is the narrator's own.
