# 02 — SUBJECTS (visual subjects, not characters)

No recurring protagonist. These are the recurring VISUAL SUBJECTS that must look accurate and consistent
in register across the volume. No reference sheets (each image standalone). The key discipline here is
**period kit across six centuries** — this book spans 100 BC → AD 1453, and the soldier's look changes a
lot. Each image prompt must specify the right era. Default-drift is the enemy.

## ⚑ THE ROMAN SOLDIER — kit evolves by era (do NOT use one look for all pages)
- **Late Republic (P2, P3 — ~100–30 BC):** mail shirt (lorica hamata), bronze/iron Montefortino or
  Coolus helmet, oval-to-oval scutum, gladius. Still close to book one's look. Veteran, sunburnt.
- **High Empire (P4 — 1st–2nd c. AD, Pax Romana):** THIS is the famous "imperial legionary" — segmented
  plate (lorica segmentata), imperial galea helmet, large **rectangular** curved scutum, pilum. (In book
  one this look was BANNED as anachronistic; here it is finally period-correct. Use it for the height.)
- **Third-Century Crisis (P5 — ~250 AD):** transitional — mail again, oval shields, ridge helmets
  beginning, long spatha sword. Grimmer, less uniform.
- **Late Empire (P6, P7, P8 — 4th–5th c. AD):** late-Roman army — **ridge helmets**, oval shields with
  painted blazons, long spatha, chip-carved belt fittings, often trousers and long-sleeve tunics; many
  soldiers are Germanic foederati in mixed Roman-and-native gear. NOT the segmented-plate look anymore.
- **Eastern Roman / "Byzantine" (P9 — much later):** lamellar armor, conical/round helmets, kite or
  round shields, the labarum/Chi-Rho and the eagle; gold-mosaic light. A different world that still calls
  itself Roman.

## The eagle standard (aquila) — the thread
The gilded eagle returns from book one as the visual through-line: it opens the cover, and by P9 it is
carried by an Eastern Roman a thousand years later. Same symbol, transformed world. Use it as the bookend.

## The Italian allies become citizens (P2)
- Visual job (map): book one's socii map ANSWERED — the Italian peninsula now rendered as ONE Roman
  whole (not Rome-plus-allied-territories in different colors). The arrows of book one (troops flowing
  in) become a single unified land. Single labels ROMA / ITALIA.

## The general and his army (P3)
- A Roman commander on horseback leading armored legionaries across a river at dawn (the Rubicon beat,
  un-named) — soldiers following the *man*, standards raised. Tense, fateful, NOT a battle. Late-Republic
  kit.

## The Empire at its greatest extent (P4)
- Map: the Mediterranean world from Britain to the Euphrates, the sea at the center, single labels
  (ROMA, the sea, a couple of corners). Conveys vast reach and a very long frontier.

## The civil-war crisis (P5)
- Two bodies of ROMAN soldiers clashing — the same eagle on both sides — in a ruined street or field;
  smoke, a toppled statue, debased coins motif possible at edge. The tragedy of same-against-same.
  Dramatic, NOT gory (no wounds/blood focus). Third-century kit.

## Constantinople rising (P6)
- A new walled imperial city on the Bosphorus at golden dawn: great land walls, domes and basilicas,
  a harbor of ships, the sense of the world's center moving east. Grand, hopeful, late-antique.

## The Danube crossing / foederati (P7)
- Columns of Gothic families and warriors crossing a great northern river into Roman territory under the
  watch of late-Roman soldiers (376) — refugees and fighters being admitted, wagons, cold grey light.
  The moment of absorption. Optionally the aftermath of Adrianople implied, but keep it non-gory.

## The quiet end (P8)
- AD 476, an interior: a Germanic king in mixed Roman-Germanic dress in a Roman hall of marble columns
  and Latin inscriptions; the imperial diadem/regalia being set into a chest to be sent east; a young
  boy-emperor departing quietly; senators present. Subdued, dignified — a title lapsing, NOT a sack.

## Eastern Rome endures (P9)
- Bookend echoing the cover: an Eastern Roman (Byzantine) soldier and the eagle/labarum before the great
  domed skyline of Constantinople, gold mosaic light — still Rome, a thousand years on. Endurance.

## Palette discipline (all subjects)
Parchment, sun-bleached stone, bronze, iron-grey, imperial purple, Mediterranean blue. Muted, warm,
period. No neon, no candy colors, no modern tints.
