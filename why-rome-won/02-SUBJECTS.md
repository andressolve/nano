# 02 — SUBJECTS (visual subjects, not characters)

This is an essay, so there is no recurring protagonist to lock. Instead, these are the recurring VISUAL
SUBJECTS that must look accurate and consistent in register across the volume. No reference sheets are
generated (each image is standalone); these notes keep period detail correct and on-palette.

## The Roman legionary (mid-Republic, ~250 BC — NOT imperial)
- IMPORTANT period accuracy: this is the **Republican** army, not the famous imperial legionary.
  - Helmet: bronze Montefortino type (rounded bowl, cheek guards), NOT the later imperial galea.
  - Body armor: mail shirt (lorica hamata) or a bronze pectoral plate for poorer men — NOT the segmented
    lorica segmentata (that's 1st century AD, too late).
  - Shield: large oval/oblong scutum, painted, with a central boss.
  - Weapon: gladius hispaniensis (short stabbing sword) + pilum (heavy javelin).
  - Tunic oxblood-red, bronze fittings. Rugged, sunburnt, veteran faces — not pretty-boy soldiers.
- Avoid the Hollywood imperial look. If unsure, prompt "Roman Republican legionary, 3rd century BC,
  Montefortino helmet, oval scutum shield, mail or bronze pectoral, NOT segmented armor."

## The eagle standard (aquila) & unit standards
- Gilded eagle atop a pole, carried by a standard-bearer. The emotional symbol of the legion. Cover hero.

## The marching camp (P2)
- A perfect square fort: outer ditch (fossa) + earth rampart (agger) topped with stakes, four gates,
  straight gridded streets of leather tents, the commander's tent (praetorium) at the center. Dusk,
  soldiers digging and raising the wall. Isometric / high-angle reconstruction.

## Hannibal & the Carthaginians (P5, P7)
- Hannibal: Carthaginian general, ~30s, dark hair and beard, bronze armor, a commanding figure — shown
  at distance/leading, NOT a locked portrait. Carthaginian troops: more varied (Libyan, Iberian, Gallic
  mercenaries), bronze and captured Roman gear. War elephants belong to the Alps crossing, not Cannae.
- Keep Hannibal a silhouette-of-genius, not a hero portrait — the book's hero is the Roman *system*.

## The corvus & ships (P7)
- Roman quinquereme (large galley, multiple oar banks) with the corvus: a long wooden boarding bridge
  raised at the bow, a heavy spike under its free end. In action: the bridge dropped onto a Carthaginian
  deck, legionaries charging across. Dramatic sea light, spray — NOT gory.

## The comparison armies (P3 triptych) — these prove discipline wasn't unique
- Assyrian: spearmen/archers in conical helmets and lamellar armor, the look of stone palace reliefs.
- Macedonian: the pike phalanx — a dense hedge of very long sarissa pikes, bronze helmets, round shields.
- Qin Chinese: ranked infantry evoking the Terracotta Army — topknots, lamellar armor, crossbows/spears.
- One canvas, three vertical panels, thin dividers, same painted register. These are NOT Romans.

## Maps (P4, P5) — geography accuracy
- Italy peninsula recognizable; Rome central-west on the Tiber; allied peoples spread around it
  (Samnites, Etruscans, Campanians, Greeks of the south) for the socii map.
- Cannae: a tactical schematic, not a geographic map — the double-envelopment shapes on parchment.
