## The point of this book (genesis)
Andres has been telling the kids "Rome won because they were so organized, unlike anyone else," then
asked honestly: *is that right?* Research says: **half right, and the wrong half is the interesting one.**
Organization/discipline was real but NOT unique (Assyria, Macedon, Qin all drilled professional armies).
What actually set Rome apart was a **system** of four things working together: discipline + bottomless
manpower (the *socii*) + resilience (Cannae didn't end the war) + adaptation (it copied any good idea).

The book honors the "order is good" lesson and **deepens** it: *order is necessary but not sufficient.*

## The honest spine (do not contort — let the facts speak)
1. The answer everyone gives (discipline) — and the strongest evidence for it (the nightly camp).
2. The myth inside it: discipline wasn't unique. → the pivot / intellectual heart.
3. The real reason, a four-part system: Manpower → the test of Cannae → Resilience → Adaptation.
4. Synthesis: take away any one part and Rome loses. Order was the floor, not the ceiling.

## Why text-led, not image-heavy (per user direction 2026-06-25)
The payload here is an **argument you reason through** (causation, comparison, counterfactual), not a
**thing you look at** (a tank's armor, a missile seeker). So prose carries the load; images do specific
evidentiary jobs only. See `feedback_explainer_text_led` memory.

## Deliberate, justified deviations from the graphic-novel pipeline
- **No character reference sheets.** There is no recurring protagonist across pages; every image is a
  standalone scene/map/diagram. Ref-sheet discipline exists to hold a character consistent across 18
  pages — not needed here. (Documented so it's intentional, not an omission.)
- **Mixed image registers, deliberately:** painted historical reconstruction for scenes (camp, corvus,
  forum), and antique map/tactical-diagram style for the two maps (socii map, Cannae). Both tied together
  by one warm parchment/bronze/iron palette so the volume reads as one piece. See 01-STYLE-GUIDE.
- **The four-part synthesis on P8 is built in HTML/CSS**, not a generated image — avoids the
  text-rendering risk of a labeled diagram and looks crisp.

## Editorial honesty flags (from research/RESEARCH-NOTES.md — DO NOT violate)
- Never say Rome "fielded 700,000 men" — that's a theoretical manpower *pool* (Polybius 2.24), disputed
  in detail by modern scholars. Say Rome could *draw on hundreds of thousands.*
- Cannae dead = a **range** (Polybius ~70k / Livy ~48k), never one false-precise number.
- Never claim discipline was unique to Rome — that's the myth the book corrects.
- The alliance was not perfect: Capua and much of the south DID defect after Cannae; the point is that
  *most* (~80%+) held, not that none did.
- The corvus was abandoned after storms — present adaptation as trial-and-error, not flawless genius.
- Don't anachronistically grant allies Roman citizenship (that came only after the Social War, 91–88 BC).
  In this window allies supplied troops and were gradually integrated.
- Closing line is author's own thematic statement, NOT a quote attributed to an ancient (no fake quotes).
