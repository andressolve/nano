# LES JEUX VIDÉO — one-page French comic

## Purpose

Make one short, enjoyable comic that lets students use French Starter lessons 1–6 as real social language. This is not a vocabulary worksheet, a thriller, or the beginning of an elaborate serial.

## Reader promise

Two children meet over a shared interest and start playing together. The exchange should feel natural and immediately understandable. The art supplies warmth, personality, and the pleasure of a real Franco-Belgian-style comic page; the dialogue supplies only language the students have already learned.

## Locked script

One portrait page, four large panels, read left-to-right and top-to-bottom.

### Panel 1 — The invitation

Wide view of a comfortable modern family living room. Léo sits near the television playing a colorful two-player space-adventure video game. A second controller is clearly visible beside him. Hugo has just entered and watches with interest. Léo looks back and greets him.

LÉO: `Salut ! Comment tu t’appelles ?`

### Panel 2 — Hugo

Medium two-shot. Hugo answers naturally while Léo pauses the game.

HUGO: `Je m’appelle Hugo. Et toi ?`

### Panel 3 — Léo

Léo smiles and holds the second controller out to Hugo. The invitation is visually unmistakable.

LÉO: `Je m’appelle Léo. Tu aimes les jeux vidéo ?`

### Panel 4 — Playing together

Large, energetic closing panel. Both boys now sit side by side, leaning into the game and enjoying themselves. The television shows two original spacecraft cooperating in the same colorful action scene. Hugo answers while taking the controller.

HUGO: `Oui, j’aime les jeux vidéo.`

## Language lock

Every balloon is assembled entirely from phrases taught in lessons 1, 3, 5, and 6 of Francisco’s French Starter:

- `Salut !`
- `Comment tu t’appelles ?`
- `Je m’appelle Hugo.`
- `Et toi ?`
- `Je m’appelle Léo.`
- `Tu aimes les jeux vidéo ?`
- `Oui, j’aime les jeux vidéo.`

No additional dialogue, captions, translations, vocabulary labels, or sound effects.

## Production limits

- One combined cast-and-prop reference image, approved before the page.
- One finished four-panel comic page.
- Two characters only: Hugo and Léo.
- One setting only: the same modern living room.
- One balloon per panel, from one visible speaker.
- Speech attribution must be unambiguous; balloon tails terminate at the correct mouth.
- Dialogue is baked into the generated page image.
- No post-hoc HTML, SVG, or canvas lettering.
- No brand names, console logos, game logos, watermarks, or unrelated text.
- No mystery, villain, magical device, twist, or manufactured lesson gimmick.

## Character locks

### Hugo

Ten years old; pale olive skin; tousled dark-brown hair; green overshirt over an orange T-shirt; charcoal trousers; red canvas trainers. Curious, friendly, initially a little reserved.

### Léo

Eleven years old; deep brown skin; short tight black curls; mustard-yellow hoodie; dark navy jeans; white trainers. Relaxed, welcoming, expressive without being cartoonishly exaggerated.

## Prop and setting locks

- Generic modern game console with no logo.
- Two matching controllers distinguished by color: coral for Léo, teal for Hugo.
- Comfortable blue sofa, low wooden media unit, warm rug, a few ordinary books and houseplants.
- Television game imagery must be original and free of recognizable commercial characters or interface branding.

## Visual register

Original all-ages Franco-Belgian clear-line adventure-comic illustration: clean confident black contours, flat warm colors, precise readable shapes, lively natural acting, and enough environmental detail to feel like a real place. The boys should look ten and eleven—not toddlers, mascots, or teenagers. No imitation of an existing copyrighted character or page.
