# OÙ EST LA BIBLIOTHÈQUE ? — one-page French comic

## Purpose

Make one short, enjoyable comic that lets students use French Starter lesson 13 ("Find your way around town") as real, useful language. Not a vocabulary worksheet, not a mystery, not the start of an elaborate serial.

## Reader promise

Hugo doesn't know where the library is. He asks a friendly neighbor. She gives him two simple directions. He and Léo follow them and arrive. The exchange should feel natural and immediately understandable — the picture carries the meaning, the words are exactly what the boys have already learned.

## Locked script

One portrait page, four large panels, read left-to-right and top-to-bottom.

### Panel 1 — The question

Sunny neighborhood sidewalk. Hugo and Léo have stopped in front of a friendly older woman (60s, silver bob, moss-green cardigan, grey skirt) walking a small tan terrier on a lead. Hugo asks her politely.

HUGO: `Bonjour ! Où est la bibliothèque ?`

### Panel 2 — Straight ahead

Same corner, moments later. The woman points clearly down the street.

WOMAN: `Va tout droit.`

### Panel 3 — Then left

Same corner. The woman makes a second, visually distinct gesture — an arm sweep indicating a left turn, not a repeat of panel 2's point.

WOMAN: `Puis, tourne à gauche.`

### Panel 4 — There it is

A different location, a quiet side street. A modest warm-stone library stands ahead — books visible through an arched window, an open-book pictogram over the door, no lettering anywhere. Léo points, delighted; Hugo follows a half-step behind.

LÉO: `La bibliothèque est là !`

## Language lock

Every balloon is assembled entirely from phrases taught in French Starter lesson 13, plus one greeting (`Bonjour !`) already established from lesson 1 and used in the two prior comics:

- `Bonjour !`
- `Où est la bibliothèque ?`
- `Va tout droit.`
- `Puis, tourne à gauche.`
- `La bibliothèque est là !`

Lesson 13 teaches three place nouns (`la bibliothèque`, `le parc`, `le café`) and three directions (`à gauche`, `à droite`, `tout droit`); this page uses the library (unused by the two prior comics, which closed on a living room and a café/park) and the straight-then-left pattern (a deliberate variant on the course's own straight-then-right reading-mission example, to exercise `à gauche` on the page).

No additional dialogue, captions, translations, vocabulary labels, or sound effects.

## Production limits

- No new reference sheet generated. Reused the existing `les-jeux-video/refs/ref-hugo-leo-gaming.png` (Hugo/Léo identity) and `demain-il-fait-beau/refs/ref-rain-cafe-football.png` (register/style guide only — none of its content appears on this page).
- One finished four-panel comic page.
- Three named/visible characters: Hugo, Léo, and one incidental adult neighbor (plus her dog). This is within the established envelope — comic #2 added a café server the same way.
- One setting family: a sunny sidewalk corner (panels 1–3) and one new side-street/library exterior (panel 4).
- One balloon per panel, from one visible speaker.
- Dialogue is baked into the generated page image.
- No brand names, shop signage, street signs, book titles, or any other in-scene lettering. The library reads as a library through pictorial cues only (books in the window, an open-book pictogram) — never through text.
- No mystery, villain, magical device, twist, or manufactured lesson gimmick.

## Character locks

### Hugo

Ten years old; pale olive skin; tousled dark-brown hair; green overshirt over an orange T-shirt; charcoal trousers; red canvas trainers. Curious, friendly, initially a little reserved.

### Léo

Eleven years old; deep brown skin; short tight black curls; mustard-yellow hoodie; dark navy jeans; white trainers. Relaxed, welcoming, expressive without being cartoonishly exaggerated.

### The neighbor (new, incidental)

Woman in her sixties; light skin with soft weathered warmth; silver hair in a short neat bob; moss-green knit cardigan over a cream blouse; soft mid-grey skirt; comfortable brown shoes. Holds the lead of one small tan short-haired terrier. Appears only in panels 1–3.

## Visual register

Original all-ages Franco-Belgian clear-line adventure-comic illustration, matching the two prior comics exactly: clean confident black contours, flat warm colors, precise readable shapes, lively natural acting. Sunny contemporary neighborhood setting — warm-stone and pale-stucco buildings, planters, trees, blue sky. No imitation of an existing copyrighted character or page.
