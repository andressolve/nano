## The one-sentence window

Seventy-five years after Hardin, the religion of science has stopped working as a weapon — and Master Trader Hober Mallow wins the Foundation's third crisis without firing a shot, by discovering that the deadliest force on the galactic rim is a broken washing machine.

## Structure

- **P1 — The bridge:** Seldon's Vol 2 warning replayed (hologram flashback panel, existing ref) → the traders go out where the priests cannot.
- **P2–P3 — Prologue, THE TRADERS (Askone, ~135 F.E.):** Gorov jailed for selling atomics → Ponyets and the transmuter → greed beats piety → the wedge. Closes on Hardin's epigram (cross-volume callback).
- **P4–P9 — Mallow's mission (155 F.E.):** Sutt sends Mallow to Korell (three lost ships, political trap) → the missionary at the airlock → the Commdor and the gifts → the foundry and the Imperial sunburst on Korellian blasters → Siwenna: Onum Barr and the dying Empire → the power plant and the fossilized tech-men.
- **P10–P12 — Terminus:** the trial → the visual record and the KSP tattoo → Mayor Mallow, Sutt's arrest ("poor atom blaster").
- **P13–P15 — The war of nothing:** Korell attacks with Imperial dreadnoughts → Mallow declares the embargo and refuses to fight → the gadgets die one by one on Korell → surrender without a battle.
- **P16 — Closing-as-invention:** the lamp of Vol 2 becomes a WEB — golden trade routes threading the rim; the Empire's core has finally noticed. END OF BOOK THREE.

## Structural calls

- **The Foundation itself is the protagonist**; Seldon's hologram recurs as the series face (P1 flashback — Merchant Princes has no canonical Vault scene, so the hologram appears only as Vol 2 replay, clearly captioned as such).
- **Mallow carries the volume** (11 pages) — new face, new ref; the first Foundation lead who is neither scientist nor priest.
- Continuity rhymes, deliberate: Mallow ends in Hardin's old office at the same window (P12/P15 mirror Vol 2 P1/P14); the embargo blackout pages (P14) visually rhyme with Vol 2's interdict midnight — same weapon, economic instead of religious.
- Talky-risk mitigation: staged spectacle beats — the transmuter gold-pour (P3), the airlock mob (P5), the atomic penknife in the foundry (P7), ruined Siwenna (P8), the dreadnought wall (P13), the dying-gadgets montage (P14), the trade-web galaxy (P16).
- Ankor Jael cut; Manlio reduced to the P12 arrest (prose lock); council/court crowds stay unnamed non-recurring.

## Research & editorial honesty

Verified 2026-07-06 (Wikipedia Foundation-novel plot section, Cora Buhlert retro-review of "The Big and the Little", TV Tropes/Fandom search corroboration, LitCharts quote verification, quote databases for Part V §13):

- Timeline: "The Traders" ≈ 135 F.E. (Ponyets/Askone); "The Merchant Princes" ≈ 155 F.E. Captions use "about twenty years" between prologue and main story, "seventy-five years after Hardin's second crisis" for the main window.
- **The Traders:** Master Trader Eskel Gorov (actually a Foundation agent) is jailed on Askone, where the Elders ban atomic "sorcery" as ancestral impiety. Trader Limmar Ponyets is sent; he builds a crude **transmuter** (iron → gold) aboard ship, sells it to the ambitious Councilor **Pherl** — then uses the recorded sale (forbidden tech, punishable) as leverage. Gorov freed, tin cargo bought, and Askone's taboo is wedged open: Pherl himself now needs Foundation goods to survive politically.
- **The Merchant Princes:** three Foundation ships vanish in Korellian space. **Jorane Sutt** (secretary to the Mayor) and **Publis Manlio** (Foreign Secretary AND Primate of the Church — the double office is canon) send Master Trader **Hober Mallow** of Smyrno to investigate — and to remove a rival. **Jaim Twer**, who tags along as Mallow's "friend," is Sutt's planted agent (Mallow later deduces he had priestly training).
- On Korell (Republic of Korell, ruled by **Commdor Asper Argo, "the Well-Beloved"**): a man calling himself **Reverend Jord Parma** appears at the isolated spaceport pursued by a mob — Foundation missionaries are death-penalty contraband on Korell. Mallow hands him over, to his crew's horror. Mallow's reasoning (stated at trial): the geography was impossible — an isolated port, the nearest city far away, yet a "hunted" priest and a mob both arrive within minutes = staged provocation. **Every second was captured on a Visual Record.**
- Mallow buys goodwill with **atomic gadgets**: a glowing necklace and household devices for the **Commdora Licia** (daughter of an Imperial viceroy, contemptuous of Korell's austerity), atomic hand-tools for the Commdor's factories. In a Korellian **steel foundry** he demonstrates an atomic shear-knife — and spots the **Spaceship-and-Sun of the Galactic Empire** stamped on the guards' blasters: the Empire is arming Korell.
- **Siwenna** (ruined former provincial capital): the impoverished patrician **Onum Barr** tells Mallow of viceroys' rebellions, massacres, and the Empire rotting from the center; **his sons are dead** in the purges. At an Imperial **power plant**, Mallow bribes his way in with a **personal force shield** (Imperial shields are building-sized; the Foundation's fits on a wrist) and finds **tech-men who tend generators they can no longer repair** — hereditary custodians of machines they don't understand.
- Back on Terminus, Mallow is **tried for the missionary's death**. He plays the Visual Record: under ultraviolet light, the "missionary's" wrist shows a tattoo — **KSP, Korellian Secret Police**. Acquitted, lionized, **elected Mayor**; he exposes Twer and arrests Sutt and Manlio when they move to raise a religious revolt against him.
- **War with Korell:** Korellian dreadnoughts (Imperial-supplied) attack; Mallow refuses battle, withdraws, and simply **stops trade**. Three years of embargo: Korell's Foundation-made tools, lights, appliances and factory machines fail one by one with no priests and no spare parts — and the Commdor's war dies in cold kitchens and dead factories. **Korell surrenders without a major battle.**
- Verbatim quotes used in this volume (verified):
  - **"This is a Seldon crisis we're facing, Sutt, and Seldon crises are not solved by individuals but by historic forces. Hari Seldon, when he planned our course of future history, did not count on brilliant heroics but on the broad sweeps of economics and sociology."** (Mallow, Part V — bubble carries the signature sentence, caption carries the rest.)
  - **"It's a poor atom blaster that won't point both ways."** (Mallow to Sutt, Part V §13.)
  - **"Never let your sense of morals prevent you from doing what is right!"** (Hardin's epigram, the epigraph of "The Traders" — used in the prologue explicitly as a quotation of Hardin, which is also a Vol 2 callback.)

### Deliberate departures / compressions

- "The Traders" (a full novella) is compressed to a 2-page prologue; Askone politics reduced to Gorov's cell, the transmuter sale, and the wedge. Pherl's long negotiation is compressed to one exchange; the blackmail is stated plainly in caption (caption-clarity rule — clear suspense, not withheld fact).
- The Korell/Siwenna itinerary is staged as one continuous voyage; the book's months of waiting are elided.
- Twer's exposure is folded into the trial page (in the book it precedes the trial).
- Ankor Jael (Mallow's political fixer) is cut; his function (political commentary) moves to captions.
- The Commdora's necklace "dying" during the embargo is our visual invention for a canonical fact (Korell's Foundation goods failing) — flagged here; the necklace is established as a gift on P6 precisely so its death on P14 lands.
- The mob's killing of Parma happens **off-page entirely** (he exits the airlock; captions state only that the mob "took him"); trial captions establish he was a police agent, not a priest. No violence depicted anywhere in the volume; the war shows pursuit and withdrawal, no ship destroyed on-page.
