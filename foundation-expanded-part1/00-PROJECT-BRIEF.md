# Foundation - Part One: The Man Who Saw the End

## Purpose

This is the expanded pilot for the *Foundation* adaptation. It gives the opening movement of Isaac Asimov's *Foundation* enough room to make Trantor, psychohistory, the trial, and the engineered exile understandable to Francisco and Sebastian without lowering the intellectual level of the story.

The shipped `../foundation/`, `../foundation-vol2/`, and `../foundation-vol3/` editions remain untouched. This volume expands the material formerly compressed into `../foundation/` Pages 1-7.

## Scope

- Source coverage: Part I, "The Psychohistorians," only.
- Length: cover + 24 landscape pages.
- Viewpoint: Gaal Dornick, a young provincial mathematician seeing Trantor and Hari Seldon for the first time.
- Arc: Gaal arrives believing he has joined the greatest civilization in history, proves mathematically that it is dying, sees the Empire try to silence the result, and discovers that Seldon designed their exile as the first move of a thousand-year Plan.
- Ending: the first Foundation departs for Terminus while Trantor still blazes with light; Seldon reveals that a second Foundation will be established at Star's End.

## The One-Sentence Window

A young mathematician reaches the world that rules the Galaxy, proves that it is already dying, and discovers that his teacher has turned their trial and exile into the first move of a thousand-year plan.

## Governing Adaptation Decision

This is an expanded Asimov adaptation, not a blend with Apple TV+'s continuity.

Do not add the Star Bridge disaster, the Genetic Dynasty, Demerzel's Imperial plot, Raych and Gaal's romance, the *Deliverance* voyage, Hari's murder, cryosleep, the Vault null field, Phara, or the *Invictus*. An optional reader afterword may explain those screen changes.

The expanded edition may learn from the television adaptation's sense of scale and visible consequence. It must do so with book-continuity events and original adaptation prose.

## Francisco's Destruction Question

The story must visibly answer why the Empire's fall matters without inventing an early battle.

Pages 10-11 dramatize Gaal's 92.5-percent calculation as a mathematical projection:

1. Outer tax and trade routes fail.
2. The fleet contracts and the Periphery separates.
3. Trantor's imported food and power systems become vulnerable.
4. Districts darken and civil order breaks.
5. The metal world becomes a ruin.

This is explicitly a probabilistic model, not a supernatural vision and not an event already happening. The emotional point is that nothing has visibly fallen yet, which is why nearly everyone can dismiss the warning.

## Structure

- **Pages 1-5 - The world that cannot fall:** view-room, landing, enclosed Trantor, observation tower, and Jerril's questions.
- **Pages 6-12 - The mathematics of collapse:** Seldon arrives, explains psychohistory, asks Gaal to verify the work, and shows what 92.5 percent means.
- **Pages 13-20 - Dangerous knowledge:** arrest, interrogation, detention, public-safety trial, the thirty-thousand-year dark age, and the Encyclopedia proposal.
- **Pages 21-24 - Exile as victory:** Linge Chen's threat and bargain, six months under martial law, Seldon's preparation, the two Foundations, and departure for Terminus.

## Editorial Standard

Every major scene must contain:

1. Orientation: place, time, and participants.
2. An immediate question or desire for Gaal.
3. Interaction or action on the page.
4. A visible turn.
5. Reaction or consequence.
6. A clear bridge to the next scene.

Foundation-specific rules:

- Translate every abstract idea into a human choice and a visible system.
- Explain both what changes and why it matters.
- Do not make psychohistory magical or individually predictive. It models very large populations in probabilities.
- Do not let captions resolve an important confrontation that the page never dramatizes.
- Hard technical and political pages may use T4-T5 text density when needed; do not make the reader infer missing causality.
- The Foundation remains the long-range protagonist even while Gaal carries this volume.

## Canon and Source Locks

The following facts were cross-checked against the shipped Foundation research and chapter summaries on 2026-07-13:

- Gaal is a newly qualified mathematician from Synnax traveling to join the Seldon Project.
- The Galactic Empire is twelve thousand years old and spans roughly twenty-five million inhabited worlds.
- Trantor is an all-city planet with forty billion inhabitants and depends on other worlds for food.
- Jerril befriends and questions Gaal, but is an Imperial agent.
- Seldon has Gaal verify the prediction. Total destruction of Trantor within three centuries has a 92.5-percent probability.
- The Commission of Public Safety effectively governs during the reign of weak emperors.
- Seldon's project includes fifty mathematicians and nearly one hundred thousand people across many specialties.
- Without the Plan, the fall is followed by thirty thousand years of disorder; the Plan can reduce that interval to one thousand.
- The Encyclopedia is the official explanation given to the Commission.
- Linge Chen is Chief Commissioner and the practical ruler of the Empire.
- Chen offers exile to Terminus instead of execution. The group has six months to prepare.
- Seldon had prepared for migration for more than two years and hoped the Commission would choose Terminus.
- A second Foundation is planned at the other end of the Galaxy, at Star's End.
- Seldon expects to live about one more year and will not see the Foundation established on Terminus.

Research links are recorded in `../foundation-expanded/HANDOFF.md`. Exact dialogue in `04-SCRIPT.md` is original condensed adaptation prose except for short, previously verified signature phrases.

## Visual Standard

Preserve the shipped Foundation register: 1970s British science-fiction paperback airbrush, monumental scale, teal/magenta/amber/space-black palette, chrome machinery, atmospheric depth, serious realistic anatomy, cream captions, and off-white speech balloons.

- Canvas: exactly 1536x1024, 3:2 landscape.
- All lettering baked into the raster image.
- No blank balloons or captions and no HTML/SVG lettering.
- Existing approved Gaal, Seldon, arrival, and trial artwork are identity/register anchors.
- The expanded edition changes pacing, not shelf identity.
