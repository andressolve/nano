# Settings - Foundation, Part One

## 1. Imperial Liner View-Room

**Pages:** 1.

A curved observation lounge inside a chrome Imperial liner. Immense viewport onto dense central stars, nebula haze, and Trantor's sunward glare. Banks of amber-lit analog-futurist consoles, navy-uniformed crew, polished metal ribs. The room closes for the dangerous final approach.

Use `../foundation/pages/page-01.png` as the shipped register and ship-interior source, but create a new page with a different panel structure.

## 2. Trantor Spaceport and Customs

**Pages:** 2.

A terminal built at the scale of a railway metropolis: vaulted bronze-teal metal roof, layered balconies, rivers of passengers, cargo lifts, customs gates, glowing directional bands. Heavier gravity and dense air make Gaal visibly adjust. No view of sky. Jerril appears as a small watchful figure in the background.

## 3. Tunnel Transit and the Enclosed City

**Pages:** 3.

A compact air-taxi moves through a vast tunnel mouth into an artificial canyon city. Layer upon layer of enclosed architecture recedes into teal haze; moving walkways and vehicle streams cross at different heights; amber artificial suns hang in the ceiling. The scale is magnificent and claustrophobic. There is no soil, weather, horizon, or natural light.

## 4. Observation Tower

**Pages:** 4-5.

A rare high platform where reinforced windows or an open shielded terrace reveal real sky above Trantor. The sun is painfully bright. Native Trantorians stand far from the glass or turn away; Gaal presses close. The metal world reaches to every horizon. Jerril approaches as a friendly stranger.

## 5. Luxor Hotel

**Pages:** 5-6, 13.

Comfortable Imperial-modern public lounge and spare private room: bronze wall panels, deep teal upholstery, amber lamps, no exterior windows. Gaal's room has a narrow bed, small desk, data screen, and travel case. Seldon's pale robe is a calm visual interruption. On Page 13, the warm room becomes cold and hard when Commission guards fill the doorway.

## 6. Seldon's Office, Streeling University

**Pages:** 7-9, 12, 23-24.

Streeling is one of the rare districts open to real sky. Ivory buildings and warm daylight contrast with the metal city. Seldon's office is spare: pale desk, data-spools, two chairs, shelves, a glowing hand-calculator pad, and a broad window onto ivory towers and blue sky. On Pages 23-24, military guards and packing crates intrude.

## 7. Psychohistory Projection Space

**Pages:** 8-11.

Not a separate magical realm. The calculator projects a luminous analytical model into Seldon's office: teal probability curves, amber population nodes, map-like trade routes, fleet and cargo symbols, and clear numeric markers. Pages 10-11 may let the projection visually dominate the panels, but desk edges, Gaal's hands, or the calculator frame must keep the reader grounded in analysis.

The projection's failure sequence:

1. healthy network of twenty-five million worlds;
2. outer routes dim and tax streams break;
3. fleet coverage contracts;
4. Trantor-bound food convoys vanish;
5. city sectors darken;
6. future metal towers crack and collapse.

## 8. Commission Interrogation and Detention

**Pages:** 14-15.

Severe rooms below the Commission complex: green-black polished stone, bare table, isolated overhead amber light, soundless heavy doors. The interrogation room positions the Advocate opposite Gaal. The detention room has a transparent partition or adjoining cell allowing Gaal and Seldon to speak without crowding one panel.

## 10. University Under Martial Law

**Pages:** 23.

The ivory University concourse is filled with ordered departure: guards at doorways, scholars sealing data cylinders, families labeling crates, technicians rolling scientific equipment, children holding small bags. This is not a refugee panic. Seldon's preparation makes it disciplined and purposeful despite the six-month deadline.

## 11. Exile Departure

**Pages:** 24 and cover.

Chrome colony transports rise from Trantor in ordered formation. The city-world below remains intact, brilliantly lit, and seemingly eternal. A projected galaxy or deep-space view shows Terminus as one hot amber point at the rim and a second, more distant point at Star's End. No Star Bridge and no Apple-series ship design.
