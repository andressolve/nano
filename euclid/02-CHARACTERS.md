# Euclid — Character Locks and Reference Prompts

All named recurring figures receive references before any story page is generated. The famous names do not appear inside generation lock blocks; the visual descriptions carry continuity.

## Euclid — Younger Teacher

### Character lock

```text
CHARACTER CONTINUITY — YOUNGER GEOMETER:
A Greek Mediterranean man about 30, lean medium build, sun-browned olive skin, long narrow face, strong straight nose, deep-set dark brown eyes, thick wavy black hair cut to the nape, short orderly black beard, no jewelry. Plain undyed linen chiton under a weathered blue-gray wool mantle. Ink stain on the side of his right middle finger. Alert inward posture: shoulders slightly forward, eyes measuring before he speaks. His intensity is controlled, never wild or mystical.
```

### Reference prompt

```text
Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: recurring-character reference sheet
Create one 3:2 landscape oil-painted character sheet with three views of the same Greek Mediterranean teacher about 30. Left: head-and-shoulders portrait showing a long narrow face, strong straight nose, deep-set dark brown eyes, thick wavy black hair to the nape, and short orderly black beard. Center: full-body in a plain undyed linen chiton and weathered blue-gray wool mantle, lean build, modest sandals, no jewelry. Right: working pose bent over a wax tablet with reed stylus, ink-stained right middle finger visible. Plain warm limestone studio background, even light. No text, no labels, no diagrams, no watermark.
```

## Philippos — Young Teacher (fictional)

### Character lock

```text
CHARACTER CONTINUITY — YOUNG TEACHER:
The same Greek-Egyptian man seven years later, now about 25. The same pale malachite-green linen chiton, now worn with a charcoal-gray shoulder wrap. Wax tablet replaced by a small pouch of counting pebbles. Plain warm limestone background. No text, labels, modern objects, or watermark.
```

## Reference gate

Before any page:

- Each age reads correctly.
- Younger and master Euclid are recognizably the same person.
- Nefru reads as Egyptian and professionally equal, not regal or servile.
- Philippos's two sheets read as the same person at 18 and 25.
- Ptolemy reads as an older Macedonian ruler, not Roman or pharaonic.
- Clothing and tools fit the Ptolemaic Mediterranean.
- All sheets share the same oil-painted register and warm limestone reference background.
