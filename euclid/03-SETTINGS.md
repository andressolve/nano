# Euclid — Setting and Object Locks

## S1 — Unnamed Aegean Port, Euclid's youth

- Era: late fourth century BCE, after years of Macedonian conquest and civil conflict.
- Place: modest coastal Greek-speaking town; never identified as a documented birthplace.
- Materials: rough limestone walls, packed-earth lanes, cedar doors, terracotta roofs, boundary stones, fishing cord, simple shrine niches.
- Light: smoke-brown late afternoon or cold dawn.
- Signature object: a waist-high boundary stone being levered from one groove to another.
- Avoid: famous Athenian monuments, Roman paving, monumental marble plazas.

## S2 — Alexandria Harbor and Building City

- Era: around 300 BCE, a young Ptolemaic capital still under construction.
- Place: long quays, warehouses, ship masts, survey stakes, gridded streets, mixed Greek and Egyptian workers, half-finished colonnades.
- Light: hard sea-white daylight, deep blue shadows, salt haze.
- Signature shapes: straight streets meeting at right angles; paired harbor arcs that foreshadow the two-circle construction.
- The Pharos lighthouse is not shown as completed.

## S3 — Mouseion Workroom

- A communal scholarly workroom, not an endless fantasy library.
- Limestone and painted-plaster walls; cedar shelving and papyrus cupboards; large central wooden tables; scroll baskets; reed pens; weights; wax tablets; oil lamps.
- One wall opens through columns to a planted courtyard.
- Geometry appears on low plaster boards, sand trays, and unrolled papyrus.
- Recurring position logic: Euclid works nearer the shadowed back wall early; Nefru's copying table catches the best window light; later they share the center table.

## S4 — Teaching Court

- Small open-air colonnaded court beside the workroom.
- Students sit on plain benches or low stools around a large sand table.
- No raised modern lectern. Euclid moves among them.
- A clean plaster wall accepts large charcoal diagrams.
- Daylight is bright but softened by linen awnings.

## S5 — Nile-edge Survey Field

- Agricultural edge after seasonal floodwaters have withdrawn.
- Dark wet silt, irrigation channels, reed markers, date palms, low mudbrick structures, survey cords and wooden stakes.
- Several landholders and workers observe, but only Euclid and Nefru are recurring faces.
- Practical geometry is physical: tensioned cord, footprints, uncertain banks, erased field boundaries.
- Avoid pyramids as background decoration; the scene is lived agricultural Egypt, not a tourism vista.

## S6 — Ptolemy's Working Hall

- Administrative room in the royal quarter, not a fantasy throne room.
- Polished limestone floor, painted Greek-Egyptian border bands, cedar map table, city plans, tax rolls, ship models, guards kept distant.
- Ptolemy often stands or sits at the worktable rather than on a throne.
- Light is narrow and controlled, reflected off pale stone.

## S7 — Night Copy Room

- Same scholarly precinct as S3, closed and intimate at night.
- Two or three olive lamps, blue-black courtyard beyond, papyrus edges weighted with smooth stones.
- Scratch copies, rejected sequences, and curled offcuts make labor visible.
- The final completion scene begins here before dawn.

## S8 — Transmission Montage Worlds

Used only after Euclid's death/absence:

1. Late-antique Greek copyist at a sloped desk.
2. Abbasid-era Arabic translator/scholar in a Baghdad study, handling a geometric manuscript with respect.
3. Medieval Latin reader working from an Arabic-to-Latin Euclid.
4. Early printer arranging a geometric page.
5. Modern hands constructing Proposition 1 on plain paper.

Each vignette is historically distinct in dress, scripts, furniture, and light. No era is depicted as a passive bridge; each scholar is actively reading, checking, translating, or teaching.

## Object lock — working copy of the Elements

- Papyrus rolls and loose working sheets, not one modern bound volume.
- Black ink main text, restrained red ink for proposition openings or guide marks.
- Diagrams are clean black lines with occasional small Greek letters.
- Edges show handling and revision but never artificial treasure-map aging.
- The title `ΣΤΟΙΧΕΙΑ` may appear once as readable Greek with the immediate helper `ELEMENTS`.

## Object lock — geometry kit

- Bronze dividers/compass with two pointed legs and a simple hinge.
- Plain cedar straightedge with no measurement markings.
- Knotted linen cord.
- Reed pens, black ink cup, red ink cup.
- Wax tablet and bronze/wood stylus.
- Smooth pebbles used as papyrus weights or counting markers.
