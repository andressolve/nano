# Euclid — Style Guide

## Style Block

Paste this first in every reference and page prompt:

```text
STYLE BLOCK:
Ptolemaic Alexandrian historical oil painting: a young Mediterranean port city around 300 BCE, built from sun-bleached limestone, painted plaster, cedar, papyrus, bronze, linen, sea haze, and lamplight. Grounded human faces and work-worn hands. Geometry appears through physical instruments and precise diagrams, never as magical glowing symbols. Cinematic landscape compositions with strong architectural lines, broad natural light, tactile brushwork, and quiet intellectual tension.

PALETTE:
Deep harbor blue, limestone cream, papyrus tan, charcoal black, oxidized bronze, Egyptian red ochre, muted malachite green, and one recurring compass-bronze accent. Day scenes use hard white Mediterranean light and blue shadow. Night interiors use olive-oil amber against blue-black windows.

CAMERA:
Human-scale historical drama. Prefer medium-wide rooms, close working hands, over-shoulder views of diagrams, and occasional monumental city wides. Faces remain readable whenever a character decision matters.

REFUSE:
No Roman imperial architecture, no medieval robes, no Renaissance compass tools, no bound codex books, no glass windows, no modern mathematical notation, no Cartesian axes, no glowing holograms, no fantasy magic, no white-marble emptiness, no pseudo-hieroglyphic wallpaper, no modern logos, no watermarks.
```

## Register Block

Paste verbatim after the Style Block:

```text
Oil-painting realism. NOT a comic. NO halftones, NO cel shading, NO ink linework. Painted brushwork, cinematic lighting, muted period palette.
```

## Visual identity

The whole volume lives between two kinds of line:

- **Lines imposed by power:** street grids, boundary stones, tax maps, royal plans, military formations.
- **Lines justified by reason:** compass arcs, straightedge marks, careful diagrams, proposition dependencies.

Early pages let architecture and rulers dominate the frame. As Euclid changes, hands, students, and copies occupy more of the image. The finale places Euclid small inside the construction rather than above it.

## Period image rules

- Alexandria is new, busy, multilingual, and unfinished. Scaffolds, quays, warehouses, survey stakes, and mixed Greek/Egyptian dress make it feel inhabited.
- The Mouseion is a scholarly precinct and communal institution, not a fantasy infinite library. Use colonnaded workrooms, papyrus cupboards, courtyards, dining rooms, lecture spaces, and copying tables.
- Papyrus rolls are stored horizontally or in baskets/cupboards; scholars unroll them across tables and weight the edges.
- Writing uses reed pens and black/red ink. Working calculations may appear on wax tablets, potsherds, sand tables, or wooden boards.
- Geometry tools are simple bronze dividers/compasses, plain unmarked straightedges, knotted cords, stakes, and plumb lines.
- Ptolemy wears a Macedonian/Hellenistic diadem and high-quality linen/wool, not a pharaonic nemes, Roman laurel crown, or jeweled fantasy armor.
- Egyptian workers and scholars wear plausible linen garments and should not be visually reduced to servants around Greek figures.

## Geometry rules

- Diagrams must be sparse, legible, and physically present on papyrus, wax, plaster, or sand.
- Use Greek letter labels only when they are large enough to render accurately and receive an English helper. Most pages should avoid letter labels.
- Proposition 1 must show exactly: one finite segment, two equal-radius intersecting circles centered at the segment's endpoints, and two straight joins from one intersection to the endpoints.
- The final construction may be stylized compositionally, but the geometric relationship must remain correct.
- Geometry never glows. Meaning comes from composition, light, and the characters' attention.

## Lettering system

Caption boxes:

```text
off-white papyrus box, dark charcoal serif text, large and readable
```

Speech balloons:

```text
warm off-white speech balloon, dark charcoal serif text, clear short tail to the visible speaker
```

Rules:

- Open every page lettering block with `LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:`.
- Close every prompt with the restrictions block below.
- Full-width top/bottom bands may be used on dense pages, but every line must remain large enough to read at the normal reader width.
- Speech bubbles should normally stay below 15 words. Longer thought belongs in narration.
- Dialogue staging follows reading order. In an A–B–A exchange, use upper-left, upper-right, lower-left.
- Do not include quotation marks inside speech balloons.
- Any readable Greek receives a nearby English helper. Decorative tiny script is allowed only when it is not meant to be read.

Restrictions block:

```text
All words spelled correctly. Render each listed text element exactly once. Do not duplicate text. Do not invent captions, labels, speech balloons, page numbers, Greek phrases, or background writing. Do not put quotation marks inside speech balloons. NO modern logos, NO watermarks, NO spurious signage.
```

## Mood progression

| Movement | Dominant light | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before Alexandria | smoke-brown dusk and cold dawn | knowledge can be erased |
| New city | hard white sun, deep blue shadow | ambition and imposed order |
| First propositions | warm papyrus and bronze | reason becoming shareable |
| Royal conflict | polished stone, narrow shafts of light | power trying to own method |
| Completion | lamplight widening into dawn | private labor becoming public inheritance |
| Transmission | each era keeps one bronze line | the proof survives the portrait |

## Anti-romance rules

- No saintly halo around Euclid.
- No mystical trance before diagrams.
- No crowds kneeling to a genius.
- No implication that Alexandria or Greece alone created mathematics.
- No invented romance subplot. Intimacy comes through teaching, argument, work, and trust.

## Cover rules

- Single full-bleed scene, no panel grid.
- Master-age Euclid in three-quarter profile at a worktable, not posed frontally as a statue.
- The two-circle construction exists physically on papyrus and echoes in Alexandria's harbor arcs behind him.
- Nefru may be a working silhouette at a second table, but the cover remains unmistakably Euclid's biography.
- Title text only: `EUCLID` and `THE MAN WHO BUILT A ROAD TO TRUTH` plus `A NOVEL OF A LIFE HISTORY DID NOT RECORD`.
