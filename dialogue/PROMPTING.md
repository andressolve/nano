# Intent-First Generation Prompting

This file governs the builder-only generation prompt. It does not govern critic
judgment. The critic never receives this prompt or grades compliance with it.
The builder derives it from the owner-approved adaptation, exact script, and
page intent—not from original sources or research archives. It never restores
material the adaptation deliberately cut or changes the story toward greater
source fidelity.

## 1. Start from the reader event

Write the one-sentence event and dominant relationship before describing the
room. Preserve exact strings, ownership, essential action, named identity, and
consequential continuity. Attach only references that actively bind those
facts. Give a few unmistakable compositional essentials and let the generator
solve the image.

Remove or demote:

- microscopic proof lists and decorative object inventories;
- exact coordinates, percentages, distances, and fragile geometry;
- tiny-prop legibility when the reader need not identify the prop;
- multiple redundant style warnings;
- accumulated correction clauses from rejected candidates.

The stable order is:

1. use case, finished output, canvas, and mode;
2. minimum approved input images and what each binds;
3. reader event and dominant relationship;
4. a small number of ordered moments;
5. every exact string once, in order and ownership;
6. moderate hierarchy/staging and concise style register;
7. consequential exclusions and deliberately demoted details;
8. native-lettering and finished-page constraints.

The page intent, generation prompt, and critic card are siblings derived from
script plus story purpose. The prompt does not author the card.

## 2. Revision modes

### Targeted

Protect the successful reader facts recorded by the blind read. Name the cited
criterion and one visible correction. The builder may see the preceding issued
prompt and latest validated report; it never receives rejected art as an image
input. Do not reopen every earlier instruction.

### Full prompt reset

When a criterion repeats, replace the complete prompt and composition strategy.
Start from intent, exact strings, story facts, approved references, and compact
validated findings. Do not open earlier prompts, rejected candidates, audits,
or builder history. A reset may rethink framing, staging, hierarchy, and panel
grammar, but not script, page count, intent, critic card, or reference locks.

## 3. Annotated gold examples

These compact excerpts preserve the operational shape of the successful Volume
II run. Character names are historical evidence, not reusable requirements.

### Page 32 — silent intent-first entrance

Representative issued wording:

> Create one finished, flattened graphic-novel story page at exactly 1024 ×
> 1536, 2:3 portrait, RGB PNG. It is a canonical production candidate, not a
> prototype, mockup, exercise, component frame, or spread.
>
> The page has two connected images in the same Chamber of Peers, seconds apart:
> (1) a narrower upper reaction band where the assembled chamber turns
> collectively toward the great door; (2) a large lower reveal where Haydée is
> there alone and the chamber remains visibly inhabited between her and distant,
> subordinate Fernand.
>
> This is a silent page. Render no text of any kind. Do not make any handheld
> prop or tiny object a focal point.

Why it worked: the prompt names one collective turn, one solitary reveal, and
one power relationship. It removes the objects sheet and seal entirely. Page 32
contains no testimony or dialogue; those belong to Page 33.

### Page 33 v1 — dramatic first pass

Representative issued wording:

> Input images: Haydée identity; Fernand identity and decorated heavy build;
> clean-shaven Count; Chamber architecture/palette; immediate continuity from
> promoted Page 32. Attach only these five images.
>
> PAGE 33 — dramatic. Haydée testifies; Fernand is finished; the Count watches
> the killing he arranged. In the same occupied chamber, create five clearly
> ordered moments…
>
> Bake all eight supplied strings exactly once, in order and ownership, with
> comfortable mixed-case transcription from a 600 × 900 reduction… The page is
> testimony becoming public fact, not microscopic document inspection. The
> President, document, and lettering outrank the seal.

Why it worked: references are minimal and functional; the event precedes room
detail; ordered beats and exact ownership are stated once; the seal is expressly
secondary rather than another proof burden.

### Page 35 v2 — targeted ownership correction

Representative issued wording:

> Preserve all successful reader facts from the prior candidate and correct
> only the Panel 3 dialogue ownership.
>
> Beauchamp visibly speaks and owns “She lives in a house on the
> Champs-Élysées.” with a tail unmistakably ending at Beauchamp; Albert visibly
> asks and owns “Whose house.” with a tail unmistakably ending at Albert.
>
> Preserve identity, continuity, four-moment order, and all successful reader
> facts; change only C1.

Why it worked: the revision does not redesign the office, restate every old
failure, or add defensive decoration. It protects the successful scene and
changes one reader-facing attribution.

### Page 38 v3 — clean prompt/composition reset

Representative issued wording:

> Use only these four attached images: Mercédès identity; Count identity; house
> architecture/palette; promoted Page 37 as immediate night continuity. Do not
> copy a rejected composition.
>
> PAGE 38 intent: Mercédès comes alone at night, strips away the Count's title
> with one word, and speaks the withheld name Edmond… Recognition and history
> becoming intimate—not furnishing or etiquette.
>
> Build four ordered moments… [then give all seven exact strings once with
> unmistakable ownership]. Preserve named identity, night continuity,
> four-moment order, and exact story facts.

Why it worked: the reset returns to locked facts and rebuilds the hierarchy
around intimacy. It does not patch the old prompt or use rejected art. The room
is simplified to serve the relationship.

### Page 49 v1 — quiet final page

Representative issued wording:

> Create one finished page for the reader turn: the Count watches the last warm
> light leave, counts Fernand, and looks toward Villefort's roof… The ending must
> feel chillingly continuous: Fernand's destruction cost the Count something
> real, and it did not stop him.
>
> Three connected night images: shadowed observer; dominant wide street with
> one receding lamp and the exact balloon “One.”; then a different distant house
> with one lit window and the exact closing caption. No other warm lights.

Why it worked: sparse staging carries emotional cost and forward motion. The
two lights and two text elements are story-bearing; furniture and decorative
precision are absent.

## 4. Final prompt check

Before issue, ask:

- Can a builder name the reader event in one sentence?
- Are all exact strings and owners stated once?
- Does every attached image bind an essential identity, setting, object, or
  immediate continuity fact?
- Are exclusions consequential rather than cosmetic?
- Could numeric geometry or tiny props be removed without changing the story?
- Is this a clean base/targeted/reset prompt rather than an accumulated history?

If the answer to the last question is no, rewrite rather than append.
