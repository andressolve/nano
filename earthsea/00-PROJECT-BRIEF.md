# A Wizard of Earthsea — Book One

**Title:** A Wizard of Earthsea
**Subtitle:** The Shadow and the Name
**Source:** Ursula K. Le Guin, *A Wizard of Earthsea* (1968). Private family adaptation (same lane as Name of the Rose, Foundation).
**Mode:** Narrative Mode (true multi-panel dialogue comic), 3:2 landscape 1536×1024, gpt-image-2 standard, one-shot whole-page bake.
**Register:** Sea-mist ink-line — a cool-palette variant of Register B. See 01-STYLE-GUIDE.md.
**Length:** cover + 20 pages + reader + 5-question WHY-quiz + landing card.

## The one-sentence window

The full novel, chapters 1–10: a gifted, prideful goatherd boy tears a hole in the world showing off, is hunted by the shadow he loosed, and — on Ogion's advice — turns to hunt it instead, until at the world's edge he names it with his own name and becomes whole.

## Structure (three acts)

1. **Pride** (P1–P9): Gont → the fog-raid → naming → Ogion's silence → Roke → the Knoll catastrophe. Midpoint hero pages P8–P9.
2. **Flight** (P10–P13): Low Torning / the dying child → Yevaud of Pendor → the gebbeth → the Court of the Terrenon.
3. **The Turn & the Hunt** (P14–P20): Ogion's "hunt the hunter" → the wreck and the castaways → first touch → Iffish hearth → the dry sea → the naming → coda.

## Editorial commitments

- **Skin-tone fidelity is the volume's first visual law.** Le Guin ("A Whitewashed Earthsea," Slate 2004): Ged is red-brown/copper-skinned; Vetch is black; Ogion dark copper-brown; the Kargish RAIDERS are the white-skinned, yellow-haired ones; Serret's pale Osskilian look is the marked exception. Every character lock enforces this. This is the thing Le Guin cared about most, and every prior adaptation broke it.
- **Verified-quote policy.** Only web-verified fragments get baked as lettering: the Creation of Éa epigraph; "To light a candle is to cast a shadow" (Master Hand); "To hear, one must be silent" (Ogion); the Yevaud bargain fragments ("If you could name it you could master it…", "With your name, Yevaud"). All other dialogue/captions are original adaptation prose — never presented as Le Guin's words.
- **The shadow's four stages** are rendered consistently: clot-of-black at the Knoll → gebbeth (empty hood, no face) → false faces at sea → Ged's own face at the naming.
- **Hoeg the otak's death** is kept deliberately vague ("lost in the escape") — sources conflict on the exact manner.
- **The broken ring** (Ch8 castaways) is included as an honest unexplained gift — "a gift he would not understand for years."

## Moderation notes

- Knoll attack (P8): black clot lunging, Ged's arm thrown up, silhouette — no gore, no wound detail. The scars appear healed on P9.
- The dying child (P10): shown as vigil at bedside + Ged's spirit at the dark wall — no dead child depicted.
- Gebbeth (P12): horror carried by the empty hood and flat-black body, not violence.
- Serret's death (P13–14): off-page; caption only.
- No falling-body compositions anywhere (self-harm filter).
