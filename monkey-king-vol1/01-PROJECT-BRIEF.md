# Monkey King, Volume I: Havoc in Heaven — Project Brief

**Form:** cinematic illustrated novel, portrait 1024 × 1536, ~47 pages.
**Audience:** Francisco (10) and Sebastian (7).
**Source:** *Journey to the West* (Xiyouji), attributed to Wu Cheng'en, 16th
century, chapters 1–7 only. Consulted in the Anthony C. Yu and W. J. F.
Jenner translations as evidence of incident, never for wording.

---

## The engine

**A nobody born from a rock, with no mother, no rank, and no fear, decides he
is the equal of Heaven — and Heaven, which cannot beat him and cannot bear him,
has to call on something bigger than itself.**

People still tell this story because of the joy of a creature who refuses every
ceiling, and because underneath the joy is a wound everyone recognizes: he was
not made by anyone, he was given the lowest job, he was not invited to the
party. The comedy is the refusal. The heart is the wound. Both are protected.

**If the protagonist reads as calm and above it all for more than a page at a
stretch, the engine has been written out.** That is a script-gate blocker.

## The spine

A monkey hatched from a stone becomes king of the monkeys, learns that even
kings die, and goes looking for a way not to. He comes back with a teacher's
secrets and a dragon's pillar, and Heaven notices. Heaven tries to manage him
with a job, then with a title, then with an army, then with its best champion,
then with a furnace, and every attempt makes him bigger. Finally the Jade
Emperor sends for the Buddha, who makes him a bet he cannot lose and loses it
in the palm of one hand. The last page is a mountain with a monkey's face in
it, and the fork it lands on is this: he has proven he is somebody, at the
price of the one thing he has never done in his life, which is wait.

## What this volume is

- A complete story: birth to mountain, no cliffhanger the reader has to
  resolve elsewhere. Later volumes are a promise, not a debt.
- A comedy of escalation with a real wound in it. Every escalation is Heaven's
  choice as much as Wukong's.
- A spectacle book: the waterfall, the sea floor, the cloud, the peach garden,
  the duel of shapes, the furnace, the five pillars at the edge of the world.
- Three tricks, each learned with a cost and paid off once.
- A book where the ending is both a defeat and a proof, and the reader can
  feel both at once.

## What this volume is not

- Not a tour of the Chinese heavens. No ranks, offices, or seating charts.
- Not a power fantasy without cost. Every trick Wukong gets, he pays for, and
  he loses the last fight completely.
- Not a moral lesson about pride. Nobody says the theme. The mountain says it.
- Not a setup for the pilgrimage. Tang Sanzang, the pilgrimage, and the
  rest of the cast of *Journey to the West* do not appear and are not named.
- Not a war comic. Heaven's army loses in a page and a half of spectacle; the
  fights that matter are one-on-one.

**Collapses.** Gold Star of Venus, the smiling diplomat who twice proposes
hiring the monkey, **merges into Laozi**, so that Heaven's peacemaker and
Heaven's executioner are the same face and the reader watches patience run
out. The Queen Mother of the West **merges into the Jade Emperor**: the peach
banquet is his party, and he is the one who did not invite Wukong. The nameless
old monkey who tells Wukong about immortals **becomes Old Ma**, and she stays
alive so he has a home to come back to and a face on the last page.

**Cut to nobody**: Nezha and Heavenly King Li, the Demon King of Havoc, the six
demon sworn brothers, the Four Heavenly Kings, the twenty-eight constellations,
Guanyin, the Ten Kings of the Underworld, Erlang's six brothers, the Star of
Longevity, the entire court of ministers. The heavenly army is scenery: no
faces, no names, no balloons. The hound is scenery that bites.

**Source names in chapters 1–7:** roughly thirty-five. **Locked here: eight.**

## The audience promise

**What these readers already enjoy.** Secret powers learned from a master.
Legendary objects nobody else can lift. A hero who talks back. Gods and
monsters at real scale. A duel where both sides keep changing shape. A trap so
big you cannot see its edges. Jokes that land in a picture.

**The repeatable pleasures.** Wukong's three tricks, each used at the moment
the reader wants it most. Each ceiling Heaven builds, and the page where he
goes through it. Wukong's mouth: he says exactly what he thinks to exactly the
person he should not say it to. A running joke that the grandest beings in the
universe keep having meetings about a monkey.

**The emotional promise.** The reader knows what it is to be the smallest, to
be given the job nobody wants, to hear about the party afterward. Wukong does
what the reader cannot: he goes anyway, and eats everything. And the reader
sees what it costs.

**The central dramatic question.** *Is there anything that can stop him, and
is stopping him the same as beating him?* The book's answer is no and no.

**Complexity budget.** Eight names. Three realms: the mountain, the sea, and
Heaven. One time jump, the years of study, carried by one prose page. Zero
assumed knowledge: no reader needs to know what a Daoist immortal or a Buddha
is; each is a person with a want when they arrive.

**What this project refuses to become.** A lecture on Chinese cosmology. A
list of ranks. A power catalogue. A solemn parable about humility. A book that
is embarrassed by its own best joke.

## Length

Working count **47**, reached by cutting and one addition at the script gate: seven movements, eleven source
sequences reduced to seven, three tricks instead of nine. The owner approved
the mid-forties on 2026-09-01. The count follows the script and will not
shrink by compression. If it grows past the high forties, the arc boundary
for a split is after Movement IV, where Wukong wins the empty title, and the
owner decides.

## The reader

A first-time reader who will never read the source. They must never have to
solve the adaptation, supply a missing causal step, or recognize a character
the book has not introduced. "Recoverable," "eventually clear," and
"understandable after looking back" are revision flags, not passing grades.
