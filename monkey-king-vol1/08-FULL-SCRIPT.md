# Full Script — Monkey King, Volume I: Havoc in Heaven

**Owner-controlled.** Production roles never edit this file. No compensation
number, generation note, or critic suggestion is ever written into it.

**How to read this document.** Each page is written as a scene first. The spec
under it is three lines and no more: mode (in the header), location, and why
the reader turns the page. Panel notes are staging, not metadata. **If the
metadata for a page is longer than the page's dramatized content, the story is
not being written.**

**Text discipline.** Every string between quotation marks in a speaker,
`PROSE FIELD`, `CAPTION`, `SOUND`, or printed-object line renders exactly as
written. Nothing else in this document renders. `tools/script_audit.py` and
`tools/verify.py` parse this format, so keep it exact.

# MOVEMENT I — THE STONE AND THE WATERFALL · pp 1–7

---

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Sunrise over an eastern sea. A green mountain
rising out of it, fruit trees down its flanks, a waterfall throwing itself off
a cliff. At the bare stone summit, an egg-shaped boulder taller than a man has
split down the middle, and light is coming out of the split. A small figure,
gold, is climbing out of it, one hand on the lip of the stone, looking straight
out of the page.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the sky)*
> "On the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain, where nobody lived, a stone cracked open."

**Why you turn the page:** something just climbed out of a rock and it is looking at you.

---

## PAGE 2 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the slopes of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** A small gold monkey in a crowd of brown ones,
under a fruit tree. Old Ma, gray, stooped, in her faded red shawl, has her hand
flat on top of his head. He is looking up at her. The birds have left the tree.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the plain ground below the figures, never over faces)*
> "He was a monkey, more or less. He was gold where the others were brown, and his eyes gave off a light that made the birds leave the tree. He had no mother. He had a rock, and the rock was open now and would not close.
>
> The monkeys of the mountain came to look. The eldest, Old Ma, put her hand flat on his head, the way she did with the new ones, and left it there a while.
>
> 'Well,' she said. 'You're here.'
>
> He stayed. He grew. He was faster than all of them, and louder, and he did not know what he was, and for a long time nobody minded."

**Why you turn the page:** he does not know what he is, and neither do you.

---

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the pool below the waterfall.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A hot day. The waterfall roaring into its pool,
monkeys crowded on every rock. OLD MA on the highest rock, left, on her stick,
shawl over her head against the sun. WUKONG at the front of the crowd, right,
gold among brown, one foot up on a boulder, staring at the water.

> **OLD MA:** "Nobody knows where the water comes from. It has been falling since before I was born, and I am very old."
>
> **WUKONG:** "So somebody should go and look."
>
> **OLD MA:** "Somebody should. Whoever goes through and comes back alive, we make king. That was always the rule. Nobody has ever done it."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The monkeys looking at each other. Nobody moves. Wukong has
a flat hand up over his eyes, looking at the white wall of water.

> **WUKONG:** "King. Of all of you?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Of all of us."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** A gold streak going into the white water. The crowd shrieking.

> **WUKONG:** "I'll go!"
>
> **SOUND** *(in the water)*
> "SPLASSSH"

**Why you turn the page:** he went into the water before anyone could tell him not to, and you want to see what is behind it.

---

## PAGE 4 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** From inside: the waterfall is a wall of silver
on the left, seen from behind. An iron bridge crosses the pool. Beyond it, a
house of stone cut into the mountain, with stone beds, stone bowls, stone
stools, a stone hearth, all dry, all empty, big enough for hundreds. Wukong is
tiny on the bridge, dripping, mouth open.

> **CAPTION** *(lower right, over the dry stone floor)*
> "Behind the water: a bridge, a house of stone, and room for everyone."

**Why you turn the page:** there is a whole home behind the waterfall and nobody has ever lived in it.

---

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Outside: Wukong bursting back out through the water onto
the rocks, drenched, monkeys scattering from the splash. He is shouting before
he has landed.

> **WUKONG:** "There's a house! Stone beds, stone bowls, a bridge. Dry as a bone. Room for every one of you!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Inside: the whole troop pouring across the iron
bridge, monkeys bouncing on stone beds and trying the bowls on their heads.
Old Ma crossing the bridge slowly on her stick. Wukong already up on the
tallest stone stool, right, arms out.

> **OLD MA:** "You went through. You came back. A rule is a rule."
>
> **OLD MA:** "Bow your head, or whatever it is kings do."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Old Ma's hand flat on his head. Monkeys cheering all round.

> **OLD MA:** "Handsome Monkey King. May you never get tired of it."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I won't! I won't ever!"
>
> **SOUND** *(the crowd)*
> "HOOOO!"

**Why you turn the page:** he is king of everything he can see, and it is the happiest anyone in this book will ever be.

---

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave, the feast.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Night. A fire in the stone hearth, fruit skins everywhere,
monkeys asleep in heaps. Wukong on his stool, left, laughing at nothing. Old
Ma beside him, right, knees up, looking at the fire.

> **WUKONG:** "How old are you, Old Ma? How old is very?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Old enough that I have seen three kings before you. None of them went through the water. They just shouted the loudest."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** His face changing. She is not looking at him.

> **WUKONG:** "Three kings. Where are they?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Where everything goes. Kings die too, little one. Monkeys, kings, mountains eventually. It is only the immortals who don't."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Who are they? Where?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong standing up on the stool, eyes wet, fists shut.
Monkeys waking.

> **OLD MA:** "Stories say: far to the west, over the sea, in the world of men. I never met one."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Then I'm going to. I'll find one, and I'll make him teach me, and none of us has to die."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just found out that he ends, and his answer is to leave.

---

## PAGE 7 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the sea, and the Master's mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A raft of pine logs on an enormous gray-green
sea, one small figure standing on it, not looking back. Far behind, the green
mountain going small.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** A monkey in a man's coat that is too big for him, walking a
dirt road on two legs, toward a gray mountain with a single pine on it.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the flat sea, never over the raft)*
> "He built a raft out of a dead pine and tied it with vines, and Old Ma stood on the shore and did not tell him to stay, because she had been the one who told him about immortals, and she knew it.
>
> The sea took him west for a season. The world of men took him for three years. He learned to walk on two legs so they would not chase him, and to wear a coat so they would not stare, and he asked every old person he met the same question. Most of them laughed.
>
> One did not. She pointed at a gray mountain with a single pine, and said: the Master lives there. He has had students for forty years, and he has never once taught any of them what you are asking for."

**Why you turn the page:** the Master has never taught anyone the one thing the monkey came for, and the monkey has never in his life taken no.

# MOVEMENT II — THE MASTER · pp 8–14

---

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard of the Cave of the Slanting Moon.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Dawn. White gravel raked in lines, a single
pine, a cave mouth in a gray cliff. SUBHUTI on the cave step, right: tall,
lean, white beard in a point, hands folded in his sleeves. Students with
shaved heads and brown robes have stopped sweeping to stare. WUKONG at the
gate, left, the coat gone, tail out, one foot up on the gatepost.

> **SUBHUTI:** "I do not teach what you came for. Nobody learns it here. Go home to your parents."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I don't have any. I came out of a rock, over the sea. It's still there if you want to see it."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Subhuti's face. Hooded eyes. The whisk in his hand, still.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Out of a rock."
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "And what do you want from me, rock's child?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "To not die. Old Ma says immortals don't. Teach me that and I'll sweep your whole mountain."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Subhuti turning away toward the cave, speaking over his
shoulder. A student holding out a broom to Wukong.

> **SUBHUTI:** "You have no name. A thing that wants to live forever needs one. Sun, for monkey. Wukong: the one who wakes to emptiness."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Sun Wukong. Sun Wukong! I have a name!"

**Why you turn the page:** nobody ever gave him anything before, and the first thing anyone gave him was a name.

---

## PAGE 9 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Cave of the Slanting Moon.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The courtyard through four seasons in one image,
left to right: blossom, heat, leaves, snow. In each, the same small gold figure
with a broom. In the last, he sits at the back of a row of students with the
broom across his knees, leaning forward.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the white gravel, never over the figures)*
> "He swept for seven years.
>
> He swept the gravel and the steps and the roof, and chopped the wood and carried the water, and at the lessons he sat at the back with the broom across his knees, and listened so hard his ears hurt. The Master talked about the way things are, and the students wrote it down, and the monkey did not write. He remembered instead, which turned out to be better.
>
> He did not learn how not to die. He asked, once a year, on the day he arrived. The Master said: not yet. Seven times."

**Why you turn the page:** seven years of not yet, and the reader wants to know what yes looks like.

---

## PAGE 10 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard, at lessons.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Students seated in rows on the gravel. Subhuti on the step,
right, whisk in hand. Wukong at the back, left, bouncing on his heels.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Wukong. Seven years. What have you learned?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Everything you said, Master. Every word. But not the thing I came for."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Subhuti has walked down between the rows and
stands over him. The handle of the whisk comes down on the top of the monkey's
head, three times. The students are laughing behind their hands. Behind
Subhuti, in the cliff, a small round door, shut.

> **SOUND** *(on the monkey's head)*
> "TAP TAP TAP"
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "Then you have learned nothing."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Subhuti walking away into the cave with his hands clasped
behind his back. The students howling. Wukong sitting very still, one hand on
his head, eyes wide, and a small smile starting.

> **WUKONG:** "Three taps. Hands behind his back. He means: midnight, the third watch, and come in by the back door."

**Why you turn the page:** everyone laughed at the monkey, and the monkey was the only one who got the joke.

---

## PAGE 11 · *dramatic*

**Location:** inside the cave, midnight.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The small round door opening from outside. A single candle.
Subhuti seated, waiting. Wukong slipping in, tail first.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Third watch. Back door. You are the first in forty years to understand a simple thing."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Subhuti, right, with two fingers on Wukong's
forehead. Wukong cross-legged, left. Between them a bronze basin of water, and
in the water the monkey's reflection is a hawk, and then a pine.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Two things. The shape: become anything with a body, and keep your own. The cloud: one somersault, a hundred thousand miles."
>
> **WUKONG:** "And dying?"
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "A thing that can be anything and be anywhere is very hard to kill. That is all immortality is. Don't tell the others."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The whisk raised, the one flick that ends things. Wukong
already half out of the round door, grinning back.

> **SUBHUTI:** "The shape is for staying alive. The cloud is for leaving. Use them to be admired, and I will send you away alone."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Master, I'd never."

**Why you turn the page:** he has been told exactly what not to do, in the voice that means it, and he is already grinning.

---

## PAGE 12 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the sky over the western sea.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Dawn. A tiny gold figure upside down in
mid-somersault on a puff of white cloud, high above the curve of a sea, the
sun enormous beside him. Below and far behind, the gray mountain with its
single pine.

> **CAPTION** *(lower left, over the sea)*
> "One somersault. A hundred thousand miles. The first one, he did just to see."

**Why you turn the page:** he can go anywhere now, and what he does with that first is show off.

---

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard, morning.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The students in a ring around a pine tree that is standing
in the middle of the raked gravel, where no pine was. The pine has a tail.
They are clapping.

> **SOUND** *(all round)*
> "clap clap clap clap"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The pine unwinding into Wukong, left, bowing with
his arms out. Subhuti on the cave step, right, whisk down, face like stone.

> **WUKONG:** "A pine! Did you see? A whole pine, with needles! Ask me for something harder."
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "Wukong."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Subhuti pointing at the gate. The students backing away.
Wukong very small.

> **SUBHUTI:** "I said what would happen. Take the shape and the cloud and go. And wherever you go, never say that you were my student."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Master, I only wanted them to—"
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "I know what you wanted. Go."

**Why you turn the page:** the one person who gave him a name has told him never to say it, and he is going to have to go home like that.

---

## PAGE 14 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A cloud coming down onto the peak beside the split
stone. Monkeys pouring up the slopes. Old Ma on the rock, right, stick, shawl,
and one flat hand up over her eyes, his gesture. Wukong jumping off the cloud,
left, before it has landed.

> **OLD MA:** "Ten years and a season. I counted."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Old Ma! I did it. I can't die. I'm going to teach every one of you."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Her hand flat on his head. His face crumpling for a second
and recovering.

> **OLD MA:** "You were thrown out."
>
> **WUKONG:** "How can you—"
>
> **OLD MA:** "You came back on a cloud and your face was wrong. Never mind. You came back."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Wukong up on the split stone, looking east at the sea, hand
over his eyes. Old Ma below him.

> **WUKONG:** "He said never to say I was his. Fine. I'll make a name of my own."
>
> **WUKONG:** "A king with no weapon is just a loud monkey. Where does a king get one?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "The old ones said the Dragon King under the eastern sea has a treasury. Nobody has ever gone and asked."

**Why you turn the page:** nobody has ever gone and asked, which is exactly the kind of sentence that gets this monkey moving.

# MOVEMENT III — THE STAFF · pp 15–19

---

## PAGE 15 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the floor of the Eastern Sea.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** From above: Wukong walking downward through
green water into blue, upright, as if down a stair, fish parting around him.
Far below, a palace of crystal and white shell glowing on the sand, and at the
very back of it, one dull red glow, the only warm thing in the sea.

> **CAPTION** *(upper right, over open water)*
> "He walked into the sea as if it were a door, and the sea let him."

**Why you turn the page:** there is a palace at the bottom of the sea and it does not know he is coming.

---

## PAGE 16 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Ao Guang's hall.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** AO GUANG on a coral throne, right, enormous: a
dragon's head on a lord's body, antlers, long white whiskers floating, robes
patterned with waves. Shrimp and crab soldiers along the walls. WUKONG tiny
before the throne, left, one foot up on its lowest step.

> **AO GUANG:** "A monkey. In my hall. Dry. Tell me what you want, little neighbor, and then tell me you are leaving."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I'm the Monkey King of Flower-Fruit Mountain, up top. A king needs a weapon. I'll take your best one."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Guards bringing out a sword. Wukong flipping it one-handed
and letting it drop. A halberd, bent across his knee. Ao Guang's fingers
drumming on the arm of the throne.

> **WUKONG:** "Too light. It's a toy. Have you got anything with some weight in it?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Ao Guang leaning forward with a slow smile, pointing back
into the treasury, toward the red glow.

> **AO GUANG:** "There is one thing. It has anchored this sea since the sea was made. Nobody has lifted it. Nobody ever will. Go and try."

**Why you turn the page:** the Dragon King just offered him the one thing nobody can lift, to be rid of him, and you know how that ends.

---

## PAGE 17 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the treasury of the sea.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The pillar: a column of black iron with gold bands, thick
as a tree, rooted in the sea floor, glowing dull red. Wukong beside it with
one hand flat on it. Ao Guang behind, filling the doorway.

> **WUKONG:** "It's warm. And it's humming. Like it's been waiting."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The pillar shrinking under his hand as he talks
to it, the gold bands sliding closer together. The sea floor shuddering. Fish
fleeing. Ao Guang's whiskers standing straight out.

> **WUKONG:** "Smaller. You're too big to swing. Smaller."
>
> **SOUND** *(the sea floor)*
> "KRRRRRMMM"
>
> **AO GUANG:** "It moved. It has never moved. The sea is anchored on that!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** A needle between his finger and thumb. He tucks it behind
his ear. Ao Guang has come off his throne for the first time.

> **WUKONG:** "Smaller. There. Now that's a weapon."
>
> **AO GUANG:** "Little neighbor. Do you know what you have just done?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Yes. I've got a staff."

**Why you turn the page:** the sea's anchor is a needle behind his ear, and the Dragon King is off his throne.

## PAGE 18 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Ao Guang's hall.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Wukong back before the throne, left, the staff full-size
over his shoulder, taller than he is, red iron with gold bands. He is pointing
at the armor on the guards.

> **WUKONG:** "A staff like this needs armor. Gold. A cap with feathers. Boots that stick to clouds."
>
> **AO GUANG:** "And who do I say took it?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Sun Wukong. Say I came out of a stone, and I can't be killed."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Wukong armored for the first time: golden scale
armor over a red tunic, a tiger-skin kilt, black cloud-boots, and a cap with
two long pheasant plumes curving back over his head. He is turning in the
water to see himself in a crystal column. The guards have backed to the walls.

> **WUKONG:** "Ha! Look at him. Now that is a king."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The hall empty. Ao Guang alone at a shell desk, right,
writing with a fish-bone pen, whiskers drooping, one eye turned up toward the
water overhead. Guards picking up the dropped sword.

> **AO GUANG:** "And the sea is still up. It seems it was holding itself all along, and nobody told me."
>
> **AO GUANG:** "To the Jade Emperor in Heaven. A monkey called Sun Wukong, of Flower-Fruit Mountain, has taken the anchor of the Eastern Sea."
>
> **AO GUANG:** "He says he came out of a stone and cannot be killed. I am too old to find out."

**Why you turn the page:** a letter is going up to Heaven with the monkey's name on it, and it is the first time Heaven has heard of him.

## PAGE 19 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Hall of Jade, in Heaven.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A hall the size of a valley: a throne at the top
of a flight of steps, jade-green pillars, a floor like still water. THE JADE
EMPEROR seated, right and high: a broad man in his prime, black beard in three
points, a flat crown with strings of beads before his face, two fingers at his
temple. LAOZI at his elbow on the step below, left: round, bald, white beard
cut square, indigo robe, a gourd at his hip, holding a shell letter and
dabbing his brow with his sleeve.

> **LAOZI:** "From the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea, Majesty. A monkey has taken the pillar that holds the sea down."
>
> **JADE EMPEROR:** "A monkey."
>
> **LAOZI:** "A monkey that came out of a stone, and says he cannot be killed. He's called Wukong."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The Emperor's eyes shut.

> **JADE EMPEROR:** "Send soldiers. Bring the pillar back. I don't want to hear this name again."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Laozi one step down, turning with the idea, one finger up.

> **LAOZI:** "Or, Majesty. Give him a job. A small one, up here. A monkey with a title holds still. Soldiers cost more than titles."
>
> **JADE EMPEROR:** "Fine, Laozi. Go and fetch him yourself."

**Why you turn the page:** Heaven has decided to manage him, and the man who thought of it has to go down and fetch him personally.

# MOVEMENT IV — THE STABLE BOY · pp 20–29

---

## PAGE 20 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Laozi standing on a small cloud a foot above the
peak, right, not touching the ground, holding a gold scroll. Wukong in full
armor on the split stone, left, staff on his shoulder. Old Ma beside the stone.
Monkeys everywhere.

> **LAOZI:** "Sun Wukong. I am Laozi, from the Jade Emperor, who rules Heaven. He has heard of you. He is offering you a post."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Heaven has heard of me?"
>
> **LAOZI:** "All of it."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Wukong turning to Old Ma, the plumes shaking.

> **WUKONG:** "Heaven, Old Ma. They sent for me. Me. Nobody has ever sent for me."
>
> **OLD MA:** "What is the post?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Who cares!"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The Buddha's hand opening flat, palm up, the size of a
courtyard.

> **BUDDHA:** "Then a small bet. Jump out of my hand, and it's yours. I'll say so myself."
>
> **BUDDHA:** "If you can't, you go back to Earth, and you stay there."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong climbing onto the palm, laughing, looking back over

**Why you turn the page:** he is standing on the palm of the only being who has ever been polite to him, and he is about to leap.

## PAGE 45 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the edge of the world.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** White. A flat white nothing, no horizon, and five
pink pillars rising out of it into a white sky, tapering, taller than sight.
At the foot of the middle one, a tiny gold figure, scratching red letters into
it with the needle from behind his ear.

**Panel 2 — 30%.** The middle pillar, close: the red letters, and beneath them
the monkey with his back to the reader, one hand at his belt, a small arc of
splash at the pillar's foot, his face turned over his shoulder, delighted.

> **PRINTED PILLAR** *(red, on the middle pillar)*
> "THE GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN WAS HERE"
>
> **CAPTION** *(in the top panel, over the white)*
> "The end of everything: five pillars holding up the sky. He signed the middle one. Then, to be sure, he marked it."

**Why you turn the page:** he has signed the end of the world, and marked it, and he is going back to collect.

## PAGE 46 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Hall of Jade.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong landing back on the open palm, arms out, bowing to
the walls.

> **WUKONG:** "I went to the end of the world. Five pillars. I wrote my name on the middle one. Send someone to look."
>
> **BUDDHA:** "I don't need to. Look down."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The Buddha's hand turning over, slowly, enormous.
On the middle finger, at the second joint, red letters, and a faint wet patch
below them. Wukong's golden eyes on it. His face.

> **PRINTED FINGER** *(red, on the middle finger)*
> "THE GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN WAS HERE"
>
> **WUKONG:** "That's my writing."
>
> **BUDDHA:** "And that, I think, is the rest of it. You never left my hand."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong bolting off the palm toward the door, staff and
plumes flat out behind him. The hand rising above him, open, coming down. The
Emperor's face behind the beads, watching.

> **WUKONG:** "That's a trick! You cheated, I went, I felt the wind—"
>
> **BUDDHA:** "Yes. Now hold still. Five hundred years should do it. Then someone will come for you. Wait for him."

**Why you turn the page:** the hand is coming down, and he has never held still in his life.

## PAGE 47 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the coast beside Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The hand coming down out of the sky over the
coast beside Flower-Fruit Mountain and becoming, as it lands, a mountain of
five bare ridges like fingers pressed into the earth. The split stone and the
red banner tiny on the green peak beside it. At the base of the new mountain,
a cleft, and in the cleft a small gold-furred face, eyes open. A gold paper
seal fluttering down to rest on the summit.

> **CAPTION** *(lower left, over the sea)*
> "The hand came down beside his own mountain and became one. A paper seal followed, to hold it shut. He fit under it with his head out."

**Why you turn the page:** he is under a mountain, and his eyes are open, and the book has one page left.

## PAGE 48 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Winter. The five ridges under snow. The cleft at
the base with the monkey's face in it, fur frosted, eyes open and still gold.
Climbing the last of the slope on a stick, a small gray monkey in a red shawl,
a peach in her free hand. Across the water, the green peak, the split stone,
and the banner gone pale pink.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the snow, never over either face)*
> "The mountain was called the Mountain of Five Elements. It did not move, and neither could he.
>
> Winter came, then spring, then winter. He could turn his head and see his own peak across the water, and the banner on it going pale. He could not reach the needle behind his ear. He learned what a year was, and how slowly five hundred of them go.
>
> Every year, when the peaches came in, an old gray monkey climbed up and set one on the rock where he could reach it, and sat with him a while, and said the Buddha's words back to him, the ones he had told her the first winter. She did that for as many years as she had. After that a younger monkey did it, who had been told to.
>
> Someone will come. Wait."

**Why you turn the page:** there is no next page. Somebody is going to come, and he is waiting, and this time for someone.
