## Blind read
A young gold monkey asks Old Ma how old she is. She explains that she has outlived three kings and that monkeys, kings, and mountains all die; only immortals do not. His face changes as he asks who and where the immortals are. When Old Ma says stories place them far west, over the sea, in the world of men, he stands with wet eyes and shut fists and vows to find one who can teach him so none of them has to die. The young monkey owns the page; Old Ma's revelation of death and immortals causes his turn from questioning fear to a declared quest.

## Visible text
WUKONG: "How old are you, Old Ma? How old is very?"

OLD MA: "Old enough that I have seen three kings before you. None of them went through the water. They just shouted the loudest."

WUKONG: "Three kings. Where are they?"

OLD MA: "Where everything goes. Kings die too, little one. Monkeys, kings, mountains eventually. It is only the immortals who don’t."

WUKONG: "Who are they? Where?"

OLD MA: "Stories say: far to the west, over the sea, in the world of men. I never met one."

WUKONG: "Then I’m going to. I’ll find one, and I’ll make him teach me, and none of us has to die."
