VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
After a feast in the quiet cave, young Wukong asks Old Ma about her age and learns that kings—and eventually monkeys and mountains—die. Young Wukong owns the page: the truth changes his laughter to alarm, then to a tearful resolve to travel west, find an immortal, and learn how to keep his people from dying.

## Visible text
WUKONG: “How old are you, Old Ma? How old is very?”

OLD MA: “Old enough that I have seen three kings before you. None of them went through the water. They just shouted the loudest.”

WUKONG: “Three kings. Where are they?”

OLD MA: “Where everything goes. Kings die too, little one. Monkeys, kings, mountains eventually. It is only the immortals who don’t.”

WUKONG: “Who are they? Where?”

OLD MA: “Stories say: far to the west, over the sea, in the world of men. I never met one.”

WUKONG: “Then I’m going to. I’ll find one, and I’ll make him teach me, and none of us has to die.”

## Findings

### Finding C4
- Observation: In the dominant middle moment, Old Ma’s face and visible eye are directed at Wukong rather than at the fire.
- Material reader harm: This changes the required impersonal truth delivered while she refuses his gaze into a face-to-face exchange, materially altering the dominant dramatic relationship at the instant Wukong learns that he will die.
- Redraw justification: The failure is in the two focal faces of the page-owning moment and contradicts C4’s explicit release gate, so it is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
