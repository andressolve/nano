VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
In a heavenly peach grove, an armored monkey warrior perches unseen in a fruit tree while an elderly blue-robed man carrying a basket speaks with him. The monkey has eaten fruit from the top trees and reacts when the old man says he needs more for tonight. In the dominant middle panel, the monkey comes down, learns that tonight is the Emperor’s peach banquet for every ranked immortal, invokes his Equal to Heaven rank, and asks where his invitation is. The monkey warrior owns the page: his shift from dry banter to alarm and direct confrontation drives it. In the last panel he stands while the old man stoops to collect fruit; the exchange turns on the old man’s unambiguous answer that the monkey is not invited.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: In panel 1, the tails for “They’re good. Bit dry.” and “For tonight?” point right into the open path rather than toward Wukong’s gold feet in the tree; the latter points toward Laozi’s side of the panel. The “No.” balloon in panel 3 likewise points into open space beside Laozi’s lower body rather than toward his mouth.
- Material reader harm: The page’s concealed-speaker exchange and final answer are not unambiguously owned by the scripted mouths from the lettering alone, so the attribution of Wukong’s interruption and Laozi’s decisive refusal is unstable.
- Redraw justification: Speaker ownership is central to both the setup and the page-ending rejection; leaving three causally important lines pointing at nobody or toward the other figure risks misreading the exchange more than replacing the page does.

### Finding C2
- Observation: In panel 3, Wukong’s “Say it plainly. Am I invited?” balloon is on the left beside the panel’s entry figure, while Laozi’s preceding “Wukong. The title was so that you would not need one.” balloon is on the right in the same horizontal reading band. The question reads first in the natural left-to-right pass.
- Material reader harm: This reverses the scripted cause and response: Wukong appears to demand a plain answer before Laozi reveals what the title meant, weakening the realization that is supposed to make him go still.
- Redraw justification: The revelation-to-stillness sequence is the page’s dominant turn and the stated reason to turn the page; a causal reversal at that moment is substantial enough to replace the page.

### Finding C6
- Observation: The objects scattered across the path and the object Laozi picks up are round, pink, intact peaches; no exposed peach stones are visible.
- Material reader harm: Laozi’s line identifying “These stones” no longer has visual evidence, and the path reads as fallen whole fruit rather than the remains of Wukong eating forbidden top-tree peaches.
- Redraw justification: The stones are the consequential continuity that lets Laozi discover the theft and begin the exchange; replacing them with whole peaches breaks the scene’s initiating cause and warrants replacing the page.
