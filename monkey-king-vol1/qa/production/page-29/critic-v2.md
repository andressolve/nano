## Blind read
The Monkey King sits alone and at ease on the roof of an ornate golden gate in Heaven, with his staff across his lap and celestial palaces behind him. He unmistakably owns the page. The narrator explains that the grand title and golden house leave him idle and unvisited; the turn comes when the Emperor, wanting him occupied, gives him the duty of guarding the Garden of Immortal Peaches, which he accepts as an honor and an improvement over being merely a monkey.

## Visible text
Object text — plaque on the golden gate:
GREAT SAGE
EQUAL TO HEAVEN

Prose — narrator:
They gave him the words, and a house made of gold,
and nothing to do in it.

He sat on the roof. He was the Great Sage Equal to
Heaven now, it said so on the gate, and nobody came
to visit, and nobody sent for him, and the Emperor’s
hall was somewhere over there, where he was not
needed. He had won. It felt like the stable, with a
better roof.

After a while the Emperor, who wanted him busy,
found him a duty. There was a garden of peach trees
on a hill in Heaven, and somebody had to guard it.
It was, Laozi said, a great honor. It was also, Laozi
did not say, a garden full of peaches, and he was
a monkey.
