## Blind read
An aged monkey, Old Ma, owns the page through her calm, protective gesture toward a young golden monkey while the mountain troop gathers around them. The golden monkey arrives without a mother from a rock that will not close; Old Ma places her hand on his head and acknowledges him, turning his strangeness and isolation into acceptance by the troop. He stays and grows among them, initially without anyone objecting to how different he is.

## Visible text
Narration: He was a monkey, more or less. He was gold where the others were brown, and his eyes gave off a light that made the birds leave the tree. He had no mother. He had a rock, and the rock was open now and would not close.

Narration: The monkeys of the mountain came to look. The eldest, Old Ma, put her hand flat on his head, the way she did with the new ones, and left it there a while.

Old Ma: ‘Well,’ she said. ‘You’re here.’

Narration: He stayed. He grew. He was faster than all of them, and louder, and he did not know what he was, and for a long time nobody minded.
