VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
The dominant top panel owns the page: a celestial official reads an imperial decree recognizing Sun Wukong as Great Sage Equal to Heaven, with a house in Heaven and no duties, while Wukong sits beneath the matching banner. The official then says Heaven accepts the words on Wukong's banner. In the smaller exchange below, an old monkey asks why Wukong would return to Heaven; Wukong answers that he wants to hear Heaven say his name as he wrote it. In the final panel Wukong leaves with the official, answering the old monkey's plea to return with confidence. The turn is that Wukong's self-declared title is formally recognized, and that recognition causes him to go back to Heaven. Wukong owns the decision and emotional turn.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: The balloon containing `Those are your words, Great Sage. I read them off your banner. Heaven agrees to them.` has a tail that points down and away from every visible mouth rather than toward Laozi's mouth.
- Material reader harm: Laozi's second required line is not visibly owned by him, so the dominant reading loses exact speaker attribution at the moment Heaven explicitly agrees to Wukong's self-declared title.
- Redraw justification: Correct ownership of both opening lines is a blocking causal requirement, and the current orphaned tail materially weakens the page's central reader event enough to justify replacing the complete page.
