## Blind read
The top, largest panel owns the page: in a wrecked banquet hall, an indigo-robed elder runs in carrying empty gourds while the seated ruler demands an explanation. The elder reports that someone ate the elixir and destroyed five hundred years of work. The exchange then turns from titles and accommodation to sending Erlang and everything available; on the terrace, an army pours through a distant gate as the ruler puts a hand on the elder's shoulder. The elder owns the action and the decisive change, but his hardened decision is not the page's dominant visual beat.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: The wrecked-hall arrival is visibly the largest panel. Laozi on the throne step with the dropped gourds and changed face, while the seated Emperor watches him, occupies a smaller middle panel.
- Material reader harm: The page visually peaks on the arrival and banquet wreckage instead of Laozi’s irreversible “No more offers” turn, so his changed face and the Emperor seeing it do not carry the dominant dramatic relationship.
- Redraw justification: This reverses the required whole-page hierarchy and materially weakens the central reader event that launches the turn to Erlang; correcting that page-level emphasis is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
