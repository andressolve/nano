VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
An elderly official in blue reports to an enthroned emperor in a wrecked banquet hall that someone ate the elixir from all five gourds, destroying five hundred years of work. The middle panel unmistakably owns the page: the official kneels in the foreground before the emperor amid the emptied gourds. The turn comes when the official ends any thought of bargaining, declares the culprit impossible to kill or manage, and urges the emperor to send Erlang and everything available. In the final panel, the pair overlooks a massed force as the emperor identifies Erlang as his nephew and the official says Erlang will come for a fight.

## Findings

### Finding C4
- Observation: In the dominant middle panel, Laozi reads as kneeling or crouched low on the throne step before the seated Emperor, not standing.
- Material reader harm: The posture turns Laozi’s decisive collision with the Emperor into a subordinate act of supplication, weakening Laozi’s ownership of the page and the force of his “No more offers” turn.
- Redraw justification: The failed posture materially reverses the dominant dramatic relationship at the page’s central turn, which is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
