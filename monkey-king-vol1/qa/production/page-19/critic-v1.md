VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
An elderly blue-robed court adviser reports that a monkey has taken the Dragon King’s sea-supporting pillar. The seated emperor owns the page through his repeated gold-robed presence and the central close-up. He first orders soldiers to recover the pillar and rejects hearing the monkey’s name again; the turn comes when the adviser argues that a small celestial title will contain the monkey more cheaply than soldiers, causing the emperor to abandon force and send the adviser to fetch him.

## Visible text
Older blue-robed court adviser: “From the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea, Majesty. A monkey has taken the pillar that holds the sea down.”

Seated emperor: “A monkey.”

Seated emperor: “Send soldiers. Bring the pillar back. I don’t want to hear this name again.”

Older blue-robed court adviser: “Or, Majesty. Give him a job. A small one, up here. A monkey with a title holds still. Soldiers cost more than titles.”

Seated emperor: “Fine, Laozi. Go and fetch him yourself.”

No speaker name, label, tag, or other extra text is lettered outside the dialogue.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The 600 × 900 proof contains only five dialogue strings. Laozi’s required third panel-one string, “A monkey that came out of a stone, and says he cannot be killed. He’s called Wukong.”, is absent.
- Material reader harm: The page omits the monkey’s stone origin and the focal name Wukong, so the Emperor’s later phrase “this name” refers to a name the reader has never been given; required named identity and the dialogue’s causal progression are broken.
- Redraw justification: Losing an entire required string and the focal character’s name is a substantial script and reader-event failure that outweighs the risk of replacing the complete page.
