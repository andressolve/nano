VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Erlang owns the page visually and verbally: he is the large upright figure in the dominant opening panel, facing Sun Wukong across the cloudscape. Erlang identifies himself and says he came to test whether Wukong's banner is true; Wukong answers with a taunt about Erlang's three eyes. Erlang laughs, the two discover a shared appetite for confrontation, and the exchange turns into a weapon clash. Erlang's final “Good.” changes the encounter from wary introduction into mutual recognition through combat.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The proof contains the unlisted dialogue balloon “(laughing) Ha! I like you already.” between the first and second required exchanges.
- Material reader harm: This adds owner-uncontrolled dialogue and an extra reaction beat to an exact-script page, changing what Erlang says and the causal pacing of the meeting.
- Redraw justification: Exact dialogue is reader-facing release content, and the added balloon is integrated into a dedicated panel; the textual and structural deviation is substantial enough to replace the complete page.

### Finding C3
- Observation: The balloon for “Nobody has ever asked me that. Everyone else just runs.” points to Wukong, while the balloon for “I don’t run. I’ve got nowhere to run to. This is it.” points to Erlang, reversing the required speakers.
- Material reader harm: The reversal gives Erlang’s reason for laughing to Wukong and gives Wukong’s declaration of resolve and lack of escape to Erlang, materially changing both characters and breaking the intended equal-to-equal turn.
- Redraw justification: The complete second exchange is owned by the wrong mouths, so the dominant character relationship and the page-turn reason do not survive; that warrants replacing the page despite redraw risk.

### Finding C5
- Observation: In the strike panel, the tail of the “Good.” balloon points down-left toward the hound at lower right rather than toward Erlang.
- Material reader harm: The climactic approval reads as the hound’s speech instead of Erlang’s response to Wukong’s hit, removing the scripted payoff from the focal relationship.
- Redraw justification: Misattributing the final line to the hound corrupts the page’s decisive reader event and violates the required object state, which is substantial enough to risk a complete redraw.
