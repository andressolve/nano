VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
An armored Sun Wukong receives a cloud-borne envoy's announcement that Heaven is offering him a post. The large opening panel unmistakably owns the page, and Wukong owns the page across the sequence: he is thrilled to have been summoned, dismisses concern about the post, and starts to leave. The turn comes when the elderly monkey reminds him of his promise to teach the troop not to die and asks only that he return; Wukong's proud departure resolves into a direct promise to come back as a lord.

## Visible text
- Panel 1, Sun Wukong (balloon tail points to the armored monkey): `Heaven has heard of me?`
- Panel 1, Laozi (balloon tail and placement point to the elderly man seated on the cloud): `Sun Wukong. I am Laozi, from the Jade Emperor, who rules Heaven. He has heard of you. He is offering you a post.`
- Panel 1, Laozi (balloon tail points up-left toward the cloud rider): `All of it.`
- Panel 2, Sun Wukong (balloon tail points right toward the armored monkey): `Heaven, Old Ma. They sent for me. Me. Nobody has ever sent for me.`
- Panel 2, Sun Wukong (balloon tail points up-right toward the armored monkey): `Who cares!`
- Panel 2, Old Ma (balloon tail points left toward the elderly monkey): `What is the post?`
- Panel 3 left, Old Ma (balloon tail points right toward the elderly monkey): `You said you’d teach us not to die. You haven’t yet.`
- Panel 3 right, Sun Wukong (balloon tail points left toward the armored monkey): `When I’m back. When I’m a lord.`
- Panel 4, Old Ma (balloon tail points right toward the elderly monkey): `Come back. That’s the whole thing I want from you. Come back.`

Names lettered in dialogue: `Sun Wukong`, `Laozi`, and `Old Ma`. No name or other text is lettered outside dialogue; there is no `EXTRA TEXT`.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: In the dominant opening panel, Wukong's `Heaven has heard of me?` balloon is at the upper left and reads before Laozi's invitation balloon at the upper right. The reply is therefore readable before the line that prompts it.
- Material reader harm: The page reverses the cause and response of its central exchange, so Wukong appears to ask whether Heaven has heard of him before the envoy announces that it has. This breaks the scripted invitation and reaction that own the page.
- Redraw justification: The defect affects the causal order of the page's dominant dramatic event rather than a secondary detail; leaving it in place makes the central exchange read incoherently, which is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
