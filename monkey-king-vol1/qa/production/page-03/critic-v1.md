VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
A gold young monkey kneels before Old Ma while she rests a hand on his head, with the other monkeys gathered around and the split-open rock behind them. The relationship between Old Ma and the gold monkey owns the page. Her touch and statement mark his acceptance, after which the narration says that he stays and grows among the monkeys.

## Visible text
Narration:

He was a monkey, more or less. He was gold where the others were brown, and his eyes gave off a light that made the birds leave the tree. He had no mother. He had a rock, and the rock was open now and would not close.

The monkeys of the mountain came to look. The eldest, Old Ma, put her hand flat on his head, the way she did with the new ones, and left it there a while.

Old Ma, quoted within the narration:

‘Well,’ she said. ‘You’re here.’

Narration:

He stayed. He grew. He was faster than all of them, and louder, and he did not know what he was, and for a long time nobody minded.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: None of the seven required strings appears on the 600 × 900 proof. The proof instead contains extra prose beginning “He was a monkey, more or less.” and ending “nobody minded.”
- Material reader harm: The reader receives none of the required dialogue that establishes the waterfall mystery, proposes the dare, names the prize, confirms its scope, or commits Wukong to the leap, and the required sound cue is absent.
- Redraw justification: The complete scripted exchange and plunge cue are missing and replaced by a different passage, so the intended page cannot be restored without replacing the complete page.

### Finding C2
- Observation: The page is a single acceptance scene in which Old Ma touches the kneeling gold monkey’s head; it contains no poolside dare, pause in which nobody moves, gold streak into white water, shrieking crowd, or closing plunge.
- Material reader harm: The dominant turn reads as the gold monkey being welcomed and growing up, not accepting the dare and entering the waterfall, so the intended suspense about what lies behind the water does not exist.
- Redraw justification: Both the dominant composition and the closing action communicate a different reader event; replacing the complete page is warranted because the required dramatic turn is wholly absent.
