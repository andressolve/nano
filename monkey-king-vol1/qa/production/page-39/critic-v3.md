VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
A chained monkey prisoner is presented before a seated emperor in a formal court. A helmeted guard immediately behind the prisoner tells the emperor that capturing the prisoner took all of them and that the prisoner wanted this acknowledged. The emperor orders the prisoner to kneel while addressing him by an exalted title. The monkey owns the page: in the dominant final panel, he refuses and turns the emperor's own publicly granted title into the reason they are equals, changing the scene from imposed submission to open rhetorical defiance.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: In the 600 × 900 proof, the first balloon's tail points to the helmeted soldier immediately behind and to the right of Wukong. Erlang is the separate silver-armored man farther right with the grounded lance and hound, and the balloon does not point to his mouth.
- Material reader harm: `It took all of us, uncle. He wanted that said.` is visibly owned by a soldier rather than Erlang, so the report and family address come from the wrong mouth and the scripted Erlang–Emperor relationship is lost.
- Redraw justification: Speaker ownership is an explicit release gate, and the tail assigns the complete first line to a specifically forbidden speaker at proof size. That material attribution failure outweighs the risk of replacing the page.
