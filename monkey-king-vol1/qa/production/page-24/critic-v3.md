VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Laozi reassures Wukong that the Emperor's horses have never looked better. Wukong asks what rank his title carries; Laozi, visibly uncomfortable, admits that it is below rank and is given to someone who is not anyone yet. The bottom action panel and Wukong own the page: Wukong breaks the desk with his staff, rejects being made a stable boy, and decides to go home while Laozi tries to call the post a beginning. The disclosure turns Wukong's pride into an open rupture and departure.

## Findings

### Finding C2
- Observation: The large desk-breaking action image, with Wukong spread across the foreground and the staff cutting through the composition, visually dominates the page; Laozi’s answer is confined to a quieter middle strip and does not own the page.
- Material reader harm: The reader experiences Wukong’s destruction as the primary event instead of Laozi’s honest, uncomfortable disclosure, so the wound that causes the outburst does not land as the dominant dramatic turn.
- Redraw justification: Page ownership is the required dramatic relationship, and the current hierarchy reverses it strongly enough that the scripted turn cannot be recovered through reading order or otherwise-correct text; correcting it warrants replacing the complete page.
