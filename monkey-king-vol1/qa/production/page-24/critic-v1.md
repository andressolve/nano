VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
In the Heavenly stables, an old keeper praises the horses while the armored golden monkey lounges at a desk and asks what rank his post carries. The keeper admits that it is below rank, a post for someone who is not anyone yet. The monkey erupts, breaks the desk with his staff, rejects being treated as a stable boy, and declares that he is going home. The broken-desk eruption owns the page. The change is the monkey's shift from lounging confidence to wounded anger and departure.

## Visible text
- Old keeper: “The Emperor’s horses have never looked better. Everyone says so.”
- Golden monkey: “Everyone? Then tell me, Laozi. Keeper of the Heavenly Horses. What rank is that? How high?”
- Old keeper: “It isn’t a rank. It’s below rank. It is the post one gives to someone who is not anyone yet.”
- SOUND, the desk: “KRAKK”
- Golden monkey: “A stable boy. You brought me up here to be a stable boy. Down there, I’m a king!”
- Old keeper: “Wukong, it is a beginning—”
- Golden monkey: “I don’t begin. I’m going home.”

The names “Laozi” and “Wukong” are lettered only as part of dialogue. No name is lettered as a speaker label or other extra text.

## Findings

### Finding C2
- Observation: The expansive, high-action final image of the broken desk, rearing horses, full-length staff, and separated figures dominates the page; Laozi’s quieter face-and-answer image does not unmistakably own it.
- Material reader harm: The page lands first as Wukong’s explosive retaliation rather than as the honest, uncomfortable revelation that wounds him, so the intended dominant turn and dramatic relationship are displaced.
- Redraw justification: This is the page’s central reader event and hierarchy, not a cosmetic detail; because the complete page materially emphasizes the consequence over its defining cause, preserving it would retain the wrong dramatic owner.
