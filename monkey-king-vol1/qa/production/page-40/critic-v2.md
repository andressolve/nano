VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
In a palace chamber, an elderly robed man presents a tray of gourd-shaped vessels to a chained monkey warrior and explains that his furnace restores whatever enters it to its original substance. He says the monkey was made from his forbidden peaches and elixir and demands their return. The top confrontation is the largest scene, but the monkey owns the page's turn: the tight central close-up isolates his realization that he is made of rock. In the final panel, the elderly man uses that realization to propose recovering both a rock and his pills; the enthroned emperor authorizes the plan and orders Laozi to make it work this time. The causal change is the monkey's recognition of his stone origin, which converts the demand into a concrete plan approved by the emperor.

## Findings

### Finding C5
- Observation: The dominant top-panel tray visibly carries more than five gourds, while the throne-panel tray carries five.
- Material reader harm: The counted set changes within the page and contradicts the locked five-gourd object state at the center of Laozi’s demand and plan.
- Redraw justification: The gourds are the consequential story objects being recovered, and their visibly inconsistent count breaks immediate continuity in the page’s central action; that story-state error is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
