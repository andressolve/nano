VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Across three panels, the Monkey King enters a red-walled storeroom lined with gourds and a large bronze vessel, identifies it as Laozi’s place, finds five gourds of what he assumes are sweets, eats their golden contents, and then realizes they held Laozi’s elixir. The Monkey King owns the page and every spoken beat; the middle panel of him crouched among opened gourds and eating the golden pellets is the dominant image. The turn is caused by his realization after eating all five gourds: alarm gives way to the delighted discovery that he feels wonderful.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: The middle panel shows five gourds, but the last panel shows only four empty gourds on the floor. The closed red jar beside Wukong is the jar he carried into the room, not a fifth emptied gourd.
- Material reader harm: The missing fifth empty gourd contradicts the spoken realization “All five gourds” at the causal aftermath and breaks the consequential object state that proves what Wukong consumed.
- Redraw justification: Five emptied gourds are an explicit, countable story fact required for the turn; showing four leaves a visible continuity error in the climactic panel, substantial enough to block release despite the risk of replacing the complete page.
