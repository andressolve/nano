VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Five immense pink pillars hold up the sky. An armored monkey warrior, who owns the page, stands tiny against them and signs the middle pillar; the lower image reveals him proudly displaying the finished red inscription while water splashes around the pillar's base. The turn is his act of marking the otherwise monumental pillar with a boast of his presence.

## Visible text
Narration: The end of everything: five pillars holding up the sky. He signed the middle one. Then, to be sure, he marked it.

Object text, painted on the middle pillar:

THE GREAT

SAGE EQUAL

TO HEAVEN

WAS HERE

No character or speaker name is lettered on the page.

## Findings

### Finding C4
- Observation: In the close view, the monkey is turned toward the reader in a three-quarter frontal pose with his chest armor visible and both hands braced on his hips. He does not have his back to the reader with one hand at his belt; the splash appears at the pillar's foot without the required marking gesture.
- Material reader harm: The focal image reads as the monkey proudly posing beside the inscription and incidental splashing water, so the caption's causal second beat—"Then, to be sure, he marked it"—does not land as an action he performs.
- Redraw justification: This is the page's dominant turn and required focal action, not a cosmetic pose variation. Without the back-to-reader, hand-at-belt staging, the finished page omits the legible physical mark that completes the scripted event, which is substantial enough to risk replacing the page.
