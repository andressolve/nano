VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Wukong returns from Heaven humiliated, tells Old Ma that the quiet laughter was worse than his empty title, then turns that insult into defiance by painting a self-chosen title on red cloth. The monkeys raise it as a banner while Old Ma warns that Heaven will answer. Wukong owns the page dramatically, but the final flying-banner image owns it visually. The change is from private humiliation to a public challenge that invites Heaven to come.

## Visible text
- OLD MA: That was fast. Lord of what?
- WUKONG: Of horses. Of nothing. They laughed at me up there, Old Ma. Quietly. Which is worse.
- WUKONG: If they won’t give me a name, I’ll give me one. Old Ma, what do you call someone Heaven can’t order around?
- OLD MA: I don’t think there’s a word for it.
- WUKONG: There is now.
- EXTRA TEXT: GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN — printed on the red cloth in the painting image.
- OLD MA: They will come for that, you know.
- WUKONG: Good. Let them see it on the way.
- EXTRA TEXT: GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN — printed on the flying banner in the final image.

A title/name is lettered outside the dialogue: yes, the banner title appears twice. No speaker name or other label is lettered.

## Findings

### Finding C1
- Observation: The five-word banner title, GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN, reads on the cloth in the painting image and then reads again on the flying banner in the final image. The required banner string therefore appears twice rather than once, making the second occurrence extra text.
- Material reader harm: The page fails the exact-text release gate at its dominant focal lettering; the scripted single title reveal is duplicated across two images.
- Redraw justification: This is large, unmistakable lettering on the central dramatic object, not a background or cosmetic flaw, so its duplication is substantial enough to require replacement of the complete page.

### Finding C2
- Observation: The bright final image and its broad flying banner pull primary attention, while the painting image does not unmistakably own the page.
- Material reader harm: The visual climax becomes the already-raised banner rather than Wukong’s consequential act of writing himself a title Heaven never gave him, changing the scripted dominant turn.
- Redraw justification: The failure is page-wide dramatic hierarchy, not local polish; correcting which image owns the page requires risking a complete redraw.
