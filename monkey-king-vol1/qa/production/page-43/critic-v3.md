## Blind read
In the Hall of Jade, the armored monkey confronts the crowned man on his throne, reciting the failed attempts to contain him and challenging him to surrender the seat. The large throne-room panel unmistakably owns the page, with the monkey driving the confrontation while the crowned man is pressed back at the top of the steps and the elderly man stands empty-handed among gourds below. The exchange changes when the elderly man has no answer; the crowned man invokes the Buddha, and the final explanation establishes that Heaven is asking help from someone who owes it nothing.

## Findings

### Finding C3
- Observation: In the final panel, "Who's the Buddha?" sits at the upper left and is read before the prompting line "Then send west. To the Buddha. Tell him Heaven is asking." at the upper right.
- Material reader harm: The response precedes its cause, so Wukong appears to ask about the Buddha before the Emperor introduces him; this breaks the scripted turn and the causal order of the exchange.
- Redraw justification: The defect reverses the page's climactic question-and-answer sequence in ordinary reading order, materially obscuring the intended reveal and justifying replacement of the complete page.
