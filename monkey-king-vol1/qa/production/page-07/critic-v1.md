VERDICT: REVISE

## Blind read
Sun Wukong owns the page. He leaves Old Ma on a raft, crosses the sea, and searches among humans for three years while learning to pass among them. Repeated failure turns into a destination when one old woman points him toward the gray mountain where the Master lives.

## Visible text
Narration: He built a raft out of a dead pine and tied it with vines, and Old Ma stood on the shore and did not tell him to stay, because she had been the one who told him about immortals, and she knew it.

Narration: The sea took him west for a season. The world of men took him for three years. He learned to walk on two legs so they would not chase him, and to wear a coat so they would not stare, and he asked every old person he met the same question. Most of them laughed.

Narration: One did not. She pointed at a gray mountain with a single pine, and said: the Master lives there. He has had students for forty years, and he has never once taught any of them what you are asking for.

## Findings

### Finding C5
- Observation: In the dominant sea image, Wukong's head, body, and stance face directly toward the green mountain, so the mountain reads as lying ahead of his raft rather than going small behind him.
- Material reader harm: This reverses the page's central departure and makes the home mountain read as a destination, undermining the crossing that causes the turn and confusing its relationship to the Master's destination below.
- Redraw justification: The error changes consequential travel continuity in the page-owning image; accepting it would publish the central crossing in the wrong direction, which is substantial enough to risk replacing the complete page.
