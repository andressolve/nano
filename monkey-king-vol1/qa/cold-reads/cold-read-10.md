# Cold read: pages 1–10

## Retell of the story so far

A golden monkey is born when a stone cracks open on the uninhabited peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain. The mountain's other monkeys come to see him. Their elder, Old Ma, accepts him even though he has no mother and is unlike the brown monkeys around him. He stays, grows up, and becomes the fastest and loudest of them without knowing what he is.

The monkeys live near a waterfall whose source nobody knows. Their rule is that anyone who can pass through the falling water and return alive will become king. The golden monkey volunteers, leaps through, and discovers a dry, furnished stone home hidden behind the waterfall, with a bridge and enough room for the whole troop. He returns to tell the others, and because he completed the challenge, they acclaim him the Handsome Monkey King.

Later he asks Old Ma about age. She tells him that she has already seen three kings die and that monkeys, kings, and even mountains eventually die; only immortals do not. She has heard that immortals live far to the west, beyond the sea, in the human world. The Monkey King immediately resolves to find one, learn immortality, and bring the knowledge home so none of his monkeys will have to die.

He builds a raft and crosses the sea while Old Ma watches from shore. In the human world he learns to walk upright and wear a coat so people will not chase or stare at him. He questions old people until an old woman directs him to a gray mountain where a Master has taught students for forty years, though apparently not the thing the monkey seeks.

At the mountain, the Master first dismisses him. The monkey insists that he has crossed the sea to learn how not to die. When the Master learns that he came from a rock and has no family name, he gives him the name Sun Wukong: Sun because he is a monkey, and Wukong because he is the one who wakes to emptiness.

Wukong then spends seven years serving at the monastery. He sweeps, chops wood, carries water, sits through lessons, listens intently, and remembers what he hears, but he is never taught immortality. When the Master, now identified as Subhuti, asks what he has learned, Wukong says he learned everything except the one thing he came for. Subhuti declares that he has learned nothing and taps Wukong three times on the head. The other students laugh, but Wukong interprets the taps and Subhuti's posture as a private instruction: come at midnight through the back door.

## Cast

- **Sun Wukong / the Handsome Monkey King:** A small, youthful golden-brown monkey with amber eyes, a long tail, and a skirt or belt of green leaves. He is born from a rock, adopted into the mountain troop, becomes king by braving the waterfall, and leaves home to seek immortality for himself and his people. He is fearless, impulsive, persistent, observant, and clever enough to understand Subhuti's covert signal.
- **Old Ma:** A large, elderly gray monkey with a deeply lined face, white-gray hair, a wooden cane, and a ragged red shawl. She is the troop's elder and maternal authority. She welcomes the newborn monkey, explains the waterfall kingship rule, crowns him, teaches him that everything dies, and directs his quest with her story about immortals.
- **Master Subhuti:** A tall, grave elderly human with long white hair and beard, pale blue-white robes, and a long white whisk or staff. He leads the mountain school, initially refuses Wukong, names him, makes him serve and listen for seven years, and finally appears to invite him to a secret midnight lesson.
- **The mountain monkeys:** A community of mostly brown and gray monkeys of varied ages. They witness Wukong's arrival and waterfall feat, move into the hidden stone house, accept him as king, and remain the family he hopes to save from death.
- **Subhuti's students:** Shaven-headed human men in plain brown robes. They study and work at the mountain school. On page 10 they laugh when their teacher taps Wukong, apparently missing the message Wukong detects.
- **An unnamed old woman in the human world:** Mentioned in narration as the one person who takes Wukong's question seriously and points him toward the Master's mountain.

## Causal chain

1. A stone opens and produces a golden monkey, so the nearby troop investigates and Old Ma accepts him.
2. The troop's rule promises kingship to anyone who survives passing through the waterfall, so the bold young monkey takes the challenge.
3. He finds a safe stone home behind the falls and returns alive, so the troop moves in and makes him king.
4. As king, he asks Old Ma about great age; learning that all ordinary beings die makes mortality an urgent problem for him.
5. Old Ma says immortals may live west across the sea, so he leaves his kingdom, crosses the sea, and searches the human world.
6. An old woman points him to a long-established Master, so he climbs the gray mountain and asks to be taught immortality.
7. The Master accepts the rock-born monkey far enough to name him Sun Wukong, but gives him years of work and ordinary lessons instead of the knowledge he requested.
8. Wukong's persistence and attention let him recognize Subhuti's three taps as a hidden invitation, setting up a private midnight meeting.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

I did not need to backtrack or reread any page to follow the story, identify the main speakers, or understand why Wukong moved from one stage of the journey to the next.

On page 9, several images of the same golden monkey appear in one monastery scene. I took this as a time-lapse montage of Wukong doing different jobs across seven years, reinforced by the narration and the different seasons shown in the trees, rather than as several new golden-monkey characters. That interpretation was immediate and did not disrupt the story.

On page 10, I did not need prior knowledge of the phrase "third watch" because Wukong immediately interprets it as midnight. I assume that Subhuti deliberately disguised the invitation from the other students, but the next scene still needs to confirm that Wukong has decoded him correctly.

## What I expect next

I expect Wukong to follow his interpretation of the signal, enter through the back door at midnight, and meet Subhuti alone. Subhuti will probably reveal that the public rebuke was a test or concealment and begin teaching Wukong the supernatural discipline he came for, especially some means of escaping death. Because Wukong promised to save the whole mountain, I also expect eventual tension between what he learns for himself and whether he can truly carry it home to everyone.
