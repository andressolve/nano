## Retell of the story so far

A golden monkey is born when a stone splits open on uninhabited Flower-Fruit Mountain. The mountain's brown monkeys accept him, and their elder, Old Ma, treats him like one of their young. He grows faster and louder than the others without knowing what he is.

At a waterfall, Old Ma repeats the monkeys' rule: anyone who can pass through the water and return alive will be king. The golden monkey takes the challenge and discovers a dry stone home behind the falls, with enough space for everyone. The others keep their promise and acclaim him as the Handsome Monkey King.

Later he asks Old Ma what being old means. She explains that three kings before him have died and that everything dies except immortals. She has heard that immortals live far to the west, so the Monkey King decides to find one and learn how to keep himself and his monkeys from dying.

He crosses the sea on a raft, spends years learning enough about humans to travel among them, and is directed to an old Master on a gray mountain. The Master initially refuses him, but his claim to have come from a rock gets the Master's attention. The Master gives the nameless monkey the name Sun Wukong, meaning one who wakes to emptiness.

Wukong spends seven years doing chores and listening to lessons, asking annually to be taught how not to die. When the Master publicly asks what he has learned, Wukong admits he still lacks the one thing he came for. The Master taps him three times and leaves; Wukong correctly deciphers this as an invitation to come secretly through the back door at the third watch. The Master says Wukong is the first student in forty years to understand the hint and privately teaches him transformation and cloud travel. These make him extremely difficult to kill, which is the immortality he sought. The Master warns him not to show off or reveal their relationship.

Wukong does show off, changing into a pine tree for the other students. The Master expels him exactly as warned and orders him never to claim he was the Master's student. Wukong returns to Flower-Fruit Mountain after ten years and a season away. Old Ma immediately recognizes from his manner and expression that he was thrown out. He says he will make a name of his own.

Deciding that a king needs a proper weapon, Wukong goes beneath the eastern sea to the Dragon King's palace. Ordinary weapons are too light for him, so the Dragon King points him toward an ancient pillar that anchors the sea. The pillar responds to Wukong, shrinking at his command into a staff he can wield. Alarmed by both Wukong's power and the loss of the sea's anchor, the Dragon King gives him armor, cloud-walking boots, and a feathered cap, then reports the theft and Wukong's claim of invulnerability to the Jade Emperor in Heaven.

The Jade Emperor first wants soldiers sent after Wukong. Laozi advises that giving the monkey a minor heavenly job and title will cost less than a military campaign. The Jade Emperor authorizes Laozi to recruit him. Laozi arrives at Flower-Fruit Mountain and offers Wukong a post. Wukong is thrilled that Heaven has heard of him, while Old Ma reminds him that his real promise was to teach the monkeys not to die. He brushes that aside, says he will return as a lord, and leaves despite her plea that he come back because his return—not his rank—is what she wants.

## Cast

- **Sun Wukong / the Handsome Monkey King:** A slim golden-brown monkey with amber eyes, a long tail, and a skirt of green leaves. He is born from a split stone, becomes king by courage, and is driven by fear of death and a need to matter. After visiting the Dragon King, he carries a red-and-gold staff and wears ornate gold armor, cloud-walking boots, a red scarf, and a feathered cap. He is brave, quick-witted, immensely strong, boastful, and impatient with limits.

- **Old Ma:** A large, elderly gray monkey with white facial hair, a walking stick, and a ragged red shawl. She is the mountain troop's elder and Wukong's parental figure, historian, conscience, and emotional home. She tells him about death and immortals, then challenges his growing vanity when Heaven calls.

- **The Master:** A tall, elderly human man with white hair tied up, a long white beard, pale robes, and a fly whisk. He runs the mountain school. He recognizes Wukong's ability, names him, secretly teaches him transformation and cloud travel, and expels him when he breaks the rule against showing off.

- **The other students:** Shaven-headed human men in plain brown robes. They study under the Master, fail to understand his coded taps, and applaud Wukong's forbidden transformation, helping tempt him into the boast that gets him expelled.

- **The monkeys of Flower-Fruit Mountain:** A troop of mostly brown monkeys of varied ages. They are Wukong's people and the intended beneficiaries of his search for immortality. They accept his kingship after he finds the home behind the waterfall.

- **The Dragon King of the Eastern Sea:** A huge green-blue dragon-bodied ruler with antler-like coral horns, long whiskers, jeweled dark-green robes, and an underwater court guarded by armored sea creatures. He first indulges Wukong as a presumptuous neighbor, then becomes frightened when Wukong takes the sea-anchoring pillar and reports him to Heaven.

- **The Jade Emperor:** A broad, bearded human ruler in elaborate gold robes and a hanging-bead crown, seated in a vast golden heavenly court. He responds to the Dragon King's complaint and is ready to use force until Laozi suggests containment through office and status.

- **Laozi:** An elderly human man with long white hair, a small beard, blue robes, and a gourd at his waist. He is the Jade Emperor's pragmatic adviser and envoy. He proposes neutralizing Wukong cheaply with a minor heavenly appointment and personally delivers the offer.

## Causal chain

The stone opens, creating a monkey with no known family or identity. Old Ma and the troop accept him. The waterfall challenge gives him a chance to prove himself; his successful crossing wins him the kingship. Learning from Old Ma that kings die gives him a concrete fear and mission. That mission sends him west to find an immortal teacher. His persistence and ability to understand the Master's hidden signal earn him secret instruction. The new powers make him difficult to kill, but his desire for recognition leads him to show off, which causes his expulsion. Back home, his desire to look and act like a king sends him to the Dragon King for a weapon. His exceptional strength lets him take the pillar that anchors the sea, and his demand for royal gear further humiliates the Dragon King. The Dragon King's report prompts Heaven to act. Laozi turns the Jade Emperor away from war and toward co-opting Wukong with status. Wukong's hunger to be recognized makes the offer work immediately, while his departure leaves his original promise to Old Ma and the troop unfulfilled.

## What I expect next

I expect Wukong to accept a heavenly title that sounds grand to him but is actually a low-status or ceremonial job designed to keep him occupied. His pride will eventually discover the insult. Because he has left before fulfilling his promise to protect the mountain monkeys from death, I expect conflict between the recognition he wants from Heaven and the responsibility Old Ma says he owes his own people. I also expect the missing sea anchor, the Dragon King's complaint, and Wukong's staff to keep producing consequences rather than being settled by the appointment.
