## Page intent

## PAGE 33

Dramatic. Laozi's laboratory: a round room of black iron and cinnabar red, shelves of gourds and jars, the bronze furnace taller than a man at the center. Wukong sways in the doorway with his jar, recognizes the peach man's place, and goes looking. The dominant turn is the five gourds on the highest shelf, unstoppered, gold pills pouring into his palm like sweets, his face lit gold from below, eating them by the handful and planning to take some home to Old Ma. Then the five gourds lie empty on the floor and he sits among them stone sober, eyes clear, looking at his own hands, which give off a faint light: those were not sweets, that was the elixir, all five, and he feels wonderful. Wukong owns the page alone; Laozi is not here. The reader turns because he ate five hundred years of Laozi's work by accident, and it worked. The furnace is scenery and stays subordinate.
