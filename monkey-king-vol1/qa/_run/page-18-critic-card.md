## Page intent

## PAGE 18

Dramatic. Wukong is back before the throne with the staff full-size over his shoulder, and now he wants armor to go with it: gold, a plumed cap, boots that stick to clouds. Ao Guang asks who he should say took it, and Wukong gives his name and his claim: he came out of a stone and cannot be killed. Then the reader sees him armored for the first time, gold scale over red, tiger-skin, cloud-boots, two long plumes, turning in the water to admire himself in a crystal column, delighted. The page belongs to Wukong's joy in his new self. The dominant turn is the armor: the Monkey King now looks like one. What follows is aftermath: the hall empty, Ao Guang alone at a shell desk writing to Heaven, drily noting that the sea is still up, and sending the monkey's name upward for the first time. The guards retrieving the dropped sword stay subordinate.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 18 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Ao Guang's hall.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Wukong back before the throne, left, the staff full-size
over his shoulder, taller than he is, red iron with gold bands. He is pointing
at the armor on the guards.

> **WUKONG:** "A staff like this needs armor. Gold. A cap with feathers. Boots that stick to clouds."
>
> **AO GUANG:** "And who do I say took it?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Sun Wukong. Say I came out of a stone, and I can't be killed."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Wukong armored for the first time: golden scale
armor over a red tunic, a tiger-skin kilt, black cloud-boots, and a cap with
two long pheasant plumes curving back over his head. He is turning in the
water to see himself in a crystal column. The guards have backed to the walls.

> **WUKONG:** "Ha! Look at him. Now that is a king."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The hall empty. Ao Guang alone at a shell desk, right,
writing with a fish-bone pen, whiskers drooping, one eye turned up toward the
water overhead. Guards picking up the dropped sword.

> **AO GUANG:** "And the sea is still up. It seems it was holding itself all along, and nobody told me."
>
> **AO GUANG:** "To the Jade Emperor in Heaven. A monkey called Sun Wukong, of Flower-Fruit Mountain, has taken the anchor of the Eastern Sea."
>
> **AO GUANG:** "He says he came out of a stone and cannot be killed. I am too old to find out."

**Why you turn the page:** a letter is going up to Heaven with the monkey's name on it, and it is the first time Heaven has heard of him.
