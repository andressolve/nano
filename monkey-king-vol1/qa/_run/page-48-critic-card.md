## Page intent

## PAGE 48

Illustrated prose. Dominant turn: five hundred years; snow; Old Ma's peach; someone will come. Winter, the five ridges under snow, the monkey's face in the cleft, fur frosted, eyes open and still gold. Climbing the last of the slope on her stick, a small gray monkey in a red shawl with a peach in her free hand. Across the water, the green peak, the split stone, and the banner gone pale pink. The prose carries the years: the mountain did not move and neither could he; he could see his own peak and the banner fading; he could not reach the needle behind his ear. Every year an old gray monkey climbed up with a peach and said the Buddha's words back to him, for as many years as she had, and after her a younger monkey who had been told to. Someone will come. Wait. The reader closes the book on a monkey waiting, for the first time, for someone.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 48 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Mountain of Five Elements.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Winter. The five ridges under snow. The cleft at
the base with the monkey's face in it, fur frosted, eyes open and still gold.
Climbing the last of the slope on a stick, a small gray monkey in a red shawl,
a peach in her free hand. Across the water, the green peak, the split stone,
and the banner gone pale pink.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the snow, never over either face)*
> "The mountain was called the Mountain of Five Elements. It did not move, and neither could he.
>
> Winter came, then spring, then winter. He could turn his head and see his own peak across the water, and the banner on it going pale. He could not reach the needle behind his ear. He learned what a year was, and how slowly five hundred of them go.
>
> Every year, when the peaches came in, an old gray monkey climbed up and set one on the rock where he could reach it, and sat with him a while, and said the Buddha's words back to him, the ones he had told her the first winter. She did that for as many years as she had. After that a younger monkey did it, who had been told to.
>
> Someone will come. Wait."

**Why you turn the page:** there is no next page. Somebody is going to come, and he is waiting, and this time for someone.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 48 — card

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth, no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — the last image owns the page
One image dominates: the snowed mountain, the face in the cleft with gold eyes open, the old gray monkey climbing with a peach. Illustrated-prose mode rendered, one field over plain snow. Blocking if the field covers a face or the peach.

### C3 — two monkeys, never one
The face in the cleft is the young gold monkey with gold eyes and frosted fur; the climber is the old gray, stooped, white-muzzled monkey in the red shawl with her stick. The reader could never take one for the other. Tolerance: her face partly hidden if stoop, stick, gray fur, and white muzzle carry her.

### C4 — the peach and the seal
She carries a peach; the gold seal is on the summit; the split stone and a pale-pink banner are visible across the water. Tolerance: the exact pink, so long as it reads faded, not fresh red.

### C5 — winter
Snow and cold, the hand-mountain bare under it, the far peak still green. Tolerance: how much snow.

### C6 — no speech
No balloon, no tail fragment, no text besides the prose field.

### C7 — focal integrity
Both faces and Old Ma's hand with the peach are whole.
