## Page intent

## PAGE 1

Spectacle. Sunrise over an eastern sea, and a green mountain rising out of it, fruit trees down its flanks, a waterfall throwing itself off a cliff. At the bare summit an egg-shaped boulder taller than a man has split down the middle, and light is coming out of the split. A small gold figure is climbing out, one hand on the lip of the stone, looking straight out of the page at the reader. One caption over the sky says where we are and that nobody lives here. The dominant turn is the stone splitting and a monkey with light in his eyes. Wukong owns the page and does not yet know he is anyone. The landscape is vast and the figure small, but the eyes find you. The reader turns because something just climbed out of a rock and it is looking at them. The mountain's beauty stays subordinate to that gaze; nothing else alive.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, tail-free, over the sky, and no other text of any kind appears. A caption that does not read, or any stray word or second string, is blocking; a caption that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as one single image: a split stone on a bare summit with light coming out of it, and a small gold figure climbing out and looking straight at the reader. If the eye does not go to the split and then to the gaze, or the page breaks into panels or a sample sheet, the spectacle has failed.

### C3 — Wukong is not himself
The figure must be a gold monkey, young, bare but for leaves, with round amber eyes, not a human child, a gray monkey, or an armored figure. An odd pose or a partly hidden body is tolerable so long as the face and gold fur read.

### C4 — The stone is not split, or someone else is here
The boulder must be one egg-shaped stone broken open down its middle with light in the break, plainly a rock and not a cave or rubble; and no other monkey, animal, or person may be visible. Exact shape and light colour are nonblocking; a second figure that reads as a figure is not.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A doubled figure, a face without eyes, a hand fused into the stone, or corruption where the eye lands blocks. Texture faults in sea, trees, or mist do not.
