## Page intent

## PAGE 25

Dramatic. Home on the peak. Wukong lands hard and tells Old Ma the truth — lord of horses, of nothing, they laughed at him quietly, which is worse. The dominant turn is the banner: he paints huge characters on red cloth while monkeys hold it flat and a pole goes up beside the split stone, and the words are readable — GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN. He asks Old Ma what you call someone Heaven cannot order around; she says there is no word for it; he says there is now. Then the banner flies over the peak and the troop shouts under it, Old Ma sitting beneath the pole with her knees up, saying they will come for that. He wants them to. What changes: he has written himself a title Heaven never gave him, in letters Heaven can read from the sky. The reader turns to see who comes. Subordinate: the crowd, the pole, the brush.

### C2 — the banner owns the page
One image owns the page: the monkey painting the banner while monkeys hold it flat and the pole goes up beside the split stone, and its words are plainly readable as the center of the picture. A page owned by the landing or the flying banner instead, or with no owner, is blocking.

### C3 — order and attribution across the two monkeys

"They will come for that, you know." reads before "Good. Let them see it on the way." Any balloon that could belong to either monkey is blocking.

### C4 — the two monkeys
Wukong golden, young, armored, plumed; Old Ma gray, white-muzzled, shawled, on her stick, sitting knees-up at the end. Confusion between them is blocking.

### C5 — state at the end
The last image shows the banner flying from its pole over the peak with the troop under it, and the split stone still split. A banner never raised, or a stone made whole, is blocking.

### C6 — focal integrity
The two faces, the painting hand, and the banner lettering whole. Background monkeys with repeated faces are tolerated.
