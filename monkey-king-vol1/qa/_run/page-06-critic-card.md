## Page intent

## PAGE 6

Dramatic. Night, the feast over, a fire in the stone hearth, fruit skins everywhere, monkeys asleep in heaps. Wukong on his stool, laughing at nothing, asks Old Ma how old she is. She has seen three kings before him, and none went through the water. His face changes as he asks where they are, and she tells him without looking at him: kings die too, only the immortals don't. He wants to know who they are and where. Stories put them far to the west, over the sea, in the world of men; she never met one. He stands on the stool, eyes wet, fists shut, and says he will find one and make him teach him, so none of them has to die. The dominant turn is kings die too, his tears, and his decision to go. Old Ma owns the truth; Wukong owns the answer. The reader turns because he has just found out that he ends, and his answer is to leave. The fire and the sleepers stay subordinate to her not looking at him.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave, the feast.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Night. A fire in the stone hearth, fruit skins everywhere,
monkeys asleep in heaps. Wukong on his stool, left, laughing at nothing. Old
Ma beside him, right, knees up, looking at the fire.

> **WUKONG:** "How old are you, Old Ma? How old is very?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Old enough that I have seen three kings before you. None of them went through the water. They just shouted the loudest."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** His face changing. She is not looking at him.

> **WUKONG:** "Three kings. Where are they?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Where everything goes. Kings die too, little one. Monkeys, kings, mountains eventually. It is only the immortals who don't."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Who are they? Where?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong standing up on the stool, eyes wet, fists shut.
Monkeys waking.

> **OLD MA:** "Stories say: far to the west, over the sea, in the world of men. I never met one."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Then I'm going to. I'll find one, and I'll make him teach me, and none of us has to die."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just found out that he ends, and his answer is to leave.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 6 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All seven strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right monkey's mouth, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as a quiet night by the fire, then Wukong's face changing as Old Ma tells him kings die while she looks at the fire and not at him, then Wukong standing on the stool, eyes wet, fists shut, saying he will go. The middle moment owns the page by eye. If the vow reads before the truth, or nothing dominates, the page fails.

### C3 — Attribution and order
Wukong's "How old are you, Old Ma?" reads before Old Ma's "Old enough". In the dominant moment Wukong's "Three kings. Where are they?" reads first, Old Ma's "Where everything goes." second, Wukong's "Who are they? Where?" clearly third and his. Old Ma's "Stories say:" must be readable before Wukong's "Then I'm going to." A balloon readable as the other's is blocking.

### C4 — Wukong and Old Ma collide, or she looks at him
Wukong gold, young, round amber eyes wet by the end; Old Ma gray, white-muzzled, stooped, knees up, shawl on, eyes on the fire. If she is looking at him in the dominant moment, or the two faces cannot be told apart in profile and firelight, the page fails.

### C5 — Someone else appears
No immortal, old man, or figure from the world of men may appear; sleepers stay brown, bare, and balloonless. A gold, gray, or shawled crowd monkey blocks; vague heaps of sleepers do not.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Doubled named figures, a speaking mouth hidden, fused hands, or corruption on either face in the dominant moment block. Faults in fruit skins, hearth, or sleepers do not.
