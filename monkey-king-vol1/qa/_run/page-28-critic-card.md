## Page intent

## PAGE 28

Dramatic. Laozi alone on his small cloud under the flying banner, reading a gold scroll to the whole troop: Sun Wukong is named Great Sage Equal to Heaven, with a house in Heaven and no duties — the monkey's own words, read off his banner. Wukong sits on the split stone, arms folded, staff across his knees, looking at the scroll. The dominant turn is the reading itself: Heaven saying his name the way he wrote it. Old Ma asks the sane question — you beat them, you have a mountain and a name, why go back up? His answer is the whole character: because they said it, and he wants to hear them say it up there. He steps onto the cloud; her hand is flat on his head; she says the two words again. What changes: he is going back to the people who laughed at him. The reader turns on "Come back" and "I always do."

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Laozi alone on his small cloud under the banner,
right, reading from a gold scroll to the whole troop. Wukong on the split
stone, left, arms folded, the staff across his knees. Old Ma below him.

> **LAOZI:** "By order of the Jade Emperor: Sun Wukong is named Great Sage Equal to Heaven, with a house in Heaven and no duties."
>
> **LAOZI:** "Those are your words, Great Sage. I read them off your banner. Heaven agrees to them."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Old Ma looking up at him. He is looking at the scroll.

> **OLD MA:** "You beat them. You have a mountain and a name. Why go back up?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Because they said it. Heaven said my name, the way I wrote it. I want to hear them say it up there."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong stepping onto Laozi's cloud. Old Ma's hand flat on
his head. His plumes going up.

> **OLD MA:** "Come back."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I always do."

**Why you turn the page:** he is going back up to the people who laughed at him, because they said his name, and Old Ma said the two words again.

### C2 — the reading owns the page
One image owns the page: Laozi on his cloud under the flying banner reading the scroll to the troop, Wukong on the split stone with the staff across his knees, Old Ma below. A page owned by the parting instead, or with no owner, is blocking.

### C3 — order and attribution
Laozi's two lines read first and are his. Old Ma's "Why go back up?" reads before Wukong's "Because they said it…". "Come back." is Old Ma's, "I always do." is Wukong's, and they read last in that order. A balloon that could belong to either monkey is blocking.

### C4 — the three named faces
Wukong young and golden in armor and plumes; Old Ma gray, white-muzzled, shawl, stick; Laozi round, bald, square white beard, indigo, gourd. Confusion within the two monkeys, or Laozi read as the Master, is blocking.

### C5 — state and the parting
The banner flies on its pole carrying the same five words Wukong painted, GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN, legible; the split stone is split; the last image has Old Ma's hand flat on Wukong's head as he steps onto the cloud. Transcribe the banner letter by letter as painted, never as expected: a banner with a crest, a blank cloth, a dropped or added word (GREAT SAGE EQUAL HEAVEN is wrong), or different words, a missing banner, or a missing hand-on-head is blocking: Laozi says he read the words off it.

### C6 — focal integrity
