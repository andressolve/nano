# Exact script — pages 1–10

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 1 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Sunrise over an eastern sea. A green mountain
rising out of it, fruit trees down its flanks, a waterfall throwing itself off
a cliff. At the bare stone summit, an egg-shaped boulder taller than a man has
split down the middle, and light is coming out of the split. A small figure,
gold, is climbing out of it, one hand on the lip of the stone, looking straight
out of the page.

> **CAPTION** *(upper left, over the sky)*
> "On the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain, where nobody lived, a stone cracked open."

**Why you turn the page:** something just climbed out of a rock and it is looking at you.

---

## PAGE 2 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the slopes of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** A small gold monkey in a crowd of brown ones,
under a fruit tree. Old Ma, gray, stooped, in her faded red shawl, has her hand
flat on top of his head. He is looking up at her. The birds have left the tree.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the plain ground below the figures, never over faces)*
> "He was a monkey, more or less. He was gold where the others were brown, and his eyes gave off a light that made the birds leave the tree. He had no mother. He had a rock, and the rock was open now and would not close.
>
> The monkeys of the mountain came to look. The eldest, Old Ma, put her hand flat on his head, the way she did with the new ones, and left it there a while.
>
> 'Well,' she said. 'You're here.'
>
> He stayed. He grew. He was faster than all of them, and louder, and he did not know what he was, and for a long time nobody minded."

**Why you turn the page:** he does not know what he is, and neither do you.

---

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the pool below the waterfall.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A hot day. The waterfall roaring into its pool,
monkeys crowded on every rock. OLD MA on the highest rock, left, on her stick,
shawl over her head against the sun. WUKONG at the front of the crowd, right,
gold among brown, one foot up on a boulder, staring at the water.

> **OLD MA:** "Nobody knows where the water comes from. It has been falling since before I was born, and I am very old."
>
> **WUKONG:** "So somebody should go and look."
>
> **OLD MA:** "Somebody should. Whoever goes through and comes back alive, we make king. That was always the rule. Nobody has ever done it."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The monkeys looking at each other. Nobody moves. Wukong has
a flat hand up over his eyes, looking at the white wall of water.

> **WUKONG:** "King. Of all of you?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Of all of us."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** A gold streak going into the white water. The crowd shrieking.

> **WUKONG:** "I'll go!"
>
> **SOUND** *(in the water)*
> "SPLASSSH"

**Why you turn the page:** he went into the water before anyone could tell him not to, and you want to see what is behind it.

---

## PAGE 4 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** From inside: the waterfall is a wall of silver
on the left, seen from behind. An iron bridge crosses the pool. Beyond it, a
house of stone cut into the mountain, with stone beds, stone bowls, stone
stools, a stone hearth, all dry, all empty, big enough for hundreds. Wukong is
tiny on the bridge, dripping, mouth open.

> **CAPTION** *(lower right, over the dry stone floor)*
> "Behind the water: a bridge, a house of stone, and room for everyone."

**Why you turn the page:** there is a whole home behind the waterfall and nobody has ever lived in it.

---

## PAGE 5 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Outside: Wukong bursting back out through the water onto
the rocks, drenched, monkeys scattering from the splash. He is shouting before
he has landed.

> **WUKONG:** "There's a house! Stone beds, stone bowls, a bridge. Dry as a bone. Room for every one of you!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Inside: the whole troop pouring across the iron
bridge, monkeys bouncing on stone beds and trying the bowls on their heads.
Old Ma crossing the bridge slowly on her stick. Wukong already up on the
tallest stone stool, right, arms out.

> **OLD MA:** "You went through. You came back. A rule is a rule."
>
> **OLD MA:** "Bow your head, or whatever it is kings do."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Old Ma's hand flat on his head. Monkeys cheering all round.

> **OLD MA:** "Handsome Monkey King. May you never get tired of it."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I won't! I won't ever!"
>
> **SOUND** *(the crowd)*
> "HOOOO!"

**Why you turn the page:** he is king of everything he can see, and it is the happiest anyone in this book will ever be.

---

## PAGE 6 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Water Curtain Cave, the feast.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Night. A fire in the stone hearth, fruit skins everywhere,
monkeys asleep in heaps. Wukong on his stool, left, laughing at nothing. Old
Ma beside him, right, knees up, looking at the fire.

> **WUKONG:** "How old are you, Old Ma? How old is very?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Old enough that I have seen three kings before you. None of them went through the water. They just shouted the loudest."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** His face changing. She is not looking at him.

> **WUKONG:** "Three kings. Where are they?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Where everything goes. Kings die too, little one. Monkeys, kings, mountains eventually. It is only the immortals who don't."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Who are they? Where?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong standing up on the stool, eyes wet, fists shut.
Monkeys waking.

> **OLD MA:** "Stories say: far to the west, over the sea, in the world of men. I never met one."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Then I'm going to. I'll find one, and I'll make him teach me, and none of us has to die."

**Why you turn the page:** he has just found out that he ends, and his answer is to leave.

---

## PAGE 7 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** two — the sea, and the Master's mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A raft of pine logs on an enormous gray-green
sea, one small figure standing on it, not looking back. Far behind, the green
mountain going small.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** A monkey in a man's coat that is too big for him, walking a
dirt road on two legs, toward a gray mountain with a single pine on it.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the flat sea, never over the raft)*
> "He built a raft out of a dead pine and tied it with vines, and Old Ma stood on the shore and did not tell him to stay, because she had been the one who told him about immortals, and she knew it.
>
> The sea took him west for a season. The world of men took him for three years. He learned to walk on two legs so they would not chase him, and to wear a coat so they would not stare, and he asked every old person he met the same question. Most of them laughed.
>
> One did not. She pointed at a gray mountain with a single pine, and said: the Master lives there. He has had students for forty years, and he has never once taught any of them what you are asking for."

**Why you turn the page:** the Master has never taught anyone the one thing the monkey came for, and the monkey has never in his life taken no.

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard of the Cave of the Slanting Moon.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Dawn. White gravel raked in lines, a single
pine, a cave mouth in a gray cliff. SUBHUTI on the cave step, right: tall,
lean, white beard in a point, hands folded in his sleeves. Students with
shaved heads and brown robes have stopped sweeping to stare. WUKONG at the
gate, left, the coat gone, tail out, one foot up on the gatepost.

> **SUBHUTI:** "I do not teach what you came for. Nobody learns it here. Go home to your parents."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I don't have any. I came out of a rock, over the sea. It's still there if you want to see it."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Subhuti's face. Hooded eyes. The whisk in his hand, still.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Out of a rock."
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "And what do you want from me, rock's child?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "To not die. Old Ma says immortals don't. Teach me that and I'll sweep your whole mountain."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Subhuti turning away toward the cave, speaking over his
shoulder. A student holding out a broom to Wukong.

> **SUBHUTI:** "You have no name. A thing that wants to live forever needs one. Sun, for monkey. Wukong: the one who wakes to emptiness."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Sun Wukong. Sun Wukong! I have a name!"

**Why you turn the page:** nobody ever gave him anything before, and the first thing anyone gave him was a name.

---

## PAGE 9 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Cave of the Slanting Moon.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The courtyard through four seasons in one image,
left to right: blossom, heat, leaves, snow. In each, the same small gold figure
with a broom. In the last, he sits at the back of a row of students with the
broom across his knees, leaning forward.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the white gravel, never over the figures)*
> "He swept for seven years.
>
> He swept the gravel and the steps and the roof, and chopped the wood and carried the water, and at the lessons he sat at the back with the broom across his knees, and listened so hard his ears hurt. The Master talked about the way things are, and the students wrote it down, and the monkey did not write. He remembered instead, which turned out to be better.
>
> He did not learn how not to die. He asked, once a year, on the day he arrived. The Master said: not yet. Seven times."

**Why you turn the page:** seven years of not yet, and the reader wants to know what yes looks like.

---

## PAGE 10 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard, at lessons.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Students seated in rows on the gravel. Subhuti on the step,
right, whisk in hand. Wukong at the back, left, bouncing on his heels.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Wukong. Seven years. What have you learned?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Everything you said, Master. Every word. But not the thing I came for."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Subhuti has walked down between the rows and
stands over him. The handle of the whisk comes down on the top of the monkey's
head, three times. The students are laughing behind their hands. Behind
Subhuti, in the cliff, a small round door, shut.

> **SOUND** *(on the monkey's head)*
> "TAP TAP TAP"
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "Then you have learned nothing."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Subhuti walking away into the cave with his hands clasped
behind his back. The students howling. Wukong sitting very still, one hand on
his head, eyes wide, and a small smile starting.

> **WUKONG:** "Three taps. Hands behind his back. He means: midnight, the third watch, and come in by the back door."

**Why you turn the page:** everyone laughed at the monkey, and the monkey was the only one who got the joke.
