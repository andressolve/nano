## Page intent

## PAGE 8

Dramatic. Dawn in the Master's courtyard: white raked gravel, one pine, a cave mouth in a gray cliff, and a small bare monkey at the gate who has walked here from the far side of the sea. Subhuti, on the cave step, refuses him before he has asked and tells him to go home to his parents. The monkey has none. He came out of a rock, and he says so, flatly, as a fact anyone could go and check. That answer turns the page's weather: the Master's hooded eyes stop and consider. Asked what he wants, the monkey says the true thing, to not die, and offers to sweep the whole mountain for it. Subhuti turns away and over his shoulder gives him what nobody ever has: a name, Sun Wukong. The dominant turn is the rock-born answer and the naming. The staring students stay subordinate. The reader turns because the first gift of his life was his name.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard of the Cave of the Slanting Moon.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Dawn. White gravel raked in lines, a single
pine, a cave mouth in a gray cliff. SUBHUTI on the cave step, right: tall,
lean, white beard in a point, hands folded in his sleeves. Students with
shaved heads and brown robes have stopped sweeping to stare. WUKONG at the
gate, left, the coat gone, tail out, one foot up on the gatepost.

> **SUBHUTI:** "I do not teach what you came for. Nobody learns it here. Go home to your parents."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I don't have any. I came out of a rock, over the sea. It's still there if you want to see it."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Subhuti's face. Hooded eyes. The whisk in his hand, still.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Out of a rock."
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "And what do you want from me, rock's child?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "To not die. Old Ma says immortals don't. Teach me that and I'll sweep your whole mountain."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Subhuti turning away toward the cave, speaking over his
shoulder. A student holding out a broom to Wukong.

> **SUBHUTI:** "You have no name. A thing that wants to live forever needs one. Sun, for monkey. Wukong: the one who wakes to emptiness."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Sun Wukong. Sun Wukong! I have a name!"

**Why you turn the page:** nobody ever gave him anything before, and the first thing anyone gave him was a name.

### C1 — Transcription
Every one of the seven listed strings reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth, with no extra text anywhere. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The gate owns the page
One image plainly carries the page: the monkey at the gate, the Master on the step, the students frozen between them; the page reads as a dramatic exchange, not a landscape with figures. Tolerance: an unequal but readable hierarchy passes.

### C3 — Order of the exchange
"Go home to your parents." must be readable before "I came out of a rock"; "Out of a rock." and "rock's child?" both before "To not die."; the naming line before "Sun Wukong. Sun Wukong! I have a name!" Tolerance: awkward tails pass if order and ownership are never in doubt.

### C4 — Two identities
Wukong is a small bare gold monkey with a tail and a leaf kilt, no coat. Subhuti is a tall lean old man with a long pointed white beard, a topknot, and a pale robe, not round, bald, or stout. Tolerance: minor drift that cannot be read as another character passes.

### C5 — Students are scenery
The students are shaved, brown-robed, young, beardless, and silent; none carries a balloon, whisk, beard, or pale robe. Tolerance: repeated or indistinct background faces pass.

### C6 — Focal integrity
Both speakers' faces are whole, and Subhuti's close face is a coherent hooded-eyed human face. Tolerance: gravel, pine, and broom detail are not graded.
