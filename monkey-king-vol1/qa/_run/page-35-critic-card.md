## Page intent

## PAGE 35

Dramatic. The sky over Flower-Fruit Mountain, storm-lit, Heaven's army hanging in a ring of cloud around the peak and not attacking. In front of it, alone on a cloud, stands the one person Laozi said would enjoy this: Erlang, young, in silver armor with a single red plume, an eye open in the middle of his forehead, a lance upright, a lean gray hound at his heel. Wukong waits on the peak under his banner with the staff out. The page belongs to their meeting. Erlang introduces himself like an equal and asks whether the banner is true; Wukong asks which eye to hit first, and Erlang laughs, really laughs, because nobody has ever asked him that. Then lance and staff meet, the shock throws the cloud outward, and Erlang says "Good." The dominant turn: Erlang introduces himself; the fight begins. The army stays scenery.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 35 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the sky over Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Storm light. The army hanging in a ring in the
clouds around the peak, not attacking. In front of it, alone on a cloud,
right, ERLANG: a young man in silver armor with a single red plume standing

> **ERLANG:** "Sun Wukong. Great Sage Equal to Heaven, it says up there. I'm Erlang. The Emperor's nephew. I came to see if the banner's true."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Three eyes. Which one do I hit first?"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Erlang laughing, really laughing. The third eye narrowing
while the other two stay still.

> **ERLANG:** "Nobody has ever asked me that. Everyone else just runs."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I don't run. I've got nowhere to run to. This is it."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The lance and the staff meeting. The shock of it throwing
the ring of cloud outward. The hound flattening itself.

> **SOUND** *(the strike)*
> "KRANNNG"
>
> **ERLANG:** "Good."

**Why you turn the page:** somebody finally spoke to him like an equal, and then hit him, and he liked both.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 35 — card

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth, with no extra text anywhere on the page, including no readable characters on the banner. A string that does not read, is missing, doubled, or in the wrong mouth is blocking. A string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — the meeting owns the page
One image plainly owns the page by eye: Erlang alone on his cloud facing Wukong on the peak, the army a ring of scenery behind them, and the page reads as dramatic. A page where the army or the strike is the largest event, or where equal bands compete, blocks.

### C3 — order of the two exchanges
Erlang's "Sun Wukong ..." must be readable before Wukong's "Three eyes ...", and Erlang's "Nobody has ever asked me that ..." before Wukong's "I don't run ...". "Good." is Erlang's, after the strike. A reply readable before its prompt, or a balloon that could belong to either, blocks.

### C4 — Erlang is not Wukong
Erlang reads as a young human man with a vertical third eye, one standing red plume, silver armor, and a three-pointed lance; Wukong as a small golden monkey in gold armor with two back-curving plumes and a straight red staff. Fur, muzzle, or tail on Erlang, or a third eye or single plume on Wukong, blocks. A soft miss on the third eye narrowing in the laugh does not.

### C5 — object state and focal integrity
The staff is full length and never a lance or needle; the banner flies; the split stone is split; the hound is a plain gray dog with no balloon. Both speakers' faces and the hands on the two weapons are whole. A staff become a lance, a hound with speech, or a mangled focal face or hand blocks; crude faces among distant soldiers do not.
