## Page intent

## PAGE 9

Illustrated prose. Years pass in one image: the same courtyard in blossom, heat, falling leaves, and snow, and in every season the same small gold figure with a broom. In the last he sits at the back of a row of students with the broom across his knees, leaning forward, listening. The prose carries the weight: he swept for seven years, chopped and carried and sat at the back, and listened so hard his ears hurt; the students wrote and he did not, and remembering turned out to be better. He did not learn the thing he came for. Once a year he asked, and the Master said not yet. The dominant turn is the years of the broom and the monkey learning to listen. Nothing dramatic happens and nothing should; the page is patient by design. Subhuti, if seen, is small and far. The reader turns because seven times not yet makes them want to see what yes looks like.

## PAGE 9 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Cave of the Slanting Moon.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** The courtyard through four seasons in one image,
left to right: blossom, heat, leaves, snow. In each, the same small gold figure

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the white gravel, never over the figures)*
> "He swept for seven years.
>
> He swept the gravel and the steps and the roof, and chopped the wood and carried the water, and at the lessons he sat at the back with the broom across his knees, and listened so hard his ears hurt. The Master talked about the way things are, and the students wrote it down, and the monkey did not write. He remembered instead, which turned out to be better.
>
> He did not learn how not to die. He asked, once a year, on the day he arrived. The Master said: not yet. Seven times."

**Why you turn the page:** seven years of not yet, and the reader wants to know what yes looks like.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 9 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The three prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, as one prose field, with no balloons and no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — Illustrated prose, rendered
The page reads as one illustration with a prose field, not a strip of panels with balloons. The field sits on plain ground and covers no figure, face, pine, or cliff. Tolerance: a field touching empty gravel or sky passes.

### C3 — Four seasons, one monkey
The same courtyard passes through blossom, heat, leaves, and snow, left to right, with the same small gold monkey and broom in each; in the last he sits at the back of a row of students, leaning forward. Tolerance: seasons that blend at their edges pass if time still reads.

### C4 — Identity at small scale
Every repeat is recognizably Wukong: gold fur, tail, leaf kilt, no coat. Subhuti, where present, is a distant tall bearded man in a pale robe, not a student. Tolerance: figures that are small but readable as themselves pass.

### C5 — Silence
Nobody speaks; there is no balloon or tail fragment anywhere. Tolerance: none for balloons; background gesture passes.

### C6 — Focal integrity
The monkey figures are whole and the broom reads as a broom in the largest repeat. Tolerance: crowd faces, tablets, and roof detail are not graded.
