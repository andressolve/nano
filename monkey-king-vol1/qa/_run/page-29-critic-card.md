## Page intent

## PAGE 29

Illustrated prose. A gold-roofed mansion on a cloud in Heaven, and Wukong sitting on the roof ridge with the staff across his knees, plumes drooping, looking out at the palaces going up forever; far away, terraces of peach blossom on a hill. The dominant turn is boredom and the handover: they gave him the words and a house and nothing to do in it, nobody visits, nobody sends for him, and winning feels like the stable with a better roof. Then the Emperor, wanting him busy, finds him a duty — a garden of peach trees to guard. The prose field owns the page; its last sentence is the trap: it was, Laozi did not say, a garden full of peaches, and he was a monkey. What changes: a monkey has been put in charge of the peaches, and Laozi knew. The reader turns to watch it happen. Subordinate: the architecture of the mansion, the distant hall.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 29 — card

### C1 — transcription
All three prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, as one prose field, with no other text anywhere except the gate plaque's five words, GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN, which are object text and not extra text. A paragraph that does not read, or a plaque that reads anything other than those five words, is blocking; text that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — prose mode rendered
One dominant illustration over one prose field on plain cloud, never over the figure or the roof. A panel grid, balloons, or prose over busy art is blocking.

### C3 — the bored Great Sage
Wukong sits alone on the ridge of a gold-roofed house on a cloud, staff across his knees, plumes drooping, looking out at Heaven's palaces; nobody visits; the plaque over the gate reads GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN, as the prose says. A blank plaque is blocking. A monkey in motion, a crowd, or a visitor contradicts the prose — blocking.

### C4 — identity and state
A young golden monkey in Great Sage armor and plumed cap with the red staff, tail visible; the armor unscorched. A gray monkey, a scorched monkey, or a human is blocking.

### C5 — the garden in the distance
Terraces of peach blossom read as a far-off place on a hill, not the page's subject. A garden that dominates the picture, or peaches close enough to eat, pre-empts the next page — blocking only if the reader would think he is already in the garden.

### C6 — focal integrity
The monkey's face and the roofline whole; the palaces coherent. Distant architecture detail is tolerated.
