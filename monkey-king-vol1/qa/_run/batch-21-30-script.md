# Exact script — pages 21–30

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 21 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the South Gate of Heaven.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A gate the size of a city, gold on white cloud,
its doors standing open. Guards the size of towers on either side, faceplates
down. Behind the gate, the palaces of Heaven going up forever. Laozi's small
cloud arriving at the bottom of the frame, and on it a monkey in gold armor
with his mouth open, his plumes tiny against the gate.

> **CAPTION** *(lower left, over the cloud)*
> "The gate was open. Nobody stopped him. He decided that meant they wanted him."

**Why you turn the page:** it is the biggest thing he has ever seen and he has just decided it is his.

## PAGE 22 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Hall of Jade.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong on the lowest step of the throne, left,
plumes up, staring around him. The Emperor far above, right, not looking at
him, fingers at his temple. Laozi on a middle step between them.

> **LAOZI:** "Majesty, Sun Wukong, of Flower-Fruit Mountain."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Hello! I'm the one that took the pillar. It's behind my ear. Where do you want me?"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The Emperor, still not looking, one hand lifting an inch.

> **JADE EMPEROR:** "The Heavenly Horses need a keeper. He'll do. Take him to the stables."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Wukong turning to Laozi, lit up. Laozi smiling and dabbing
his brow.

> **WUKONG:** "Keeper of the Heavenly Horses. Did you hear that? Heavenly! Is that a high post?"
>
> **LAOZI:** "It is a post in Heaven. Come along, Keeper."

**Why you turn the page:** he asked whether it was a high post and Laozi did not answer the question, and the reader heard that.

## PAGE 23 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Heavenly stables.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** A long stable of white wood under a roof of
cloud, horses of every colour down its whole length. Wukong asleep on the back
of a black horse, plumes hanging over one ear. Another horse eating from his
open hand. By the door, a small desk with a ledger on it, and his cap on a nail.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the plain stable wall, never over the horses)*
> "There were a thousand horses, and none of them had ever been happy.
>
> He learned their names in a week. He rode the wild ones until they laughed, and walked the frightened ones until they slept, and he slept with them, up on their backs, his plumes hanging over one ear. He wrote nothing in the ledger on the little desk. The horses were fat and shining, and that was the ledger.
>
> He was good at it. He had never been good at anything that was not a dare. He liked it more than he would ever admit, and for a month he did not ask anyone what rank he was."

**Why you turn the page:** he is happy again, and the sentence says for a month, which means it is about to end.

## PAGE 24 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Heavenly stables.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Laozi visiting, right, admiring a fat horse, gourd at his
hip. Wukong at the little desk by the door, left, feet up on it, cap on the nail.

> **LAOZI:** "The Emperor's horses have never looked better. Everyone says so."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Everyone? Then tell me, Laozi. Keeper of the Heavenly Horses. What rank is that? How high?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Laozi's face. He does not lie. He dabs his brow.

> **LAOZI:** "It isn't a rank. It's below rank. It is the post one gives to someone who is not anyone yet."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The desk in two pieces. The ledger in the air. Horses
rearing. Wukong with the staff out at full length, plumes straight up. Laozi
backing toward the door.

> **SOUND** *(the desk)*
> "KRAKK"
>
> **WUKONG:** "A stable boy. You brought me up here to be a stable boy. Down there, I'm a king!"
>
> **LAOZI:** "Wukong, it is a beginning—"
>
> **WUKONG:** "I don't begin. I'm going home."

**Why you turn the page:** the wound is open, and he is going home with it.

## PAGE 25 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Wukong landing hard on the peak. Monkeys. Old Ma looking up.

> **OLD MA:** "That was fast. Lord of what?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Of horses. Of nothing. They laughed at me up there, Old Ma. Quietly. Which is worse."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong painting huge characters on a red banner
with a brush, monkeys holding it flat. A pole being raised beside the split
stone. The words are readable.

> **WUKONG:** "If they won't give me a name, I'll give me one. Old Ma, what do you call someone Heaven can't order around?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "I don't think there's a word for it."
>
> **WUKONG:** "There is now."
>
> **PRINTED BANNER** *(the whole banner, large)*
> "GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The banner flying over the peak, the troop shouting under
it. Old Ma sitting beneath the pole with her knees up.

> **OLD MA:** "They will come for that, you know."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Good. Let them see it on the way."

**Why you turn the page:** he has written a title Heaven never gave him, in letters Heaven can read from the sky.

## PAGE 26 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the sky over Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 80%.** From the peak, looking up: the clouds torn open
and an army pouring down out of Heaven in a spiral, thousands of soldiers in
black lacquered armor with their faceplates down, spears like rain. On the
peak, one small gold figure under the banner, the staff grown to the size of a
tree trunk in his hands.

**Panel 2 — 20%.** A strip across the bottom: the army going back up, in a
hurry, a few helmets falling.

> **CAPTION** *(in the bottom strip)*
> "They brought everyone. It took him until lunch."

**Why you turn the page:** Heaven's whole army just went home, and now Heaven has to have a meeting about it.

## PAGE 27 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Hall of Jade.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The Emperor on his feet for the first time,
right, beads swinging. Laozi on the step below, left, both hands up.

> **JADE EMPEROR:** "My army. My whole army, and one monkey with a stick. What did you say he holds still for?"
>
> **LAOZI:** "A title, Majesty. I said the wrong title. Give him the one he wrote. Great Sage Equal to Heaven."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The Emperor's face. Disgust.

> **JADE EMPEROR:** "Equal to Heaven. Equal to me."
>
> **LAOZI:** "It's words. Words cost nothing. Give him the words, a house up here, and no duties, and he'll sit in it."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The Emperor sitting back down, fingers to his temple.
Laozi already backing away with a scroll.

> **JADE EMPEROR:** "Give him the words. Keep him out of this hall."
>
> **LAOZI:** "He'll never need to come in it, Majesty."

**Why you turn the page:** Heaven just gave a monkey the title Equal to Heaven, and Laozi has promised he will never need to use it.

## PAGE 28 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Laozi alone on his small cloud under the banner,
right, reading from a gold scroll to the whole troop. Wukong on the split
stone, left, arms folded, the staff across his knees. Old Ma below him.

> **LAOZI:** "By order of the Jade Emperor: Sun Wukong is named Great Sage Equal to Heaven, with a house in Heaven and no duties."
>
> **LAOZI:** "Those are your words, Great Sage. I read them off your banner. Heaven agrees to them."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Old Ma looking up at him. He is looking at the scroll.

> **OLD MA:** "You beat them. You have a mountain and a name. Why go back up?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Because they said it. Heaven said my name, the way I wrote it. I want to hear them say it up there."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Wukong stepping onto Laozi's cloud. Old Ma's hand flat on
his head. His plumes going up.

> **OLD MA:** "Come back."
>
> **WUKONG:** "I always do."

**Why you turn the page:** he is going back up to the people who laughed at him, because they said his name, and Old Ma said the two words again.

## PAGE 29 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the Great Sage's mansion, in Heaven.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** A gold-roofed mansion on a cloud. Wukong sitting
on the roof ridge with the staff across his knees, plumes drooping, looking
out at the palaces of Heaven going up forever. In the far distance, terraces of
peach blossom on a hill.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the flat cloud below the roof, never over the figure)*
> "They gave him the words, and a house made of gold, and nothing to do in it.
>
> He sat on the roof. He was the Great Sage Equal to Heaven now, it said so on the gate, and nobody came to visit, and nobody sent for him, and the Emperor's hall was somewhere over there, where he was not needed. He had won. It felt like the stable, with a better roof.
>
> After a while the Emperor, who wanted him busy, found him a duty. There was a garden of peach trees on a hill in Heaven, and somebody had to guard it. It was, Laozi said, a great honor. It was also, Laozi did not say, a garden full of peaches, and he was a monkey."

**Why you turn the page:** they have just put a monkey in charge of the peaches, and Laozi knew.

# MOVEMENT V — THE PEACHES · pp 30–34

---

## PAGE 30 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the peach garden.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** Terraces of peach trees climbing a hill in
Heaven, three orchards in three colours of blossom. In the fork of the biggest
tree at the top, Wukong lying on his back with a peach the size of his head.
Peach stones on the ground below, a lot of them.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the plain hillside, never over the tree)*
> "The garden had three thousand six hundred trees. The ones at the bottom ripened every three thousand years, and made you wise. The ones in the middle ripened every six thousand years, and made you live forever. The ones at the top ripened every nine thousand years, and nobody remembered what they did, because nobody had ever been allowed to eat one.
>
> He started at the top.
>
> He ate one a day, then one an hour. He ate them in the tree, and the stones fell where the gardeners would find them, and he did not care. He was the Great Sage Equal to Heaven, and it was his garden, and nobody had said not to."

**Why you turn the page:** he is eating the peaches of Heaven one an hour, and someone is about to come and count them.
