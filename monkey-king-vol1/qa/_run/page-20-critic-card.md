## Page intent

## PAGE 20

Dramatic. Heaven comes to the peak: Laozi, round and quick, hovering on a small cloud with a gold scroll, tells the armored monkey that the Jade Emperor has heard of him and is offering him a post. Wukong owns the page — he says yes before he asks what the job is, and the reader sees him already climbing onto the cloud. The dominant turn is Laozi's invitation and Old Ma's answer to it: she asks the one question he will not ask, "What is the post?", and then asks for the only thing she wants, that he come back. What changes: he is leaving again, this time invited. The reader turns because he took the offer blind and Old Ma noticed. Subordinate: the crowd of monkeys, Laozi's scroll, the scenery of the peak. Laozi is polite and warm and never touches the ground.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 20 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** Laozi standing on a small cloud a foot above the
peak, right, not touching the ground, holding a gold scroll. Wukong in full
armor on the split stone, left, staff on his shoulder. Old Ma beside the stone.
Monkeys everywhere.

> **LAOZI:** "Sun Wukong. I am Laozi, from the Jade Emperor, who rules Heaven. He has heard of you. He is offering you a post."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Heaven has heard of me?"
>
> **LAOZI:** "All of it."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Wukong turning to Old Ma, the plumes shaking.

> **WUKONG:** "Heaven, Old Ma. They sent for me. Me. Nobody has ever sent for me."
>
> **OLD MA:** "What is the post?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Who cares!"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Old Ma's hand flat on his head, her face turned up to
his. He is already half onto Laozi's cloud.

> **OLD MA:** "You said you'd teach us not to die. You haven't yet."
>
> **WUKONG:** "When I'm back. When I'm a lord."
>
> **OLD MA:** "Come back. That's the whole thing I want from you. Come back."

**Why you turn the page:** he said yes before he asked what the job was, and Old Ma noticed.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 20 — card

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth, with no extra text anywhere. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — the invitation owns the page
One image plainly owns the page: Laozi on his cloud delivering Heaven's offer to the armored monkey on the split stone, with Old Ma present. If the page reads as three equal bands with no owner, or the mode reads as prose or spectacle, that is blocking.

### C3 — order and attribution of the three exchanges
Laozi's "All of it." reads only after Wukong's "Heaven has heard of me?". Old Ma's "What is the post?" sits between Wukong's two lines and belongs to her; "Who cares!" is his. "You said you'd teach us not to die. You haven't yet." is Old Ma's and "When I'm back. When I'm a lord." is Wukong's: each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail and placement, not merely make sense as his or hers. "Come back. That's the whole thing I want from you. Come back." is unmistakably Old Ma's and reads last. A reply readable before its prompt, or a balloon whose tail or placement points at the wrong monkey or at neither, is blocking.

### C4 — identity and the two monkeys
Wukong is a young golden monkey in gold armor and plumed cap; Old Ma an old gray monkey with white muzzle, shawl, and stick; Laozi a round bald old man with a square white beard in indigo. Confusing the two monkeys, or reading Laozi as the Master, is blocking. Background monkeys with similar faces are tolerated.

### C5 — Old Ma's hand and the cloud
Old Ma's hand rests flat on his head while he is already stepping toward the cloud. Blocking only if the reader cannot tell he is leaving.

### C6 — focal integrity
The three named faces and the speaking mouths are whole. Background monkey anatomy, the scroll, and the scenery are tolerated.
