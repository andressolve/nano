## Page intent

## PAGE 13

Dramatic. Morning in the courtyard. A pine tree stands in the middle of the raked gravel where no pine was, and the pine has a tail, and the students in a ring around it are clapping. The pine unwinds into Wukong, bowing with his arms out, asking for something harder. Subhuti on the step says one word, his name, and his face is stone. That is the dominant turn: the pine tree, then never say you were my student, then out. Subhuti points at the gate. He said what would happen. Take the shape and the cloud and go, and wherever you go, never say you were his student. Wukong starts to explain what he only wanted and is cut off: I know what you wanted. Go. The students backing away are subordinate; Wukong very small is the last image. The reader turns because the one who gave him a name has told him never to say it, and he has to go home like that.

### C2 — The unwinding owns the page
One image plainly carries the page: the pine turning back into the bowing monkey while the Master watches from the step with a face of stone. Tolerance: an unequal but readable hierarchy passes.

### C3 — Order of the exchange
"clap clap clap clap" first, on the ring around the pine; "A pine! Did you see?" before "Wukong."; "I said what would happen." before "Master, I only wanted them to—"; "I know what you wanted. Go." last and Subhuti's. Tolerance: awkward tails pass if order and ownership are never in doubt.

### C4 — The pine with a tail

### C5 — Identities
Wukong is the small bare gold monkey with a tail, bowing, then very small; Subhuti the tall lean pointed-bearded old man in a pale robe with the whisk, unsmiling, not round, bald, or stout. The students are silent, shaved, brown-robed. Tolerance: minor drift that cannot be read as another character passes.

### C6 — Focal integrity
