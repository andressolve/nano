## Page intent

## PAGE 3

Dramatic. A hot day at the pool below the waterfall, monkeys crowded on every rock. Old Ma, on the highest rock under her shawl, says nobody knows where the water comes from; Wukong, gold at the front of the crowd, says somebody should go and look. She names the prize: whoever goes through and comes back alive is made king, and nobody has ever done it. He checks the terms, king of all of them, and she confirms it. Nobody else moves. He goes, a gold streak into the white water, and the crowd shrieks. The dominant turn is the dare and the prize, and him going through the water before anyone can stop him. Wukong owns the page; Old Ma sets it in motion. The reader turns to see what is behind the water. The crowd and the rocks stay subordinate to the exchange and the leap.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 3 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the pool below the waterfall.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A hot day. The waterfall roaring into its pool,
monkeys crowded on every rock. OLD MA on the highest rock, left, on her stick,
shawl over her head against the sun. WUKONG at the front of the crowd, right,
gold among brown, one foot up on a boulder, staring at the water.

> **OLD MA:** "Nobody knows where the water comes from. It has been falling since before I was born, and I am very old."
>
> **WUKONG:** "So somebody should go and look."
>
> **OLD MA:** "Somebody should. Whoever goes through and comes back alive, we make king. That was always the rule. Nobody has ever done it."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** The monkeys looking at each other. Nobody moves. Wukong has
a flat hand up over his eyes, looking at the white wall of water.

> **WUKONG:** "King. Of all of you?"
>
> **OLD MA:** "Of all of us."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** A gold streak going into the white water. The crowd shrieking.

> **WUKONG:** "I'll go!"
>
> **SOUND** *(in the water)*
> "SPLASSSH"

**Why you turn the page:** he went into the water before anyone could tell him not to, and you want to see what is behind it.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 3 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All seven strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each speech string owned by the right monkey's mouth and the sound cue in the water, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read top to bottom as the dare named at the pool, nobody moving, then a gold streak into the white water with the crowd shrieking. The dare owns the page by eye; the plunge closes it. If the plunge reads before the dare, or nothing dominates, the page fails.

### C3 — Attribution and order
Old Ma's "Nobody knows where the water comes from." must read first, Wukong's "So somebody should go and look." second, Old Ma's "Somebody should." third, each on its owner's side. Old Ma's "Of all of us." must be readable only after Wukong's "King. Of all of you?", and "I'll go!" must be Wukong's alone. A balloon readable as the other monkey's is blocking.

### C4 — Wukong and Old Ma collide
Wukong gold, young, round amber eyes, one foot up, then a flat hand shading his eyes; Old Ma gray, stooped, white-muzzled, on her stick, shawl over her head. Both faces must stay distinct at reduced scale and in profile. Any drape of the shawl is tolerable while her face reads.

### C5 — The crowd steals an identity or a line
No crowd monkey may be gold, gray, shawled, sticked, or given a balloon. Repeated anonymous crowd faces are nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Doubled named figures, a speaking mouth hidden, fused hands on boulder or stick, or corruption in the plunge panel block. Faults in rocks, spray, or distant monkeys do not.
