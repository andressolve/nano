## Page intent

## PAGE 5

Dramatic. Wukong bursts back out through the water onto the rocks, drenched, shouting about the house before he has landed. Then the whole troop pours across the iron bridge into the cave, monkeys bouncing on stone beds and trying bowls on their heads, Old Ma crossing slowly on her stick, Wukong already up on the tallest stone stool with his arms out. She says a rule is a rule and tells him to bow his head. Her hand goes flat on his head; she names him Handsome Monkey King; he swears he will never tire of it; the crowd roars. The dominant turn is that he leads them in and Old Ma crowns him, and the joy of it. Wukong owns the page; Old Ma gives it. The reader turns because he is king of everything he can see, and this is the happiest anyone in the book will ever be. The furniture and the bowls stay subordinate.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 5 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All six strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each speech string owned by the right monkey's mouth and the cheer as a sound cue, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as Wukong bursting out shouting, then the whole troop pouring across the bridge with Wukong up on the tallest stool, then Old Ma's hand on his head and the cheer. The crossing owns the page by eye. If the crowning reads before the crossing, or nothing dominates, the page fails.

### C3 — Attribution and order
Wukong's "There's a house!" stands alone in the first moment. Old Ma's "You went through." must read before her "Bow your head," and both must be hers. Old Ma's "Handsome Monkey King." must read before Wukong's "I won't! I won't ever!", which must be his. A balloon readable as the other's is blocking.

### C4 — Wukong and Old Ma collide
Wukong gold, young, wet then dry, arms out on the stool; Old Ma gray, stooped, on her stick, red shawl, her hand flat on his head at the end. Both must be distinct at every scale on the page and neither may have a human face.

### C5 — The crowning is wrong, or an identity leaks
The crowning must be a hand on a head and nothing else: no crown, cap, armor, or staff on Wukong. No crowd monkey may be gold, gray, shawled, sticked, or given a balloon. Bowls on heads and bouncing on beds are nonblocking however drawn.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Doubled named figures, a speaking mouth hidden, a hand that does not sit on the head, or corruption at bridge or stool block. Faults in crowd, water, or furniture do not.
