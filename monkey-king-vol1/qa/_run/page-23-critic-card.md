> "There were a thousand horses, and none of them had ever been happy.
>
> He learned their names in a week. He rode the wild ones until they laughed, and walked the frightened ones until they slept, and he slept with them, up on their backs, his plumes hanging over one ear. He wrote nothing in the ledger on the little desk. The horses were fat and shining, and that was the ledger.
>
> He was good at it. He had never been good at anything that was not a dare. He liked it more than he would ever admit, and for a month he did not ask anyone what rank he was."

**Why you turn the page:** he is happy again, and the sentence says for a month, which means it is about to end.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 23 — card

### C1 — transcription
All three prose paragraphs read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in order, as one prose field, with no other text anywhere. A paragraph that does not read is blocking; one that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — prose mode rendered
The page is one dominant illustration with one prose field over plain ground, not a panel grid and not balloons. Prose laid over horses, the monkey, or busy art, or scattered into several boxes, is blocking.

### C3 — the sleeping keeper
The picture shows Wukong asleep on the back of a black horse with plumes over one ear, another horse eating from his hand, and a long stable of many horses. A monkey awake and working, or no horses, contradicts the prose — blocking. The exact number and colours of horses are not.

### C4 — identity and state
Wukong is a golden monkey in Great Sage armor and plumed cap, small, with his tail visible. No human, no Laozi, nobody else in the stable. A second figure is blocking.

### C5 — the desk stays whole
A small desk with a ledger by the door, intact, unemphasized, with nothing legible on it. A broken desk here is blocking; a desk that is hard to find is tolerated.

### C6 — focal integrity
The monkey's face and the two foreground horses are whole. Distant horse anatomy is tolerated.
