## Page intent

## PAGE 31

Dramatic. Laozi comes up the terrace with a basket and finds peach stones on the path, from the top trees that nobody in Heaven was ever allowed. Two gold feet hang out of the biggest tree. He needs forty peaches for his elixir and forty for tonight, and the word slips. Wukong drops out of the tree to land in front of him and asks what tonight is: the Emperor's peach banquet, every immortal of rank. The dominant turn is Wukong's question, that he is Equal to Heaven, that is a rank, where is his invitation, and Laozi's answer, that the title was so he would not need one. Wukong goes very still, without a smile, and asks it plainly. Laozi says no. Laozi owns the first half; Wukong takes the page at the stillness. The reader turns because he now knows what the title meant. The basket and the stones stay subordinate.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 31 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the peach garden.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Laozi coming up the terrace with a basket, gourd at his
hip, bending to the peach stones on the path. His eyes go up the terraces to
the biggest tree at the top, where two gold feet hang down.

> **LAOZI:** "Great Sage. These stones are from the top trees. Nobody in Heaven has ever been allowed one."
>
> **WUKONG:** "They're good. Bit dry."
>
> **LAOZI:** "I need forty of the middle peaches for my elixir and forty more for tonight. Where are the—"
>
> **WUKONG:** "For tonight?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Wukong dropping out of the tree to land in front
of Laozi, left, peach juice on his chin. The basket between them. Laozi, right,
dabbing his brow.

> **LAOZI:** "The banquet. The Emperor's peach banquet. It's tonight, in the hall. Every immortal of rank in Heaven."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Every immortal of rank. I'm Equal to Heaven. That's a rank. Where's my invitation, Laozi?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Laozi not answering, bending to pick up a peach stone.
Wukong very still, the way he was still under the three taps, but with no
smile.

> **LAOZI:** "Wukong. The title was so that you would not need one."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Say it plainly. Am I invited?"
>
> **LAOZI:** "No."

**Why you turn the page:** the title was so that he would not need an invitation, and now he knows what that means.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 31 — card

### C1 — Dialogue transcription or ownership fails
All nine strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth, including the broken-off `Where are the—` and the single `No.`, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; one that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — Reading order breaks the exchange
`For tonight?` is readable before Laozi's `…forty more for tonight. Where are the—`; `Say it plainly. Am I invited?` before `The title was so that you would not need one.`; or `No.` before `Am I invited?`. In the first panel the feet in the tree must own Wukong's lines and Laozi on the path his.

### C3 — The turn does not land
The largest panel is not Wukong landed face to face with Laozi asking for his invitation, or the last panel does not show a still, unsmiling monkey while Laozi looks away.

### C4 — Laozi is not himself
The old man does not read as round, bald with white side hair, square white beard, indigo robe, one gourd. A long face, topknot, pointed beard, whisk, or black beard is blocking: the reader would think the Master or the Emperor had come.

### C5 — Wukong is not himself
The monkey does not read as golden, in Great Sage armor and plumed cap, a head shorter than Laozi. The feet in the tree must belong to the monkey who lands below.

### C6 — Consequential continuity fails
Peach stones are missing from the path, the basket is absent or already full, or the setting is not the terraced peach garden. Stone count is nonblocking.

### C7 — Focal generation failure
Either speaker's face or hands is malformed or duplicated. Distant trees do not count.
