## Page intent

## PAGE 11

Dramatic. Midnight inside the cave, one candle. The round back door opens and Wukong slips in, tail first, and Subhuti, seated and waiting, tells him he is the first in forty years to understand a simple thing. Then the lesson, which owns the page: two fingers on the monkey's forehead, a bronze basin of water between them, and in the water the monkey's reflection becoming a hawk and then a pine. The shape, and the cloud. Wukong asks about dying, and the answer is the whole of immortality in one plain sentence: a thing that can be anything and be anywhere is very hard to kill. The dominant turn is the shape-change and the cloud, and their price. The price comes last, with the whisk raised for the flick that ends things: use them to be admired and I will send you away alone. Wukong, half out the door, grinning, swears he never would. The reader turns because he has been told exactly what not to do and is already grinning.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 11 · *dramatic*

**Location:** inside the cave, midnight.

**Panel 1 — 20%.** The small round door opening from outside. A single candle.
Subhuti seated, waiting. Wukong slipping in, tail first.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Third watch. Back door. You are the first in forty years to understand a simple thing."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Subhuti, right, with two fingers on Wukong's
forehead. Wukong cross-legged, left. Between them a bronze basin of water, and
in the water the monkey's reflection is a hawk, and then a pine.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Two things. The shape: become anything with a body, and keep your own. The cloud: one somersault, a hundred thousand miles."
>
> **WUKONG:** "And dying?"
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "A thing that can be anything and be anywhere is very hard to kill. That is all immortality is. Don't tell the others."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** The whisk raised, the one flick that ends things. Wukong
already half out of the round door, grinning back.

> **SUBHUTI:** "The shape is for staying alive. The cloud is for leaving. Use them to be admired, and I will send you away alone."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Master, I'd never."

**Why you turn the page:** he has been told exactly what not to do, in the voice that means it, and he is already grinning.

### C1 — Transcription
The six listed strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The lesson owns the page
One image plainly carries the page: the Master's two fingers on the seated monkey's forehead by candlelight, the basin between them; a secret midnight lesson, not three equal night scenes. Tolerance: an unequal but readable hierarchy passes.

### C3 — Order of the exchange
"Two things." before "And dying?", and "A thing that can be anything" only after it; "Use them to be admired" before "Master, I'd never." Tolerance: awkward tails pass if order and ownership are never in doubt.

### C4 — The reflection
In the basin's water the reflection is a hawk and a pine while the monkey keeps his own shape; the reader understands this is the shape being taught, not a second creature in the room. Tolerance: one of the two reflections faint passes.

### C5 — Identities and the open door
Wukong is the small bare gold monkey with a tail; Subhuti the tall lean pointed-bearded old man in a pale robe with the whisk, not round, bald, or stout. The round door stands open; no student is present. Tolerance: minor drift that cannot be read as another character passes.

### C6 — Focal integrity
Both faces, Subhuti's two fingers, and the raised whisk are whole and legible. Tolerance: candle, basin rim, and wall texture are not graded.
