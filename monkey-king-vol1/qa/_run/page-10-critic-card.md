## Page intent

## PAGE 10

Dramatic. Lessons on the gravel. Subhuti asks Wukong, at the back and bouncing on his heels, what he has learned in seven years; Wukong says every word, but not the thing he came for. Then the Master walks down between the rows and brings the handle of his whisk down on the monkey's head, three times, says he has learned nothing, and the students laugh behind their hands. That is the dominant turn: three taps on the head, the students laugh, and he alone understands. Subhuti walks away into the cave with his hands clasped behind his back, the students howl, and Wukong sits very still with one hand on his head, eyes wide, a small smile starting, reading the taps as a message: midnight, the third watch, the back door. The small round door in the cliff, shut, is quietly present. The reader turns because everyone laughed at the monkey and he was the only one who got the joke.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 10 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the courtyard, at lessons.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Students seated in rows on the gravel. Subhuti on the step,
right, whisk in hand. Wukong at the back, left, bouncing on his heels.

> **SUBHUTI:** "Wukong. Seven years. What have you learned?"
>
> **WUKONG:** "Everything you said, Master. Every word. But not the thing I came for."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Subhuti has walked down between the rows and
stands over him. The handle of the whisk comes down on the top of the monkey's
head, three times. The students are laughing behind their hands. Behind
Subhuti, in the cliff, a small round door, shut.

> **SOUND** *(on the monkey's head)*
> "TAP TAP TAP"
>
> **SUBHUTI:** "Then you have learned nothing."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Subhuti walking away into the cave with his hands clasped
behind his back. The students howling. Wukong sitting very still, one hand on
his head, eyes wide, and a small smile starting.

> **WUKONG:** "Three taps. Hands behind his back. He means: midnight, the third watch, and come in by the back door."

**Why you turn the page:** everyone laughed at the monkey, and the monkey was the only one who got the joke.

### C2 — The taps own the page
One image plainly carries the page: the Master standing over the seated monkey with the whisk handle on his head, students laughing around them. Tolerance: an unequal but readable hierarchy passes.

### C3 — Order of the exchange
"What have you learned?" before "Everything you said, Master."; "TAP TAP TAP" on the monkey's head and before "Then you have learned nothing."; "Three taps." last, Wukong's alone, with the Master already walking away. Tolerance: awkward tails pass if order and ownership are never in doubt.

### C4 — Identities
Wukong is the small bare gold monkey with a tail; Subhuti the tall lean pointed-bearded old man in a pale robe with a whisk, not round, bald, or stout. Tolerance: minor drift that cannot be read as another character passes.

### C5 — The laughing and the leaving
The students visibly laugh and are silent; in the last beat Subhuti walks away with his hands clasped behind his back where the reader can see them. The round door in the cliff, if seen, is shut. Tolerance: repeated or indistinct student faces pass.

### C6 — Focal integrity
