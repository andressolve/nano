## Page intent

## PAGE 24

Dramatic. The stables. Laozi visits, admiring a fat horse; Wukong sits at the little desk with his feet up and asks, since everyone is talking, what rank Keeper of the Heavenly Horses is. The dominant turn is Laozi's answer, given without a lie: it is not a rank, it is below rank, the post one gives to someone who is not anyone yet. Laozi's face owns the page at that moment. Then the desk is in two pieces, the ledger is in the air, the horses rear, the staff is out at full length, and Wukong says the word — stable boy — and that down there he is a king. Laozi tries "it is a beginning" and is cut off. What changes: the wound is open and he is going home with it. The reader turns to see what he does with it. Subordinate: the horses, the hay, the wreckage.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 24 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the Heavenly stables.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** Laozi visiting, right, admiring a fat horse, gourd at his
hip. Wukong at the little desk by the door, left, feet up on it, cap on the nail.

> **LAOZI:** "The Emperor's horses have never looked better. Everyone says so."
>
> **WUKONG:** "Everyone? Then tell me, Laozi. Keeper of the Heavenly Horses. What rank is that? How high?"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Laozi's face. He does not lie. He dabs his brow.

> **LAOZI:** "It isn't a rank. It's below rank. It is the post one gives to someone who is not anyone yet."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** The desk in two pieces. The ledger in the air. Horses
rearing. Wukong with the staff out at full length, plumes straight up. Laozi
backing toward the door.

> **SOUND** *(the desk)*
> "KRAKK"
>
> **WUKONG:** "A stable boy. You brought me up here to be a stable boy. Down there, I'm a king!"
>
> **LAOZI:** "Wukong, it is a beginning—"
>
> **WUKONG:** "I don't begin. I'm going home."

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 24 — card

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string, including the sound cue, reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth; the truncated "Wukong, it is a beginning—" reads as written. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — Laozi's answer owns the page
One image owns the page: Laozi's face as he says it is below rank, honest and uncomfortable. If the broken desk or the opening dominates instead, or the page has no owner, the turn is lost — blocking.

### C3 — order of question, answer, break
"Everyone says so." is Laozi's and "Everyone? Then tell me, Laozi…" is Wukong's: each must hang from its own speaker's mouth by tail and placement, not merely make sense as his. "What rank is that? How high?" must read before "It isn't a rank…", and "It isn't a rank…" before the desk breaks; "I don't begin. I'm going home." must read last and belong to Wukong. Laozi's "Wukong, it is a beginning—" must be his, sitting between the monkey's two lines. Any reversal or swap is blocking.

### C4 — the desk breaks
The desk is whole while Wukong sits at it and in two pieces with the ledger in the air at the end; the staff is out at full length in his hands. A desk that never breaks, or a break with no staff, is blocking.

### C5 — identity
Wukong is the young golden monkey in gold armor with plumes; Laozi the round bald old man with square white beard, indigo robe, gourd. Laozi read as the Master, or Wukong as an old or gray monkey, is blocking.

### C6 — focal integrity
Both faces and both speaking mouths whole; Wukong's hands on the staff readable. Rearing horses in the background are tolerated.
