## Page intent

## PAGE 21

Spectacle. One image: the South Gate of Heaven, a gate the size of a city, gold on white cloud, its doors standing open, tower-sized guards on either side with faceplates down, and the palaces of Heaven going up forever behind it. At the very bottom, Laozi's small cloud arriving with a monkey in gold armor on it, mouth open, plumes tiny against the gate. The dominant turn is scale: this is the biggest thing he has ever seen. One caption owns the page's meaning — the gate was open, nobody stopped him, and he decided that meant they wanted him. What changes: he has entered Heaven and already believes it is his. The reader turns on that mistaken confidence. Subordinate: everything but the gate and the tiny figure; Laozi is a small silent shape beside him.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 21 — card

### C1 — transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, as a caption with no tail, with no other text anywhere — no words on the gate, doors, or banners. A caption that does not read is blocking; one that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — scale is the event
The page is one image, spectacle mode: the open gate towers over everything, tower-guards flank it, Heaven rises behind, and the monkey on the cloud is tiny by comparison. If the monkey reads as large, or the page breaks into panels, the turn is lost — blocking.

### C3 — the gate is open and unopposed
Both doors stand open and nothing bars, challenges, or attacks the cloud. A closed gate or guards visibly stopping him contradicts the caption — blocking.

### C4 — identity at tiny scale
The small figure reads as Wukong in gold armor with plumes and a tail, mouth open, and the round indigo shape beside him as Laozi; the guards have no faces. A guard with a face, third eye, plume, or beard that reads as a named character is blocking; indistinct armor detail is tolerated.

### C5 — register and generation integrity
Gold leaf and cloud-white, no green; the gate and figures whole, no doubled cloud or duplicated monkey. Soft detail in the far palaces is tolerated.
