## Page intent

## PAGE 22

Dramatic. The Hall of Jade. Wukong stands on the lowest step of the throne, plumes up, staring around; the Emperor sits far above, not looking at him, fingers at his temple; Laozi stands between them on a middle step. The dominant turn is the appointment: Keeper of the Heavenly Horses, granted without a glance, and Wukong is thrilled by it. He asks whether it is a high post; Laozi answers a different question, "It is a post in Heaven," and the reader hears the evasion even though Wukong does not. What changes: the monkey has a job in Heaven and has already mistaken it for honor. The Emperor owns the decision; Wukong owns the joy; Laozi owns the dodge. The reader turns because the question was not answered. Subordinate: the hall's size, the pillars, the water floor.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 22 — card

### C1 — transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth, no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — the appointment owns the page
One image owns the page: the monkey on the lowest step, the Emperor high and not looking, Laozi between. If no panel owns the page or the mode reads as anything but dramatic, that is blocking.

### C3 — the unanswered question
Wukong's "Is that a high post?" must read before Laozi's "It is a post in Heaven. Come along, Keeper.", each from its own mouth; the Emperor's line belongs to the Emperor alone. A reply that could be given to the wrong speaker is blocking.

### C4 — the Emperor does not look, does not stand
He is seated throughout, fingers at his temple, face turned away from the monkey. An Emperor standing or engaged with Wukong changes the politics — blocking.

### C5 — the two old men of Heaven
The Emperor is broad, black-bearded, crowned, in yellow; Laozi is round, bald, white square beard, in indigo. If a reader could mistake one for the other, or Laozi for the Master from the earlier pages, that is blocking.

### C6 — focal integrity
Three named faces and the speaking mouths are whole; Wukong stands low on the steps, never level with the Emperor. Pillars, floor reflections, and distant courtiers are tolerated.
