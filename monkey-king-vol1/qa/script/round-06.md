## Cold read

A stone cracks on an empty peak and a gold monkey climbs out looking at me. The brown monkeys take him; Old Ma puts a hand on his head; he grows up loud and does not know what he is. She names the prize — through the waterfall and back, and we make you king — and he is in the water before anyone can stop him. A dry stone house for everyone. She crowns him and it is the happiest page in the book. That night: kings die too, only immortals don't. He leaves to find one.

A raft, three years on two legs in a coat, one old woman who points at the Master's mountain. The Master says no at the gate, hears "I came out of a rock," and gives him a name. Seven years of the broom. Three taps, back door, midnight: the shape and the cloud, and the warning not to be admired with them. A pine tree in the courtyard by morning; thrown out and told never to say whose student he was. Home, Old Ma reads his face. A king needs a weapon; nobody has ever asked the Dragon King.

The Dragon King, polite to the edge of panic, offers him the sea's anchor to be rid of him; it shrinks to a needle behind his ear; armor; the sea stays up anyway; a letter goes to Heaven. The Emperor wants never to hear the name; Laozi says give him a job; the Emperor makes Laozi fetch him. He says yes before asking what the job is; Old Ma says come back. Keeper of the Heavenly Horses, a month of being good at it, then "below rank" and the desk in two pieces. The banner. The army, until lunch. Give him the words. He goes back up because they said his name. A gold house with nothing in it; the peaches.

He eats the top ones. The banquet is tonight and he is not invited, and Laozi finally says so plainly. He eats the party, walks toward the red door, eats five gourds of "sweets" for Old Ma, and sits up sober and glowing. No more offers; send Erlang. Erlang asks if the banner is true; "Which one do I hit first?"; shape for shape; the temple with the flagpole round the back; "That was before the dog!"; Laozi's ring from behind, with an apology. Face-down, laughing: it took all of you, say that in the hall.

In the hall Erlang says it — "It took all of us, uncle." The Emperor stands: "Kneel." The soldiers press on his shoulders and get nowhere: "You gave me the title. Equals don't kneel." *(page turn)* Laozi comes down to his level with the empty gourds: the furnace burns forty-nine days and gives back what a thing is made of, and he is made of my peaches and my elixir, and I want them back. "I'm made of a rock." Then we'll get a rock. "Do it. And Laozi. This time it works." *(page turn)* Dark, then hot. He fights the dark for a day, finds the thread of cold, curls round it, and waits, which he has never done. The lid, the staff leaping to his hand, fire in his eyes. "That's every idea you had, and I'm still here." Laozi has nothing. Send west, to the one who owes Heaven nothing. The Buddha sits, asks what he wants the throne for, offers the bet. Five pillars; he signs the middle one and marks it. Look down. His writing on the finger, and the wet patch. "Now hold still. Five hundred years should do it. Then someone will come for you." The hand becomes a mountain beside his own. Winters. An old gray monkey with a peach, every year she has, then a younger one who was told to. Someone will come. Wait.

## Is it alive?

Yes. I would read it, and read it aloud, and the seven-year-old would ask for the temple page and the finger page again.

**The reopened pages, judged first.**

*Page 39.* Three lines, and they carry a page — better than eight would have. Heat in one sentence: the Emperor of Heaven has ordered a chained monkey to kneel in front of his court, and the monkey is using the Emperor's own words against him. Want-from: the Emperor wants submission, on his feet for it; Wukong wants Heaven to say his name and mean it — the same appetite as p28, now with chains on; Erlang wants the credit assigned honestly, which is the only thing Erlang has ever wanted. Subtext: the Emperor says "The Great Sage Equal to Heaven" as a sneer, and Wukong takes it as a contract — that gap is the whole page. Attribution-strip: "uncle," "Kneel," "Equals don't kneel" — three voices, no labels needed. Enacted: the refusal is not reported, it is the soldiers' hands pressing down and getting nowhere, and Erlang watching the monkey instead of the throne. Page-turn: the thesis line of the book now sits at the bottom of a page with nothing after it, and the reader turns to find out who dares answer. Thirty-four words is under the 45–80 guide in `06-TYPOGRAPHY.md`; that guide is a construction budget, not a floor, and the page's sparseness is the throne room's silence, not rationed text. Nothing is missing that a reader would ask for. It is not a page that exists to hold text; it is the trial.

*Page 40.* The sentence. Heat: Laozi has stopped offering and started collecting — the peaches and elixir are inside the monkey's body and he wants them out. Want-from: Laozi from Wukong, his property; Laozi from the Emperor, permission ("Majesty?"); the Emperor from Laozi, that it finally work ("This time it works" is a threat, and in his voice). Wukong's "I'm made of a rock" is the best line on either page: he is not disputing chemistry, he is refusing to be defined as Laozi's stolen goods, and the reader hears the p1/p8 rock under it without being told. Subtext in Laozi too — he never answers "Equals don't kneel"; he sidesteps it by redefining the monkey as ingredients, and the staging (Laozi *coming down to his level*) is the visual answer to a question nobody in Heaven will say aloud. Attribution-strip: Laozi's two balloons (reasonable, possessive, quick), Wukong's five words, the Emperor's declaratives — assignable blind. Not aphorism: "Whatever goes in comes out as what it was made of" is Laozi's operational plan, spoken to the person it will be done to, and it fails on p42. Page-turn: forty-nine days of fire, and "made of a rock" as if that helps.

*Transitions.* 38→39: "Say that in the hall when they ask" → "It took all of us, uncle. He wanted that said." Direct causal hand-off; "uncle" re-identifies Erlang as the nephew from p34 before recognition matters. 39→40: the refusal is answered on the first balloon of the next page, no dead air, no missing beat — the Emperor's non-answer *is* the beat, and Laozi filling it is in character (p19, p27, p34 all show Laozi answering for him). 40→41: "Do it" → "It was dark, and then it was hot." Location continuity holds (furnace introduced p33, named p40, entered p41). The dramatic run 37–40 is four pages, under the five-page rule, with p41 prose as the breather.

**The rest of the book, movement by movement, in my own voice.**

*I (1–7).* Appetite from the first spoken line — "So somebody should go and look." The heat on p6 is an old mother telling a child that he ends and then watching what that does to him; p7 knows she knows. Old Ma's voice is complete by p5.

*II (8–14).* The three taps are a puzzle the reader solves with him and the page-turn line is true. Subhuti's lines are all tests and are stripped of names easily. "You came back on a cloud and your face was wrong" is the tenderest line in the movement.

*III (15–19).* Ao Guang is the best minor character: every line polite, every line wanting the monkey gone. "Do you know what you have just done?" / "Yes. I've got a staff." "Go and fetch him yourself" is punishment as assent.

*IV (20–29).* The spine. Every dramatic page has someone saying less than they mean: "It is a post in Heaven." "Who cares!" "He'll never need to come in it." p24 is the honest man telling the truth and it detonating. "It felt like the stable, with a better roof."

*V (30–34).* "Am I invited?" / "No." earns the one-word answer. He talks to a peach and thinks of Old Ma while eating the elixir — the funniest and saddest page, and it pays off on p48 without a word.

*VI (35–38).* The most heat between equals. Erlang is the one person who talks to him the way he talks to everyone; "That wasn't me. I want that understood" keeps that intact in victory.

*VII (39–48).* The split has improved this movement: the trial now has two clean turns instead of one crowded page, and "He had never waited for anything. He waited." arrives on p41 with the sentence still ringing. "Now hold still." The wet patch on the finger. The banner going pink. I felt the ending.
