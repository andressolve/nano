## Drift review

- **Register and finish:** All ten pages remain in the Mineral Ink register: visible brush-and-ink contour, toothed paper, flat mineral washes, pigment edge variation, and cinematic depth. None drifts into glossy concept art, airbrushed rendering, engraved cross-hatching, plastic 3D, or soft children's-book treatment.
- **Palette, contrast, and lighting:** The temperature changes follow place and time rather than style drift: the blue-black cave on p11, sunlit gold on p12, cool courtyard on p13, warm Flower-Fruit peak on p14, azurite sea sequence on pp15–18, jade-and-gold heavenly hall on p19, and the warm peak again on p20. Blacks remain ink-black without crushing the paper or wash detail. Light direction and cast depth are coherent within each page and recurring setting.
- **Line and paint treatment:** Fine contour, dry-brush texture, mineral pooling, and restrained gold treatment remain compatible with the approved sheets and setting plates. The greater scenic openness of pp12 and 15 is a compositional variation, not a finish change.
- **Lettering and balloons:** Dialogue pages use a consistent dark hand-lettered weight and irregular shell-white balloons with ink outlines. The serif narration on the full-page scenic beats, pp12 and 15, is a stable narration mode rather than lettering drift. Balloon shape and tail treatment remain compatible across the sequence.
- **Wukong:** On pp11–17 he retains the approved stone-born state: golden-tawny fur, pale short muzzle and cheek area, round amber eyes, small wiry body, long arms and tail, leaf kilt, and restless off-square posture. The armor arrival on p18 is a clear story-state change and matches the approved Great Sage lock; p20 preserves the same gold scale armor, red tunic, tiger-skin kilt, black cloud-boots, paired back-curving plumes, and red staff with gold ends. Scale and expression vary without confusing identity.
- **Other recurring characters:** Subhuti on pp11 and 13 retains the long narrow face, topknot, single-point white beard, pale gray-blue robe, lean build, and whisk. Old Ma on pp14 and 20 retains gray fur, long white lined muzzle, stoop, faded red shawl, and peach-wood stick, remaining unmistakably distinct from Wukong. Ao Guang on pp16–18 preserves the teal dragon head, coral antlers, long whiskers, pearl, dark green-gold robe, and shell throne. Laozi and the Jade Emperor on p19 match their locks and remain structurally distinct; Laozi remains stable again on p20.
- **Recurring settings and consequential object:** The courtyard on p13, Flower-Fruit peak on pp14 and 20, sea palace on pp15–18, and Hall of Jade on p19 preserve their approved architectural silhouettes, materials, palette families, and spatial cues. The sea anchor becomes the red-lacquered, gold-ended staff without an identity-breaking redesign between pp17, 18, and 20.
