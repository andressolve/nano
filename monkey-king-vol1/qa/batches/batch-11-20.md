# Sequence gate — canonical pages 11–20

## Blind sequence record

Subhuti secretly teaches Wukong transformation and cloud travel, defines those
skills as practical immortality, and warns that using them for admiration will
mean exile. Wukong immediately tests the cloud, then performs as a pine for the
other students. Their applause confirms Subhuti's warning: he sends Wukong away
and forbids him to claim the school.

Wukong returns to Flower-Fruit Mountain proud of what he learned but wounded by
the expulsion. Old Ma sees through him, welcomes him without qualification, and
then answers his new desire to make a name by pointing him toward the Dragon
King's treasury. Wukong walks into the Eastern Sea, rejects ordinary weapons,
and discovers that the sea-anchor pillar responds to him. It shrinks at his
command into a staff, alarming Ao Guang.

Wukong presses the advantage for royal armor and declares himself Sun Wukong.
After he leaves, Ao Guang verifies that the sea remains up and reports the theft
and Wukong's claimed immortality to Heaven. Laozi relays that report to the Jade
Emperor and persuades him that a minor title will contain the monkey more
cheaply than soldiers. Laozi brings the offer to Flower-Fruit Mountain. Wukong,
delighted that Heaven knows him, accepts before asking what the post is; Old Ma
answers his vanity with the unfinished promise he made to his people and asks
only that he come back.

## Script comparison and gate judgment

The visible material story and the script agree across the full run. Dialogue,
captions, named identity, and attribution remain exact and legible through the
page turns. Wukong, Old Ma, Ao Guang, Laozi, the staff, the armor, the split
stone, the undersea palace, and the heavenly court retain the continuity needed
for each consequence to read without repair.

The focal handoffs are controlled: the brief move from Wukong to Ao Guang and
then Heaven follows the letter he provokes, and Laozi carries that thread back
to Wukong. The page turns preserve cause and effect: instruction leads to the
forbidden display, expulsion to homecoming, ambition to the treasury, removal
of the anchor to Ao Guang's report, and Heaven's decision to the summons.

The batch does not read as compressed summary. The two spectacle pages give
scale and breath between dramatic passages, while pages 16–20 vary their work
across challenge, discovery, self-coronation, political reaction, and farewell.
Consequential beats receive aftermath, especially the expulsion, the staff's
removal, Ao Guang's complaint, and Wukong's decision to leave again. No material
sequence or page-turn failure justifies a complete-page redraw.
