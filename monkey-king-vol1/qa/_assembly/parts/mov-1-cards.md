## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, tail-free, over the sky, and no other text of any kind appears. A caption that does not read, or any stray word or second string, is blocking; a caption that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as one single image: a split stone on a bare summit with light coming out of it, and a small gold figure climbing out and looking straight at the reader. If the eye does not go to the split and then to the gaze, or the page breaks into panels or a sample sheet, the spectacle has failed.

### C3 — Wukong is not himself
The figure must be a gold monkey, young, bare but for leaves, with round amber eyes, not a human child, a gray monkey, or an armored figure. An odd pose or a partly hidden body is tolerable so long as the face and gold fur read.

### C4 — The stone is not split, or someone else is here
The boulder must be one egg-shaped stone broken open down its middle with light in the break, plainly a rock and not a cave or rubble; and no other monkey, animal, or person may be visible. Exact shape and light colour are nonblocking; a second figure that reads as a figure is not.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A doubled figure, a face without eyes, a hand fused into the stone, or corruption where the eye lands blocks. Texture faults in sea, trees, or mist do not.

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every paragraph of the prose field reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, as one continuous field on plain ground, and no other text appears. A paragraph that does not read, is out of order, or lies over a face or hand is blocking; text that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as one illustrated-prose page whose single image is Old Ma's hand laid flat on the small gold monkey's head inside a ring of brown monkeys under an empty tree, with the prose beneath. If the image splits into panels, or the hand on the head is not what the eye finds, the page fails.

### C3 — Wukong and Old Ma collide or are not themselves
He is small, gold, smooth-faced, round amber eyes, looking up; she is gray, stooped, white-muzzled, half-lidded, on a stick, in a faded red shawl. They must be told apart instantly and neither may have a human face. Slight shade differences are tolerable; two monkeys of one colour, or shawl or stick on the wrong one, are not.

### C4 — The crowd steals an identity
No brown monkey may be gold, gray, shawled, or carry a stick, and no crowd monkey may have a balloon. Repeated or vague crowd faces are nonblocking.

### C5 — Focal generation failure
A doubled named figure, a hand that does not sit on the head, a fused or missing face on either named monkey, or corruption at the point of contact blocks. Faults in tree, slope, or distant monkeys do not.

## Page 3 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All seven strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each speech string owned by the right monkey's mouth and the sound cue in the water, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read top to bottom as the dare named at the pool, nobody moving, then a gold streak into the white water with the crowd shrieking. The dare owns the page by eye; the plunge closes it. If the plunge reads before the dare, or nothing dominates, the page fails.

### C3 — Attribution and order
Old Ma's "Nobody knows where the water comes from." must read first, Wukong's "So somebody should go and look." second, Old Ma's "Somebody should." third, each on its owner's side. Old Ma's "Of all of us." must be readable only after Wukong's "King. Of all of you?", and "I'll go!" must be Wukong's alone. A balloon readable as the other monkey's is blocking.

### C4 — Wukong and Old Ma collide
Wukong gold, young, round amber eyes, one foot up, then a flat hand shading his eyes; Old Ma gray, stooped, white-muzzled, on her stick, shawl over her head. Both faces must stay distinct at reduced scale and in profile. Any drape of the shawl is tolerable while her face reads.

### C5 — The crowd steals an identity or a line
No crowd monkey may be gold, gray, shawled, sticked, or given a balloon. Repeated anonymous crowd faces are nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Doubled named figures, a speaking mouth hidden, fused hands on boulder or stick, or corruption in the plunge panel block. Faults in rocks, spray, or distant monkeys do not.

## Page 4 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, tail-free, over the dry stone floor, and no other text appears. A caption that does not read, or any stray label or second string, is blocking; a caption that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as one interior seen from behind the waterfall: a silver wall of water, an iron bridge, and a stone house with beds, bowls, stools, and a hearth going back into the mountain, plainly empty and dry. If the page breaks into panels, or the house does not read as a home, the spectacle fails.

### C3 — Wukong is not the small witness
A tiny gold monkey, drenched, mouth open, must be findable on the bridge, and he must be the only living thing. A second monkey or a person inside blocks; his exact size or pose does not.

### C4 — The house is not stone, or is already lived in
Beds, bowls, stools, and hearth must read as cut stone, with no fire, fruit, bedding, or sign of anyone. Wooden furniture, a lit hearth, or signs of occupation block; the count of beds or bowls does not.

### C5 — The waterfall is not seen from inside
The falling water must read as a wall seen from behind, on one side, with light coming through it. A waterfall seen from outside, or a dry doorway with no water, blocks.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A doubled figure, corrupt geometry where bridge meets house, or a figure fused into the bridge blocks. Texture faults in water or stone do not.

## Page 5 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All six strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each speech string owned by the right monkey's mouth and the cheer as a sound cue, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as Wukong bursting out shouting, then the whole troop pouring across the bridge with Wukong up on the tallest stool, then Old Ma's hand on his head and the cheer. The crossing owns the page by eye. If the crowning reads before the crossing, or nothing dominates, the page fails.

### C3 — Attribution and order
Wukong's "There's a house!" stands alone in the first moment. Old Ma's "You went through." must read before her "Bow your head," and both must be hers. Old Ma's "Handsome Monkey King." must read before Wukong's "I won't! I won't ever!", which must be his. A balloon readable as the other's is blocking.

### C4 — Wukong and Old Ma collide
Wukong gold, young, wet then dry, arms out on the stool; Old Ma gray, stooped, on her stick, red shawl, her hand flat on his head at the end. Both must be distinct at every scale on the page and neither may have a human face.

### C5 — The crowning is wrong, or an identity leaks
The crowning must be a hand on a head and nothing else: no crown, cap, armor, or staff on Wukong. No crowd monkey may be gold, gray, shawled, sticked, or given a balloon. Bowls on heads and bouncing on beds are nonblocking however drawn.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Doubled named figures, a speaking mouth hidden, a hand that does not sit on the head, or corruption at bridge or stool block. Faults in crowd, water, or furniture do not.

## Page 6 — card

### C1 — Transcription
All seven strings read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right monkey's mouth, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as a quiet night by the fire, then Wukong's face changing as Old Ma tells him kings die while she looks at the fire and not at him, then Wukong standing on the stool, eyes wet, fists shut, saying he will go. The middle moment owns the page by eye. If the vow reads before the truth, or nothing dominates, the page fails.

### C3 — Attribution and order
Wukong's "How old are you, Old Ma?" reads before Old Ma's "Old enough". In the dominant moment Wukong's "Three kings. Where are they?" reads first, Old Ma's "Where everything goes." second, Wukong's "Who are they? Where?" clearly third and his. Old Ma's "Stories say:" must be readable before Wukong's "Then I'm going to." A balloon readable as the other's is blocking.

### C4 — Wukong and Old Ma collide, or she looks at him
Wukong gold, young, round amber eyes wet by the end; Old Ma gray, white-muzzled, stooped, knees up, shawl on, eyes on the fire. If she is looking at him in the dominant moment, or the two faces cannot be told apart in profile and firelight, the page fails.

### C5 — Someone else appears
No immortal, old man, or figure from the world of men may appear; sleepers stay brown, bare, and balloonless. A gold, gray, or shawled crowd monkey blocks; vague heaps of sleepers do not.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Doubled named figures, a speaking mouth hidden, fused hands, or corruption on either face in the dominant moment block. Faults in fruit skins, hearth, or sleepers do not.

## Page 7 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every paragraph of the prose field reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, as one continuous field over open water, never over the raft or the figure, and no other text appears. A paragraph that does not read or is out of order is blocking; text that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page must read as a small figure on a raft on a huge sea with the green mountain going small behind him, then, below, the same monkey in a too-big coat walking a road on two legs toward a gray mountain with a single pine. The raft owns the page by eye. If the road image dominates, or the order is unclear, the page fails.

### C3 — Wukong is not himself, or does not carry across
On the raft, the gold monkey, bare but for leaves, not looking back; on the road, the same gold monkey in a man's coat too big for him, upright, tail showing. A different creature in either image, or a human on the road, blocks. The cut of the coat does not.

### C4 — Old Ma or the Master appears
No Old Ma and no red shawl on the page; no Master, students, courtyard, or cave. The pine mountain stays a distant destination. A speck on a far shore is nonblocking; a readable figure is not.

### C5 — The raft is not a raft, or the mountain is not going small
The raft must read as rough pine logs lashed together, not a boat, and the green mountain behind must read as far away and diminishing. Log count and vine detail are nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
A doubled figure in either image, a figure fused into raft or road, or corruption where the eye lands blocks. Texture faults in water, sky, or road do not.
