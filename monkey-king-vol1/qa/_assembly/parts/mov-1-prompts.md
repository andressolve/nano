## PAGE 1 — the stone cracks open

### Reader turn
Something just climbed out of a rock on an empty mountain, and it is looking at you.

### Ordered moments
1. One full-page image, the dominant panel at about 100% of the page. Sunrise over an eastern sea, low warm light. A green mountain rises out of the water, fruit trees down its flanks, a waterfall off a cliff on one side. At the bare stone summit an egg-shaped boulder taller than a man has split down the middle and light comes out of the split. Wukong, small and gold, climbs out of the split, one hand on the lip of the stone, tail out behind him, looking straight out of the page at the viewer. His eyes are the brightest point after the split. Summit and figure in the upper-middle of the page; flank and sea fill the lower half.
2. The caption sits upper left over open sky, tail-free, clear of the figure.

### Exact strings
1. CAPTION: `On the peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain, where nobody lived, a stone cracked open.`

Render exactly this 1 string once, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
Wukong, from the attached sheet, view (d) stone-born: a monkey, not a man; short heart-shaped face, pale hairless muzzle and cheek patches on golden-tawny fur, wide mouth, large round amber eyes set wide, low brow, small standing ears, golden ruff of fur around the head; small and wiry, long arms, long tail in frame; crouched on the lip of the stone, not standing square; bare with a kilt of leaves, no ornament, no armor, no cap, no staff. Silent.

### Consequential exclusions
Old Ma is absent. No other monkey, animal, or person anywhere; nobody lives here. The stone is split open and stays split: one clean vertical break through one egg-shaped boulder, not rubble. No red shawl, no gray fur, no armor, plumes, or tiger skin on any figure. Trees and waterfall are landscape, not focal.

At the center Wukong, small and gold, on all fours, looking up. Old Ma, gray and stooped, on the LEFT of him, leaning on her peach-wood stick, faded red shawl on her shoulders, her free hand laid flat on top of his head. The tree's branches are empty of birds. Morning malachite light. Both faces fully visible, his tilted up to hers.
2. The prose field sits in the lower part of the page over plain ground (grass, bare slope, mist), never over faces or hands. One matte parchment field, four paragraphs, in order.

### Exact strings
All of the following belong to PROSE FIELD 1:
1. `He was a monkey, more or less. He was gold where the others were brown, and his eyes gave off a light that made the birds leave the tree. He had no mother. He had a rock, and the rock was open now and would not close.`
2. `The monkeys of the mountain came to look. The eldest, Old Ma, put her hand flat on his head, the way she did with the new ones, and left it there a while.`
3. `'Well,' she said. 'You're here.'`
4. `He stayed. He grew. He was faster than all of them, and louder, and he did not know what he was, and for a long time nobody minded.`

## PAGE 3 — the dare and the water

### Reader turn

Old Ma on the highest rock at the LEFT, on her stick, red shawl over her head against the sun. Wukong at the RIGHT at the front of the crowd, gold among brown, one foot up on a boulder, staring at the water. Three balloons: Old Ma upper left above her; Wukong upper right above him; Old Ma's second line lower left, clearly third.
2. Middle strip, about 20%. The monkeys look at each other; nobody moves. Wukong at the RIGHT, flat hand up over his eyes, looking at the white wall of water; Old Ma at the LEFT. His question on the right, her answer on the left below it.
3. Bottom panel, about 30%. A gold streak going into the white water; the crowd shrieking on the rocks. His shout on his side leading the streak; the sound cue in the water where he hits.

### Exact strings
1. OLD MA: `Nobody knows where the water comes from. It has been falling since before I was born, and I am very old.`
2. WUKONG: `So somebody should go and look.`
3. OLD MA: `Somebody should. Whoever goes through and comes back alive, we make king. That was always the rule. Nobody has ever done it.`
4. WUKONG: `King. Of all of you?`
5. OLD MA: `Of all of us.`
6. WUKONG: `I'll go!`
7. SOUND: `SPLASSSH`

## PAGE 6 — kings die too

### Reader turn

### Ordered moments

Old Ma beside him at the RIGHT, knees up, arms wrapped round them, looking at the fire. His balloon upper left above him; her reply on the right above her.
2. Dominant panel, about 45% of the page, the middle. Close on the two at the fire: Wukong at the LEFT, his face changing, the laugh gone; Old Ma at the RIGHT, not looking at him, eyes on the fire. Three balloons in A-B-A order: Wukong upper left; Old Ma's long answer middle right on her side; Wukong's second question lower left, clearly third.
3. Bottom panel, about 25%. Wukong standing up on the stool at the LEFT, eyes wet, fists shut; Old Ma at the RIGHT, still seated, still looking at the fire; monkeys waking behind. Her line upper right on her side; his vow lower left, clearly after hers.

### Exact strings
1. WUKONG: `How old are you, Old Ma? How old is very?`
2. OLD MA: `Old enough that I have seen three kings before you. None of them went through the water. They just shouted the loudest.`
3. WUKONG: `Three kings. Where are they?`
4. OLD MA: `Where everything goes. Kings die too, little one. Monkeys, kings, mountains eventually. It is only the immortals who don't.`
5. WUKONG: `Who are they? Where?`
6. OLD MA: `Stories say: far to the west, over the sea, in the world of men. I never met one.`
7. WUKONG: `Then I'm going to. I'll find one, and I'll make him teach me, and none of us has to die.`

## PAGE 7 — the raft and the Master's mountain

### Reader turn
He crosses the sea and the years among men to a Master who has never taught anyone the one thing he came for.

### Ordered moments
1. Dominant illustration, about 65% of the page, the upper part. A raft of pine logs lashed with vines on an enormous gray-green sea under a wide pale sky. One small gold figure, Wukong, stands on the raft, back three-quarters to us, not looking back. Far behind at the horizon, the green mountain of Flower-Fruit going small. Water fills most of the image.
2. The prose field sits over the flat open sea below the raft, never over the raft or the figure. One matte parchment field, three paragraphs, in order.
3. Lower panel, about 35%. The world of men: a monkey in a man's coat far too big for him walking upright on two legs along a dirt road, seen from behind or three-quarter, toward a gray mountain with a single pine at the far end of the road. Cool pale light.

### Exact strings
All of the following belong to PROSE FIELD 1:
1. `He built a raft out of a dead pine and tied it with vines, and Old Ma stood on the shore and did not tell him to stay, because she had been the one who told him about immortals, and she knew it.`
2. `The sea took him west for a season. The world of men took him for three years. He learned to walk on two legs so they would not chase him, and to wear a coat so they would not stare, and he asked every old person he met the same question. Most of them laughed.`
3. `One did not. She pointed at a gray mountain with a single pine, and said: the Master lives there. He has had students for forty years, and he has never once taught any of them what you are asking for.`

Render exactly these 3 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.
