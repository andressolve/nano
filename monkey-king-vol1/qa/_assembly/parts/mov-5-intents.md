## PAGE 30

Illustrated prose. The peach garden in late-afternoon light: three terraces of peach trees in three colours of blossom, and at the top, in the fork of the biggest tree, Wukong on his back with a peach the size of his head. The prose does the telling. The bottom trees make you wise, the middle ones make you live forever, and nobody remembers what the top ones do because nobody was ever allowed to eat one. He started at the top. The dominant turn is that he eats, one a day and then one an hour, and the stones pile up under the tree where the gardeners will find them, and he does not care, because it is his garden and nobody said not to. Wukong owns the page alone. The reader turns because someone is about to come and count. The blossom and the terraces stay subordinate to one monkey in one tree with one peach.

## PAGE 31

Dramatic. Laozi comes up the terrace with a basket and finds peach stones on the path, from the top trees that nobody in Heaven was ever allowed. Two gold feet hang out of the biggest tree. He needs forty peaches for his elixir and forty for tonight, and the word slips. Wukong drops out of the tree to land in front of him and asks what tonight is: the Emperor's peach banquet, every immortal of rank. The dominant turn is Wukong's question, that he is Equal to Heaven, that is a rank, where is his invitation, and Laozi's answer, that the title was so he would not need one. Wukong goes very still, without a smile, and asks it plainly. Laozi says no. Laozi owns the first half; Wukong takes the page at the stillness. The reader turns because he now knows what the title meant. The basket and the stones stay subordinate.

## PAGE 32

Dramatic. The Hall of Jade laid for the banquet and empty of guests: long tables, trays of peaches, jars of wine, covered dishes, the throne bare at the far end. Wukong alone in the doorway decides the food is going to waste. The dominant turn is the eating: he is up on the top table beneath the empty throne, in it to the elbows, a wine jar tipped to his mouth, trays going over, apologizing to a peach for being the one who eats it. Then he weaves out through a side door with a jar under his arm and his cap crooked, into a corridor lit red at the far end, and chooses red. Wukong owns the page alone; no one else is in the hall. The reader turns because he is drunk and has walked toward the one room in Heaven he should not enter. The feast's inventory stays subordinate to one monkey on one table.

## PAGE 33

Dramatic. Laozi's laboratory: a round room of black iron and cinnabar red, shelves of gourds and jars, the bronze furnace taller than a man at the center. Wukong sways in the doorway, recognizes the peach man's place, and goes looking. The dominant turn is the five gourds on the highest shelf, unstoppered, gold pills pouring into his palm like sweets, his face lit gold from below, eating them by the handful and planning to take some home to Old Ma. Then the five gourds lie empty on the floor and he sits among them stone sober, eyes clear, looking at his own hands, which give off a faint light: those were not sweets, that was the elixir, all five, and he feels wonderful. Wukong owns the page alone; Laozi is not here. The reader turns because he ate five hundred years of Laozi's work by accident, and it worked. The furnace is scenery and stays subordinate.

## PAGE 34

Dramatic. The Hall of Jade wrecked after the banquet: tables over, trays empty, robed guests standing about with nothing to do, the Emperor at the throne. Laozi arrives at a run with five empty gourds and tells the Emperor where he was: counting. The monkey ate the elixir, five hundred years of work. The dominant turn is Laozi's face changing. The Emperor asks what now, another title, a bigger house, and the man who invented every offer says no more offers; he cannot be killed and cannot be managed; send everything, and send Erlang. On the terrace the army pours out of the South Gate like ink, and the Emperor lays a hand on Laozi's shoulder, the first time he has touched anyone. Laozi owns the page; the Emperor's touch is the aftermath. The reader turns to meet the one person in Heaven who will enjoy this. The guests and the army stay subordinate to two faces.
