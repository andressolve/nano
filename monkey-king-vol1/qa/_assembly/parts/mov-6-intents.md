Dramatic. The sky over Flower-Fruit Mountain, storm-lit, Heaven's army hanging in a ring of cloud around the peak and not attacking. In front of it, alone on a cloud, stands the one person Laozi said would enjoy this: Erlang, young, in silver armor with a single red plume, an eye open in the middle of his forehead, a lance upright, a lean gray hound at his heel. Wukong waits on the peak under his banner with the staff out. The page belongs to their meeting. Erlang introduces himself like an equal and asks whether the banner is true; Wukong asks which eye to hit first, and Erlang laughs, really laughs, because nobody has ever asked him that. Then lance and staff meet, the shock throws the cloud outward, and Erlang says "Good." The dominant turn: Erlang introduces himself; the fight begins. The army stays scenery.

## PAGE 36

Spectacle, nearly silent. The duel of shapes, read top to bottom, from the sky over Flower-Fruit Mountain down to the pool under the waterfall. High up, a gold sparrow dives with a hawk on its tail, and the hawk has an eye in its forehead. In the pool, a gold fish flashes under the surface and a cormorant with a red crest strikes down through the water; this middle image owns the page. In the reeds at the pool's edge, a gold water snake, and a crane with a red plume stepping down onto it, one foot raised. No monkey and no man appear; the two fighters are only their shapes, gold against silver-and-red. One caption over the sky says what the reader is watching. The dominant turn: the duel of shapes, top to bottom. The reader turns because for the first time someone can keep up, and it will not end in the water.

## PAGE 37

Dramatic, and funny. The slope of Flower-Fruit Mountain below the peak. A small temple stands where no temple was: red doors, a round window, an offering box, and behind it a flagpole that is furred, with a tuft on the end. Erlang, human again, walks round it with the lance on his shoulder, admiring it aloud, then says he has never seen a temple with the flagpole round the back. The hound sniffs the pole. The round window blinks and says it is a new style. Erlang announces he will kick the door in and hit whatever is inside. The temple bursts back into a monkey mid-leap, running up the slope toward the peak, Erlang after him, the hound after both, the two of them shouting about whether he said he never runs. The dominant turn: the temple and the flagpole; he runs. Something above him in the clouds has been waiting for exactly this.

## PAGE 38

Dramatic. The peak of Flower-Fruit Mountain. Wukong runs, looking back at Erlang, not up. High in the clouds, Laozi on his own small cloud holds out a dull iron ring the size of a bracelet, unsmiling, and asks forgiveness: he offered everything Heaven had, and this is the last thing he has that is his. The ring, grown to a cartwheel, comes down on the back of Wukong's head. He pitches forward, the staff flies from his hand, the hound is already in the air. Then the aftermath: face-down under his own banner, the ring round his head, the hound's jaws on his leg, Erlang's boot on the staff, soldiers closing with chains, and he is laughing. Erlang wants it understood this was not him; Wukong counts them all, army, Erlang, dog, bracelet, and wants that said in the hall. The dominant turn: the ring from behind; the hound; chains; it took all of you.
