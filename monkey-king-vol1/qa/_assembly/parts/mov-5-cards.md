### C1 — Prose transcription fails
Each of the three prose paragraphs reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, as one field over plain ground, with no other text on the page. A paragraph that does not read or is split into scattered boxes is blocking; a paragraph that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — The turn does not land
The page does not read as illustrated prose in which one image owns it: a golden monkey lying in the fork of the topmost tree, eating a peach the size of his head, above three terraces. If the reader cannot tell he is at the top and eating, the page fails.

### C3 — Wukong is not himself
The figure does not read as the golden monkey in Great Sage armor with plumed cap and tail. A human face or gray fur is blocking; armor detail is not.

### C4 — The stones do not tell the story
The reader cannot see many peach stones on the ground beneath his tree. Their exact count is nonblocking.

A gardener, an old man, or any figure but Wukong is visible, spoiling the arrival the next page owes.

### C6 — Prose sits over busy art
The field lies across blossom, the tree, or the figure so that reading it means fighting the picture. Field colour is nonblocking when the text reads over calm ground.

### C7 — Focal generation failure
Wukong's face, hands, or the peach is malformed or duplicated. Leaf texture and distant blossom do not count.
