## Mandatory findings

1. **The eye geometry misses the lock.** Across the sheet, the exposed eye openings read as narrow, slanted almonds; the lock requires round golden eyes with slit pupils. Enlarge and round the visible golden eye aperture in all four views so its height is at least three-quarters of its width, retain one centered vertical slit pupil, and keep the surrounding brow heavy without compressing the eye back into a slit. The standing panic view should widen this same round eye rather than substitute a different shape.

2. **The facial-hair mass is hair-like rather than a beard of fins.** The chin carries a long, stringy white goatee that can read as a human elder's beard, while the required fin structure is displaced into the mane behind the jaw. Replace the chin goatee in every view with overlapping blue-green fin plates rooted continuously along the underside of the jaw and converging beneath the chin. Keep the two long white whiskers as the only filament-like facial elements; do not leave a separate hanging hair beard.

3. **The scale witness makes Ao Guang far larger than the locked proportion.** In the seated view, the human silhouette is roughly one quarter of Ao Guang's antler-tip-to-foot height, so the image reads closer to four human heights than the specified two. Enlarge the human witness, or reduce Ao Guang and the throne together, until the witness's head-to-foot height is one half of Ao Guang's seated antler-tip-to-foot height. Preserve the immense body and the fact that he remains larger than every ordinary man.

4. **The habitual gesture does not read as drumming.** Both seated hands currently read as resting on or gripping coral. Put one hand flat over a clear horizontal section of the throne arm and stagger the fingers into an unmistakable beat: index and ring fingertips touching the arm, middle fingertip visibly raised, little finger relaxed. Preserve that hand arrangement as the seated primary marker rather than a clawed grip.
