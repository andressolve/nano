## Mandatory findings

1. A bright horizontal white divider runs across the full sheet between the throne-room view and the banquet view. This is a blocking border. Remove the rule entirely and rebuild both views as separate unframed studies that vignette into one continuous warm-paper ground; no hard line, box edge, panel frame, or contrasting gutter may remain anywhere between them.
2. The banquet view introduces saturated cinnabar-red side pillars and strong azurite-blue wall bands, so the color hierarchy no longer reads as the specified pale gold and jade; at thumbnail and in grayscale, those side pillars and the rows of dark lanterns compete with the throne steps, the location's primary marker. Repaint every red side pillar jade-green and reduce the blue wall bands to pale, desaturated jade shadow; keep pale gold on the throne axis and step nosings. Lower the lantern poles and tabletop clutter below the top of the stair flight, preserve a fully uninterrupted central aisle, and increase the value separation of each step edge so the throne's steps are the single dominant compact marker in both views at thumbnail and after a long absence.

## Nonblocking observations

- Both required set states are present: an empty throne room and the same hall laid with long banquet tables.
- The throne, raised flight of steps, jade-green pillars, and reflective still-water floor are all structurally legible, including in the reduced view.
- No figures, lettering, labels, or watermarks appear, and no consequential object is shown in a wrong state.
- The top view already establishes convincing monumental scale and a useful centered politics axis; retain that spatial organization through the redesign.
