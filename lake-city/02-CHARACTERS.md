# 02 — CHARACTERS

**Only Olin is invented.** Everyone else on this list is a real person and is handled per `RESEARCH.md`. (One exception: Xiuhtototzin, the Mexica boy, is also invented — see his entry.)

## Rules for using these locks

- **Never put the historical name in the prompt.** The model knows "Moctezuma" and "Cortés" and will drift toward stock portraiture. The visual description *is* the lock; the name lives in the script, the ref filename, and the captions.
- **Look at the ref PNG before you write a page prompt that uses it.** Not the lock block — the actual pixels. Write a one-line observation of what you see. Every bad-drift incident in this collection's history traces back to describing a character from memory.
- Multi-reference `edit_image` is available: pass every locked character on the page via `imagePaths` (up to 16). Do not describe an unanchored character in prose when you could just attach their ref.
- **Costume is not decoration in this book — it is law.** Nahua sumptuary rules meant cotton and ankle-length mantles were legally restricted to nobles, and violation could mean death. Olin's coarse knee-length maguey cloth beside Xiuhtototzin's ankle-length cotton is a *plot point* rendered in fabric. Never let the model prettify Olin's clothes.
- Two visual castes, and they must never blur:
  - **Commoner (Olin, Tototl):** coarse pale-brown **maguey fibre**, mantle ending **above the knee**, no jewellery, **barefoot in the city** (plain fibre sandals only on the road).
  - **Noble (the lords, Moctezuma, Xiuhtototzin):** fine **cotton**, mantle to the **ankle**, embroidered borders, **jade earspools**, sandals.

## 1. OLIN — the protagonist ⭐

Seventeen. Tlaxcalan commoner. A runner and porter. The boy nobody looks at, in every room where something gets decided.

**LOCK — paste verbatim:**
> A lean, wiry seventeen-year-old youth, sun-darkened copper-brown skin, sharp watchful face with high cheekbones and a strong straight nose, dark brown eyes. Straight black hair cut bluntly at the jawline, with ONE narrow uncut lock hanging at the nape of the neck. A twisted headband of RED AND WHITE cloth tied around his forehead. He wears a coarse pale-brown fibre cloak, plainly woven and undyed, knotted over the RIGHT shoulder and ending ABOVE THE KNEE, over a plain wrapped loincloth. NO jewellery of any kind, NO feathers, NO ornament. Barefoot, or plain fibre sandals. A rolled bundle carried on his back by a strap across his forehead. Lean runner's build, upright, chin slightly raised, alert.
> Realistic teenage anatomy. NOT cute, NOT mascot proportions, NOT oversized eyes.

**Why the nape lock matters.** The *piochtli* was cut only when a boy took his first captive in war. Olin still has his. It is visible in nearly every panel, it is the first thing another Nahua character would read about him, and it means: *this one has never fought.* When he finally understands what war costs, the lock is still there. **Do not let the model tidy it away** — if a page comes back without it, regenerate.

**Voice.** First person, past tense, in the caption boxes. Plain, physical, a little proud, increasingly unsure. He notices *how things are made* — that's his eye. He never explains a custom to the reader as a teacher would; he says what he wanted and what happened.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_olin.png`

## 2. TOTOTL — the older porter

About forty. Tlaxcalan, same village world as Olin. Carried loads all his life. Sour, funny, and the only person who tells Olin the truth in plain words. He is Olin's own class talking back to him — without him, Olin only ever talks to nobles.

**LOCK:**
> A short, powerfully built man of about forty, deep copper-brown weathered skin, broad flat face, heavy shoulders and thick forearms, grey streaking his cropped black hair, a deep old callus-scar across his forehead from years of carrying loads by a tumpline strap. A twisted RED AND WHITE cloth headband. Coarse pale-brown undyed fibre cloak knotted at the shoulder, ending above the knee, plain wrapped loincloth. NO jewellery. Barefoot or plain fibre sandals. A large bundle roped on his back. Sceptical, tired, unimpressed expression.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_tototl.png`

## 3. XICOTENCATL THE YOUNGER — the man who was right

Real. Captain-general of the Tlaxcalan armies. **The only lord who refused the alliance** — he massed twenty thousand men to attack the strangers in defiance of his own father and Maxixcatzin, who sent four sets of messengers ordering him to stop. In this book he is not a hothead; he is the character whose warning the reader remembers on the last page.

**LOCK:**
> A hard-faced Nahua warrior in his early thirties, copper-brown skin, broad jaw, a thin old scar along one cheekbone, black eyes. Black hair bound up into a tall stiff topknot at the crown, tied with red cord. His mantle is divided vertically down the middle — one half solid RED, the other half solid WHITE — worn over a thick quilted white cotton torso armour. Jade earspools. Standing squarely, weight forward, arms folded or one hand on a wooden club. Contained anger, absolute certainty.

**Voice.** Short sentences. He argues about consequences, never about honour.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_xicotencatl_younger.png`

## 4. XICOTENCATL THE ELDER — the blind father

Real. Lord of Tizatlan, blind with age. **For the alliance** — against his son. He is also, per the sources, one of the lords who then warned *against* marching on Mexico at all. Pro-alliance did not mean pro-adventure, and he carries that whole contradiction in one scene.

**LOCK:**
> A very old Nahua lord, perhaps seventy-five, deeply lined dark copper-brown face, thin white hair, blind — pale clouded eyes that do not focus, chin lifted to listen rather than to look. A long fine cotton mantle to the ankles, divided vertically half RED and half WHITE, embroidered border. Large jade earspools, a jade pendant. Seated on a low reed-backed seat, one hand out on the arm of an attendant. Frail, absolutely composed.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_xicotencatl_elder.png`

## 5. MAXIXCATZIN — the lord who opened the gate

Real. Lord of Ocotelolco, the wealthiest of the four Tlaxcalan states. Argued **for** the alliance. Not naive — he is trading one enemy for one risk and he knows exactly what he's doing.

**LOCK:**
> A heavy-set Nahua lord in his late fifties, dark copper-brown skin, broad calm face, deep folds at the mouth, black hair greying and bound at the nape. Long fine white cotton mantle to the ankles with a wide embroidered red-and-jade border, worn over an embroidered loincloth. Large jade earspools, a turquoise pendant, sandals. Standing with both hands loosely clasped. Shrewd, courteous, unhurried.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_maxixcatzin.png`

## 6. MALINTZIN — the interpreter

Real. About nineteen or twenty. Every word between the Spanish and the Nahua world passes through her, which makes her the most powerful person in the book who holds no title. She takes Olin on because he is fast and keeps his mouth shut. She is not warm and she is not cruel; she is a professional in an impossible position, and she reads Olin better than he reads himself.

**LOCK:**
> A composed young Nahua woman of about twenty, smooth copper-brown skin, oval face, level dark eyes, black eyebrows. Long straight black hair, parted centrally and falling loose past her shoulders. A fine WHITE cotton sleeveless tunic with a broad woven band of red and jade-green geometric pattern at the square neckline, over a long white wrapped skirt with an embroidered hem. Jade earspools, a single jade bead necklace, sandals. She stands very straight, weight even, hands still, head slightly turned as if listening to two people at once. Watchful, unreadable, entirely self-possessed.

**Voice.** Precise. She never wastes a word, and she asks Olin questions instead of answering his.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_malintzin.png`

## 7. THE SPANISH CAPTAIN (Cortés)

Real. Mid-thirties. **Do not write him as a villain and do not write him as a hero** — Olin's whole problem is that the man is courteous, attentive, and impossible to read. He is generally at middle distance, seen past somebody's shoulder. Keep his face out of close-up except where the script calls for it.

**LOCK:**
> A Spanish captain in his mid-thirties, pale olive skin, dark close-trimmed beard and moustache, dark alert eyes, high forehead, black hair to the collar. A black quilted velvet doublet with slashed sleeves, a steel gorget and breastplate over it, a dull red-brown wool cloak thrown back over one shoulder, high dusty riding boots, a sword at the hip. Compact build, still posture, hands quiet. Watchful and courteous, giving nothing away.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_captain.png`

## 8. THE SPEAKER (Moctezuma II)

Real. **Per the eyewitness who saw him: he looked about forty, well proportioned, slender and muscular, "not very attractive," hair only just covering his ears, a thin well-shaped scanty beard, a longish cheerful face, good eyes — and he was merry and ironic in speech.** Play him that way: a witty, curious, exhausted man, not a doomed idol. He bathed every day and never wore the same clothes twice within four days, so his mantle may change colour between scenes — his *face and build must not.*

**LOCK:**
> A Nahua ruler who appears about forty to fifty: slender, well proportioned, lightly muscular, of good height, coppery-brown skin. A long face, cheerful and intelligent, dark expressive eyes, a THIN, sparse, neatly shaped black beard along the jaw and chin, and straight black hair cut short so that it just covers the ears. A magnificent ankle-length fine cotton mantle, turquoise and white with a deep gold-and-jade embroidered border, over an embroidered loincloth. A narrow TURQUOISE MOSAIC DIADEM across the brow, large jade earspools, a turquoise pendant. Sandals with GOLD SOLES — he is the ONLY figure wearing shoes in any scene with other Nahua people. Relaxed, upright, faintly amused.
> He must NOT be gaunt, NOT sinister, NOT heavily bearded, NOT wearing a giant feather headdress.

**The gold-soled sandals are the whole class system in one object.** In the causeway scene every other lord is barefoot, including four-tier nobility. Keep the feet visible.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_speaker.png`

## 9. XIUHTOTOTZIN — "Turquoise Bird" (invented)

Fourteen. A Mexica noble boy attached to the palace household, in school at the *calmecac*. **Invented** — checked against the record, the name belongs to no known historical figure. He is the reason the fall of this city will hurt.

He is Olin's exact opposite and exact mirror: same age-ish, same curiosity, opposite side of a hundred-year war, and separated by a fabric law. **He must never be written as cruel or as a snob-for-laughs.** He is generous, a bit grand, funny about his own city, and completely unable to imagine that it could end.

**LOCK:**
> A well-fed, straight-backed Nahua boy of about fourteen, smooth light copper-brown skin, round open face, bright curious dark eyes, straight black hair cut cleanly at the level of his ears. A fine WHITE cotton mantle reaching his ANKLES, knotted on one shoulder, with a woven turquoise-and-red embroidered border, over an embroidered white loincloth. Jade earspools and a small carved TURQUOISE BIRD pendant at his throat. Neat fibre sandals. Clean, unmarked hands. Direct, friendly, slightly grand posture.
> Realistic child anatomy. NOT cute, NOT mascot proportions, NOT oversized eyes.

**Ref:** `refs/ref_xiuhtototzin.png`

## Reference-sheet generation

Nine refs. Generate one at a time with `generate_image`, review each against its lock, regenerate until right. **No page is generated until all nine pass the gate.** Cost ≈ $1.90.

Template — substitute the character's LOCK block:

```text
Character reference sheet, landscape format. TWO views of the SAME single
person side by side on one plain warm-toned neutral background: a
three-quarter FULL BODY standing view on the left, and a larger HEAD AND
SHOULDERS portrait on the right. Neutral expression, neutral standing pose,
even light, no scenery, no props beyond those named.

STYLE: Bold ink-line comic art with FLAT vivid color — clean confident black
linework, flat saturated color fills, minimal soft rendering, graphic and
heroic. The linework and shape language are informed by Mesoamerican codex
painting: strong black contours, stylized stepped and scrolling forms,
ornament rendered as clean flat pattern. Palette: turquoise and jade,
cochineal red, ochre and maize yellow, lime-white, obsidian black.

NOT a children's book. Serious mature graphic novel, realistic heroic
proportions, no cuteness, no oversized eyes, no pastel softness.

THE PERSON:
[LOCK BLOCK, verbatim]

NO text, NO labels, NO captions, NO watermark, NO signature. One person only.
```

Size `1536x1024`, `quality: "high"`.

**Casting gate — check each ref before it passes:**
- Right age? (Olin must read seventeen, not twelve and not twenty-five.)
- **Caste right?** Coarse knee-length fibre on the commoners; cotton to the ankle plus jade on the nobles. If Olin looks well-dressed, the ref has failed.
- **Olin's nape lock present?** **Moctezuma's beard thin and his hair just over the ears?** **The gold soles visible?** **The red-and-white headbands?**
- Register matches `style-samples/sample-A-codex-inkline.png`?
- Could you pick this person out of a line-up of the other eight?
