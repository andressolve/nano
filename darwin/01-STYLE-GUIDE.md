# 01 — STYLE GUIDE

## Register
Oil-painting realism, matching the Newton / Gauss / da Vinci / Tesla volumes. This is a 19th-century
naturalist's drama — the quiet world of the collector, the ship's deck, the specimen cabinet, the
garden laboratory. The visual signature is **the green-gold of living nature breaking into the
scholar's brown, paper-and-mahogany world**, and a recurring **branching-form motif** (coral,
roots, veins, rigging, branches) that culminates in the closing Tree of Life.

## STYLE BLOCK — paste verbatim into every page prompt
> Oil-painting realism, cinematic period illustration. Muted 19th-century naturalist palette: warm specimen-cabinet sepia, mahogany and aged paper, ship-timber grey and cold sea-light — pierced by the green-gold of living nature (leaves, moss, plumage, sunlit foliage) as the warm accent. Painterly brushwork, soft natural light, film-still composition, shallow depth of field. Georgian/Victorian England and the wild coasts of South America. Visible canvas texture, no photographic sheen.

## REGISTER BLOCK — paste verbatim into every page prompt
> Oil-painting realism. NOT a comic. NO halftones, NO cel shading, NO ink linework. Painted brushwork, cinematic lighting, muted period palette.

## ANTI-DRIFT DIRECTIVE — paste verbatim into every page prompt
> NOT a children's book. Serious mature graphic novel, realistic proportions, natural lighting, cinematic composition.

## Palette
- **Base:** warm sepia, umber, mahogany, aged-paper cream, candle/oil-lamp amber for interiors.
- **Nature / the accent:** the **green-gold of living things** — sunlit leaves, moss, foliage, bird plumage, the sea's blue-green. Wherever life itself is the subject, this warm living color appears against the brown study-world. This is the through-line: *nature speaking.*
- **Sea / voyage scenes:** cold grey-green ocean, pale sky, weathered ship timber, brass instruments.
- **Late / Down House pages:** soft English greens, garden light, study-brown — settled, patient, domestic.

## The branching-form motif (quiet through-line)
Let a **branching shape** sit somewhere in many pages — coral on a specimen tray, tree roots, the veins of a leaf, the ship's rigging against sky, a river delta from the deck, the branching cracks of the lifted Chilean shore. Never labeled, never forced. It primes the eye for the **P18 Tree of Life**, where the motif becomes the structure of the page.

## Camera language
- Cinematic single-image pages (default). Eye-level intimacy for the study and garden beats; low wide horizons for the voyage; close-up on hands-and-specimens for the collector beats.
- Soft natural light — window light, candlelight, tropical sun, grey sea-light. Less hard chiaroscuro than Tesla; this is a gentler, more contemplative book.
- Montage finale (P18): the branching Tree of Life as the page's structure, life-vignettes plotted along the branches.

## Narration treatment (the Honda formula)
- **Caption boxes:** "off-white box, dark serif text, readable." Hold this style across the whole volume. Period variant allowed: "aged-cream paper with serif ink."
- **Hero pages (T4–T5):** a top caption band sets the moment, a bottom band closes it; 50–80 words each is fine on gpt-image-2.
- **Speech bubbles:** round, off-white, dark serif, tail explicitly described. Under 15 words. **No quotation marks inside bubbles — the bubble shape IS the quote.**
- **Quote artifacts:** the **"I think"** sketch (P8), the **"like confessing a murder"** line (P10), and the closing **"endless forms most beautiful"** passage (P15/P18) are rendered as the page's emotional artwork — period serif / period handwriting.
- **Lettering block** opens with `LETTERING — verbatim, render exactly:` and lists each element by position + exact string. Close every prompt with the restrictions block.

## In-image text → always clear to a first-time reader
- All captions stand alone: a reader who has never heard of Darwin must follow on first read. No teaser captions, no withheld punchlines, no ungrounded names (gloss "Henslow," "Gould," "Wallace," "the Beagle" on first use).
- The Latin of *Megatherium* etc., if shown, gets a plain-English gloss in the caption.

## Restrictions block — close every prompt with this
> All words spelled correctly. Do not duplicate text. Do not invent extra captions. NO modern logos, NO watermarks, NO spurious signage, NO modern clothing or objects in period scenes.

## Period-accuracy guardrails
- 1809–1882 England + 1830s South America. Georgian → Victorian dress: frock coats, high collars, cravats, waistcoats; bonnets and full skirts for women.
- HMS Beagle = a small three-masted survey ship (barque-rigged 10-gun brig-sloop), wooden, weathered — NOT a galleon, NOT a modern ship.
- Specimen science: glass-fronted cabinets, cork trays of pinned beetles, jars, calipers, field notebooks, a brass microscope, pressed plants.
- NO anachronisms: no modern field gear, no DNA imagery, no modern zoo enclosures, no 20th-century clothing.

## BEARD DISCIPLINE (the wig-on/wig-off rule for this volume — load-bearing)
Darwin grew his famous beard only ~1862. **State the facial-hair state explicitly in EVERY prompt:**
- **Boy / Young / Mid Darwin (P1–P14): CLEAN-SHAVEN, no beard** (young = fresh-faced; mid = side-whiskers at most, balding crown). Without the explicit "clean-shaven, NO beard," the model reverts to the iconic bearded portrait and the whole pre-1862 arc drifts old.
- **Old Darwin (cover, P16–P18): long full WHITE beard, bald crown, heavy brow.**

## Anti-drift watchlist
- Young Darwin must read **early-20s, slim, fresh-faced, clean-shaven** — NOT a young version of the bearded sage.
- Keep the green-gold accent disciplined: it marks where *life* is the subject. Don't flood every page green.
- The book is contemplative, not melodramatic. Soft light, patient framing. No heroic posturing.
- Old Darwin (P16–P18) must look genuinely ~70 — bald, long white beard, heavy overhanging brow.
