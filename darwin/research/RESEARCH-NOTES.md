## Verified chronology

- **1809-02-12** — Born, Shrewsbury, England. Wealthy family (father Robert, a doctor; mother Susannah Wedgwood, of the pottery family).
- **1817** — Mother dies when Charles is 8. He is already an avid collector (minerals, shells, beetles, birds' eggs).
- **1825–1827** — University of Edinburgh, to study **medicine**. Hates it. Loathed anatomy; could not stand watching **surgery performed without anaesthetic** (pre-chloroform) — it sickened him. Bored by lectures. Leaves after two years without a degree.
- **1828–1831** — **Christ's College, Cambridge**, intended for the clergy (a respectable berth for an aimless naturalist). Famous **beetle-collecting** passion. Mentored by the **Rev. John Stevens Henslow** (botany professor); took Henslow's course three times. Henslow is the man who conveys the **invitation to join HMS Beagle**. Graduated 10th in his BA, 1831.
- **1831-12-27** — **HMS Beagle sails from Plymouth** under **Captain Robert FitzRoy**. Darwin is **22**, aboard as a self-funded gentleman naturalist / companion to the captain. (Earlier departures on 10 & 21 Dec were turned back by gales.)
- **1831–1836** — The voyage. Planned for two years; lasted **almost five**. Darwin spent **~3 years 3 months on land**, ~18 months at sea. South America dominates: fossil giant mammals (e.g. *Megatherium*), the **1835 Chile earthquake** (saw the land lifted), **seashells high in the Andes** — evidence the earth and its life are not fixed. (Lyell's *Principles of Geology*, which he read aboard, framed deep, gradual change.)
- **1835 (Sept–Oct)** — **Galápagos**. Tortoises, mockingbirds, marine iguanas, finches. **CRITICAL HONEST BEAT:** Darwin did **NOT** have an epiphany on the islands. He kept believing species were fixed for months afterward. He barely labelled the finches by island and had to reconstruct it later. His **earliest doubts came from the mockingbirds and the tortoises**, noticed on the homeward voyage — not the finches, and not on the spot.
- **1836-10-02** — Beagle **returns** to England.
- **1837-01-04 / 01-10** — Darwin's bird specimens go to ornithologist **John Gould** at the Zoological Society. Gould reports (10 Jan 1837) that the birds Darwin took for blackbirds, "gross-beaks" and finches are in fact **twelve species of one entirely new group of ground-finches**. The mockingbirds are distinct species island to island. **The pattern emerged from expert ANALYSIS after the voyage — the facts, carefully read, spoke. This is the book's central methodological milestone.**
- **1837 (~March)** — "the penny dropped": Darwin becomes convinced species change.
- **1837 (~July)** — Opens **Notebook B**, labelled **"Transmutation of Species."** On ~page 36 he draws a **branching tree** and writes **"I think"** above it — the first sketch of common descent. Branches labelled A/B/C/D; some lines stop short = **extinction**.
- **1838-09 (late)** — Reads **Thomas Malthus**, *Essay on the Principle of Population*. Struggle for existence → **the mechanism: natural selection** (favourable variations preserved, unfavourable destroyed). The tree now has an engine.
- **1839-01-29** — Marries his first cousin **Emma Wedgwood**.
- **1842** — Moves to **Down House**, Kent — his home and outdoor laboratory for the rest of his life.
- **1844** — Writes a **230-page essay** of the theory — but does **not publish**. Letter to botanist **Joseph Hooker (11 Jan 1844)**, verbatim: *"I am almost convinced (quite contrary to opinion I started with) that species are not (it is like confessing a murder) immutable."*
- **1846–1854** — **Eight years on barnacles** (cirripedes). Establishes his credentials as a working naturalist and studies variation in the smallest creatures. Patience as method.
- **1851** — Daughter **Annie dies at age 10.** Devastating. (Handle with restraint — a true beat of the cost of these years, NOT a manufactured emotional spine.)
- **1858-06-18** — Receives an essay from **Alfred Russel Wallace**, written at **Ternate** (Malay Archipelago) in Feb 1858, independently setting out **the same theory of natural selection**. Proof the idea was *in the facts* — reachable by another careful mind.
- **1858-07-01** — **Linnean Society of London.** The **joint Darwin–Wallace papers** are read (by the secretary; arranged by **Charles Lyell** and **Joseph Hooker**). **Neither author is present:** Darwin is at home **burying his infant son** (Charles Waring Darwin, d. scarlet fever); Wallace is in **New Guinea**. The shared, generous first announcement.
- **1859-11-24** — ***On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection*** published by John Murray. **1,250 copies printed** (~1,170 for sale after review/presentation copies); **sold through on the first day** (Mudie's circulating library alone took 500). Price 15 shillings. 2nd ed. (3,000 copies) 7 Jan 1860.
- **1860-06** — Famous **Oxford debate**: T.H. **Huxley** ("Darwin's bulldog") vs. Bishop **Samuel Wilberforce**. Darwin himself avoided the public fights; others carried the argument.
- **1882-04-19** — Dies at Down House. Buried in **Westminster Abbey**, near **Isaac Newton**.

## Myths / errors to AVOID (editorial honesty)

1. **NO Galápagos lightning-bolt epiphany.** The single most common Darwin myth. The islands did not shout the answer; the pattern came from Gould's later analysis. Telling it honestly IS the "let the facts speak" milestone — lean into it, don't fake the legend.
2. **Darwin did not invent the idea of evolution alone, or first.** Earlier transmutationists existed (Lamarck; his own grandfather **Erasmus Darwin**). The Darwin–**Wallace** contribution is the **mechanism (natural selection)** — and Wallace reached it independently. Credit the convergence.
3. **"Survival of the fittest" is NOT Darwin's phrase** — Herbert Spencer's; Darwin only adopted it in later editions. Avoid putting it in his mouth in 1838/1859.
4. **The *Origin* (1859) is barely about humans.** "We descend from monkeys" belongs to *The Descent of Man* (1871) and the caricaturists. Do not collapse the two.
5. **The deathbed-conversion / "Lady Hope" story is a fabrication.** Do not use.
6. **Don't overstate the finches.** The finches became the textbook icon later; at the time the **mockingbirds and tortoises** drove his doubt. Keep the finches as part of the specimen haul, with Gould's analysis as the turn.
7. **FitzRoy** later opposed Darwin's conclusions (devout); fine to leave him as the captain who brought the naturalist aboard — don't anachronistically make him an ally of the theory.

## Appearance notes for refs (BEARD DISCIPLINE = the wig discipline)

- **Boy (~8–12):** Shrewsbury, brown hair, collector. Early-1820s child's clothing.
- **Young (~19–27):** Edinburgh → Cambridge → Beagle. **CLEAN-SHAVEN. NO beard.** Brown hair, sideburns/whiskers at most, fresh-faced, slim. Regency/early-Victorian gentleman's dress.
- **Mid (~45–50):** barnacles → Origin era (1850s). **STILL CLEAN-SHAVEN / only side-whiskers — NO full beard.** Balding at the crown, high forehead, heavier brow, Victorian frock coat. (Darwin grew the famous beard only ~1862.)
- **Old (~70):** the **iconic** Darwin — **bald crown, very long full WHITE beard, heavy overhanging brow**, dark cloak. This is the recognizable portrait — use for cover + late pages.
