# Salt and Stone — Life Three Style Guide

## Style Block (Copy Verbatim to Every Prompt)

```
Art Style: Cinematic painterly realism with restrained brush texture, historical graphic novel aesthetic
Color palette: steppe green, golden grassland, pale sky blue, earth brown, amber firelight
Effects: Vast open landscapes, natural light, atmospheric depth, wind in grass
```

## Palette Notes

Life Three is the most visually distinct chapter. The claustrophobic dark blues of Salamis give way to enormous open space.

- **Steppe green** — the dominant color. Endless grassland, alive and moving
- **Golden grassland** — sun-bleached grass, wheat-gold, the warmth of the land
- **Pale sky blue** — enormous skies stretching to every horizon, light and open
- **Earth brown** — horses, leather, felt tents, Nikos's weathered skin, the ground
- **Amber firelight** — campfires at night, warm interiors of felt tents, sunset light

### Palette Transitions Across the Chapter

- **Pages 1-4 (Athens):** Carry forward Life Two's bronze gold and warm stone tones. Familiar but slightly cramped — the city should feel smaller than before.
- **Pages 5-7 (Sea/Storm):** Brief return to dark ocean blue and storm gray. This is the last time we see the sea.
- **Pages 8-10 (Arrival on steppe):** The palette cracks open. First wide steppe shot should feel like a visual exhale. Greens and golds flood in.
- **Pages 11-20 (Steppe life):** Full steppe palette. The warmest, most expansive visuals in the entire trilogy. By the final pages, the light should feel golden and eternal.

---

## Panel Rules

- **3-4 panels per page maximum**
- Black panel borders, consistent weight
- Clean white gutters
- No decorative borders
- Caption boxes: simple rectangular, muted parchment background, dark text
- Speech bubbles: classic white with black outline
- **NEW FOR LIFE THREE:** Several pages should have a single full-page panel (the steppe demands it). Use these for: first steppe vista (page 8), the horse-riding lesson (page 13), and the final page (page 20).

## Shot Type Reference

| Shot | Use For |
|------|---------|
| Ultra-Wide/Panoramic | The steppe — this chapter's signature shot. Huge sky, tiny figures. |
| Wide/Establishing | Athens skyline, storm at sea, Scythian camp |
| Medium | Conversation, riding, camp life |
| Close-up | Emotion, the shell necklace, the wooden ship, hands |
| Low Angle | Horses, Scythian riders, the sky |
| High Angle | Nikos small on the steppe, vulnerability |
| POV | Looking out across endless grass, looking up at stars |

## What This Should Look Like

- The steppe sequences: Terrence Malick's "The New World" — natural light, wind, grass, real bodies in real landscape
- The riding scenes: Sergei Bodrov's "Mongol" — grounded, physical, cinematic
- Athens pages: continuation of Life Two's Ridley Scott feel
- The storm: same as Life Two's Salamis palette but briefer

## What This Should NOT Look Like

- NOT fantasy pastoral (no soft-focus meadows with butterflies)
- NOT children's book illustration at any point
- NOT romanticized "noble savage" imagery — the Scythians should feel like real people with a real culture, not a Western fantasy
- NOT anime or manga
- The steppe is beautiful but also harsh — wind, dust, cold nights, hard ground
