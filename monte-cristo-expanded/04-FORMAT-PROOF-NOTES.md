# The Count of Monte Cristo — Portrait Format Proof

## Decision

**The expanded edition is locked to 1024 × 1536, 2:3 portrait.**

The three monochrome proofs were completed and reviewed on 2026-07-26. Portrait
passed the three page types most likely to expose a format weakness:

1. opposed dialogue and recognition;
2. illustrated prose across elapsed time;
3. large-scale spectacle.

The reader will not mix portrait and landscape story canvases. Wide cinematic
moments will be created as full-width bands or dominant fields inside the
portrait page.

## Proof 1 — Page 15: Noirtier

Narrative job: Villefort recognizes his father's name while Edmond continues to
trust him.

### Portrait result

- The envelope and name establish the cause at the top.
- A wide central two-shot preserves the opposition between Villefort and
  Edmond.
- Separate reaction insets distinguish Villefort's fear from Edmond's trust.
- The silent decision occupies the bottom of the page, turning reading order
  into moral descent.

### Landscape result

Landscape gives the two men more lateral space, but the envelope, reaction, and
decision become a side column. Recognition and consequence compete on the same
horizontal plane.

### Finding

Portrait is stronger because it makes the causal sequence unavoidable:
**name → recognition → unequal knowledge → decision**.

## Proof 2 — Page 21: Years Without an Answer

Narrative job: make elapsed prison time physically felt before the sound behind
the wall introduces Faria.

### Portrait result

- A generous prose field establishes petitions, seasons, and institutional
  silence.
- Repeated images of Edmond descend through the page: demands, counts, stops.
- The small final sound becomes a precise page-turn trigger.

### Landscape result

The three moments are clear side by side, but they read as a comparison rather
than accumulated duration. The prose field is shallower, and the final sound
has less gravitational force.

### Finding

Portrait wins decisively. Time becomes a downward experience instead of a row
of adjacent illustrations.

## Proof 3 — Page 42: Monte Cristo

Narrative job: turn Faria's instructions into a physical discovery whose scale
changes Edmond's future.

### Portrait result

- The split rock, ring, and descent form a legible approach.
- The treasure chamber receives the dominant lower field.
- Edmond can appear tiny inside a wide internal cavern, preserving scale.
- The page itself performs descent before revelation.

### Landscape result

Landscape produces the widest raw panorama and remains an excellent
composition inside a single panel. As a complete page, however, the approach
beats read more like adjacent illustrations than necessary steps.

### Finding

This was the closest comparison. Portrait still wins the sequence because a
wide lower cavern preserves spectacle while the vertical approach preserves
causality.

## What the Proofs Lock

- Every expanded-edition story page is 1024 × 1536 portrait.
- One dominant field normally receives 45–70% of visual attention.
- Full-width internal panels preserve the first edition's cinematic breadth.
- Vertical order carries time, descent, consequence, isolation, and page-turn
  rhythm.
- Prose fields are composed before art and never treated as leftover space.
- Landscape first-edition pages remain references; they are not resized into
  the expanded edition.
- No full production run begins before the Pages 12–18 continuous prototype
  passes editorial and visual QA.

## Why This Is Not a Reversal of the First Edition

The landscape first edition was designed as a dialogue-first cinematic graphic
novel. Its format remains coherent for that work.

The expanded edition has a different reading problem and a different form: a
cinematic illustrated novel with more prose, slower causal sequencing, and
greater emotional duration. Portrait serves that form without abandoning
Velvet Cinema or the use of wide internal compositions.
