Every story page is **1024 × 1536, 2:3 portrait**. Page titles and production
labels below do not appear in the finished art. Only text inside each page's
**Exact image text** section may appear on that page.

This sequence must be read continuously. It is not seven unrelated page
samples.

## Sequence Logic

The reader enters knowing that Edmond has been arrested at his betrothal feast
because an anonymous accusation connected him to Elba. He believes an honest
answer will send him home.

The prototype must make this chain effortless:

> Villefort needs the Saint-Mérans' royalist future → Noirtier is Villefort's
> Bonapartist father → Edmond innocently carries a letter to Noirtier →
> Villefort recognizes Edmond's innocence → Villefort destroys the evidence →
> Villefort creates a prison order → the order becomes Edmond's official
> identity.

## Page 12 — Two Betrothals

### Page record

- **Narrative job:** Introduce Villefort's desired future and the father he is
  trying to leave behind before Edmond enters the room.
- **Reader state entering:** Edmond has been taken from his betrothal feast for
  questioning about a Bonapartist accusation.
- **Desire and opposition:** Villefort wants marriage into a powerful royalist
  family; his father Noirtier's Bonapartist name threatens that future.
- **Dominant mode:** Illustrated prose / dramatic scene.
- **Density tier:** T4.
- **Visual engine:** Hierarchy and interrupted celebration. The grand room
  narrows from public warmth to a dangerous paper entering through a doorway.
- **Page archetypes:** Hierarchy page; doorway as interruption.

### Panel map

1. **Prose field — upper 15%.** Calm matte-parchment field with generous
   margins. No art behind the text.
2. **Dominant panel — next 43%.** Villefort and Renée at the center of an
   elegant royalist betrothal supper. Renée is left, Villefort right. Guests
   and candles establish privilege without becoming a crowd puzzle.
3. **Opposed panel — next 22%.** The Marquise de Saint-Méran left; Villefort
   right. Renée remains visible but silent behind Villefort.
4. **Consequence panel — lower 16%.** An official enters from the left holding
   the anonymous accusation. Villefort faces him from the right. Renée is a
   silent background figure.

### Exact image text

**Prose field**

> At the same hour, another betrothal filled a grander room. Renée de
> Saint-Méran's family had returned with the King. Gérard de Villefort meant to
> join their future—and leave his father's name behind.

**Panel 2**

1. Renée: “Tonight, no trials.”
2. Villefort: “Tonight, only us.”

**Panel 3**

3. Marquise: “Your father made us fear the name Noirtier.”
4. Villefort: “Then remember mine. He is Noirtier. I am de Villefort—and I
   serve the King.”

**Panel 4**

5. Official: “A sailor arrested at his betrothal. Accused of carrying a
   Bonapartist letter from Elba.”
6. Villefort: “If he is innocent, I will send him home.”

## Page 13 — A Dying Captain's Word

### Page record

- **Narrative job:** Turn the suspicious Elba journey into an honorable promise
  made to a dying captain.
- **Reader state entering:** Villefort has promised mercy if the arrested
  sailor is innocent.
- **Desire and opposition:** Edmond wants to tell the simple truth and return
  to his feast; Villefort must determine whether obedience concealed political
  intent.
- **Dominant mode:** Dramatic scene.
- **Density tier:** T3.
- **Visual engine:** The sealed letter remains between the two men while
  Edmond's account moves from identity to duty to ignorance.
- **Page archetype:** Examination as opposed dialogue.

### Panel map

1. **Establishing panel — upper 21%.** Villefort seated left behind the desk;
   Edmond standing right with open space around him. One question and one
   answer.
2. **Dominant panel — next 43%.** Edmond alone in three-quarter view, telling
   the story. Behind him, a soft, clearly subordinate memory-image of Captain
   Leclère dying aboard the *Pharaon*. The memory contains no text and no
   additional speaking character.
3. **Question panel — next 13%.** Villefort alone with the sealed
   envelope.
4. **Answer panel — next 13%.** Edmond alone, looking directly at Villefort.
5. **Honor strip — lower 9%.** Villefort left and Edmond right. One question,
   one answer.

### Exact image text

**Panel 1**

1. Villefort: “Your name?”
2. Edmond: “Edmond Dantès. Nineteen. Mate of the *Pharaon*—captain, when she
   sails again.”

**Panel 2**

3. Edmond: “Captain Leclère was dying. He made me promise to deliver a packet
   to Grand Marshal Bertrand on Elba. I obeyed him.”

**Panel 3**

4. Villefort: “And Bertrand gave you this sealed letter?”

**Panel 4**

5. Edmond: “Yes. For a man in Paris. I never opened it.”

**Panel 5**

6. Villefort: “Why risk your wedding for a dying man's errand?”
7. Edmond: “Because I gave my word.”

## Page 14 — Innocent

### Page record

- **Narrative job:** Let Villefort reach and state the correct judgment before
  the addressee changes his personal stakes.
- **Reader state entering:** Edmond carried the letter because Captain Leclère
  made him promise; Edmond never opened it.
- **Desire and opposition:** Edmond wants release; Villefort tests whether
  Edmond had political intent or a knowing enemy.
- **Dominant mode:** Dramatic scene.
- **Density tier:** T3.
- **Visual engine:** The anonymous accusation loses visual authority as
  Villefort compares it with the living man before him.
- **Page archetype:** Evidence weighed against character.

### Panel map

1. **Opposed panel — upper 25%.** Villefort left; Edmond right. Two balloons.
2. **Dominant panel — next 40%.** Edmond in the open half of the room while
   Villefort studies him from behind the desk. Two balloons.
3. **Evidence panel — next 16%.** Villefort alone, holding the unsigned
   accusation away from himself with visible distaste.
4. **Release panel — lower 15%.** Edmond foreground left, already turning
   slightly toward the door; Villefort right with the sealed letter still on
   the desk. This reversed camera angle puts the first speaker first in the
   reading path.

**Panel 1**

**Panel 3**

**Panel 4**

6. Edmond: “Then I may return to my feast?”

## Page 16 — The Choice

### Page record

- **Narrative job:** Show Villefort destroy the evidence, exploit Edmond's
  trust, and deliberately replace the letter with a prison order.
- **Reader state entering:** Villefort knows Edmond is innocent; the letter to
  Villefort's father threatens Villefort's marriage and career.
- **Desire and opposition:** Villefort wants the evidence gone and Edmond
  silent; Edmond wants to keep his word to the man he believes has saved him.
- **Dominant mode:** Dramatic scene.
- **Density tier:** T2.
- **Visual engine:** One paper burns while another paper is signed.
- **Page archetype:** Causal chain; repeated hands; decision becoming
  institution.

### Panel map

1. **Fire panel — upper 27%.** Villefort left burning the letter; Edmond right.
2. **Trust panel — next 23%.** Opposed close two-shot. Villefort asks for
   silence; Edmond gives his word.
3. **Dominant decision panel — next 31%.** Villefort at the desk, Edmond small
   near the door. Villefort's pen touches the detention order. This panel must
   contain a visible silent beat between Edmond's trust and Villefort's call.
4. **Consequence panel — lower 15%.** Villefort foreground left; guard appears
   in the doorway; Edmond turns back from the right.

### Exact image text

**Panel 1**

1. Villefort: “This letter is the only evidence. Watch it burn.”
2. Edmond: “Then it is over.”

**Panel 2**

3. Villefort: “You must never speak of Noirtier or the letter.”
4. Edmond: “You have my word.”

**Panel 3**

5. Villefort: “You will remain here only until I consult the magistrate.”
6. Edmond: “I trust you, monsieur.”

**Panel 4**

7. Villefort: “Guard.”
8. Edmond: “Monsieur?”

## Page 17 — The Black Island

### Page record

- **Narrative job:** Carry Edmond through the physical machinery of Villefort's
  order until Château d'If makes its meaning visible.
- **Reader state entering:** Villefort called a guard after signing an order;
  Edmond still believes the delay is temporary.
- **Desire and opposition:** Edmond wants to understand the transfer; the
  guards obey the document and are not authorized to reconsider it.
- **Dominant mode:** Spectacle / illustrated prose / dramatic scene.
- **Density tier:** T3.
- **Visual engine:** Geography before action. Corridors descend to the harbor;
  Marseille recedes; the fortress grows.
- **Page archetypes:** Geography before action; distance as consequence.

### Panel map

## Continuous Editorial Test

The prototype fails unless a first-time reader can answer all of the following
from the finished pages alone:

1. What future does Villefort want?
2. Who is Noirtier?
3. Why is Noirtier dangerous to Villefort?
4. Why did Edmond stop at Elba?
5. Did Edmond read the letter?
6. Does Villefort believe Edmond is guilty?
7. What changes when Villefort hears the addressee's name?
8. What evidence does Villefort destroy?
9. What new document does Villefort create?
10. Why do the guards and prison staff ignore Edmond's innocence?
11. How does Edmond become “Thirty-four”?

The intended answers require no knowledge of the Hundred Days, Restoration
politics, the source novel, or the first-edition planning notes.
