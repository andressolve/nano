# The Count of Monte Cristo — Character-Distinctness Critic Report

## Round 1 — Current reference library

**Review date:** 2026-07-31  
**Verdict:** **APPROVAL WITHHELD**

I inspected every character sheet in [`../monte-cristo/refs/`](../monte-cristo/refs/),
the current expanded-edition prototype pages, the character specifications,
the Velvet Cinema continuity rules, the complete 52-page cast and staging, and
the expanded-edition readability and page-intelligence contracts. There is no
separate expanded-edition character-reference folder at present; the expanded
edition inherits the first-edition sheets.

The user's diagnosis is correct: **young Edmond Dantès and Gérard de Villefort
look too much alike.** This is not a subtle resemblance. At first glance they
can read as the same handsome, clean-shaven, dark-haired man in different
clothes. The finished Pages 13–16 examination sequence puts that failure into
the most dangerous possible context: two opposed men, of similar apparent age,
sharing panels while the story depends on the reader tracking which one knows,
speaks, decides, and betrays.

The problem is broader than that one pair. The library repeatedly uses a lean,
handsome, dark-haired, clean-shaven male face with an oval-to-angular jaw,
straight nose, dark brows, and formal dark clothing. Young Edmond, Villefort,
the Count, Jacopo, Fernand, and Emmanuel occupy overlapping parts of that
design space. Costume currently performs too much identity work. Costume is
the first cue lost in close-ups, night scenes, prison light, memory insets,
disguises, and small mobile display.

This library is not safe for full production until the blocking collisions are
redesigned and new reference sheets are approved.

## Review standard

The target is first-glance identification at the effortless clarity level of
George O'Connor's *Olympians*. An unfamiliar reader should not have to inspect
a collar, remember who stood on which side, or infer identity from dialogue.

Each principal recurring character was tested against the others under these
conditions:

- face-only crop with costume removed;
- full-body silhouette at thumbnail size;
- grayscale value pattern;
- warm Marseille light, cold prison light, and near-black night light;
- profile, three-quarter, and reaction views rather than only the approved
  presentation pose;
- age progression, beard growth, formal dress, and deliberate disguise;
- same-panel opposition and small silent memory inset;
- a first-time reader seeing the character after a long absence.

The required identity hierarchy is:

1. face shape and age;
2. hair or facial-hair mass;
3. body silhouette and posture;
4. costume value pattern;
5. color accent and recurring object.

Color or costume alone cannot rescue a failed face and silhouette.

## Blocking issues

### 1. Edmond and Villefort are presently the same visual casting type

The root specification gives Edmond a long clean nose, high cheekbones, strong
brow, dark eyes, black curls, and a lean handsome face. It gives Villefort only
“handsome,” “dark neatly arranged hair,” “clean-shaven,” and “intelligent
eyes.” With no explicit anti-Edmond instructions, the generation inherited
Edmond's facial grammar.

The result is a story-comprehension error, not a cosmetic preference. In the
examination, the reader must understand that Edmond is open and trusting while
Villefort is older, socially armored, and concealing recognition. If the men
look related or identical, every balloon and reaction requires additional
decoding.

**Required:** Replace the Villefort reference before regenerating any page
where he appears. Do not solve this by changing only his coat, cravat, or side
of panel.

### 2. The Count repeats the same collision in formal black

The Count must remain Edmond, but the current sheet does not show enough age,
prison consequence, cultivated stillness, or unique silhouette. Because
Villefort also wears black with a white throat shape, the two become near
duplicates under Paris values.

**Required:** Rebuild the Count as a visibly late-thirties continuation of
Edmond, not as young Edmond in evening clothes. Approve him side by side with
both young Edmond and the redesigned Villefort.

### 3. Jacopo duplicates the protagonist's complete sailor code

Jacopo and Edmond are not separated by one weak attribute; they share almost
the entire identity stack: age band, complexion, build, black curls, open blue
shirt, red waist accent, practical rope work, and energetic sailor posture.
They appear together precisely when a half-drowned, changing Edmond needs to
remain immediately identifiable.

**Required:** Replace Jacopo's sheet before Pages 40–41 or 52 enter production.

### 4. Several supporting characters lack approved identity locks

The 52-page script depends on characters not present in the approved library:

- Captain Leclère, whose deathbed request drives the entire catastrophe and
  recurs in memory;
- Renée and the Marquise de Saint-Méran, whose family and marriage make
  Villefort's motive legible;
- Noirtier, who appears in a crucial father/son memory and becomes a major
  later-volume character;
- the principal guard, jailer, clerk, and governor, whose separate
  institutional roles make Edmond's erasure understandable.

The prototype generated some of these ad hoc. That violates the current rule
that recurring characters receive approved references before finished pages.
It also invites the same generic-face problem from page to page.

**Required:** Create a secondary-cast reference group before full production.
At minimum, lock Leclère, Renée, the Marquise, Noirtier, principal guard,
jailer, clerk, and governor. Minor one-panel sailors, guests, messengers,
agents, and workers may use controlled role sheets rather than individual
hero sheets, provided no face is reused for two roles.

### 5. The library has no explicit anti-collision rules

The current specification records each person in isolation. It does not say
which facial geometry, hair mass, posture, palette, or costume shapes are
reserved and which are forbidden because another lead already owns them.

**Required:** Add a cross-character identity ledger to the specification after
new sheets are approved. Every prompt should name the relevant positive locks
and the nearest character it must not resemble.

## Exact redesign prescriptions

These are identity prescriptions, not suggestions to make the cast more
caricatured. Velvet Cinema realism can support them without turning anyone into
a visual villain.

### Gérard de Villefort — full redesign

- Present him as clearly **29–31**, visibly eight to ten years older than
  nineteen-year-old Edmond.
- Give him a **long, narrow inverted-triangle face**, high smooth forehead,
  close-set gray-hazel eyes, thin arched brows, a convex aquiline nose, narrow
  compressed lips, and a pointed chin.
- Use a cooler, paler complexion than Edmond's sun-warmed olive.
- Change raven curls to **short chestnut-brown hair**, precisely side-parted and
  swept back from a clearly visible forehead and temples. No curl may fall over
  his brow.
- Make the silhouette narrow-shouldered, high-necked, and vertically rigid.
  Keep elbows close and hands controlled or folded.
- Preserve black Restoration legal dress, but make the high white
  collar/cravat a sharp, narrow wedge. A restrained bottle-green or steel-gray
  waistcoat may separate the value pattern.
- Give him a still-paper gesture: fingertips flatten a document while the rest
  of the body remains motionless.
- Explicit prohibitions: no loose black curls, no broad cheekbones, no open
  white V-neck, no red waist accent, no sailor stance, no Edmond mouth
  asymmetry.

### The Count — full redesign while preserving Edmond

- Preserve Edmond's deep-set black-brown eyes, strong straight brow, long clean
  nose, high cheekbones, slight left-mouth asymmetry, poised right hand, and
  doorway habit.
- Make him visibly late thirties: leaner facial planes, hollow temples, finer
  lines beneath the eyes, a more severe mouth at rest, and prison pallor over
  the original olive undertone.
- Arrange black hair **straight back in one controlled wave**, without
  Villefort's side part and without young Edmond's loose forehead curls. A
  restrained silver thread at one temple may be tested, but only if continuity
  remains unmistakable.
- Build an iconic columnar silhouette: long immaculate black coat, high collar,
  black gloves used selectively, one signet ring, and almost no exposed shirt
  beyond a small ivory throat shape.
- Make stillness the body identifier. Young Edmond leans into action;
  Villefort holds himself rigid for approval; the Count makes other people
  cross the room toward him.
- The Count should be the darkest stable mass in a room. His single optional
  accent is oxblood at a ring stone, lining, or seal—not Edmond's sash.
- Approve three comparison views: Count beside young Edmond, Count beside
  Villefort in formal black, and Count under low warm salon light with collars
  partially cropped.

### Jacopo — full redesign

- Make Jacopo shorter and compact-wiry rather than tall and long-limbed.
- Use a broader, rounder face, wider nose, prominent ears, heavy horizontal
  brows, and a fast asymmetrical smile. A small neat moustache is a useful
  persistent marker if it survives all required views.
- Use tightly cropped black curls rather than Edmond's loose medium curls.
- Deepen the sun-browned complexion without making ethnicity the only cue.
- Replace the open cobalt shirt and red sash with a sea-green or faded teal
  rolled-sleeve shirt, ochre trousers, and a mustard rope belt. Do not give him
  Edmond's indigo-white-red value stack.
- Give him quick, low, asymmetrical movement: crouching on a gunwale, balanced
  on the balls of his feet, hands already reaching. Edmond's physical grammar
  is upright competence; Jacopo's is compact rescue energy.

### Louis Dantès — targeted redesign

- Make him smaller, narrow-shouldered, and distinctly stooped.
- Use a bald crown with close-cropped white side hair and a short, tidy white
  beard rather than Faria's long wild hair-and-beard mass.
- Give him a soft rectangular face, wider gentle eyes, and a rounded nose;
  avoid Faria's high domed forehead and deep electric gaze.
- Shift the clothes to muted blue-gray and moss-brown, with a buttoned, tidy,
  repeatedly mended domestic silhouette. Avoid Faria's open worn white shirt
  and patched dark vest arrangement.
- His hands fold, ration, and hide weakness. Faria's hands point, measure,
  write, and build.

### Fernand — targeted redesign

- Retain long straight black hair tied low; it is the strongest current cue.
- Strengthen a heavier rectangular jaw, low brow, longer aquiline nose, and
  deep-set eyes. He should not inherit Edmond's high cheekbone/clean-nose face.
- Make his build broader through chest and shoulders, with weight planted in
  doorways. He occupies space to prevent passage; Edmond occupies it before
  entering.
- Use a dark umber Catalan jacket and more closed cream shirt. Reserve a deep
  oxblood belt or black waist wrap rather than repeating Edmond's faded red
  sailor sash.
- Do not shorten or curl his hair in subsequent generations.

### Emmanuel — high-priority redesign

- Move him entirely out of the black-curled male cluster: sandy or light
  auburn straight hair, neat side part, soft round face, and lighter brows.
- Add small period-appropriate spectacles as a stable clerk identifier.
- Make him slightly shorter than Edmond, with a narrow but relaxed posture and
  an eager forward inclination rather than heroic uprightness.
- Use warm tan, muted bottle green, or tobacco-brown tailoring with a visible
  pale waistcoat. Avoid black hair plus black coat plus white collar.
- Keep a pencil, account book, or inked finger as his recurring action cue.

### Julie Morrel — targeted redesign

- Use warm medium-brown rather than near-black hair, with one visible loose curl
  or a higher youthful arrangement instead of Mercédès's center-parted low bun.
- Give Julie a rounder face, softer arched brows, lighter complexion, and more
  visibly youthful open expression.
- Preserve teal or sky-blue as her color family and connect her to the office
  through letters, keys, or the red purse rather than through Mercédès-like
  shawl shapes.

### Danglars — continuity lock, not a new concept

- Preserve and exaggerate only enough for consistency: high receding hairline,
  thinning brown hair, sallow skin, narrow triangular face, close-set light
  eyes, and a mustard-olive waistcoat.
- Keep shoulders slightly raised and body closed around the ledger.
- Reject any generation that restores a full dark hairline, broadens the face,
  or puts him in Villefort's pure black-and-white value pattern.

### Mercédès — maturation pass

- Preserve the young Mercédès eye shape, brows, nose, and decisive gaze.
- For 1838, show more than formal styling: slightly leaner mature cheeks,
  heavier lower eyelids, faint temple lines, and a few restrained gray threads.
- Use a sculpted formal hair mass and burgundy/black vertical silhouette, while
  young Mercédès retains practical hair, rust cloth, and an active forward
  stance.

### Busoni and Wilmore — continuity refinement

- Keep both disguise silhouettes and palettes; they are among the library's
  clearest designs.
- Preserve three quiet Edmond invariants under each disguise: the deep-set eye
  spacing, long clean nose, and slight left-mouth asymmetry.
- Busoni's beard, priest mantle, and glasses must not erase those bones.
- Wilmore's blond hair, sideburns, spectacles, gloves, and cane must not turn
  him into an unrelated face. The prose tells the reader these are disguises;
  the art should reward that knowledge.

### Hardest proof: Page 15

[`qa/page15-edmond-villefort-identity-proof.png`](qa/page15-edmond-villefort-identity-proof.png)
passes the condition that failed the original library.

- In the top low-light two-shot, body, age, complexion, hairline, throat shape,
  and posture separate the men before the eye reaches clothing color.
- In the opposed profile strip, where most costume information disappears,
  Villefort's convex nose, high forehead, narrow jaw, and swept hair oppose
  Edmond's straighter nose, lower curl mass, broad cheek, and stronger jaw.
- The separate reaction panels preserve the same faces rather than reverting
  to a shared generic handsome-male template.
- At 390 pixels wide, both profiles remain distinct.
