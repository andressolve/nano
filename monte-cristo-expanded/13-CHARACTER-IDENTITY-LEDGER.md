# The Count of Monte Cristo — Expanded Character Identity Ledger

## Status

**Approved for expanded-edition production on 2026-07-31.**

The approval is recorded in Round 2 of
[`12-CHARACTER-DISTINCTNESS-CRITIC-REPORT.md`](12-CHARACTER-DISTINCTNESS-CRITIC-REPORT.md).
It applies to the reference system; finished pages still require fresh
identity, reading-order, lettering, and balloon-attribution review.

This ledger is the expanded edition's casting authority. It supplements the
individual first-edition specifications in
[`../monte-cristo/02-CHARACTERS.md`](../monte-cristo/02-CHARACTERS.md) and
supersedes the first-edition image locks wherever a revised reference is named
below.

The goal is not caricature. It is instant recognition. A reader must identify
an important character from face, age, hair mass, and silhouette before
costume, dialogue, position, or scene context is considered.

## Identity hierarchy

Every generation must preserve identity in this order:

1. face shape and apparent age;
2. hair and facial-hair mass;
3. body silhouette and habitual posture;
4. costume value pattern;
5. color accent, prop, and repeated gesture.

Costume and color may reinforce a character; they may never be the only cues.
Every page prompt must name both the positive lock and the nearest forbidden
lookalike.

## Reserved design space

- Loose raven curls, white/indigo workwear, and a red-brown sash belong to young
  Edmond.
- A short precise chestnut side part, narrow aquiline face, and sharp white
  throat wedge belong to Villefort.
- Long straight black hair tied low and a broad doorway-blocking silhouette
  belong to Fernand.
- Tight cropped curls, moustache, teal shirt, ochre trousers, and low reaching
  movement belong to Jacopo.
- Black columnar stillness with swept-back hair belongs to the Count.
- Sandy straight hair, round spectacles, and tan/green clerk values belong to
  Emmanuel.
- A receding hairline with mustard-olive waistcoat belongs to Danglars.
- Dark practical low hair and rust cloth belong to young Mercédès; high loose
  brown curls and blue cloth belong to Julie.

No background character may accidentally inherit one of these complete stacks.

## Binding near-neighbor controls

- **Emmanuel / Wilmore:** preserve Emmanuel's youth, soft round face, sandy
  straight hair, relaxed posture, and account book; preserve Wilmore's mature
  angular face, sideburns, high ivory throat mass, gloves, and cane.
- **Noirtier / governor:** Noirtier remains lean and moustached with an Empire
  officer's hands-behind-back line; the governor remains heavy, clean-shaven,
  ivory-fronted, and petition-led.
- **Leclère / jailer:** Leclère owns iron-gray mutton chops, clean chin,
  brick-red captain's coat, and barrel authority; the jailer owns all-over
  gray-red stubble, bulbous nose, stoop, keys, and prison-brown outerwear.
- **Renée / Julie:** Renée remains pale strawberry-blond, heart-faced,
  high-ringleted, powder-blue and old-gold; Julie remains brown-haired,
  rounder, practically dressed, working-teal, and active with letters or keys.
- **Mercédès 1838:** every prompt must state early forties, visible
  temple/lower-lid lines, restrained gray threads, and sculpted formal hair.
  Reject a beautiful but youth-washed face.

## Production prompt contract

Before generating a story page:

1. attach every named character's approved sheet;
2. state the positive face, hair, silhouette, and value locks needed in that
   scene;
3. name the nearest same-scene anti-reference and say what may not drift;
4. preserve apparent age even in low light and memory insets;
5. keep silent characters visibly distinct from live speakers;
6. inspect face-only crops and grayscale silhouettes before approving the
   page;
7. regenerate the full page if identity drifts. Never patch a face.

The references define identity, not composition. The approved 55-page script
still defines the page's speaker lanes, exact text, panel order, and tail
endpoints.
