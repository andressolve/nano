# The Count of Monte Cristo — Pages 1–10 Production Prompts

## Status

Production prompt set for the first ten finished expanded-edition pages. The
exact wording and panel logic come from the critic-approved
[`10-FULL-52-PAGE-SCRIPT.md`](10-FULL-52-PAGE-SCRIPT.md). Character identity
comes from the critic-approved
[`13-CHARACTER-IDENTITY-LEDGER.md`](13-CHARACTER-IDENTITY-LEDGER.md).

## Common production block

Use case: `illustration-story`.

Create a complete new finished historical graphic-novel page at exactly
**1024 × 1536 portrait, 2:3**. Input images are identity, setting, palette,
lettering, and continuity references, not edit targets.

Use the locked **Velvet Cinema** finish: mature historical graphic-novel
realism painted in layered matte gouache and opaque watercolor over sparse
charcoal and ink construction; broad visible brushstrokes; simplified
interlocking color shapes; bold shadow masses; tactile cloth, wood, rope,
paper, stone, metal, food, and water; expressive anatomically credible faces;
selective hard edges at eyes, mouths, hands, and decisive objects. Marseille in
1815 uses salt white, cobalt and working blue, warm ochre, sun-warmed skin,
sailcloth, weathered wood, and open sky. Avoid glossy game art, steampunk,
anime, children's-book softness, pirate fantasy, photographic lens effects,
and dense engraved cross-hatching.

Keep one unmistakable top-to-bottom reading path, 64 px safe outer margins,
at least 24 px calm gutters, and one dominant panel. No title, page number,
speaker labels, production notes, signature, or watermark.

Bake every word below into the finished image **verbatim, exactly once, and in
the numbered order**. No other text. Use large reader-size dark upright
mixed-case lettering. Speech balloons are warm ivory with restrained
charcoal-brown painted outlines. Keep every balloon on its named speaker's
side. Each tail ends in open space immediately beside that speaker's mouth.
Silent characters receive no balloons and no tail fragments. Do not cross a
panel border or cover a face, hand, or decisive object. If the exchange is
dense, protect lettering space before rendering faces and scenery.

The identity sheets define casting, not composition. Preserve each named
character's apparent age, face, hair mass, body silhouette, costume value
pattern, and posture. Do not merge faces. Background characters must not
inherit a lead character's full identity stack.

## Page 1 — Bring Her Home

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; young Edmond; Monsieur Morrel; the
*Pharaon*.

Four horizontal stages: (1) 14% calm parchment caption field above a bright
Marseille harbor; (2) 38% dominant wide ship approach, pilot clearly left and
Edmond clearly right; (3) 25% deck action, same pilot left and Edmond right;
(4) 19% Morrel in a skiff below the rail at left, Edmond above/right. The
merchant ship has damaged canvas and is nearing shoal water; Edmond's rope and
sail command must read instantly. Crew remain silent.

Young Edmond is nineteen, tall, lean, olive-skinned, loose raven curls, long
clean nose, high cheekbones, white shirt, indigo waistcoat, dark trousers, and
faded red-brown sash. Never give him Villefort's pale narrow aquiline face or
Fernand's broad planted mass. Morrel has a square clean-shaven face, silver
temples, navy/burgundy merchant clothing, and open commanding posture.

Exact text:

- Caption, top field: `Marseille · February 1815`
- P2 upper-left, Pilot: `Shoal water ahead!`
- P2 upper-right, Edmond: `Ease the mainsail! Larboard line ready!`
- P3 upper-left, Pilot: `She is answering!`
- P3 upper-right, Edmond: `Then let her answer. Bring her home.`
- P4 upper-left, Morrel: `Dantès! Where is Captain Leclère?`
- P4 upper-right, Edmond: `Dead, sir. But the Pharaon and every man aboard are safe.`

## Page 2 — Captain at Nineteen

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; young Edmond; Monsieur Morrel;
Danglars; the *Pharaon*; accepted Page 1 for immediate continuity.

Five horizontal stages on the *Pharaon*: (1) 23% Morrel left and Edmond right;
(2) 16% Danglars alone with ledger and stopped pen; (3) 21% Morrel left and
Edmond right with silent crew behind, one balloon apiece; (4) 18% private
offer, Morrel left and Edmond right; (5) 18% Danglars left challenges Edmond
right. The offer travels across the deck while Danglars's pen stops. Crew are
silent.

Preserve Page 1 Edmond and Morrel. Danglars has a high receding hairline,
thinning brown hair, sallow triangular face, close-set light eyes,
mustard-olive waistcoat, and a body closed around his ledger. Reject a full
dark hairline, broad face, or Villefort's black-and-white value stack.

Exact text:

- P1 upper-left, Morrel: `The crew obeyed you as captain.`
- P1 upper-right, Edmond: `They obeyed because the ship needed them.`
- P2 upper-left, Danglars: `A fortunate performance.`
- P3 upper-left, Morrel: `Not fortune. Judgment. When the Pharaon sails again, I mean you to command her.`
- P3 upper-right, Edmond: `Me? At nineteen?`
- P4 upper-left, Morrel: `You brought my ship home. Shall I wait until you forget how?`
- P5 upper-left, Danglars: `After his unexplained stop at Elba?`
- P5 upper-right, Edmond: `Captain Leclère asked it of me before he died.`

## Page 3 — The Empty Cupboard

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; young Edmond; Louis Dantès; accepted
Page 2 for Edmond continuity.

Four stages in a cramped but cared-for Marseille apartment: (1) 24% doorway
embrace; (2) 22% opposed close-up, Edmond left and Louis right; (3) 24%
dominant empty cupboard and almost bare food shelf, Edmond left and Louis
right; (4) 26% intimate table, Edmond left and Louis right, Edmond putting food
down. The cupboard—not poverty spectacle—is the decisive object.

Preserve Edmond. Louis is small and stooped, with a bald crown, short tidy
white beard, gentle rectangular face, blue-gray/moss clothing, and folded
rationing hands. Never give Louis Faria's wild white mane, long beard, open
shirt, or energetic pointing hands.

Exact text:

- P1 upper-left, Edmond: `Father!`
- P1 upper-right, Louis: `You came back taller.`
- P2 upper-left, Edmond: `And you have grown thinner.`
- P2 upper-right, Louis: `You left two hundred francs. I paid what we owed Caderousse.`
- P3 upper-left, Edmond: `All of it? What did you eat?`
- P3 upper-right, Louis: `A debt is a debt. Food can wait.`
- P4 upper-left, Edmond: `No. Morrel has offered me the Pharaon. From today, there will always be food in this house.`
- P4 upper-right, Louis: `Captain Dantès. Your mother would have filled the whole street with that news.`

## Page 4 — The Catalans

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; young Edmond; young Mercédès; Fernand;
accepted Page 3 for Edmond continuity.

Four stages in the sunlit Catalans district: (1) 24% limewashed walls, nets,
rough timber, neighbors, and Fernand occupying a doorway at left while
Mercédès stands right; (2) 29% Fernand left and Mercédès right; (3) 20% Fernand
alone after her refusal; (4) 23% Edmond clearly left and Mercédès clearly
right as she crosses the doorway toward him. Fernand is visible but silent in
Panel 4 and is omitted from both balloon lanes.

Preserve Edmond. Mercédès has dark practical center-parted hair, a warm olive
face, direct dark gaze, rust/cream working dress, and active forward stance.
Never give her Julie's lighter round face, high loose brown curls, or blue
office palette. Fernand has a broad planted build, rectangular jaw, low brow,
long aquiline nose, long straight black hair tied low, and umber/cream Catalan
clothing. Never give him Edmond's loose curls, lean sailor body, clean straight
nose, indigo vest, or red sash.

Exact text:

- P1 upper-left, Fernand: `Mercédès, Edmond has been gone for months. The sea may keep him for years.`
- P1 upper-right, Mercédès: `The sea does not choose my husband.`
- P2 upper-left, Fernand: `I am here. I am your cousin. I would never leave you waiting.`
- P2 upper-right, Mercédès: `You are my family, Fernand. You will not be my husband.`
- P3 upper-left, Fernand: `You would still choose Edmond, even after all these months?`
- P4 upper-left, Edmond: `Do you still want the life we planned?`
- P4 upper-right, Mercédès: `I waited because I still want it.`

## Page 5 — Tomorrow

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; young Edmond; young Mercédès; Louis
Dantès; accepted Page 4 for immediate continuity.

Six panels in four warm tiers: (1) 20% Edmond left and Mercédès right walking
away from the Catalans; (2) 28% close two-shot, Edmond left and Mercédès right,
with the A-B-A-B exchange in separate upper and lower lanes; (3) 24% Louis
joins them at a modest table with one intentionally reserved chair; (4–6) 24%
bottom-row toast triptych, three equal vertical face-led panels in reading
order: Edmond, Mercédès, Louis. Each toast panel has one visible foreground
speaker and one balloon. Distant Fernand is outside the warm center in the
background of Louis's panel, unmistakably silent. Nearby guests are silent.

Preserve the accepted Edmond, Mercédès, and Louis identities. The empty chair
must visibly become Louis's family place. Fernand, if visible, keeps his broad
long-haired silhouette and never receives a tail.

Exact text:

- P1 upper-left, Edmond: `Morrel will make me captain.`
- P1 upper-right, Mercédès: `Then Captain Dantès must learn to arrive on time.`
- P2 upper-left, Edmond: `With a captain's wages, we can find a home with room for Father. He will never face an empty cupboard again.`
- P2 upper-right, Mercédès: `And a wife who will know every storm that delayed you.`
- P2 lower-left, Edmond: `Will you marry me tomorrow?`
- P2 lower-right, Mercédès: `Tomorrow—and every day after.`
- P3 upper-left, Louis: `Then this old man must find his good coat.`
- P4 upper-left, Edmond: `To tomorrow.`
- P5 upper-left, Mercédès: `Tomorrow.`
- P6 upper-left, Louis: `Tomorrow.`

## Page 6 — What Each Man Wanted

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; Danglars; Fernand; Caderousse; accepted
Page 5 for Marseille continuity.

Five clear horizontal bands: (1) 24% single stable parchment prose field;
(2) 22% silent Danglars with ledger and celebrating crew far beyond; (3) 22%
silent Fernand outside distant betrothal preparations; (4) 22% silent
Caderousse with cup and debt receipt; (5) 6% silent convergence toward the same
seaside tavern table. No figure receives a balloon or tail. Do not depict these
three as interchangeable jealous men: ledger,
doorway, and cup must distinguish their motives before all text is read.

Danglars keeps the receding hairline, sallow triangle, mustard-olive waistcoat,
and closed ledger posture. Fernand keeps the broad planted mass, rectangular
jaw, tied-low long black hair, and umber/cream stack; never Edmond's curls or
indigo-red sailor stack. Caderousse is stocky and round with curly brown hair,
stubble, rust coat, cup, and a posture that collapses with guilt; never slim
him into Edmond or darken him into Busoni.

Exact text:

- Prose field: `Three men approached the same table. Danglars had expected the captaincy now promised to Edmond. Fernand knew Mercédès would marry Edmond tomorrow. Caderousse recognized what their anger was becoming and followed them into the tavern anyway. A drink would make it easier to tell himself that other men's choices were not his concern.`

## Page 7 — The Table

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; Danglars; Fernand; Caderousse; accepted
Page 6 for immediate identity, tavern, and light continuity.

Four stages at one seaside tavern table: (1) 25% three-shot, Danglars left,
Caderousse center, Fernand right; (2) 28% Danglars left and Fernand right, with
Danglars's A-B-A balloons in upper-left, upper-right, then lower-left lanes;
(3) 22% Caderousse left interrupts and Fernand right asks; (4) 21% paper and
pen dominate the lower foreground, Danglars left and Caderousse right.
Danglars controls the table with questions. Background patrons are silent.

Preserve all three distinct approved identities from Page 6. Never move a
speaker to the other side of the panel. Keep the blank sheet visually legible
without readable handwriting.

Exact text:

- P1 upper-left, Danglars: `Captain Dantès. Husband Dantès. A fortunate day for one man.`
- P1 upper-right, Fernand: `Do not speak her name.`
- P2 upper-left, Danglars: `Would Mercédès forgive the man who killed him?`
- P2 upper-right, Fernand: `Never.`
- P2 lower-left, Danglars: `Then remove him without touching him.`
- P3 upper-left, Caderousse: `You are talking about sending an innocent man to prison.`
- P3 upper-right, Fernand: `How?`
- P4 upper-left, Danglars: `Edmond stopped at Elba and carried a letter for the dead captain. If the police believe he serves Napoleon, they will imprison him without you laying a hand on him.`
- P4 upper-right, Caderousse: `That would be a lie. Put the pen down.`

## Page 8 — The Denunciation

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; Danglars; Fernand; Caderousse; key
objects; accepted Page 7 for immediate tavern continuity.

Five strictly ordered panels built around one accusation and three visible
moral choices: (1) 24% wide table tableau with one intact accusation centered
between all three men; (2) 20% Caderousse left and Danglars right, same paper
between them; (3) 20% Caderousse left as Fernand's hand enters from right
toward the paper; (4) 20% Fernand right holding the accusation while Danglars
withdraws left; (5) 12% silent Fernand at an official door placing the
accusation into the hands of a uniformed official. No Danglars or Caderousse
in Panel 5. There is exactly one paper throughout. No torn scrap, decoy paper,
legible handwriting, or subtle prop distinction carries story logic.

Preserve the approved identities and Page 7 costumes. The page's dominant
turn is whether any of the three men will destroy the accusation; each refuses
or chooses in full view. Dialogue must sound like men shifting responsibility,
not like an explanation of paper mechanics.

Exact text:

- P1 top narration field, tail-free: `The accusation lay between them. Any one could still destroy it.`
- P1 upper-left, Danglars: `Edmond Dantès carried a Bonapartist letter from Elba to Paris. That is all the police will need.`
- P2 upper-left, Caderousse: `You do not know that he is a Bonapartist. This could send an innocent man to prison.`
- P2 lower-right, Danglars: `Then tear it up.`
- P3 upper-left, Caderousse: `You wrote it. You destroy it.`
- P3 lower-left, Caderousse: `Fernand, leave that accusation on the table.`
- P4 upper-right, Fernand: `I will not kill Edmond. I will let the law decide whether he is guilty.`
- P4 lower-left, Danglars: `If you deliver it, that is your decision. Do not speak my name.`
- P5 bottom narration field, tail-free: `Before Edmond's betrothal feast had ended, Fernand placed the accusation in official hands.`

## Page 9 — The Feast

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; young Edmond; young Mercédès; Louis
Dantès; Monsieur Morrel; Fernand; accepted Page 5 for the family table and
accepted Page 8 for immediate time continuity.

Four stages: (1) 36% dominant warm feast with one unmistakable foreground
guest at upper-left as the first speaker and Edmond upper-right; (2) 25%
Edmond clearly right and Mercédès clearly left, with Mercédès A-B-A balloons
upper-left, upper-right, lower-left; (3) 20% Morrel left and Louis right; (4)
15% full table lifts cups in a centered tail-free chorus, while the doorway is
still empty. Fernand is outside the warm center and silent. Other guests are
silent except for the one designated speaker and the final collective chorus.

Preserve all approved identities. The designated guest must not resemble any
lead. The feast is shared food, rough timber, open doors, close community, and
warmth—not an aristocratic banquet.

Exact text:

- P1 upper-left, Guest: `A captain and a wedding in two days!`
- P1 upper-right, Edmond: `One day. The wedding first.`
- P2 upper-left, Mercédès: `You keep looking at the door.`
- P2 upper-right, Edmond: `I am teaching myself that no one will call me back to sea tonight.`
- P2 lower-left, Mercédès: `Then look at me.`
- P3 upper-left, Morrel: `To the man who brought every sailor home.`
- P3 upper-right, Louis: `And to the woman who brought my son home.`
- P4 centered crowd chorus with no tail: `To Edmond and Mercédès!`

## Page 10 — The Arrest

**Inputs:** Velvet Cinema style sample; young Edmond; young Mercédès; Louis
Dantès; Monsieur Morrel; Fernand; principal escort guard; accepted Page 9 for
exact room, table, costume, and lighting continuity.

Four stages: (1) 35% dominant entrance as uniformed officers darken the open
doorway, principal officer clearly left and Edmond clearly right; (2) 21%
officer left and Edmond right; (3) 21% Mercédès left and officer right; (4)
19% Edmond at the threshold on the right with his family and feast behind,
Morrel clearly left. Uniformed figures spatially split Edmond from the table.
Louis, Fernand, guests, and additional officers are silent.

Preserve all accepted Page 9 identities. Principal escort guard is a tall
military rectangle with square jaw, cropped auburn hair, straight auburn
moustache, left-brow scar, and immaculate navy uniform and cap. Never resemble
Villefort, Danglars, or a generic dark-haired lead. Other officers are
subordinate and visually simpler.

Exact text:

- P1 upper-left, Officer: `Which of you is Edmond Dantès?`
- P1 upper-right, Edmond: `I am.`
- P2 upper-left, Officer: `You must answer an accusation before the deputy crown prosecutor.`
- P2 upper-right, Edmond: `Then I will answer it.`
- P3 upper-left, Mercédès: `On whose word do you take him?`
- P3 upper-right, Officer: `The order is lawful. Monsieur de Villefort will examine him.`
- P4 upper-left, Morrel: `I will follow.`
- P4 upper-right, Edmond: `No need. Keep my place. I will be home before the wine is gone.`

## Rejection rule

Reject and regenerate the complete page if any text, letterform, attribution,
tail, face, apparent age, costume, dimension, panel order, or causal beat fails.
Never patch a balloon, face, tail, or panel crop into an otherwise accepted
page.
