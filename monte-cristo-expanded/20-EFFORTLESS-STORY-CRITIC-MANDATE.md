The adaptation must work as a gripping story for a reader who has never read
*The Count of Monte Cristo*. The target is the effortless first-read clarity of
a strong all-ages graphic novel, with *Olympians* by George O'Connor serving as
a readability benchmark rather than a visual or verbal style to imitate.

> The reader should experience the story, not solve the adaptation.

## Why the standard changed

Earlier critic passes sometimes accepted a confusing beat as “recoverable”
from context. Andres's reader review of Page 8 showed that this was too
forgiving. The page was technically decodable, but its two similar papers,
false destruction, handoff, denials, and location change made the reader work
to reconstruct a simple betrayal.

“Recoverable,” “eventually clear,” and “understandable after looking back” are
not approvals. They are revision flags.

## Binding story tests

1. **Effortless first read.** A first-time reader should understand the page in
   normal reading order without rereading, consulting notes, or knowing the
   novel.
2. **Natural spoken dialogue.** Characters speak to one another from desire,
   fear, love, anger, or strategy. Dialogue that exists mainly to explain a
   procedure or historical fact fails even when the information is correct.
3. **One dominant dramatic turn per page.** A page may contain several beats,
   but one important choice, revelation, reversal, or consequence must govern
   them.
4. **No tiny-prop dependency.** Essential causality may not depend on the
   reader distinguishing two similar scraps, noticing subtle handwriting, or
   decoding a small background action at mobile size.
5. **Choice over procedure.** Dialogue should carry desire, decision, conflict,
   and consequence. Narration may carry concise orientation, elapsed time, or
   mechanical connective tissue when dramatizing it would clutter the scene.
6. **Complete causal chain.** The page and its neighbors establish who acts,
   what they choose, why they choose it, what changes, and why the next scene
   follows.
7. **Natural completeness over terseness.** A technically correct line still
   fails if it sounds clipped, evasive, archaically compressed, or like an
   outline. Apply every test in
   [`19-ANTI-TERSE-DIALOGUE-MANDATE.md`](19-ANTI-TERSE-DIALOGUE-MANDATE.md).
8. **No text scarcity.** The critic must not reward low word count. Add words,
   balloons, prose, panels, or pages when they create clarity and emotional
   duration. Never solve density by shrinking type.
9. **Story, not treatise.** Historical context appears only when a character's
   immediate situation needs it. The page must remain driven by people in
   conflict, not by explanation of the era or the novel.
10. **Paraphrase test.** After one read, a cold reader should be able to say in
    plain language what happened, why it happened, and what changed without
    reopening the page.

## Critic instructions

The same critic must audit the complete 55-page script, including previously
approved Pages 1–10. For each page and each page-to-page transition, the critic
must inspect:

- first-read orientation, motivation, causality, and emotional duration;
- whether dialogue sounds like people pursuing wants rather than writers
  transmitting plot;
- every short, ambiguous, underwritten, or unnaturally compressed line;
- whether the dramatic turn is buried beneath mechanics or excess incident;
- whether an essential fact depends on a small prop, subtle gesture, prior-novel
  knowledge, or inference from production notes;
- whether narration could clarify procedure without stealing a confrontation
  that belongs in dialogue;
- whether any passage reads as historical summary instead of story;
- whether a reader can paraphrase the page after one normal read.

Every finding must identify the page, quote or describe the problem, explain
the first-time-reader failure, and prescribe a concrete revision. The critic
may recommend structural rewrites or additional pages. “Recoverable” is not a
nonblocking grade.

The critic withholds approval until the complete script passes all tests. The
script is revised from the report, then the same critic re-audits the complete
revision. Repeat until approved.

## Page 8 precedent

The rejected Page 8 used two similar papers and required the reader to track a
false torn scrap. The accepted rewrite uses one accusation and one dramatic
question: will any of the three men stop it?

The accusation explicitly retains the necessary plot:

> **Danglars:** “Edmond Dantès carried a Bonapartist letter from Elba to Paris.
> That is all the police will need.”

The scene then makes three moral positions visible: Danglars authored the lie,
Caderousse recognizes the danger but refuses to act, and Fernand chooses to
deliver it while pretending that the law will bear responsibility.

This is the model for future revision: preserve the consequential fact, remove
the puzzle, and dramatize the human choice.
