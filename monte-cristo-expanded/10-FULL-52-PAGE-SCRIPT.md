# The Count of Monte Cristo — Expanded Volume I Production Script

## Status

Complete exact script for the 55-page expanded edition of **Volume I —
Edmond**. The legacy filename records the earlier 52-page architecture; the
three added pages protect relationship and revelation time.

Every finished story page is **1024 × 1536, 2:3 portrait**. Page titles,
production notes, panel labels, speaker names, and density codes below do not
appear in the art. Only wording explicitly quoted under **Exact image text**
may appear.

This script obeys the readability mandate: a first-time reader needs no
knowledge of the novel, Napoleon, Restoration politics, nineteenth-century
banking, or the first edition. Dialogue carries decisions; prose carries time,
place, identity, and connective logic.

## Lettering and attribution lock

- Speech uses one consistent mixed-case face across all 55 pages, normally
  44–50 px at production size and never below 40 px.
- Each numbered speech line below is one balloon unless the record explicitly
  says otherwise.
- Balloon position is on the named speaker's side. Every tail ends in clear
  open space beside that speaker's mouth—not at a head, torso, prop, silent
  character, or memory figure.
- First visible speaker equals first balloon encountered. A–B–A exchanges use
  upper A, opposing B, lower A.
- A named silent character receives no balloon or tail.
- Prose is set in one stable field, 36–42 px, with no busy art behind it.
- Essential facts are spoken or typeset; generated handwriting never carries
  the only copy of necessary information.

## Anti-terse dialogue lock

- Consequential questions name what is being asked; the reader never supplies
  a missing object merely because a fragment sounds cinematic.
- Every balloon of five words or fewer receives an ambiguity audit against
  [`19-ANTI-TERSE-DIALOGUE-MANDATE.md`](19-ANTI-TERSE-DIALOGUE-MANDATE.md).
- A short reply is valid only when it answers a complete thought and has one
  possible referent in the visible scene.
- If a cold listener can reasonably ask “What?”, “Who?”, or “Tomorrow for
  what?”, the line must be rewritten before generation.
- There is no target to make dialogue as short as possible. Complete natural
  exchanges outrank terse elegance; add words, balloons, panels, prose, or
  pages when comprehension and emotional duration require them.
- The complete script must also pass the effortless first-read, natural
  dialogue, dominant-turn, no-tiny-prop, and one-read paraphrase tests in
  [`20-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC-MANDATE.md`](20-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC-MANDATE.md).

# Full-book cold-read gate

A reader who has never encountered *The Count of Monte Cristo* must be able to
answer all of the following from these 55 pages alone:

1. What future had Edmond earned before his arrest?
2. Why did Danglars, Fernand, and Caderousse each contribute to the crime?
3. Why did Edmond stop at Elba and why was he innocent?
4. Who is Noirtier, and exactly why did his name threaten Villefort?
5. What choice did Villefort make after recognizing Edmond's innocence?
6. Why did help from Mercédès, Morrel, and Louis fail to reach Edmond?
7. How much time passed before Edmond heard Faria?
8. How did Faria and Edmond become partners, teacher and student, then father
   and son?
9. How did they identify all four betrayals?
10. What moral limit did Faria ask Edmond to remember?
11. How did Edmond exchange places with Faria and escape the burial sack?
12. Why did the smugglers trust him and leave him alone on Monte Cristo?
13. What are Busoni and Wilmore, and why did Edmond create each identity?
14. What happened to Louis and Mercédès during Edmond's imprisonment?
15. What did Morrel do for the Dantès family?
16. Why was Morrel & Son about to fail, and how did Thomson & French stop it?
17. Why did Julie trust the letter, and what did the red purse mean?
18. How could Edmond provide the replacement *Pharaon* without the event being
   magic?
19. What did Edmond choose to do with power first?

The script fails if any answer depends on source knowledge, planning notes, an
unreadable document, an ambiguous speaker, or a reader repairing a missing
step.
