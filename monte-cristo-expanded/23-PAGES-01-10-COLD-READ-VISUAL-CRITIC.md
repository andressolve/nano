# Pages 1–10 — Cold-Read and Visual-Defect Critic

**Critic:** Critic 2 — independent cold-read/readability and generation-defect gate  
**Scope:** final mobile proofs and full-resolution Pages 1–10  
**Standard:** a reader with no prior knowledge of *The Count of Monte Cristo* should be able to follow the story as effortlessly as a strong middle-grade literary comic  
**Final verdict:** **APPROVED**

## Method and blind-read integrity

I first read only the ten 390 × 585 mobile proofs, in order. I did not open the
script, prompts, mandates, QA notes, handoff, or prior critic reports before
recording the sequence I understood. I then inspected the ten 1024 × 1536
production pages for lettering and image-generation defects. Only after those
two passes did I consult the production script and Pages 1–10 prompts to check
whether a perceived ambiguity reflected an actual omitted requirement.

## Sequence-level causal chain

1. Edmond saves Morrel's ship after Captain Leclère dies.
2. Morrel rewards the judgment he witnessed by promising Edmond command.
3. Danglars resents losing the captaincy and notices the usable fact of Edmond's
   Elba stop.
4. Edmond's promotion promises food and security for his father.
5. Mercédès chooses Edmond over Fernand, and she and Edmond plan to marry the
   next day.
6. Danglars's envy, Fernand's rejection, and Caderousse's cowardice converge at
   one table.
7. Danglars proposes transforming the real Elba stop and letter into a false
   political accusation that will make the police remove Edmond.
8. Caderousse objects but does not destroy the accusation; Fernand delivers
   it; Danglars distances himself from the act he authored.
9. Edmond's family celebrates, unaware that the accusation is already moving
   through official hands.
10. Officers interrupt the feast and take Edmond to Villefort for what Edmond
    believes will be a short examination.

Every link is present on the page where the reader needs it. No link required
knowledge of the novel, French political history, or a later reveal to make
the immediate action intelligible.

## Post-blind-read diagnosis against script and prompts

Consulting the script and prompts confirmed that the blind understanding
matches every page's stated reader exit. The strongest potentially fragile
requirements also survived generation:

- Page 5 asks a complete proposal rather than the former cryptic “Tomorrow?”;
  the generated page preserves the complete question and answer.
- Page 6 names and visually orders all three conspirators before the dense
  tavern exchange.
- Pages 7–8 state the Elba letter, the false Bonapartist inference, the threat
  of prison, each man's moral choice, and the official delivery without making
  the reader infer a two-paper trick.
- Page 8 preserves one intact accusation throughout.
- Page 10 identifies the accusation, destination, and examiner while making
  Edmond's expectation of a quick return explicit.

Two observations are non-blocking: Fernand and Edmond both belong to the same
dark-haired visual world, but silhouette, hair treatment, costume, attitude,
and scene geography distinguish them; and Pages 6–8 carry more text than the
surrounding pages, but mobile legibility remains good and that text prevents
the exact comprehension gaps the expanded edition is meant to solve.

## Mandatory fixes

**None.** I found no failed page that warrants regeneration. Regenerating any
of Pages 1–10 would create more risk than it would solve.

## Final verdict

# APPROVED

Pages 1–10 form an immediately understandable, emotionally grounded opening
for a reader who has never read *The Count of Monte Cristo*. The reader knows
what Edmond earned, what personal future is being stolen, what each conspirator
wants, how the Elba letter becomes a weapon, who chooses to deliver the lie,
and why officers arrive at the feast. Dialogue attribution is secure, prose is
purposeful, mobile text is readable, and the full-resolution art contains no
approval-blocking generation defect.
