# The Count of Monte Cristo — Full-Script Critic Report

## Round 1 — Complete 52-page script

**Review date:** 2026-07-31  
**Verdict:** **APPROVAL WITHHELD**

I cold-read all 52 pages in order before consulting the readability mandate or
page-intelligence contract. The standard was the effortless first-read logic
of George O'Connor's *Olympians*: clear dramatic wants, economical dialogue,
unmistakable speakers, and page turns that do not require repair by the reader.

This is a strong adaptation. The central betrayal, prison duration, Faria
relationship, escape, disguises, and Morrel rescue are substantially clearer
than a compressed plot summary. A first-time ten-year-old can follow most of
it. It is not ready for production yet: three causal/mechanical contradictions
and several binding speaker-map conflicts could create exactly the balloon
errors this edition is meant to prevent. A seven-year-old will also need a few
prose and vocabulary simplifications.

## Blocking story and mechanics issues

### 1. Pages 30–31 give Edmond knowledge he does not possess

At Page 31, script lines 1148–1151, Edmond identifies Caderousse as the person
who “could have stopped them” and concludes:

> Envy wrote it. Desire carried it. Cowardice let it pass.

The reader witnessed that division of responsibility on Pages 7–8, but Edmond
did not. His stated facts on Page 30 establish only who knew pieces of his life
and who might gain. Nothing available to him proves that Danglars wrote,
Fernand delivered, or Caderousse watched.

**Required revision:** Let Pages 30–31 identify suspects, motives, and possible
roles as hypotheses. Edmond should explicitly know that he still lacks proof.
Caderousse's Pages 44–45 confession can then confirm the table roles, and Page
46 can confirm Danglars's handwriting and Villefort's signature. Alternatively,
add an earlier witnessed fact that legitimately gives Edmond the missing
knowledge; do not let him borrow the reader's omniscience.

### 2. Page 37 removes the body needed for Page 38

Page 37's panel map says “guards remove Faria” (script line 1352). Its exact
prose says they cover his face, leave him in the cell, and later place an empty
burial sack beside him. Page 38 requires the body to remain there so Edmond can
move it through the tunnel.

**Required revision:** Change Panel 3 to the guards examining/covering Faria
and leaving him in his cell. Panel 4 should show them delivering the empty sack
and departing. The body must never leave Faria's cell before Edmond moves it.

### 3. The replacement *Pharaon* appears to be prepared overnight

Page 47 ends with Morrel saying the firm ends “tomorrow at eleven” (line 1765).
Page 48 then says Edmond's agents “bought and refitted a sound ship, loaded
replacement cargo, and paid a captain and crew” (lines 1804–1807). The current
sequence implies that a complete replacement ship, cargo, crew, name, and
figurehead are prepared between the wreck report and the next morning.

**Required revision:** Establish that Edmond learned of Morrel's danger and
began the ship rescue weeks earlier while the original *Pharaon* was overdue.
Use past-perfect wording on Page 48: the agents **had already** bought,
refitted, named, crewed, and loaded the ship. Only the final debt cancellation
and reveal should occur against the eleven-o'clock deadline.

### System-wide speaker-map blocker

The script's global lettering lock is good, but the page-intelligence contract
requires every balloon to have an ordinal, speaker, position, side, and **tail
endpoint**. The 52-page records provide ordinal, speaker, and approximate
position but do not enumerate the endpoint per balloon. Given the prior Page
13 failure, a global “beside the mouth” sentence is not enough for image
production.

**Required revision:** Before generation, add a per-page speaker/tail map or
equivalent binding line record. Every line must specify the visible speaker,
lane, reading tier, and “tail to open space immediately beside [speaker]'s
mouth.” Sounds, chorus lines, remembered lines, prose, and off-panel voices
must be explicitly marked tail-free or directed to a crack/opening.

All unlisted dialogue panels have a plausible scripted reading order. That is
not approval of final tail geometry; it means no conflict is visible in the
production record.

## Movement-by-movement first-time-reader comprehension

### Movement I — The Homecoming, Pages 1–5

**Comprehension:** Strong. Edmond earns command through competence; the future
means food for Louis and marriage to Mercédès; Danglars and Fernand have
distinct losses. Mercédès is an active chooser. No source knowledge is needed.

**Weakness:** Maritime words such as “shoal,” “mainsail,” and “larboard” add
flavor but will not be effortless for age 7. The pictures can carry the danger,
so this is polish rather than a missing causal step. Pages 1, 2, and 4 have the
speaker-map blockers listed above.

### Movement II — Three Men at a Table, Pages 6–11

**Comprehension:** The best conspiracy explanation I have seen in this
project. Envy, possessiveness, and cowardice remain distinct; authorship,
delivery, and passive permission are physically clear. The warm feast gives
the theft emotional weight.

**Weakness:** Page 6 scatters prose into three fields, contrary to the one-or-
two-field contract, and tells Caderousse's moral conclusion before he acts.
Consolidate the prose or let the repeated cup/showing-him-stay-seated carry more
of it. Pages 8, 9, and 11 need staging corrections.

### Movement III — The Prosecutor's Choice, Pages 12–17

**Comprehension:** Excellent. A child can answer who Noirtier is, why he
threatens Villefort, why Edmond is innocent, and exactly when Villefort chooses
betrayal. Page 13's revised memory separation is sound on paper. The transfer
to Château d'If is inevitable rather than abrupt.

**Weakness:** Only Page 15's envelope close-up conflicts with the two speaking
mouths. Otherwise this movement is script-ready.

### Movement IV — The Man Without a Name, Pages 18–22

**Comprehension:** Strong institutional logic. Register, petition, file, and
door make clear why innocence cannot free Edmond. Page 20 proves he was
remembered; Page 21 earns despair through duration; Page 22 makes the smallest
sound feel like rescue.

**Weakness:** Page 19's governor panel is crowded and Page 20's second panel
has a first-speaker/first-face conflict. Page 21 is emotionally beautiful but
phrases such as “thin seasons of light” and “anger began to sound like a story
told about someone else” exceed effortless age-seven comprehension. Preserve
the emotion while making at least one of those time markers more concrete.

### Movement V — The Voice in the Wall, Pages 23–34

**Comprehension:** Faria arrives as a funny, fallible person rather than a
solution machine. Tunnel geography, partnership, education, affection, moral
disagreement, and treasure all receive time. The father-son bond is earned.

**Blocking weakness:** Pages 30–31 confuse reader knowledge with Edmond's
knowledge. The surrounding memory/symbolic panels also need live-speaker lanes.
Once corrected, this movement should be one of the volume's strongest.

### Movement VI — The Dead Man Leaves, Pages 35–42

**Comprehension:** Faria's decline, inheritance, death, exchange, sack escape,
recovery, staged injury, and treasure discovery form a clear physical and
emotional chain. The escape is exciting without being spatially confusing.

**Blocking weakness:** Page 37's body-removal contradiction breaks Page 38's
mechanics. Page 40 Panel 1 contains a direct silent-Edmond/smuggler attribution
collision. Everything else is strong.

### Movement VII — The Lost Years, Pages 43–46

**Comprehension:** Busoni and Wilmore are unmistakably Edmond's constructed
identities, with distinct jobs. Louis's death, Mercédès's survival, the
conspirators' prosperity, and the archive proof are understandable without
source knowledge.

**Weakness:** Pages 44–45 repeatedly place live confession over silent memory
figures. Fixing the lanes is mandatory. Page 43's prose is clear for age 10 but
words such as “institutions,” “status,” and “performances” should be tested
aloud for age 7.

### Movement VIII — The First Gift, Pages 47–52

**Comprehension:** The debt mechanism is unusually clear: Edmond buys the
papers, becomes the creditor, marks them paid, and uses the red purse to put
proof in Julie's hands. Julie has agency; the purse repays a remembered
kindness; the final *Pharaon* mirrors Page 1 beautifully.

**Blocking weakness:** The replacement ship needs weeks of preparation, not
an implied night. Pages 51 and 52 also need visible speakers in the pistol and
harbor panels. Once the timeline is repaired, the ending earns its wonder.

## Nonblocking polish

- Pages 14–15 and 19: “formality,” “conspirators,” and “appeals” are readable
  in context for age 10 but should be read aloud to an age-seven listener.
- Page 21: simplify one or two literary time metaphors while keeping the prose
  emotionally exact.
- Page 29: “the person the banker owes” is clever but abstract; the imagined

- Page 45: “Truth has not finished appraising it” is stylish but difficult for

- Density metadata is slightly inconsistent: Page 18 has 46 exact words while
  marked T2, Page 19 has 76 while marked T3, and Pages 39 and 42 have 21 while

## Round 2 — Revised complete script

**Review date:** 2026-07-31  
**Verdict:** **APPROVED**

I cold-read the complete revised 52-page script from Page 1 through Page 52,
then re-audited every Round 1 blocker. The revision passes the script gate.

A first-time reader can now follow the entire causal chain without source
knowledge: Edmond earns a future; three men make distinct choices against him;
Villefort knowingly converts innocence into imprisonment; years and failed
help earn Edmond's despair; Faria becomes partner, teacher, and father; the
body exchange and escape are spatially legible; Edmond distinguishes suspicion
from proof; Busoni and Wilmore have separate functions; and the Morrel rescue
works through understandable preparation, debt ownership, paid notes, and a
replacement ship prepared weeks in advance.

The dialogue is economical enough for the form, the important prose is now
concrete enough to read aloud to an intelligent seven-year-old, and a
ten-year-old should be able to follow it independently. The page-turn rhythm
alternates warmth, interruption, deduction, silence, action, grief, and wonder
without becoming a plot lecture.

### Round 2 attribution verdict

The scripted attribution system passes. I found no remaining panel in which a
numbered balloon plausibly belongs to a silent character, memory figure,
object, crowd member, or wrong side of an exchange. A–B–A and A–B–A–B panels
have causal vertical tiers. Wall sounds, crowd chorus, remembered text,
documents, the hidden sack voice, and harbor voice are explicitly tail-free or
directed to their physical source.

This approval applies to the **production script**, not automatically to the
generated pages. Finished art must still be rejected if a model ignores these
instructions, changes the wording, omits a mouth, crosses a reading lane, or
lands a tail on a silent figure.
