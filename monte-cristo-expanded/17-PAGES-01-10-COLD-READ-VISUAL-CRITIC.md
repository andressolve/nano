## BLOCKERS

### B1 — Page 4, Panels 3–4: Edmond's entrance reads as an unexplained identity swap

At native size I can distinguish Fernand's tied-back hair and black coat from Edmond's short curls and blue vest. At 390 px, however, Panel 3 establishes a dark-haired man alone at left and Panel 4 places another dark-haired man in the same dialogue lane without an entrance action. Fernand becomes a smaller background figure. Mercédès's “Only if you have forgotten the answer” can therefore appear to reverse her prior rejection.

**Repair prescription:** Regenerate the transition, not just the faces. End Panel 3 with Fernand alone and looking past Mercédès toward the harbor. In Panel 4, show Edmond visibly entering from the bright harbor edge in his locked blue vest and short curls, Mercédès crossing or turning decisively toward him, and Fernand displaced deep into the shadowed doorway with his locked tied hair and black coat. Give Edmond and Fernand different silhouettes at thumbnail size. If that cannot be made unmistakable in one panel, insert a narrow arrival beat in which Mercédès visibly recognizes Edmond before “Must I ask the question too?” Do not rely on facial nuance alone.

### B2 — Pages 9–10, especially Page 10 Panels 1 and 4: duplicate Edmond silhouettes corrupt the arrest staging

The feast includes multiple generic young men with Edmond's short dark curls, white shirt, and dark vest. Page 10 Panel 1 places a particularly strong Edmond-like seated face beside Louis while the real Edmond stands at right; another matching dark-curled back remains in the foreground. Page 10 Panel 4 repeats the back-of-head double. This is a visual-generation defect, not useful crowd variety, and it is maximally distracting when the officer asks which man is Edmond. The script's required silent Fernand is not visually identifiable by his established lock.

**Repair prescription:** Re-stage the feast crowd across both pages with a simple cast map. Edmond must be the only short-curled young man in a white shirt and blue/dark vest. Replace the bottom-center short-curled male guest on Page 9 Panels 1 and 4 and Page 10 Panels 1 and 4 with unmistakably different silhouettes—an older gray-haired guest, a bonneted woman, or a hat-wearing sailor. In Page 10 Panel 1, either replace the seated Edmond-like man beside Louis or, if he is intended as Fernand, restore Fernand exactly: long hair tied back, leaner face, black coat, red sash, and shadowed withdrawn posture. Preserve those same identities and seats through Page 10 Panel 4. At mobile size there should be one and only one plausible answer to “Which of you is Edmond Dantès?”

## NONBLOCKING FINDINGS

### N1 — Page 3, Panel 3: the full loaf contradicts the empty-cupboard beat

Louis says he spent all two hundred francs and let food wait, but a prominent intact loaf sits behind him. The script's stated visual engine is an empty cupboard and small food shelf. Dialogue carries the poverty, so comprehension survives, but the art weakens the page's central evidence.

**Repair prescription:** Remove the full loaf from Panel 3 and show an empty shelf, empty bowl, crumbs, or a single onion. Make the food in Panel 4 visibly new—ideally entering with Edmond or newly unpacked—so the material change from deprivation to provision is legible without dialogue.

### N2 — Page 6, Panel 1: “gives him the ship” briefly suggests ownership

The preceding offer is command, not transfer of the vessel. The surrounding caption says Danglars “expected command,” so this is recoverable.

**Repair prescription:** On any lettering revision, change the line to “Nineteen—and Morrel puts him in command” or “Nineteen—and Morrel gives him command of the ship.”

### N3 — Page 8, Panels 2–4: the two-paper trick depends more on dialogue than on visible paper continuity

At 390 px, Danglars's tearing action is not visible until the next panel, and the accusation is not graphically distinct from the harmless sheet. “Not that paper. The other one” repairs the logic, but the script's intended pen-to-paper-to-hand chain is not physically unmistakable.

**Repair prescription:** In Panel 2, show Danglars visibly tearing a small blank scrap while the intact accusation remains in a fixed, unobstructed location. Give the accusation a persistent nonverbal shape cue—a strong diagonal fold, dog-eared corner, or distinctive fold count—and carry that same silhouette through Caderousse's point in Panel 3, Fernand's hand in Panel 4, and the official handoff in Panel 6. Do not depend on generated handwriting for identification.

### N4 — Page 9, Panel 1: the generic toast speaker can be mistaken for Caderousse

The script identifies this man only as “Guest,” but his broad face and tavern-worker styling echo Caderousse, who has just participated in the plot. The confusion does not change the toast's meaning, yet it invites an unnecessary question about why Caderousse is celebrating with the family.

**Repair prescription:** Make the guest unmistakably unlike Caderousse in age, build, hair, and clothing, or use one of the already established female guests as the toast speaker.
