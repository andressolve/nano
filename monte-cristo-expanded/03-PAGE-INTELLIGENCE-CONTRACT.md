# The Count of Monte Cristo — Expanded Page-Intelligence Contract

## Purpose

This contract imports the strongest page-level disciplines from the Heike
project without importing its failed historical-survey approach.

The goal is not to make every page complex. It is to make every page
**intentional, causal, readable, and memorable**.

The Heike lesson is:

> Page intelligence comes from visual causality, hierarchy, rhythm, continuity,
> and transformation—not from explanatory density.

This file governs every expanded-edition script page, layout proof, prompt,
generation, and editorial review.

## Required Page Record

Before illustration, every page must specify:

1. **Narrative job** — the one indispensable reason the page exists.
2. **Reader state entering** — what a first-time reader securely knows.
3. **Desire and opposition** — who wants what and what prevents it.
4. **Dominant page mode** — dramatic scene, illustrated prose, or
   spectacle/silence.
5. **Density tier** — the intended total word load.
6. **Visual engine** — the composition, movement, object, or spatial
   relationship that makes the page understandable before all text is read.
7. **Panel map** — exact panel order, relative size, and reading direction.
8. **Exact text** — every word appearing on the finished page.
9. **Speaker map** — balloon ordinal, speaker, position, side, tail endpoint,
   and explicitly silent visible characters.
10. **Continuity references** — character, costume, object, setting, and prior
    accepted page references.
11. **Motif state** — whether a recurring object or composition appears and
    what it means at this point in the story.
12. **Reader state exiting** — what changed and why the next page follows.

A page is not ready for production while any of these fields remains implicit.

## Density Tiers

Density is chosen before layout, not discovered after generation.

| Tier | Words | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| S1 | 0–20 | Spectacle, silence, reveal, grief, decisive action |
| T2 | 20–45 | Atmospheric or lightly dialogued page |
| T3 | 45–75 | Standard dramatic page |
| T4 | 75–105 | Complex dramatic page with protected text fields |
| P5 | 105–140 | Illustrated-prose page designed around reading |

Rules:

- Never shrink text to rescue an overfull page.
- Never remove causal information merely to preserve an image.
- Never use P5 density inside conventional speech balloons.
- Split a page when two different dramatic turns compete for the same visual
  emphasis.
- A quiet page is not underwritten when silence performs a clear consequence.

## Portrait Composition Contract

Production canvas: **1024 × 1536, 2:3 portrait**.

Default:

- three to five panels;
- one dominant panel or image receiving 45–70% of visual attention;
- no more than two small reaction insets;
- one unmistakable top-to-bottom reading path;
- lettering areas reserved before rendering;
- important text never placed over high-detail faces, hands, maps, documents,
  rigging, water, or patterned clothing.

Portrait does not forbid wide cinema:

- ships, harbors, cells, tables, courtrooms, and opposed speakers may occupy
  full-width horizontal bands;
- successive wide bands may show time, distance, or changed power;
- a full-height figure or object may organize several smaller moments around
  it;
- a dominant vertical field may end in a narrow consequence strip.

Do not fill the page simply because portrait provides more vertical room.

## Page Archetypes

These are compositional tools, not templates to repeat mechanically.

### 1. The hierarchy page

Spatial level and distance explain power.

Use for:

- Villefort's betrothal world;
- prosecutor and accused;
- governor and prisoner;
- creditor and debtor.

The reader should know who controls the room before reading every line.

### 2. The causal chain

One object or action moves through several hands or institutions.

Use for:

- Leclère's letter;
- the anonymous denunciation;
- Villefort's prison order;
- Morrel's red purse;
- the purchased and cancelled notes.

The page fails if the panels can be reordered without changing meaning.

### 3. Continuous time

The same person appears at several points within one coherent environment.

Use for:

- Edmond's years in one cell;
- Faria's school;
- recovery aboard the *Jeune-Amélie*;
- Julie's race through Marseille.

Costume, body, light, objects, and posture must show which moment comes first.

### 4. Geography before action

The page first establishes terrain, exits, obstacles, and forces. Only then
does the decisive action occur.

Use for:

- Château d'If transfer and landing;
- tunnel orientation;
- burial-sack escape;
- Monte Cristo's split rock and cave;
- harbor arrival of the restored *Pharaon*.

An action sequence fails when the feat is exciting but the reader cannot
explain where bodies or objects moved.

### 5. Distance as consequence

Figures diminish, separate, or become unreachable while text decreases.

Use for:

- Marseille receding behind Edmond;
- years separating Edmond from his former life;
- Faria's absence after death;
- Edmond watching Morrel's family from a departing boat.

Distance should be felt spatially before narration names it.

### 6. Repeated composition, changed meaning

An earlier arrangement returns after the story has transformed its emotional
value.

Core Volume I repetitions:

- Page 1: Edmond brings Morrel's *Pharaon* home as its acting captain.
- Page 52: Edmond watches another *Pharaon* come home and refuses the center.
- Page 5: a family table imagines tomorrow.
- Pages 7–8: a conspirators' table destroys it.
- Page 3/20: Morrel's red purse carries help toward Louis.
- Page 50: the same purse carries Edmond's help toward Julie.
- Page 17: Château d'If is an enclosing black mass.
- Page 42: Monte Cristo is an opening black mass.

The repeated composition must change the reader's understanding, not merely
reward recognition.

## Motif Transformation Ledger

| Motif | Early state | Middle state | Volume-ending state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rope | Sailor's competence and command | tunnel measure, burial restraint, escape obstacle | rigging on the restored *Pharaon* |
| Paper | promise to a dying captain | denunciation, prison register, signed betrayal | cancelled notes and anonymous gift |
| Door/opening | Fernand blocks Mercédès; Villefort controls entry | cell door, tunnel hole, sack seam | cave entrance and Morrel's opened future |
| Red | sash, wine, wax, betrothal warmth | sealing wax, prison memory, danger | red silk purse carrying restored trust |
| Horizon | home available | home receding; prison has none | open sea and home restored for another |
| Name | Edmond Dantès spoken with pride | prisoner number, Busoni, Wilmore | “A friend” chosen freely |

Every appearance need not be literal. Avoid decorative motif repetition without
dramatic function.

## Character Entrance Contract

Every important character's first expanded-edition appearance establishes:

1. a present desire;
2. a relationship to someone already legible;
3. a social, professional, or institutional place;
4. a distinct silhouette;
5. a meaningful object, action, or gesture.

Examples:

- **Danglars:** wants authority aboard the *Pharaon*; displaced by Edmond;
  supercargo; controlled upright silhouette; ledger and pen.
- **Fernand:** wants Mercédès to choose him; her cousin and Edmond's rival;
  Catalan fisherman; tense forward posture; hand repeatedly occupying a
  doorway.
- **Caderousse:** wants comfort without responsibility; neighbor and creditor;
  lower Marseille tradesman; loose seated silhouette; cup he refuses to set
  down.
- **Villefort:** wants a royalist marriage and career unshadowed by his father;
  Renée's fiancé and crown prosecutor; polished formal silhouette; a hand that
  becomes still over dangerous paper.
- **Faria:** wants escape and intellectual companionship; Edmond's neighboring
  prisoner; priest and scholar; compact energetic movement despite age;
  improvised lamp and tool.

No plot turn may depend on remembering a character who was never introduced
through this contract or naturally reintroduced after a long absence.

## Text-Field Intelligence

### Dramatic pages

- Balloons stay within speaker-side lanes.
- First encountered balloon belongs to the first visible speaker.
- One visible speaker per panel is preferred when dialogue density rises.
- A–B–A uses upper A, opposing B, lower A only when necessary.
- Tail ends in open space immediately adjacent to the speaker's mouth.
- Silent visible characters are named in the page record.
- A consequential question names its action or object; no floating `Tomorrow?`,
  `Him?`, `This?`, or similarly incomplete fragment may carry necessary logic.
- Every balloon of five words or fewer must be understandable aloud without
  production notes and must have only one possible referent.
- Brevity that makes the reader reconstruct a missing noun, verb, intention,
  or promise is a blocker under
  [`19-ANTI-TERSE-DIALOGUE-MANDATE.md`](19-ANTI-TERSE-DIALOGUE-MANDATE.md).
- Do not treat low word count as page intelligence. If natural complete
  dialogue needs more room, increase the text allocation, restage the panel,
  or split the beat before removing necessary language or shrinking type.
- Do not treat technical decodability as page intelligence. Under
  [`20-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC-MANDATE.md`](20-EFFORTLESS-STORY-CRITIC-MANDATE.md),
  essential logic may not depend on rereading, prior-novel knowledge, tiny
  props, or subtle mechanics. The reader must be able to paraphrase the page's
  action, motive, and change after one normal read.

### Illustrated-prose pages

- Prose is set in one or two stable fields, never scattered into many fragments.
- Use short paragraphs and comfortable line lengths.
- Image and prose divide the page intentionally; neither is leftover space.
- Prose may accompany a continuous-time image but must not describe every
  repeated figure.
- A prose field is part of the original page concept, not a repair placed over
  failed art.

### Poems, letters, and documents

- Story-world documents appear only when a character reads, uses, hides, or
  acts on them.
- Long exact text receives a dedicated printed field.
- Do not ask generated handwriting to carry essential information.
- The letter, register, and paid notes must be readable as objects even when
  their full wording is not reproduced.

## Visual-Causality Tests

Before approving a page, ask:

- Can the reader identify the dominant action or relationship at thumbnail
  size?
- Does the eye encounter events in causal order?
- Could two panels be swapped without changing meaning? If yes, the sequence
  may be decorative rather than causal.
- Does the largest panel contain the page's most important moment?
- Does the final panel or lower field show a changed situation rather than
  merely another reaction?
- If all narration vanished, would the visual still communicate the essential
  movement?
- If all art vanished, would the text reveal an omitted scene rather than
  support the designed one?

## Continuity Map

Every image prompt must name:

- locked character references;
- locked setting and object references;
- the immediately previous accepted page when visual continuity matters;
- the earlier repeated composition or motif page when transformation matters;
- characters who must not appear;
- the exact number of visible people when crowd or identity drift is risky.

Continuity is story information. A costume, object, or face change that the
reader interprets as a plot event is not a cosmetic error.

## Page-Turn Contract

The bottom of a portrait page should create one of:

- a question;
- a decision;
- an interrupted action;
- a revealed object;
- a changed spatial relationship;
- an emotional consequence;
- a visual promise fulfilled on the next page.

Do not end every page on spoken surprise. Alternate dialogue turns with silent,
object, spatial, and consequence turns.

## Rejection Conditions

Regenerate or redesign the full page when:

- the page has more than one competing narrative job;
- a character's motive is present only in planning notes;
- the page starts in a location the reader cannot identify;
- prose replaces a scene that should be dramatized;
- a plan succeeds without showing its necessary step;
- a returning character is not naturally reintroduced;
- panel order is ambiguous;
- the dominant panel emphasizes the wrong beat;
- text is cramped, small, or placed over critical art;
- a balloon is misattributed or its tail points at another character;
- time, distance, identity, or object continuity can be misread;
- the actual canvas is not 1024 × 1536 portrait.

## Production Standard

The page-level test is:

> Does this page make the next fact easier to understand, the current emotion
> harder to escape, and the next turn impossible not to read?

If it does only one of those weakly, it needs another design pass before image
generation.
