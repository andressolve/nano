VERDICT: APPROVED

## Blind read
An old vellum chronicle page compares three eras of Arthurian armour. The page is owned by the paired full-length costume studies: an earlier spear-and-round-shield warrior on the left and a later red-surcoat knight on the right, with a faint plate-armoured figure behind them. The turn comes when the lower field explains that storytellers dressed Arthur in the armour of their own time, accounting for the shift from the likely warrior of about 500 to the later knightly image.

## Visible text
Chronicle prose, top field (the illuminated initial supplies the opening W): “What did warriors in Arthur’s time really look like? The songs made Arthur bigger, and so did the pictures. If a real Arthur lived around the year 500, he and his warriors probably looked like the man on the left: a long mail shirt if they were rich, a round wooden shield, a spear, and perhaps a helmet like the Romans wore. Knights in shining armour were still nearly a thousand years away.”

Label beneath the left warrior: “About 500”

Label beneath the right knight: “About 1200”

Label beside the faint plate-armoured figure: “The 1400s”

Chronicle prose, bottom field: “So why is Arthur almost never drawn like the man on the left? Because every storyteller dressed him in the armour of their own time. The storytellers and painters of about 1200 pictured him in mail and a great helm, so that is how this book draws him. Sir Thomas Malory, who wrote the most famous book of Arthur stories, lived in the 1400s among knights in steel plate, and that is how his readers pictured Arthur.”

Names are lettered only within the chronicle prose: “Arthur” and “Sir Thomas Malory.” No speaker name is separately lettered.
