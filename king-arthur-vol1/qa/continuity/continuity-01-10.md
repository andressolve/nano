# Visual-continuity gate — canonical pages 1–10

## Verdict

APPROVED

No page in pages 1–10 falls outside the visual identity of the book, and no
drift rises to the level of confusing character recognition, story state, or
reading.

## Continuity judgment

- The Greenwood Romance register remains coherent across the sequence: warm
  raw-umber foundations, visibly worked oil surfaces, weighty materials,
  simple readable shapes, and strong staged light. The cooler storm, snow,
  moonlight, and vellum passages are motivated variations within that system,
  not finish drift.
- Black levels and contrast stay consistent. Pages 1–2 use appropriately deep
  storm and chamber blacks; later snow scenes preserve dark costume and
  architectural anchors without becoming flatter or glossier.
- Arthur, Merlin, Ector, Kay, Lot, and Uther retain their approved facial,
  silhouette, costume, and color separations. Arthur remains the freckled,
  soft-jawed blond boy in patched brown; Kay remains the older black-haired,
  sharp-cheeked knight in black-and-yellow; Merlin remains the thin,
  wild-haired old man with hooked nose, forked beard, dark robe, and crooked
  staff. Ector, Lot, and Uther likewise remain immediately distinct and in
  their locked states.
- The sea-cliff castle and bedchamber, Ector farm and forge, London wall and
  locked snowy street, churchyard stone, and tournament field all preserve
  the approved setting designs and lighting logic.
- The sword in the stone reads as the locked plain sword: dull iron-grey,
  straight dark cross-guard, round pommel, and worn grip. It cannot be confused
  with Excalibur.
- Lettering weight, cream balloon treatment, tails, panel borders, and
  narration fields remain visually stable. Page 1's title hierarchy is an
  appropriate title-page exception. Page 7 correctly changes to the approved
  Chronicle register—warm ivory vellum, ruled ornament, iron-gall-style text,
  ink-and-wash map, and red/lapis/gold illumination—without importing the
  cast-page oil treatment.

## Revision findings

None.
