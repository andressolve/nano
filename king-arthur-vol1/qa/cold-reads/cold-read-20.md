## Retell of the story so far

On a stormy night, the wizard Merlin secretly carries a newborn away from a coastal castle. Two winters later, King Uther is dying. In front of his assembled lords, he asks Merlin whether his son Arthur is safe. Merlin says Arthur is safe only because the lords do not know where he is. Uther publicly declares that he has a son who should succeed him, then dies. The lords have never heard of the boy, Merlin will not reveal his hiding place, and Britain falls into years of rival claims, fighting, robbery, and neglect.

Far away, Arthur grows up on the farm of Sir Ector, an old knight, never suspecting that he is Uther's missing son. Arthur serves Ector's older son Kay, who has just been knighted. Kay teases him, but they are plainly close. When an invitation arrives for a great New Year tournament in London, Arthur begs to go as Kay's squire. That night he asks Ector whether he too will become a knight someday. Ector avoids the deeper issue but promises Arthur a place wherever he himself is, revealing strong paternal love and private sadness.

They travel to London, where the whole country has gathered and every lord hopes to become king. A historical interlude explains that Britain's Roman roads, towns, and walls outlasted Roman rule, after which the island split into small kingdoms while Angles, Saxons, and Jutes arrived. The book distinguishes that history from the legend now being told.

On Christmas morning, a sword appears embedded in a stone in a churchyard. Gold letters declare that whoever can draw it is Britain's true-born king. Every lord tries and fails. Merlin says there is one person for whom the sword is waiting.

At the tournament, Kay realizes that his own sword is still at the locked inn and sends Arthur running for it. Unable to enter the inn, Arthur remembers the unused churchyard sword. Thinking only of helping Kay, he pulls it from the stone as easily as if it were stuck in snow. He brings it back. Kay recognizes it, briefly claims that he himself drew it, and announces that he is king. Sir Ector immediately sees through the claim. He makes Kay swear on a holy book, and Kay admits Arthur gave him the sword. Ector has Arthur return it to the stone and pull it out again in front of them.

When Arthur succeeds, Ector and Kay kneel. Arthur is baffled and orders them up. Ector calls him "my lord" and reveals that he is not Arthur's birth father: fifteen years earlier Merlin brought the baby to his door and ordered him to raise the child as his own and never ask whose he was. Arthur is devastated. In one morning, he feels he has lost both father and brother and been left with only a sword. Ector assures him that their bond remains, and Arthur asks if he may still call him Father. Arthur also asks that Kay become his steward. Kay admits his momentary lie; Arthur answers by giving him lifelong trust, and the brothers reconcile.

That night Arthur sits alone in the church with the sword, unable to sleep. He knows exactly who he was that morning but now faces a city whispering his name, an unknown birth father, Merlin's unexplained intervention, and the prospect of making Britain's lords kneel. A second historical interlude explains that the sword-in-the-stone episode was added to Arthurian tradition centuries after any possible historical Arthur.

The next morning Arthur demonstrates the sword before the lords. King Lot of Orkney refuses to accept a farm-raised squire as king, demands to know Arthur's father, and mocks his upbringing as a pigsty. Arthur angrily defends Ector's farm. Lot turns the answer into another insult and encourages the other lords to laugh. Arthur has proved the sign, but not yet won their allegiance.

## Cast

- **Arthur:** A slim, fair-skinned boy of about fifteen with tousled blond hair and freckles, dressed in rough brown farm clothes. He begins as Kay's eager squire and Ector's loving younger son. He is helpful enough to fetch a sword without thinking of himself, honest once questioned, deeply wounded by the truth of his adoption, loyal to the family that raised him, and now the reluctant apparent king.
- **Merlin:** A gaunt, very old man with long white hair and beard, dark weather-beaten robes, and a staff. He secretly rescues the baby Arthur, protects his hiding place, delivers him to Ector, stages or oversees the sword test, and knows the truth that the rest of the cast lacks.
- **King Uther:** Arthur's birth father and the dead king of Britain. He is an aged, gray-haired and gray-bearded man shown frail in an ornate red-and-gold bed. His last public act is to declare that his hidden son should rule after him.
- **Sir Ector:** A broad, heavy, balding older man, usually in a worn red tunic or cloak over mail. He is an old knight living as a farmer and smith, Kay's father and Arthur's foster father. He kept Merlin's secret for fifteen years, loves Arthur as his own, recognizes Kay's false claim immediately, and is the first to accept Arthur as king.
- **Kay:** A tall, lean young man with black hair and a dark moustache, wearing mail and a yellow-and-black quartered surcoat. He is Ector's biological son, Arthur's foster brother, and a newly made knight. He is teasing and proud, briefly gives in to temptation when handed the royal sword, then tells the truth and reconciles with Arthur. Arthur names him steward.
- **King Lot of Orkney:** A massive, powerful northern lord with long red hair and beard, braids, a small crown, mail, heavy black fur, and gold ornaments. He is the strongest visible challenger to Arthur's claim and uses Arthur's unknown parentage and humble upbringing to turn the assembled lords against him.
- **The lords of Britain:** A varied crowd of armed noblemen and kings. Their rivalry has kept Britain divided; they accept the sword test enough to attempt it themselves, but at least some are ready to reject its result when it favors Arthur.

## Causal chain

Uther hides Arthur because rival lords would kill the boy -> Merlin places Arthur with Ector and keeps his location secret -> Uther dies before Arthur can be publicly established -> the lords compete for the crown and Britain fractures -> the tournament brings the rival lords, Ector, Kay, and Arthur to London -> the sword test offers a non-military way to identify a king -> Kay forgets his sword and sends Arthur to fetch it -> the inn is locked, so Arthur takes the churchyard sword -> Kay's false claim prompts Ector to investigate -> Arthur repeats the feat before Ector and Kay -> Ector reveals Arthur's adoption and accepts him as king -> Arthur preserves his family bond and appoints Kay steward -> Arthur publicly repeats the feat -> Lot attacks the legitimacy of the unknown, farm-raised boy, turning proof by wonder into a political confrontation.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

No page required backtracking or rereading.

Pages 1–3 invite the inference that the rescued baby and Ector's blond farm boy are Uther's missing son Arthur before the connection is fully explained. That inference is easy, intentional, and not needed to understand the immediate action. Pages 15 and 18 later confirm that Merlin delivered Arthur as a baby, while Arthur's exact biological parentage remains unknown to Arthur himself.

The history pages (7 and 19) clearly signal a shift from the story to historical context. They interrupt the action briefly but do not confuse the sequence or require recovery when the story resumes.

## What I expect next

I expect Lot and other lords to refuse Arthur despite the sword, forcing Arthur to prove more than the magical sign. Merlin will likely step forward to explain Arthur's identity and connection to Uther, but the disclosure may still provoke political or military resistance. Arthur's immediate challenge will be to withstand public humiliation, defend the worth of the family who raised him, and turn reluctant rivals into followers. Kay, as steward, and Ector, as the first man to kneel, should become his first dependable allies.
