# Cold read — canonical pages 1–40

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without outside documentation. I could follow the identity reveal, the loyalties within Arthur's family, the political challenge to his crown, the first battle, and the beginning of the forest-knight episode on one normal-paced read. No character was materially misidentified, and no page caused reader harm that would justify revision.

## Retell of the story so far

During a storm, an old wizard named Merlin secretly carries a newborn out of a coastal castle. Two winters later, the dying King Uther asks whether his hidden son Arthur is safe. Merlin assures him that the boy is far away, because the frightened lords would kill him if they found him. Uther publicly declares that he has a son who should succeed him, then dies. The lords have never seen this son, however, and Britain splinters into rival kingdoms while the boy grows up on the farm of Sir Ector, unaware that he is Uther's heir.

Fifteen years later Arthur is a poor, cheerful squire to Ector's proud older son Kay. Arthur and Kay tease each other like brothers, though Arthur aches to become a knight and Ector reacts sadly when Arthur calls the sword he carries his only inheritance. The family travels to London for a great New Year tournament where every lord hopes to become king. On Christmas morning a sword appears through an anvil on a stone in a churchyard, with an inscription naming whoever can draw it as Britain's true king. All the lords fail. King Lot, a huge red-haired northern king, calls it one of Merlin's tricks.

On tournament day Kay discovers his sword is missing and sends Arthur to fetch it. The inn is locked, so Arthur remembers the unused sword in the churchyard. Thinking only of helping Kay, he pulls it free with no effort and brings it back. Kay briefly claims the deed, but Ector makes him swear on a holy book and Kay admits Arthur gave him the sword. Arthur openly explains what happened and pulls it again. Ector and Kay kneel to him. Ector reveals that Merlin brought Arthur to him as a baby and ordered him to raise the child as his own. Arthur is wounded by the discovery that Ector and Kay are not his blood family, but chooses them again: Ector may still be his father in private, and Kay will be his steward for life.

Arthur spends a troubled night wondering who he is and whether the lords will accept a nameless squire. Lot mocks him as a farm boy, but Arthur's honest defense of Ector's farm wins the people of London. They acclaim him, and he is crowned after swearing to be just and protect them. Merlin promises to tell Arthur about his father in private. At Arthur's first feast as king, Lot returns Arthur's gifts contemptuously and threatens to take his head. Merlin stops the exchange and announces that Arthur is Uther's son. Lot rejects the wizard's word and leaves to settle the question in battle.

Arthur is furious because Merlin had promised that Arthur would hear the truth first. Merlin says that he kept silent in the churchyard so the people could choose Arthur for himself, and spoke at the feast only after Lot branded him a nobody. He also admits taking Arthur from Uther and says he would do it again, because the rival lords would have murdered the baby. Arthur learns that Uther saw him once and named him. Merlin postpones the story of Arthur's mother. Kay confirms that Arthur has never fought a real battle, but promises to stay beside him. Merlin points out that the ordinary people chose Arthur without knowing his father and says Lot is coming for them as much as for him. Arthur decides they will all fight together.

Arthur's army is far smaller than Lot's. On the eve of battle he lies awake thinking of the people who trusted him, then arms himself like the squire he has always been. At Bedegraine he impetuously charges ahead into the stream and is surrounded. Kay rides into the enemy mass, names himself the king's steward and Arthur's brother, and pulls Arthur back to their line. Arthur raises the sword among his frightened people; it shines with an uncanny blaze, rallies them, and terrifies Lot into retreat. Arthur wins his first battle but sees the wounded and dead instead of celebrating. Merlin tells him that this grief is the cost a good king must keep looking at.

Arthur establishes his court at Camelot. Songs turn the frightened boy into an invincible shining hero and omit Kay's rescue. Arthur first laughs, then begins to believe them. When Kay reports that a knight at a forest fountain defeats everyone who passes, Arthur says he will fight the knight himself. Kay refuses: kings in songs may act alone, but real kings send knights and listen to their stewards. Arthur slips out at night anyway, happy to escape crowns, counsel, and doubts. At the fountain he meets the massive, weathered King Pellinore. Arthur invokes his crown, but Pellinore has never yielded to anyone and agrees eagerly to fight. During their mounted and then dismounted combat, Pellinore's blow shatters Arthur's sword. The section ends with Arthur horrified at the loss of the sword that proved and sustained his kingship.

The historical interludes establish that the legend sits on uncertain ground: post-Roman Britain fractured after Rome withdrew; the sword-in-the-stone episode appeared centuries after any possible historical Arthur; early accounts disagree or omit Arthur; and the armour of the familiar legend belongs to later storytellers rather than the likely period.

## Cast as read

- **Arthur:** A slim, fair-skinned, freckled blond boy of about fifteen, first dressed in patched brown farm clothes and later in mail with a blue surcoat. He begins as Kay's affectionate, quick-witted squire, is revealed as Uther's hidden son, and becomes Britain's young king. His compassion and honesty are clear, but so are his insecurity, impulsiveness, and growing susceptibility to his own legend.
- **Merlin:** A gaunt, very old man with wild white hair and beard, dark ragged robes, a twisted staff, and a green ring. He is the wizard who hid and protected Arthur, engineers the public revelation of his claim, counsels him, and withholds parts of his parentage until he judges the time right.
- **Sir Ector:** A heavy, balding older man in a worn red tunic, with a blacksmith/farmer's practical appearance. He is Arthur's loving foster father and Kay's father. He obeyed Merlin, kept Arthur's origin secret, verifies the sword miracle, and kneels even though Arthur still wants to call him Father.
- **Kay:** A tall, lean young man with black hair, a thin moustache, and initially a black-and-yellow quartered surcoat over mail; later he wears plainer black-and-yellow steward's clothing. He is Ector's biological son, Arthur's foster brother, knight, protector, and appointed steward. Proud and sharp-tongued, he lies for a moment about drawing the sword but confesses, repeatedly risks himself for Arthur, and serves as the practical check on Arthur's romantic impulses.
- **King Uther:** A frail, grey-haired dying king shown in a richly covered bed. He is Arthur's biological father, whose last act is to acknowledge an unseen son and bless his succession.
- **King Lot:** A huge, broad red-haired and red-bearded king with braided hair, a crown, dark mail, heavy furs, and a gold neck ring. He is the strongest rival lord: contemptuous of Arthur's low upbringing, unwilling to accept Merlin's claim, and commander of the much larger army defeated at Bedegraine.
- **King Pellinore:** A massive, older grey-bearded fighter in battered plate and a ragged green surcoat bearing a pale animal. He guards a forest fountain, challenges every passerby, recognizes no special privilege in Arthur's title, and immediately proves dangerous enough to break Arthur's sword.
- **The people of London / Arthur's army:** Townspeople, farmers, laborers, and a few knights who choose Arthur after hearing his honest defense of his upbringing. Their trust gives him both legitimacy and a responsibility he feels keenly after the battle.

## Causal chain

1. Rival lords would murder Uther's infant heir, so Merlin hides Arthur with Ector.
2. Uther dies before presenting the boy, so Britain fractures and the lords compete for the crown.
3. The sword appears as a public test; no established lord can draw it.
4. Kay needs a replacement sword, so Arthur unknowingly takes the churchyard sword and exposes his claim.
5. Kay's false claim prompts Ector to demand an oath, which produces the truth and a repeat of the test.
6. Ector's revelation gives Arthur a royal possibility but also strips away his assumed identity as Ector's natural son.
7. Lot's mockery makes Arthur defend the farm and the people who raised him; London's people choose him.
8. Lot's rejection of a nameless boy forces Merlin to reveal that Arthur is Uther's son.
9. Lot chooses battle, so the untested Arthur must lead the people who acclaimed him.
10. Arthur charges alone and is nearly killed; Kay rescues him and restores him to the line.
11. Arthur raises the supernatural sword, rallying his smaller army and routing Lot.
12. Songs erase the cost and Kay's rescue, encouraging Arthur to imitate a fearless storybook king.
13. Kay forbids Arthur to answer the fountain challenge personally, so Arthur sneaks away without counsel.
14. Arthur challenges Pellinore and discovers that neither his title nor his famous sword makes him invulnerable; Pellinore breaks the blade.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

None. The chronicle pages changed the reading mode and invited a slower look, but their placement and purpose were immediately clear and did not interrupt the fictional causal chain. The wounded man Arthur helps after Bedegraine reads as one of his ordinary soldiers, not as a newly introduced named character, so no identification was needed.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text failed at reading pace. Balloon tails and speaker placement remained clear, including the crowded churchyard, feast, and battle pages. The narration was readable in sequence, and the large crowd cry on page 21 functioned as a collective voice. No line required outside knowledge to understand who spoke or what it referred to.

## What I expect next

Arthur will have to survive Pellinore without the magical symbol on which his confidence and public authority have partly rested. I expect Merlin to intervene or lead Arthur toward another sword, but the more important consequence should be Arthur learning the lesson Kay tried to teach him: kingship is not solitary heroics. Pellinore may become an ally once the fight establishes mutual respect. I also expect the withheld story of Arthur's mother, Lot's return, and the tension between Arthur's real deeds and the songs about him to remain active beyond this encounter.
