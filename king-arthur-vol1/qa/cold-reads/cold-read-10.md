# Cold read: canonical pages 1–10

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without documentation. I understood the hidden-heir setup, the family relationships, the reason Arthur goes looking for a sword, and the significance of the sword in the churchyard on one normal-paced read.

## Retell of the story so far

During a violent storm, Merlin secretly carries a newborn away from a castle. Two years later, King Uther is dying. His lords want to know who will rule after him, but Uther's concern is for his hidden son, Arthur. Merlin assures him that Arthur is safe only because he is far away and unknown. Uther publicly declares that he has a son who should succeed him, then dies before anyone learns where the boy is.

For the next fourteen years, none of the ambitious lords can claim an uncontested crown. Britain fractures into warfare and lawlessness. Arthur grows up unaware of his birth on Sir Ector's modest farm. He serves as squire to Ector's older son, Kay, teasing him while polishing his helmet. A messenger announces a great New Year tournament in London, and Ector agrees that the whole household will attend.

That night Arthur asks Ector whether he will ever become a real knight. Ector promises that he will when he is grown, but his private sadness suggests he knows Arthur's future and cannot explain it yet.

After a six-day journey, they reach crowded London, where all Britain's lords have gathered for the tournament and each hopes to become king. A historical pause explains that Roman rule once held Britain together, that the Romans withdrew, and that competing kingdoms and later invaders produced the divided country in which the legend takes place.

On Christmas morning, a stone bearing a sword appears in the churchyard. Its inscription declares that whoever draws the sword is Britain's true-born king. King Lot of Orkney, presented as the strongest northern lord, cannot pull it free and mocks it as a trick, while Merlin says that the sword is waiting for the right man.

At the tournament, Kay discovers that his own sword is still at the inn and orders Arthur to fetch it before the trumpets sound. The inn is locked and deserted because everyone is at the tournament. Afraid that Kay will be humiliated, Arthur remembers the unused sword in the churchyard and goes to get that one instead.

## Cast

- **Arthur:** A slim, fair-skinned blond youth of about sixteen, dressed in rough brown farm clothes. He is energetic, good-humored, loyal, and eager to become a knight. He does not know that he is Uther's son or the rightful heir.
- **Merlin:** A tall, gaunt elderly man with wild white hair and a very long white beard, wearing dark, weathered robes and carrying a staff. He rescued and concealed Arthur, advises Uther, and understands the sword's purpose.
- **King Uther:** Arthur's elderly, dying father, with long gray-white hair and beard, shown in bed wearing rich red and gold. His final public act is to name his hidden son as heir.
- **Sir Ector:** A broad, heavyset, balding older man in a rust-red tunic over mail. He is a knight, farmer, and father figure raising Arthur alongside Kay. His guarded answer about Arthur's future suggests loving concern and secret knowledge.
- **Kay:** A tall, athletic young knight with dark wavy hair and a small mustache, wearing black-and-yellow clothing and mail. He is Ector's older son and Arthur's foster brother; Arthur serves as his squire. He is playful at home but anxious about his reputation at his first tournament.
- **King Lot of Orkney:** A huge, red-haired and red-bearded northern king in heavy armor, fur, ornaments, and a crown. He is physically imposing and confident, but cannot draw the sword.
- **Uther's lords:** A crowd of armored and fur-clad nobles who compete for the vacant crown and contribute to Britain's long disorder.

## Causal chain

Merlin hides Uther's infant son to keep him alive -> Uther dies before revealing the child's location -> the lords cannot identify or accept an heir -> Britain spends fourteen years divided by rival claimants -> the lords converge on London for a tournament where each hopes to advance his claim -> the sword appears as a test that can identify the true-born king -> the strongest lord fails to draw it -> Kay leaves his sword at the inn -> the locked inn forces Arthur to seek another sword -> Arthur remembers the churchyard sword and approaches it without pursuing the crown.

## Backtracking, rereading, or guesses

None. I did not need to backtrack or reread any page. The time jumps on pages 2 and 3 were explicit, and I could distinguish Arthur, Kay, Ector, Merlin, Uther, and Lot when each became important. I did not misidentify any character.

Page 7 pauses the immediate action to supply historical context, but it clearly distinguishes history from legend and did not make me lose the story thread.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No text failed at reading pace. Every caption, inscription, and speech balloon was legible in sequence. Speaker attribution was clear from placement, tails, character poses, and the surrounding exchange. In particular, the dialogue on page 8 made it clear that the large crowned man was King Lot and that Merlin, not Lot, knew the sword was waiting for someone else.

## What I expect next

Arthur will pull the churchyard sword free easily because he is Uther's hidden son, but he will initially treat it simply as a replacement for Kay's missing sword. Kay or others may claim the feat or misunderstand how Arthur obtained it. Merlin will recognize what has happened, and Arthur's foster family and the gathered lords will be forced to confront his identity. Because the lords have spent years fighting for power, proof from the sword will begin the struggle over whether they will accept him as king rather than end it immediately.
