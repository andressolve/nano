# Cold read: canonical pages 1–30

## Verdict

APPROVED

The story lands without documentation. I could follow the characters, relationships, revelations, and change from private family crisis to public war on one normal-paced read.

## Retell of the story so far

During a storm, the wizard Merlin secretly carries a newborn away from King Uther's castle. Two winters later, as Uther lies dying, Merlin reassures him that his hidden son Arthur is safe. Uther declares that son his heir, but the assembled lords have never heard that he exists. After Uther's death, no one can find the boy. Rival lords fight over the crown while Britain falls into disorder.

Fifteen years later, Arthur is a farm boy being raised by Sir Ector alongside Ector's older son, Kay. Kay is about to attend a great New Year tournament in London, where every knight in Britain has been summoned and each powerful lord hopes to become king. Arthur goes as Kay's squire. In London, a sword has appeared in a stone in a churchyard, with a promise that whoever draws it is Britain's rightful king. Even King Lot, the strongest northern lord, cannot move it.

Just before Kay's tournament bout, he discovers that his sword was left at the locked inn and sends Arthur to fetch it. Desperate not to fail him, Arthur remembers the unused sword in the churchyard. He pulls it from the stone without effort and brings it to Kay. Kay briefly presents it as his own, but when Ector questions him, he admits Arthur gave it to him. Arthur innocently explains what happened and repeats the feat. Ector and Kay kneel. Ector then reveals that Merlin brought Arthur to him as a baby and ordered him to raise the child as his own without questions. Arthur is devastated to learn that Ector is not his birth father and that Kay is not his biological brother, but both reassure him that they remain his family. Arthur promises that Kay will be his steward.

Alone that night, Arthur struggles with suddenly being king and still knowing nothing about his birth. The next morning, King Lot publicly mocks the idea that a nameless farm boy can rule. Arthur answers back, and the listening London crowd chooses Arthur. He is crowned and swears to rule justly and protect everyone. Merlin privately confirms that Arthur is Uther's son but postpones the story of Arthur's mother.

At Arthur's feast, Lot returns gifts Arthur had sent and replaces them with a threat: a sharp sword meant for Arthur's neck. Merlin publicly identifies Arthur as Uther's son, but Lot refuses to accept him and promises battle. Arthur angrily challenges Merlin over keeping him ignorant of his father. Merlin says secrecy saved Arthur's life and that he would make the same choice again. Arthur asks whether Uther ever saw him; Merlin says Uther saw and named him on the night of his birth. Merlin also warns Arthur not to dwell on how many people truly chose him, because Lot is coming for Arthur's supporters as well as for him. Arthur decides they will all fight together.

Arthur's army is small: a few knights and many ordinary people who answered his call. Facing Lot's much larger force at Bedegraine, Arthur spends the night thinking about those who may die because they chose him. Before dawn, he puts on his own mail like the squire he still feels himself to be. At the start of battle, Arthur charges ahead before the line. Kay tries to stop him, but Arthur rides into the clash and ends the section knocked or thrown into the water amid the fighting.

## Cast

- **Arthur:** A slim, fair-haired, freckled boy of about fifteen, first dressed in worn brown farm clothes and later in mail and a blue royal surcoat. He is Ector's foster son, Kay's foster brother and squire, Uther's hidden son, and the newly acclaimed king. He is loyal, direct, emotionally young, and brave enough to become reckless.
- **Merlin:** An old, gaunt man with long wild white hair and beard, dark weathered robes, and a staff. He is the wizard who hid Arthur, entrusted him to Ector, arranged or understands the sword test, establishes Arthur's parentage, and advises him.
- **Sir Ector:** A large, balding older man in a red tunic, mail, and a brown cloak. He is Arthur's loving foster father and Kay's father. He raised Arthur under Merlin's instructions, reveals the truth when the sword identifies Arthur, and immediately accepts him as king while still treating him as a son.
- **Kay:** A tall, dark-haired young knight with a moustache, usually in chain mail and a yellow-and-black surcoat. He is Ector's biological son, Arthur's foster brother, and later Arthur's promised steward. He briefly claims the sword, then tells the truth; he is proud and sharp-tongued but loyal and protective.
- **King Uther:** Arthur's elderly, grey-haired birth father, seen dying in a richly furnished bed while wearing red and gold. He secretly names Arthur as his heir, but dies before that claim can be made credible to the lords.
- **King Lot:** A huge, broad red-haired and red-bearded northern king in heavy dark armor, furs, gold ornaments, and later a crown. He is the strongest rival claimant, publicly rejects Arthur as an unknown farm boy, and leads the army now fighting him.
- **The lords:** Rival nobles and armored kings who gather around Uther, try the sword, witness Arthur's rise, and divide between acceptance and opposition. Apart from Lot, they function so far as the contested ruling class rather than as distinct individuals.
- **The people of London and Arthur's volunteers:** Townspeople, farmers, smiths, laborers, and a few knights who acclaim Arthur and form his badly outnumbered army. They turn the sword's sign into public consent and give Arthur people he feels responsible for protecting.

Arthur's mother is discussed but has not yet been identified or shown.

## Causal chain

1. Merlin hides Uther's newborn son because the rival lords would kill him.
2. Uther dies after naming the absent boy his heir, but the lords do not know or believe there is a son.
3. With no accepted king, the lords fight and Britain deteriorates.
4. Merlin's sword test offers a public sign that can identify a rightful king without relying on a disputed private claim.
5. Kay forgets his sword, and the inn is locked, so Arthur takes the churchyard sword solely to help him.
6. Arthur's effortless success exposes him as the chosen king; Kay's failed claim and confession identify Arthur as the true person who drew it.
7. Ector reveals Arthur's adoption, costing Arthur his secure sense of family at the exact moment he gains a kingdom.
8. Lot's public mockery forces the question from magical sign to popular acceptance, and London's people openly choose Arthur.
9. Arthur's coronation and Merlin's claim that he is Uther's son threaten Lot's own bid for power.
10. Lot rejects Arthur, issues a death threat, and marches against him.
11. Arthur chooses to defend the ordinary people who chose him, even with a much smaller army.
12. His courage and urgency outrun his judgment, so he charges before his battle line and ends page 30 in immediate danger.

## Backtracking, rereading, guesses, and misidentifications

None. I did not need to go back to identify a person, recover a missing action, determine who possessed the sword, or understand why the battle began. I did not misidentify any recurring character.

The historical interludes on pages 7, 20, and 27 deliberately pause the dramatic story, but each was understandable on its first pass and the return to the narrative was clear.

## Text and attribution at reading pace

No dialogue, narration, or speaker attribution failed at reading pace. The exchanges among Arthur, Kay, Ector, Merlin, and Lot remained attributable through staging, balloon placement, and consistent appearance. The sword inscription and the large crowd declaration were also immediately legible and narratively clear.

## What I expect next

Arthur's premature charge will force him to survive his first real fight without the protection of ceremony or legend, while Kay and the small army try to reach him and hold formation. I expect this mistake to test whether Arthur can become a leader rather than merely a brave boy. The immediate conflict should be the battle with Lot; beyond it, I expect Lot either to be defeated or forced into a larger continuing rivalry. I also expect Merlin's withheld account of Arthur's mother and birth to return later, because Arthur's unanswered family question remains emotionally active.
