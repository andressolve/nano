## Page 1 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two title-card lines and the caption must each read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. The title lines are printed title text over the sky, not a balloon. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The flight does not land
The page must read as one spectacle image: a castle on a storm cliff and one small hooded figure carrying a bundle down the path, the warm lantern light on a tiny baby's hand. If the reader cannot find the bundle or the hand, or the figure reads as walking toward the castle rather than away, the turn fails.

### C3 — The man is not Merlin
A very tall, thin, stooped figure in a dark grey-green hooded robe with a crooked staff taller than himself, face hidden. A pointed hat, stars, a glowing spell, or a short heavy figure is blocking.

### C4 — Extra presence
No other person on the page and no balloon. A second figure, or the baby drawn with a face as a toddler, is blocking; rain, waves and rock texture are nonblocking.

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All six strings, two captions and four spoken lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match. A line placed directly beside its speaker is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The secret does not land
The reader must see that the name passes in a whisper between Uther and Merlin alone, and that Uther then tells the lords only that he has a son. If the whisper reads as said to the lords, or the closing caption reads before Uther's public line, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution in the whisper
"Is he safe? My son? My Arthur?" hangs from Uther's mouth; "Safe, and far from here…" from Merlin's, clearly after. Merlin's opening line is his; "I have a son…" is Uther's. A swapped or shared tail is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Uther: gaunt, grey moustache with no beard, in the bed under the red dragon. Merlin: very tall, thin, forked white beard, wild white hair, hooked nose. A bearded Uther, or a Merlin with a short or square beard, is blocking; repeated faces among the shadowed lords are nonblocking.

### C5 — Death drawn
Uther must be alive in every panel; no deathbed corpse image. Blocking if shown dead.

### C2 — The news does not land
The reader must see the brothers' banter in the yard, then Ector arriving with a letter, then the boy begging to go. If the letter's arrival is lost or the last panel does not read as Arthur pleading and Ector agreeing, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the last exchange
"My first tournament!" is Kay's; "Can I come? As Kay's squire? Please, Father." is Arthur's; "We'll all go…" is Ector's, read last. In the first panel the order teaser and the "squire" line are Kay's and the reply is Arthur's. A swapped owner is blocking.

### C4 — Brothers confused
Arthur is a slight, fair-haired, round-faced beardless boy in a patched brown tunic; Kay is a lean, sharp-faced, black-haired young man with a thin moustache in black-and-yellow over mail. If the two can be mistaken, or Arthur wears deep blue, it is blocking.

### C5 — Ector is not Ector
Short, broad, bald-topped with a grey fringe, round clean-shaven ruddy face, russet tunic. A beard or a tall thin build is blocking.

The reader must see Ector avoiding Arthur's eyes and, at the end, Ector alone looking after the boy, unhappy. If Ector meets Arthur's gaze warmly while answering, or the final image lacks him alone with the empty doorway, the secret is lost and it is blocking.

## Page 7 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two chronicle fields and four labels must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Each label must sit by its map feature: roads, the northern wall, London, the ships. The illuminated W is the first letter of "Who", not an extra letter. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — Every claim reads as written
The page's facts (Rome for almost four hundred years, the soldiers leaving around 410, Angles, Saxons, and Jutes, Uther, Merlin and the lost boy as legend) must read exactly as written. A changed date, number, or name is blocking.

### C3 — Not visibly a chronicle
The whole page must read at a glance as a page from an old chronicle: ivory vellum, ruled margin, brown ink and watercolour, an illuminated initial in red and blue with gold. A painted oil scene, a story character, a balloon, or a caption box is blocking.

### C4 — The map does not carry the idea
The map of Britain must own the page, with red roads meeting at London, a wall across the north, and ships coming from the east. An unrecognizable island or a missing wall is blocking; minor coastline inaccuracy is nonblocking.
