## PAGE 1 — The storm and the bundle

### Reader turn
On a storm night a tall hooded old man carries a newborn down a cliff path away from a castle, and tells no one where.

### Ordered moments
1. One full-page panel, the whole page. High in the top third, a black castle on a sea cliff with one lit window, against a dark storm sky. Rain in slanting sheets; waves bursting white on the rocks far below. A narrow switchback path cut into the cliff face. On it, small in the middle-lower part of the page, one tall, thin, stooped figure in a dark hooded robe, face hidden in the hood, leaning on a crooked staff taller than himself, a bundle held against his chest under his cloak. A small lantern hangs from the staff and throws the only warm light on the page onto the bundle, where a tiny baby's hand reaches out of the cloth. The lantern and the tiny hand are the focal point; the figure stays small.
2. The title card is lettered over the open storm sky at the top, above the castle: two lines, a large title and a smaller subtitle under it, in a clean, dignified book-title lettering, no box.
3. The caption sits lower left over plain dark rock, a tail-free caption field, clear of the path and the figure.

### Exact strings
TITLE CARD (line 1): `KING ARTHUR`
TITLE CARD (line 2): `The Sword and the Table`
CAPTION: `On a night of storm, an old man named Merlin carried a newborn baby away from the castle, and told no one where.`

Render exactly these 3 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page. The two title-card lines are printed title text over the sky, not a balloon or a sign.

### Character locks
Merlin (hooded, carrying the bundle, p1 only): very tall, very thin, stooped from the shoulders, long neck; hood up and face hidden in shadow, only perhaps the tip of a long forked white beard catching the lantern; very dark grey-green hooded robe frayed at the hem, rope belt; big-knuckled hand on a crooked staff taller than himself; the bundle in the crook of his other arm. He is silent: no balloon.

### Consequential exclusions
No other person anywhere: no guards, no riders, no figure in the window. No pointed hat, no stars, no glowing magic; the lantern is an ordinary small flame. The baby is only a swaddled bundle and one tiny hand, no face. No balloons. No words on any object; no page number, no credits.

## PAGE 2 — My Arthur

### Reader turn
The dying king whispers his son's name to Merlin alone, then tells his lords only that he has a son, and dies.

### Ordered moments
1. Dominant panel, about 50% of the page, top. A dark stone chamber lit by one brazier. A great bed with a red-and-gold coverlet embroidered with a red dragon. Uther in the bed, propped on pillows. At the bedside, standing over him on the right, Merlin, hood back, leaning on his staff, mouth visible. Crowded at the foot of the bed in shadow, a dozen lords in furs and mail leaning in, hungry and anxious. The caption sits top left over the dark wall; Merlin's balloon beside his mouth, its tail to his mouth.
2. Panel two, about 30%: close on the bed. Uther on the LEFT grips Merlin's sleeve and pulls him down; Merlin on the RIGHT bends so close his beard touches the pillow. Lords behind out of focus. Uther's whisper balloon upper left beside Uther's mouth; Merlin's answer lower right beside Merlin's mouth, clearly lower.
3. Panel three, about 20%, bottom: Uther, with the last of his strength, lifts his head toward the lords at the foot of the bed; his balloon beside his mouth, high in the panel. The closing caption runs across the bottom of the panel, clearly after and below Uther's line.

### Exact strings
CAPTION: `Two winters later, King Uther lay dying.`
MERLIN: `Your lords are frightened, Uther. They want to know who will be king when you are gone. Tell them.`
UTHER: `Is he safe? My son? My Arthur?`
MERLIN: `Safe, and far from here. If your lords knew where, he wouldn't live a week.`
UTHER: `I have a son. He will be king after me. He has my blessing.`
CAPTION: `King Uther died that night. Not one of his lords had ever heard of a son.`

Render exactly these 6 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page.

### Character locks
Uther (dying in the bed, p2 only): gaunt, hollow-cheeked old man, sunken eyes, long thin grey hair spread on the pillow, a long grey moustache and no beard; red-and-gold coverlet with the red dragon. Merlin (hood back): very tall, very thin, stooped, long narrow deeply lined face, great hooked nose, deep-set bright black eyes under wild brows, wild white hair standing away from his head, a long narrow forked white beard to the chest; very dark grey-green robe, rope belt, crooked staff, silver ring with a green stone. Merlin and Uther: forked white beard and standing versus grey moustache only and lying in bed. Lords: middle-aged, brown or grey hair, short beards, furs, mail and muted surcoats; no copper-red braids, no forked white beards, no blond boys.

### Consequential exclusions
No baby, no Arthur, no Kay, no Ector. No death drawn: Uther is alive in every panel. No glowing magic. No crown on the pillow. No other text.

## PAGE 4 — My first tournament

### Reader turn
News of a London tournament arrives at the farm, and Arthur begs his way onto the journey.

### Ordered moments
1. Dominant panel, about 50% of the page: a muddy farmyard, a forge glowing in an open shed, hens underfoot. Kay on the LEFT, posing with one foot on a mounting block, a new sword on his hip, chin up, half-smile. Arthur on the RIGHT, sitting on an upturned bucket, polishing a shining new helmet, looking up. Kay's balloon upper left beside his mouth; Arthur's balloon right, clearly lower, beside his mouth.
2. Panel two, about 25%: through the farm gate strides Ector, waving a letter with a red seal, mouth open, his balloon beside him.
3. Panel three, about 25%: Kay on the LEFT punches the air; Arthur in the middle-right jumps up, helmet forgotten in his hands, turned toward Ector; Ector at the right edge, facing them. Kay's balloon upper left; Arthur's balloon in the middle, lower; Ector's balloon lowest, at the right, beside Ector's mouth.

### Exact strings
KAY: `Polish it properly, squire. A knight's helmet should shine so bright it blinds his enemies.`
ARTHUR: `Your enemies won't need to see you, Kay. They'll hear you coming. You haven't stopped talking since they knighted you.`
ECTOR: `Kay! Arthur! A rider from London. There's to be a great tournament at New Year, and every knight in Britain is called.`
KAY: `My first tournament!`
ARTHUR: `Can I come? As Kay's squire? Please, Father.`
ECTOR: `We'll all go. Somebody has to carry your brother's sword.`

Render exactly these 6 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page. The letter carries no readable words.

Arthur and Kay are built nothing alike: blond round-faced boy against black-haired sharp-faced young man.

## PAGE 7 — Who built the walls

### Reader turn
The chronicle steps out of the story to show that Rome built the walls and left, and that the lost boy is legend.

### Ordered moments
1. The whole canvas is one chronicle page on warm ivory vellum with a ruled brown-ink margin, iron-gall brown ink and transparent watercolour wash; no oil, no story scene.
2. Dominant image, about 70% of the page, upper right: a hand-drawn ink-and-watercolour map of the island of Britain about the year 500, sea in pale blue wash. Roman roads as straight thin red lines meeting at London; Hadrian's Wall drawn across the north; the western hills shaded green; three or four small oared ships with rows of oars crossing from the east. Each label sits on its point: along the red lines, across the north at the wall, at the meeting of the roads, beside the ships.
3. Chronicle field 1 to the left of the map on plain vellum. It begins with a large illuminated initial W in vermilion red and lapis blue with a touch of raised gold leaf, which is the first letter of the field's first word; the field continues straight on from it, no letter doubled.
4. Chronicle field 2 below the map on plain vellum, full width inside the margin.

### Exact strings
CHRONICLE FIELD 1: `Who built those old walls, and why does Britain have no king? Here is something the story doesn't tell you. For almost four hundred years, Britain was part of the Roman Empire. The Romans built its roads, its towns, and London's walls. Then, around the year 410, Rome was in trouble at home. The soldiers left Britain, and they never came back.`
CHRONICLE FIELD 2: `Without Rome, Britain broke into small kingdoms, each fighting the others. Across the sea came new people, the Angles, Saxons, and Jutes, first to raid and then to stay. Hardly anyone wrote down what happened next. Into that real darkness, storytellers later put a king called Uther, a wizard called Merlin, and a lost boy. That part is legend.`
LABEL: `Roman roads`
LABEL: `Hadrian's Wall`
LABEL: `London`
LABEL: `Saxon ships`

Render exactly these 6 strings once each, in this order, with clear ownership; no other text anywhere on the page. The labels are small hand-lettered ink labels on the map.

### Character locks
No story character appears. No people at all; the ships have no visible figures.

### Consequential exclusions
No Merlin, Arthur, Uther, or any person. No balloons, no captions, no oil paint. No other place names, dates, compass rose lettering, or pseudo-writing. No date on the page. The initial is the W of "Who", never an extra letter.
