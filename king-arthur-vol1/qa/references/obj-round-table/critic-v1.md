## Mandatory findings

1. The plate does not establish the Round Table's decisive continuity state: in the overhead study, every clearly paired chair position appears to have a glowing gold dash before it. Any possible omission is too ambiguous to survive thumbnail or grayscale reduction, while the floor-level study distinguishes a chair only by moonlight, not by a visibly bare place on the rim. Remove the gold dash at exactly one chair station; leave a clean bare pale-oak interval at least as wide as the neighboring gold dashes; and align that same station with the single shaft-lit chair in the floor-level study. Keep all other stations marked so the one absent mark reads immediately after a long absence.

2. The enormous central tree roundel, repeated tree devices on hangings and rugs, and additional animal hangings create an unlisted heraldic system that is more memorable at thumbnail than the required one-blank-place marker and is likely to propagate through every Round Table scene. Replace the central roundel with uninterrupted pale-oak boards and make the hangings and rugs plain, muted earth-colour fields without devices; retain the wide plain rim, the short gold dashes, and the one bare station as the table's only recurring graphic markers.

## Nonblocking observations

- The two required object views are present: a high overhead study and a floor-level night study.
- The plate has no figures, lettering, labels, watermark, legible names, pseudo-writing, or explicit dividing rule.
- The pale oak, warm great-hall firelight, cool moonlit second view, tall chair ring, and roughly twenty-four-seat scale fit the Camelot location. The gold dashes carry the specified accent once the unique bare station is made structural and unmistakable.
- Face-crop, profile, reaction-expression, and costume-removal tests are not applicable to an object plate. In silhouette and grayscale the round table remains legible, but the missing-name state currently does not.
