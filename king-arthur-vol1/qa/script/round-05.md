# Script gate, round 5: King Arthur, Volume I

## Cold read

**What happens.** On a stormy night an old man, Merlin, carries a newborn baby away from a castle. Two winters later King Uther is dying. He whispers "My Arthur" to Merlin and tells his lords only that he has a son. The lords fight over the crown for thirteen years. Meanwhile a boy grows up on Sir Ector's muddy farm, polishing his brother Kay's helmet. They all ride to a tournament in London, where a sword stands in a stone that King Lot cannot move. Kay forgets his sword. Arthur can't get into the locked inn, so he pulls the stone sword out to help his brother. Kay claims it for himself for about a minute, then tells the truth. Ector kneels and says "I'm not your father." Arthur asks if he can still call him Father: "When nobody's listening, lad. Always." Then the brothers make peace.

The lords laugh at him and the people of London shout for him. He is crowned. Merlin promises to tell him his father's name in private, then shouts it at Lot in front of the hall. Arthur goes to war, charges alone, and Kay saves him. The ordinary people's spear line holds and the sword blazes. Arthur wins, and it doesn't feel like winning. Then come the songs, which leave Kay out, and Arthur starts to believe them. He sneaks out to fight Pellinore, the stone sword breaks, Merlin saves him, and Arthur admits it was his own fault ("Me."). At the lake he gets Excalibur and chooses the scabbard over the sword. He refuses a fight he can't lose, hands Kay the broken sword and says "You were right." Finally he builds a round table, Lot walks out, the knights swear, and one chair is kept for a knight who has not been born yet.

**Where I stopped:**
- **p53, panel 2.** The prose says "the eve of the next Pentecost", but the picture is snow, a snowy evening and a surcoat white with snow. p51 called Pentecost "the spring feast". I stopped to work out what season it was.
- **p55, panel 1 → p57.** Arthur offers Lot "an empty chair". Two pages later Kay points at "that seat" with no name on it and Merlin says it is kept. I stopped to ask whether this is the chair Arthur offered Lot, or whether the table now has two empty chairs.
- **p57.** Merlin answers Kay's "Somebody should." with "And there won't be." I had to look back two sentences to find what "there won't be" refers to.
- **p55, panel 2.** Kay and Pellinore both speak in a panel that shows only Lot.
- **p49.** If Kay knew where Arthur had gone, why did he wait at the gate all day? This was only a flicker.

Everything else read straight through, without rereading.

## Is it alive?

Yes. This is a good book, and Kay is its heart.

- **Movement I (pp 1–7).** Clean and quick. Ector's evasion at the forge ("whatever you were born to be… It's the only one I've got. Go to bed.") is real subtext. Ector wants Arthur to stop asking, Arthur wants the truth, and nobody wins. The page-turn reasons are all genuine.
- **Movement II (pp 8–19).** The best run in the book. Kay forgetting the sword because it "spoiled the look of your cloak" is character, not plot. Arthur's "Nobody was using it" is funny and innocent. Kay's lie, his "Arthur gave it to me" after a long pause, and "Don't make a song out of it" all carry heat: a brother deciding whether to be jealous or loyal. Every voice survives the attribution-strip test. I felt p15–16.
- **Movement III (pp 20–27).**
  - "It wasn't a pigsty! It was a good farm!" is exactly the appetite the mandate asks for: a boy who answers back before he thinks.
  - Merlin breaking his promise and then defending it ("I'd do it again"; "He named you") has heat. Arthur wants an apology and gets a reason, then a gift.
  - Kay's "It's true. I've seen him fight. It's terrible." is subtext doing the work of love.
- **Movement IV (pp 28–36).**
  - The charge, the rescue and "You're very bad at this" work.
  - The aftermath page (p34) earns its quiet.
  - "Nobody in the songs was called Kay" is the best sentence in the script. It sets up the next movement through the reader's own indignation.
- **Movement V (pp 37–43).** "Kings in the songs don't have stewards" is a perfect Kay line. Merlin's catechism ending in "Me." is enacted, not preached. The prose on p43 ("He had nearly died for a song") lands.
- **Movement VI (pp 44–50).**
  - The lake is wonder, and Merlin's alarm at "whatever you ask" gives it a cost.
  - p48 is the moral turn, done as a choice in the character's own voice ("That isn't a fight. That's cheating."). No moral is stated.
  - p49 is the emotional payoff of the book: "Will you tell me no again, next time?" / "Every day."
- **Movement VII (pp 51–59).** The coolest movement, and the one where my two blockers sit.
  - p51–54 are pageant: necessary, and well staged.
  - The heat comes back with Lot on p55 and with "I'll swear first, because I need it most" on p56, which is Arthur's arc in one line.
  - p57 gives Kay a want, but he never answers Merlin's warning, so the warning lands on nobody's face.
  - p58's "Goodnight, Father" in front of Merlin quietly pays off "when nobody's listening".

The ending is earned. My blockers are about clarity, not life.

## Mandatory findings

**1. p53 (panel 2 and PROSE FIELD 2): the picture contradicts the season the page is there to establish.**
- **Failure.** The prose says "On the eve of the next Pentecost, someone knocked at the hall door." The art direction is "The hall doors open on a snowy evening… his old green surcoat white with snow." p51 told the reader Pentecost is "the spring feast."
- **What the reader has to repair.** This page's whole job is elapsed time ("most of a year"). The image says winter and the words say the next spring. The reader must either decide the picture is wrong, or read it as winter and conclude the year has not turned. That makes p54's seating feel like it happens in the wrong season. It is the same kind of picture/prose contradiction that round 4 fixed on this page ("half-built"). It appeared when "the eve of the next Pentecost" was added over a staging written for winter.
- **Revision.** Restage panel 2 as a late-spring evening:
  - green branches or blossom outside the doors
  - Pellinore's surcoat dusty and travel-stained from the road, not white with snow
  - the finished table bare and waiting

  Alternatively, keep the snow and have the prose say he arrived in the winter and waited for Pentecost. Spring is the cleaner fix. Do not cut the "eve of the next Pentecost" anchor.

**2. p55 → p57: the empty chair is ambiguous, and it is the volume's closing hook.**
- **Failure.** p54 sets up "One chair, empty, has no glow in front of it." On p55, panel 1, Arthur "gestures to an empty chair beside the others" for Lot. On p57 Kay points at "that seat. There's no name on it," and Merlin says it is kept for someone who hasn't come.
  - Names only appear when a knight sits, so every empty chair has no name. Once Lot walks out, the table holds either two nameless empty chairs, or one chair that Arthur just offered Lot.
  - If it is the same chair, Merlin stood silent while Arthur offered Lot a seat that makes its sitter "very sorry."
  - If it is a different chair, Kay's "that seat" has two possible referents (§7 rule 4).
- **What the reader has to repair.** Which chair is the kept one, and whether Arthur nearly put Lot in it. This hook carries p56's page-turn, p57 and p58's final caption, so the reader should not have to work it out.
- **Revision.**
  - On p55, have Arthur offer Lot **his own place**, not an empty chair: "Take my chair, King Lot, if you like. It's no higher than anyone's." This sharpens the round-table idea and leaves exactly one empty chair in the book.
  - Then fix p57's antecedent. Merlin's first line should name the thing: "There's no name on it, Kay, and there won't be. Not yet. It is kept for someone who hasn't come."
  - Give Kay a reaction to the warning in panel 3. He sits back down with his hands raised: "I'll keep my own, then." The warning that round 5 aimed at a person then visibly reaches him.

**3. p55, panel 2: two of the panel's three speakers are not staged.**
- **Failure.** The staging reads "Lot, sneering, looking round the table," and that panel then holds three balloons: LOT, KAY and PELLINORE. Kay and Pellinore are not placed anywhere in the panel. The page has four speakers and about 97 words.
- **What the reader has to repair.** Balloons from people who are not visible, or tails into a crowd. "This steward chased you off a battlefield" and "The same as me" only work if the reader sees who says them, and to whom.
- **Revision.** Write the staging for the panel: Lot in the foreground left, his back half to us. Across the table on the right, seated, Kay leaning forward with his chin up and Pellinore huge and still. Put opposed speakers on opposite sides. If the three lines will not sit in one panel, split it: Lot's sneer in panel 2, then Kay's and Pellinore's answers in a new panel 3 that shows the two of them seated together, with Lot's exit as panel 4. Keep panel 1 dominant. Do not cut any of the lines.

## Nonblocking observations

- **p14.** It is labelled spectacle, but its turn is carried by a spoken line ("I'm not your father."). The builder must protect the three balloons, which hold about 23 words. Dramatic would be the more honest label, and the mode balance holds either way (it would be 36/9/8).
- **p25, panel 1.** "At your own table, I wouldn't let Lot take one from you." Read aloud, "one" needs a beat to resolve to "a father". Consider "I wouldn't let Lot take your father from you."
- **p38 / p49.** Arthur leads his horse out "past the dozing guard", but on p49 "The guard says you rode out alone at midnight." Also, Kay knew where Arthur had gone and still waited at the gate all day, which is out of character for the brother who rode into a battle alone. One clause would close both gaps: "The guard woke up in time to see your horse's tail. Merlin went after you and told me to wait. I hate waiting."
- **p45, panel 3, and p58, panel 1.** Each has three speakers in one panel. These are already carried to packets. On p58 Ector speaks from the far doorway, so his balloon needs its own lane, away from the Merlin/Arthur exchange.
- **p52, panel 1.** Kay speaks but is not placed. Arthur is on the left and Merlin on the right, so put Kay between them or behind Arthur.
- **p50, the Question.** The story has already shown two swords plainly. The question this reader is more likely to have is "Wasn't the sword in the stone Excalibur?", and the page answers it well. Consider rewording the Question to match.
- **p59.** The audit warns that the book ends on a chronicle page. I confirm the story's last turn lands on p58 (the empty chair in the moonlight, Merlin's warning, the Lady's gift still owed). The closing field, "They had a great many more knights to add," works as a coda.
- **Chronicle checks.** All six pages pass these checks:
  - each sits at a movement break
  - each has one dominant image
  - each is within the word budget (about 105–145 words)
  - legend and history are labelled
  - the facts are sound
  - the story still passes the paraphrase test with every chronicle page removed

  The Winchester "made around 1290" is the conventional reading (the tree-ring dating of the timber is about 1250–1280). It is acceptable.
- **Mode balance.** 35 dramatic / 9 prose / 9 spectacle over 53 story pages, which is 66/17/17. Healthy.
- **Five-word audit.** All 24 short balloons pass. Each is a complete thought or a direct answer to a complete question in the same panel, with one visible referent. "Me." on p42 and "Every day." on p49 are forceful, not compressed.
