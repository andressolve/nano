# Whole-book release gate: King Arthur, Volume I, *The Sword and the Table* (pages 1–59)

I read all 59 proofs at 600 × 900 in order, once, with all story material closed. I wrote the account below before opening `08-FULL-SCRIPT.md` and `07-PAGE-CONTRACT.md`.

## 1. Blind account

### Retell
On a stormy night an old man named Merlin carries a newborn baby down a cliff path away from a castle (p1). Two winters later King Uther is dying. His lords want to know who will be king. Uther whispers to Merlin, "My Arthur?", and Merlin says the boy is safe and hidden because the lords would kill him. In public Uther says only "I have a son," then dies, and no lord has ever heard of a son (p2). For thirteen years the lords fight over the crown. Far away, on old Sir Ector's farm, a boy grows up (p3).

The boy is Arthur, squire to Ector's newly knighted son Kay, who is vain and talks constantly. A letter calls every knight to a New Year tournament in London, and the whole family goes (p4). That night Arthur asks Ector whether he will ever be a real knight. Ector says "you'll be whatever you were born to be," which Arthur notices is not an answer, and Ector sits alone afterwards, troubled (p5). They ride into London, a huge, crumbling, walled city full of lords who each want to be king (p6). A chronicle page explains the Roman walls, Rome leaving Britain, the Saxons coming, and which parts are legend (p7).

On Christmas morning a stone has appeared in the churchyard with a sword through it and the inscription "WHOEVER PULLS THIS SWORD OUT OF THIS STONE IS THE TRUE-BORN KING OF ALL BRITAIN." King Lot of Orkney, a big red-bearded man, fails to pull it and laughs it off as Merlin's trick. Merlin, in the church doorway, says the sword is waiting for someone else (p8). At the tournament Kay has left his sword at the inn and sends Arthur running for it (p9). The inn is locked. Arthur remembers a sword in the churchyard "that nobody was using" (p10). He pulls it out easily, and nobody sees (p11). He hands it to Kay, and Kay shouts to Ector that he, Kay, is king (p12). Ector makes Kay swear on a book, and Kay admits Arthur gave it to him. Ector has Arthur put it back and draw it again (p13). Ector and Kay kneel, and Ector says "I'm not your father" (p14). Ector explains that Merlin brought him a baby fifteen years ago. Arthur, crying, says that this morning he had a father and a brother and now he has only a sword (p15). Ector says Arthur can still call him Father "when nobody's listening," and asks him to make Kay his steward (p16). Kay admits that for about a minute he wanted the sword to be his. Arthur forgives him and swears him in as steward for life (p17). That night Arthur sits alone in the church. All London is whispering his name, and he does not expect the lords to kneel (p18). A chronicle page explains that the sword in the stone first appears around 1200 with Robert de Boron (p19).

In the morning Lot mocks him in front of the lords: "a squire who grew up in a pigsty… the king of the hens" (p20). The people of London pour into the churchyard shouting "WE WILL HAVE ARTHUR!" (p21). Arthur is crowned and takes the king's oath. He asks Merlin who his father was, and Merlin promises to tell him that night (p22). At the coronation feast Lot returns Arthur's gifts and threatens his neck (p23). Merlin announces to the whole hall that Arthur is Uther's son. Lot laughs and promises battle (p24). Alone with Merlin, Arthur is furious: Merlin promised to tell him first, and Merlin took him from his father. Merlin says he would do it again, and that Uther saw him once and named him (p25). Arthur asks about his mother, and Merlin says that is "another night's story." Arthur fears he cannot fight. Kay jokes that he will be beside him. Merlin points out that the people never asked how many fights he had won, and Arthur decides: "Then we fight him. All of us, together" (p26). A chronicle page covers Gildas, Badon and the History of the Britons, and whether Arthur was real (p27).

The night before the battle Arthur's small army of townspeople faces Lot's many campfires, and Arthur puts on his own mail (p28). At dawn at Bedegraine (p29) he charges alone against Kay's shouted warning, his horse goes down in the stream, and he is surrounded (p30). Kay rides in, "that's my brother!", hauls him back to the line, and tells him "You're very bad at this" (p31). The spear line holds against Lot's charge, the sword blazes "like thirty torches," and Lot flees vowing "This isn't over" (p32). At noon the field is grey and wrecked (p33). Arthur, tending a wounded smith, asks Merlin why winning feels like this. Merlin says it is because he is looking at what it cost. Arthur admits he would be dead without Kay, and Merlin says "Remember that" (p34). Arthur makes his court at Camelot. Songs show him in shining armour charging alone, with no Kay in them, and he begins to believe them (p35). A chronicle page shows what warriors looked like around 500, around 1200 and in the 1400s (p36).

Kay reports a knight at a forest fountain who knocks knights into the water. Arthur wants to go himself. Kay says no: "Kings send knights" (p37). Arthur sneaks out alone at night, happy and making up his own song (p38). The knight is King Pellinore, enormous, with a white boar on his surcoat, who has "never yielded to any man" (p39). They joust, then fight on foot, and the sword from the stone snaps (p40). Arthur refuses to yield, and Pellinore raises his sword to strike. A ringed hand says "Sleep, Pellinore," and Pellinore falls (p41). Arthur accuses Merlin of killing him. Merlin says Pellinore is snoring, and makes Arthur answer three questions until Arthur says "Me" (p42). At dawn they walk out of the forest, and Merlin says there is another sword (p43). An arm in white silk rises from a lake holding a sheathed sword (p44). The Lady of the Lake offers Excalibur in return for a future gift, whatever she asks. Arthur promises at once, which alarms Merlin (p45). Arthur takes the sword and its scabbard from the hand (p46). Merlin asks which he prefers. Arthur says the sword, and Merlin calls him a fool: whoever wears the scabbard loses no blood. Arthur buckles on the scabbard first (p47). Back at the fountain Arthur declines to fight Pellinore because he cannot bleed, says that would be cheating, and invites him to Camelot. Pellinore says "I'll think about it" (p48). At Camelot's gate Arthur hands Kay the broken sword and says "You told me no… You were right." Asked whether he will say no again, Kay answers "Every day" (p49). A chronicle page explains the two swords and Excalibur's names (p50).

At Pentecost the knights brawl over seats, and Arthur bangs Excalibur's pommel on the table: "Nobody bleeds in my hall over a chair!" (p51). He decides on a table with no head. Kay calls it a cartwheel, and Merlin says it has never been tried (p52). The table takes most of a year to build, and Pellinore arrives (p53). The knights sit, their names appear in gold, and Pellinore sits first (p54). Lot comes under a white flag. Arthur offers him his own chair. Lot sneers at sitting level with a steward and "an old boar," Kay and Pellinore answer him, and Lot leaves: "When you need a real king, send to Orkney" (p55). Arthur kneels and swears first, "because I need it most," and the knights answer "WE SWEAR!" (p56). Kay offers to sit in the one empty, nameless chair. Merlin says it is kept for someone who has not yet come, and anyone else who sits in it will be sorry. Kay keeps his own seat (p57). Arthur says "Goodnight, Father" to Ector. Merlin reminds Arthur that he still owes the Lady a gift and that Merlin will not always be beside him; Kay will tell him when he is being a fool. The empty seat waits for a knight "not even born" (p58). A final chronicle page shows the legend growing like a snowball and the Winchester table: "The storytellers weren't finished" (p59).

### Cast as I identified them, all confirmed by the script
- **Arthur:** blond and freckled. Brown patched tunic, then blue surcoat over mail.
- **Merlin:** wild white hair, forked beard, crooked staff, green-stone ring.
- **Sir Ector:** bald, round, russet tunic.
- **Sir Kay:** black hair, moustache, black-and-yellow checked surcoat, later steward's black with yellow trim and keys.
- **King Lot of Orkney:** copper beard in iron-ringed braids, bearskin cloak, torc.
- **King Uther.**
- **King Pellinore:** huge, grey, green surcoat with a white boar.
- **The Lady of the Lake:** long black hair, white gown, silver circlet.

I misidentified no one and assigned no line to the wrong speaker. On p41 Merlin appears only as a hand, ring, beard tip and staff. I knew it was him before p42 confirmed it.

### Causal chain
1. Uther's heir is hidden because the lords would kill him, so thirteen years of civil war follow.
2. The tournament brings the family to London.
3. Kay forgets his sword and the inn is locked, so Arthur innocently takes the sword from the stone.
4. Kay's brief lie and Ector's oath test confirm that Arthur drew it.
5. Ector's revelation costs Arthur his family; he partly regains it through "Father, when nobody's listening" and the steward oath.
6. The lords refuse him, the people choose him, and he is crowned.
7. Lot's threat makes Merlin reveal Uther publicly, which costs Arthur the private telling he was promised.
8. War: the people's line holds and Kay saves the reckless charge, but the lesson ("I charged alone") gets lost in the songs.
9. That pride leads to the fountain, where Arthur defies Kay and breaks the sword from the stone.
10. Merlin's three questions turn Arthur to humility, which leads to the Lake: Excalibur at the price of an open-ended promise, and the scabbard over the sword.
11. Arthur declines an unfair fight and recruits his enemy, and makes peace with Kay.
12. The seating brawl leads to the Round Table. Lot refuses it, the oath is sworn, and the empty seat is left waiting.

### Ending, its cost, and why the final image matters
Arthur ends a king who has chosen equality over height. On the way he lost the life he knew and the sword that made him king. He still owes the Lady an unnamed gift, and Merlin is warning that he will leave. The last story image, the empty chair in moonlight beside Excalibur on the round table, makes the open promise and the waiting knight into a visible question. The final chronicle turns the book outward: the legend is still being written.

### Reading-pace record
The book read at even pace with no stalls on the story. I slowed in these places:

- **p15:** Kay, seen from behind, wears a black-lion surcoat instead of his checks.
- **p26:** five balloons in panel 1. The order resolved correctly top to bottom.
- **p41:** the hand alone; it resolved on the turn.
- **p54:** I could not pick out the single empty chair from above. p57 supplied it.

## 2. Script and contract check

**Text and attribution.** Every page's rendered text matches the script verbatim, with every line on its scripted speaker. The exceptions are two punctuation errors (p2 and p54) listed in the typo section below. No line is missing, reordered or misattributed. The stage directions in `07-PAGE-CONTRACT.md` are honoured or harmlessly varied:
- p2: the closing caption comes after Uther's public line.
- p12: Kay's shout is aimed at Ector.
- p26: Kay is low on his own side.
- p45: three speakers, with Merlin behind Arthur.
- p58: Ector has his own goodnight panel.
- p55 renders Lot on the left and Arthur on the right, the reverse of the script. The balloon tails make the speakers clear.

**Modes.** Every declared mode rendered as declared.
- **Spectacle (9):** pages 1, 11, 14, 21, 29, 33, 44, 46, 54.
- **Illustrated prose (9):** pages 3, 6, 10, 18, 28, 35, 38, 43, 53.
- **Chronicle (6):** pages 7, 19, 27, 36, 50, 59.
- **Dramatic (35):** all remaining pages.

The book has exactly one mode per page and at least one illustrated-prose page. Its rhythm is good: no run of dramatic pages goes longer than about five pages without a prose, spectacle or chronicle beat.

**Chronicle pages.** All six read as Mode 4. Each is a vellum page at a chapter break, opens with its own question, and has an illuminated initial matching the first letter of that question (W, D, W, W, T, W). Each separates history from legend and hands back to the story at its contracted turn. None of them gets in the way of the story: each sits where the story has just raised its question (the walls, the stone, a real Arthur, the armour in the songs, a second sword, the table).

## 3. Acceptance questions by movement

**I. The Hidden King (pp 1–7).** Merlin acts to keep Uther's heir alive by hiding him. The lords are left to war, and Ector raises the boy. Arthur believes he is a squire with an ordinary father who will not quite answer him. The tournament moves the story to London. All of this is answerable from the pages.

**II. The Sword in the Stone (pp 8–19).** Arthur acts out of loyalty ("Kay needs a sword"), not ambition. Kay is briefly tempted and confesses under oath. Ector tells the truth at the cost of being his father. Arthur now believes he is "not anybody's son," then that Ector is still Father in private and Kay is still his brother. The lords must still be faced, which leads to the morning. Answerable from the pages.

**III. The Kings Who Laughed (pp 20–27).** Lot wants the crown and refuses a nobody. The people choose Arthur. Merlin breaks his promise to stop Lot's "nobody's son" speech. Arthur chooses war for the people who chose him. He now believes he is Uther's son, named by him, and responsible to the people. Lot's threat drives the move to the battlefield. Answerable from the pages.

**IV. The First Battle (pp 28–36).** Arthur charges alone out of pride and courage, and Kay saves him. The people's line wins. Arthur learns the cost and his own recklessness, then forgets the lesson inside the songs. His pride makes the fountain story inevitable. Answerable from the pages.

**V. The Knight at the Fountain (pp 37–43).** Arthur defies Kay to live up to the songs and loses the sword from the stone. Merlin saves him and makes him name his own fault. He now believes it was "Me." Merlin's "another sword" pulls the story to the lake. Answerable from the pages.

**VI. The Lady of the Lake (pp 44–50).** The Lady wants a future gift, and Arthur promises it rashly. He chooses the scabbard, refuses an unfair fight, recruits Pellinore and admits Kay was right. He now believes in restraint and in counsel. The new sword and the unruly court lead to the hall. Answerable from the pages.

**VII. The Round Table (pp 51–59).** The knights want rank. Arthur chooses a table with no head and offers Lot his own chair. Lot refuses, and the others swear. Arthur believes the king needs the oath most. The open threads are the empty seat, the Lady's gift and Merlin's coming absence, plus the history coda. Answerable from the pages.

No movement depends on information outside the pages.

## 5. Nonblocking observations
These are not material reader harm and do not justify replacing a promoted page.

- **The stone and anvil change between pages (pp 8, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 20, 21).** The script puts an anvil on the stone throughout. p8 and p13 have no anvil, and p11, p14, p15, p17 and p20 do. p21's stone also shows no anvil. On p11 the gold inscription has become a gold knot emblem with no letters, although p10's prose mentions "the gold letters." The church, the churchyard and the action keep the stone's identity clear, and no reader would think it is a different stone. Not worth redrawing a page for.
- **Kay's surcoat on p15, panel 3.** It shows a black rampant beast on yellow instead of his checks. The black-and-yellow colours and the staging carry his identity.
- **No sword glow on p21.** The script wants the sword to flare as the crowd roars, as a setup for p32. The flare is not visible. p32's caption carries the blaze on its own.
- **Arthur's horse on p48.** It is a brown horse with a white blaze. He rides a grey on pp 29–30, 38–40, 43 and 49.
- **The empty chair on p54 is not legible from the overhead view, and the names show as gold bars rather than letters.** p57 states and shows the empty chair outright, so nothing is lost.
- **Camelot's hall banners on pp 54 and 56 show Pellinore's white boar.** It reads as heraldic decoration and causes no confusion.
- **Merlin is absent from p56 and the open doors behind the oath are not shown.** This is a staging variance with no harm.

## 6. Typos, wrong letters and small object defects (nonblocking, all candidates for the edit pass)

- **p2, panel 2, Merlin:** renders "Safe, and far from here, If your lords knew where…". The script has a period after "here", so the comma is wrong and "If" is wrongly capitalised.
- **p54, caption:** opens with a stray period, ".As each knight sat down…", where the script has "As each knight sat down…".
- **p11, stone:** a gold knot emblem where the script calls for gold letters, and an anvil that p8 lacks.
- **p13, stone:** no anvil, only a slot in the top of the stone, where the script calls for "an empty slot in the anvil."
- **p15, panel 3, Kay's surcoat:** a black lion on yellow instead of his black-and-yellow checks.
- **p48, Arthur's horse:** brown with a white blaze instead of grey.
- **p54, the table:** the names render as gold bars, not letters, and the empty chair cannot be picked out.
