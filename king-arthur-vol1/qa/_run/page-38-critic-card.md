## Page intent

## PAGE 38

Illustrated prose. Night in the great oak forest. Arthur has slipped out of the sleeping castle alone, and he rides his grey horse down a moonlit road in mail and his blue surcoat, his helmet hanging from the saddle, grinning up at the moon. The prose lets the reader feel his happiness and hear the pride under it: no steward, no wizard, only the moon and a sword that beat an army, and a song about tonight already made up in his head. Below, far ahead through the trees, firelight glows beside a glint of water. The one dominant turn: he's reached the fountain, and someone is waiting there. Arthur's joy owns the page; the fire stays small and distant, a promise rather than a person.

### C1 — Transcription

Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant (armour, armor), and line breaks inside a paragraph are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. ### C2 — Joy, alone, toward a fire

The page reads as one boy riding happily alone through a moonlit forest, with a small fire by water far ahead. Blocking if a second person rides with him, if a figure stands at the fire, or if his face reads afraid or grim.

### C3 — Arthur

Arthur is recognisable: fair-haired, beardless boy in mail and deep blue on a grey horse. Blocking if he reads as a grown man or wears anything but blue.

### C4 — Prose over plain ground

The prose sits over sky or dark trees, never over Arthur's face or the fire. Blocking only if it hides the face or the fire.
