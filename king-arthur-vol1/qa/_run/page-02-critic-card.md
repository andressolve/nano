## Page intent

## PAGE 2

Dramatic. Two winters later, King Uther lies dying in a dark stone chamber under a red-dragon coverlet, his lords crowding in the shadows at the foot of the bed. Merlin, standing over him, tells him to name an heir. Then the private moment: Uther pulls Merlin close and whispers about his son, Arthur, and Merlin answers that the boy is safe and hidden because the lords would kill him. Last, Uther lifts his head and tells the lords only that he has a son, and the closing caption says he died and none of them had ever heard of one. The dominant turn: the king has a son named Arthur, and only the wizard knows it. The whisper is the secret; the lords stay hungry shapes in the dark. The reader turns to find the boy.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 2 · *dramatic*

**Location:** King Uther's chamber, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A dark stone chamber lit by one brazier. A great
bed with a red-and-gold coverlet embroidered with a red dragon. In it, KING
UTHER: gaunt, hollow-cheeked, long thin grey hair on the pillow, a long grey
moustache and no beard. Crowded at the foot of the bed in shadow, a dozen
lords in furs and mail, leaning in, hungry and anxious. At the bedside,
standing over the king, MERLIN, hood back now: tall, very thin, a long
hooked nose, wild white hair, a long forked white beard, leaning on his
staff.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the dark wall)*
> "Two winters later, King Uther lay dying."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Your lords are frightened, Uther. They want to know who will be king when you are gone. Tell them."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close on the bed. Uther grips Merlin's sleeve and pulls
him down; Merlin bends so close that his beard touches the pillow. The lords
behind are out of focus, out of earshot. Uther on the left, Merlin on the
right.

> **UTHER:** "Is he safe? My son? My Arthur?"
>
> **MERLIN:** "Safe, and far from here. If your lords knew where, he wouldn't live a week."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Uther, with the last of his strength, lifts his head
toward the lords.

> **UTHER:** "I have a son. He will be king after me. He has my blessing."
>
> **CAPTION** *(across the bottom of the panel)*
> "King Uther died that night. Not one of his lords had ever heard of a son."

**Why you turn the page:** the king has a son named Arthur, and only the wizard knows it.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 2 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All six strings, two captions and four spoken lines, must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match. A line placed directly beside its speaker is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The secret does not land
The reader must see that the name passes in a whisper between Uther and Merlin alone, and that Uther then tells the lords only that he has a son. If the whisper reads as said to the lords, or the closing caption reads before Uther's public line, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution in the whisper
"Is he safe? My son? My Arthur?" hangs from Uther's mouth; "Safe, and far from here…" from Merlin's, clearly after. Merlin's opening line is his; "I have a son…" is Uther's. A swapped or shared tail is blocking.

### C4 — Identity
Uther: gaunt, grey moustache with no beard, in the bed under the red dragon. Merlin: very tall, thin, forked white beard, wild white hair, hooked nose. A bearded Uther, or a Merlin with a short or square beard, is blocking; repeated faces among the shadowed lords are nonblocking.

### C5 — Death drawn
Uther must be alive in every panel; no deathbed corpse image. Blocking if shown dead.
