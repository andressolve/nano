## Page intent

## PAGE 4

Dramatic. In Sir Ector's muddy farmyard, Arthur, a fair-haired farm boy in a patched brown tunic, polishes the shining new helmet of his brother Kay, newly knighted and showing off in black-and-yellow. Kay orders him about; Arthur teases back that Kay hasn't stopped talking since they knighted him. Then Ector strides through the gate waving a letter: a great tournament in London at New Year. Kay cheers; Arthur begs to come as Kay's squire; Ector says they'll all go, someone has to carry Kay's sword. The dominant turn: the farm boy is going to London. The brothers' teasing is warm, not cruel, and owns the top of the page; the letter is the hinge. The reader turns because the journey has begun.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 4 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Sir Ector's farmyard, autumn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** A muddy farmyard with a forge glowing in an
open shed, hens underfoot. ARTHUR, fifteen, slight and long-limbed, thick
straw-blond hair falling in his eyes, a patched brown wool tunic, sits on an
upturned bucket polishing a shining new helmet. Over him, posing with one
foot on a mounting block, a new sword on his hip, SIR KAY, nineteen: tall,
lean, sharp-faced, short black hair, a proud first moustache, brand-new mail
and a black-and-yellow surcoat. Kay is on the left, Arthur on the right.

> **KAY:** "Polish it properly, squire. A knight's helmet should shine so bright it blinds his enemies."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Your enemies won't need to see you, Kay. They'll hear you coming. You haven't stopped talking since they knighted you."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Through the gate strides SIR ECTOR: short, broad,
barrel-bodied, bald on top with a grey fringe, round ruddy clean-shaven face,
a russet tunic, waving a letter with a red seal.

> **ECTOR:** "Kay! Arthur! A rider from London. There's to be a great tournament at New Year, and every knight in Britain is called."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Kay punches the air on the left. Arthur, on the right,
jumps up, the helmet forgotten in his hands, looking at Ector.

> **KAY:** "My first tournament!"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Can I come? As Kay's squire? Please, Father."
>
> **ECTOR:** "We'll all go. Somebody has to carry your brother's sword."

**Why you turn the page:** the farm boy is going to London.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 4 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
All six spoken lines must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match. A line naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The news does not land
The reader must see the brothers' banter in the yard, then Ector arriving with a letter, then the boy begging to go. If the letter's arrival is lost or the last panel does not read as Arthur pleading and Ector agreeing, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the last exchange
"My first tournament!" is Kay's; "Can I come? As Kay's squire? Please, Father." is Arthur's; "We'll all go…" is Ector's, read last. In the first panel the order teaser and the "squire" line are Kay's and the reply is Arthur's. A swapped owner is blocking.

### C4 — Brothers confused
Arthur is a slight, fair-haired, round-faced beardless boy in a patched brown tunic; Kay is a lean, sharp-faced, black-haired young man with a thin moustache in black-and-yellow over mail. If the two can be mistaken, or Arthur wears deep blue, it is blocking.

### C5 — Ector is not Ector
Short, broad, bald-topped with a grey fringe, round clean-shaven ruddy face, russet tunic. A beard or a tall thin build is blocking.
