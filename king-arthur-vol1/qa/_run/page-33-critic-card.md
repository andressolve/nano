## Page intent

## PAGE 33

Spectacle. At noon under a grey sky the mist is gone and the valley shows what the battle cost: riderless horses standing with their heads down, dropped shields, broken spears, torn banners in the stream, crows on the far hill, and townspeople helping the wounded walk. In the middle, small, Arthur stands alone with his helmet in his hand and his blond hair blowing, looking at all of it. No bodies and no blood are shown; the cost is in the empty things and in the living. Arthur owns the page by being alone in the middle of it. The dominant turn: he has won, and it doesn't look like winning. The reader turns to find out what Arthur makes of it. The wreckage is quiet aftermath, not horror, and nothing in it competes with the lone figure.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 33 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text anywhere. A caption that reads is not to be measured. Quote style, a comma present or missing, and spacing are nonblocking; the words must match.

### C2 — Aftermath owns the page
The eye must find the valley after the battle: riderless horses, dropped shields, broken spears, torn banners in the stream, crows, the wounded helped along. If it reads as a victory celebration, or is too empty to show a cost, the turn fails. Spectacle mode. The exact count of any object is nonblocking.

### C3 — Arthur alone
Arthur, helmet in hand, blond hair blowing, must be findable alone in the middle, looking at it. If he is lost or surrounded, the page loses its point.

### C4 — No bodies, no blood
Any drawn body, blood, wound, or severed thing is blocking. The cost must be carried by empty things and the living.
