## Page intent

## PAGE 48

A dramatic page in the fountain clearing by daylight. Pellinore, awake and enormous, blocks the road with his sword planted point-down and offers to try the new sword. Arthur's hand goes to Excalibur's hilt, then deliberately leaves it and rests on the scabbard: with that on, he cannot bleed, so a fight would be cheating. Then he holds out his open hand and invites Pellinore to Camelot, because he needs knights strong enough to tell him no. Pellinore stares at the hand, suspicious, and says he'll think about it. Arthur owns the page; the dominant turn: Arthur turned down a fight he couldn't lose, and asked his enemy to join him. The reader turns to see whether Pellinore comes. What stays subordinate: Merlin, silent behind Arthur, and the pavilion.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 48 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the fountain in the forest, day.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The fountain clearing in daylight. Pellinore,
awake, huge, stands in the road with his sword planted point-down, blocking
the way. Arthur on his horse, left, Excalibur in its red scabbard at his
hip; Merlin behind him.

> **PELLINORE:** "You again. You've got a new sword, I see. Shall we try it?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Arthur's hand on Excalibur's hilt; then, deliberately,
letting it go and touching the scabbard instead.

> **ARTHUR:** "No. With this scabbard on, I can't bleed. That isn't a fight. That's cheating."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Arthur holding out his open hand to Pellinore. Pellinore
staring at it, suspicious.

> **ARTHUR:** "Come to Camelot instead, King Pellinore. I need knights who are stronger than me, and who aren't afraid to tell me no."
>
> **PELLINORE:** "I'll think about it."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur turned down a fight he couldn't lose, and asked his enemy to join him.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 48 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (it names its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The dominant turn
The confrontation in the clearing owns the page: Pellinore blocking the road, Arthur mounted facing him. Arthur's hand leaves the hilt for the scabbard, and he ends offering an open hand. If the refusal reads as drawing the sword, the turn reverses: blocking.

### C3 — Attribution
Pellinore's challenge and "I'll think about it." hang from Pellinore; the refusal and the invitation, which names King Pellinore, hang from Arthur. A swap is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and the risky pair
Pellinore: enormous, cropped iron-grey hair, short square grey beard, scarred horse-like face, faded green surcoat with a white boar. Merlin, silent behind Arthur: thin, stooped, long forked white beard. If Merlin and Pellinore read as one type of old man, or Merlin has a balloon, it is blocking.

### C5 — Object state
Excalibur stays sheathed in the red scabbard throughout; Pellinore's sword is planted point-down. Excalibur drawn is blocking.

### C6 — Focal integrity
Arthur's open hand and the hand on the scabbard are cleanly drawn; faces of both speakers intact. A broken focal hand or face is blocking.
