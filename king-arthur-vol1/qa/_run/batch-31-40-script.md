# Exact script — pages 31–40

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 31 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the battlefield, in the stream.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** Arthur on his feet in the shallow stream, the
sword up, a ring of enemy riders and spears closing on him. Bursting into
the ring from the left on his black horse, sword swinging, comes Kay, his
black-and-yellow surcoat flying.

> **KAY:** "Get away from him! I am Sir Kay, the king's steward, and that's my brother!"

**Panel 2 — 45%.** Kay, on horseback, hauls Arthur up behind his saddle and
wheels the horse back toward their own side, where the townspeople's line
of spears is coming down the slope. Kay in front, Arthur clinging on behind.

> **KAY:** "Back to the line, where you belong! You charged out alone!"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "And you came for me alone."
>
> **KAY:** "Somebody had to. You're very bad at this."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur is back with his own people, and King Lot is coming for him.

## PAGE 32 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the battlefield.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Arthur on foot in the front rank of his own army,
shoulder to shoulder with smiths and farmers, spears and pitchforks braced.
Charging down at them, Lot on a huge horse with his riders behind him, braids
flying, sword up. Arthur on the left, Lot on the right.

> **LOT:** "There you are, farm boy! Now we'll see whose crown it is!"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Hold together! Hold!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The charge hits the spear line and the line
does not break. In the middle of it, among his people, Arthur raises the
sword from the stone, and the blade blazes with white light, as bright as
thirty torches, lighting up the whole field. Lot's horse rears; Lot's riders
throw their arms up over their eyes.

> **CAPTION** *(top of the panel)*
> "The line held. And when Arthur raised the sword among his people, it blazed like thirty torches."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Lot's army turning and running up the far slope; Lot
looking back over his shoulder as he rides away.

> **LOT:** "This isn't over, boy! Do you hear me? This isn't over!"

**Why you turn the page:** Lot's army has broken, and Arthur has won.

## PAGE 33 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the valley of Bedegraine, after the battle.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The same valley at noon under a grey sky. The
mist is gone. All over the slope: riderless horses standing with their
heads down, dropped shields, broken spears, torn banners in the stream,
crows on the far hill. No bodies and no blood are drawn. Townspeople help
the wounded walk, arms over shoulders. In the middle, small, Arthur stands
alone with his helmet off in his hand, his blond hair blowing, looking at
all of it.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the grey sky)*
> "By noon it was over. Arthur had won his first battle."

**Why you turn the page:** he has won, and it doesn't look like winning.

## PAGE 34 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the battlefield, the wounded.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Arthur kneels in the grass beside a wounded
young man in a smith's leather apron, holding a water-skin to his mouth.
Merlin stands over them both, leaning on his staff. Arthur left, Merlin
right.

> **ARTHUR:** "We won, Merlin. Everyone keeps telling me we won."
>
> **MERLIN:** "You did."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Then why does it feel like this?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Merlin, looking out at the field.

> **MERLIN:** "Because you're looking at what it cost. Good."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Arthur, quietly, not looking up.

> **ARTHUR:** "If Kay hadn't come, I'd be dead. I charged alone."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Yes. Remember that. I may not always be there to remind you."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has learned the lesson, and Merlin is afraid he won't keep it.

## PAGE 35 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** Camelot, the hall, winter.

**Panel 1 — 40%.** Camelot: a castle of pale stone on a green hill above a
river, banners flying, knights riding up the road to its gate.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(over the pale sky above the castle)*
> "Arthur made his court at Camelot, a castle on a green hill above a river, and knights came from all over Britain to serve the king who had beaten King Lot."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 60%.** Inside the hall at night, a minstrel with a
harp sings to a crowd of knights by the great hearth. In the painted
hanging behind him, a boy king charges alone with a blazing sword, dressed
from head to foot in shining steel plate armour that Arthur has never worn. At the
edge of the firelight, Arthur leans on a pillar listening, and he is
smiling.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(over the plain dark wall above the hearth)*
> "By the end of the winter there were songs about him. In the songs, the boy king wore shining steel from head to foot, which he had never owned, and charged alone into a whole army, and his sword shone like the sun, and nobody could stand against him. Nobody in the songs was called Kay.
>
> At first Arthur laughed at them. Then he stopped laughing and started listening. After a while, he began to think they might be true."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has forgotten the lesson.

## PAGE 36 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** In the songs, Arthur wore shining armour. What did warriors in his time really look like?

**Sources:** late-Roman and post-Roman military equipment in Britain (mail, round shields, spears; Roman ridge helmets such as the 4th-century finds from Burgh Castle and Richborough); mail hauberks, surcoats, and great helms of c. 1200–1250 (e.g. the Maciejowski Bible, c. 1250); full plate armour of the 15th century, Malory's own time.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant
image is two full-length warriors painted side by side as costume studies in
ink and watercolour, on plain vellum. Left: a warrior of about the year 500
in a long mail shirt, a dark cloak, a simple Roman-style iron helmet with a
ridge over the top, a round painted wooden shield, and a spear. Right: a
knight of about 1200, as in this story, in mail from head to foot, a long
red surcoat, and a flat-topped great helm, holding a long sword and a kite
shield. Behind the right-hand knight, faint, a ghost outline in pale ink of a
knight in full shining plate armour. An illuminated initial begins the first
field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(top of the page)*
> "What did warriors in Arthur's time really look like? The songs made Arthur bigger, and so did the pictures. If a real Arthur lived around the year 500, he and his warriors probably looked like the man on the left: a long mail shirt if they were rich, a round wooden shield, a spear, and perhaps a helmet like the Romans wore. Knights in shining armour were still nearly a thousand years away."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(bottom of the page)*
> "So why is Arthur almost never drawn like the man on the left? Because every storyteller dressed him in the armour of their own time. The storytellers and painters of about 1200 pictured him in mail and a great helm, so that is how this book draws him. Sir Thomas Malory, who wrote the most famous book of Arthur stories, lived in the 1400s among knights in steel plate, and that is how his readers pictured Arthur."

> **LABEL** *(under the left warrior)*
> "About 500"

> **LABEL** *(under the right knight)*
> "About 1200"

> **LABEL** *(beside the ghost outline)*
> "The 1400s"

**Why you turn the page:** back in the story, the boy king in mail is about to go looking for trouble.

## PAGE 37 · *dramatic*

**Location:** Camelot, the hall, morning.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The hall in daylight. Kay, in his steward's
black with a yellow border, stands before Arthur's chair with his arms
folded, giving news. Arthur, in blue, sits forward eagerly. Kay left, Arthur
right.

> **KAY:** "There's a knight in the forest who's set up his tent beside a fountain."
>
> **KAY:** "Nobody passes without fighting him. He's knocked three of our knights into the water."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Then I'll go and fight him myself."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Kay, flat.

> **KAY:** "No, you won't. You're the king. Kings send knights."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Kings in the songs don't."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Kay walking away, waving a hand over his shoulder;
Arthur, behind him, has gone quiet, his jaw set.

> **KAY:** "Kings in the songs don't have stewards. The answer is no."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has been told no, and he isn't going to listen.

## PAGE 38 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the forest at night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A great forest of old oaks in moonlight, the
road a silver ribbon. Arthur riding alone on his grey horse in full mail
and blue surcoat, his helmet hanging from his saddle, his face turned up to
the moon, grinning.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the dark sky between the branches)*
> "That night, when the castle was asleep, Arthur put on his armour by himself, led his horse out past the dozing guard, and rode into the forest alone.
>
> He had never felt so happy. No steward, no wizard, no lords telling him he was only a squire. Just the moon through the oak leaves, and a sword that had beaten an army."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Far ahead through the trees, a glow of firelight beside
the glint of water.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(over the plain dark of the trees)*
> "By the time he saw the fountain, he had already made up the song they would sing about tonight."

**Why you turn the page:** he's reached the fountain, and someone is waiting there.

## PAGE 39 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the fountain in the forest, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** A clearing with an old stone fountain spilling
into a moonlit pool. A battered green-and-white pavilion; a dented shield
hanging from an oak branch. Rising from his fire, KING PELLINORE: enormous,
the biggest man in the book, a long heavy scarred face, iron-grey hair
cropped close, a short square-cut grey beard, old dented black-painted
armour and a faded green surcoat with a white boar. He stands with both
hands on the pommel of a huge sword planted point-down. Arthur on his horse
on the left; Pellinore on the right.

> **PELLINORE:** "Nobody passes my fountain without fighting me. That's the rule."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Do you know who I am? I'm the king."

**Panel 2 — 45%.** Pellinore lifting his great helm onto his head, calm.

> **PELLINORE:** "I am King Pellinore, and I have never yielded to any man. If you're a king, then fight like one."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Gladly."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy king is about to fight the biggest man he has ever seen.

## PAGE 40 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the fountain.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The joust: two riders crashing together in the
moonlight, both lances shattering into splinters.

> **SOUND** *(over the crash)*
> "CRACK!"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** On foot, swords out, circling by the pool; Arthur
smaller, attacking, Pellinore planted.

**Panel 3 — dominant, 55%.** Arthur's sword from the stone meets
Pellinore's huge blade with all Arthur's strength behind it, and the sword
from the stone snaps in two. The broken half spins away through the air,
flashing in the moonlight, toward the pool. Arthur's face: disbelief.

> **SOUND** *(at the break)*
> "KRANG!"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "No! Not this sword!"

**Why you turn the page:** the sword that made Arthur king is broken, and he has nothing left to fight with.
