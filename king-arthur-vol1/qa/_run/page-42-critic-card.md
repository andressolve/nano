## Page intent

## PAGE 42

Dramatic. Arthur scrambles up in a fury, sure that Merlin has killed the bravest knight he ever fought with a spell; Merlin, walking into the firelight, tells him the giant is only asleep and snoring. Then Arthur sinks to his knees holding the two halves of the sword from the stone, and Merlin, standing over him, convicts him with three questions: who told you not to come, who offered to let you walk home, who was too proud to take it. ## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every

### C1 — Transcription

Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The kneeling boy owns the page

The eye finds Arthur on his knees holding two halves of a plain iron sword, Merlin standing over him. Blocking if the sword reads whole or the kneeling image is lost among the others.

### C3 — The questions and answers

Merlin owns "He's asleep…" and all three questions; Arthur owns the opening accusation and "Kay.", "Pellinore.", "Me." Each answer must sit after its question and hang from Arthur's mouth. Blocking if any answer reads as Merlin's, or reads before its question.

### C4 — Identity

Merlin: tall, thin, hooked nose, long forked white beard, wild white hair, staff. The sleeping giant: square grey beard, faded green. Arthur: fair-haired boy in deep blue. Blocking if Merlin and Pellinore merge, or Merlin wears a pointed hat.

### C5 — Sword count
The page shows exactly the two broken halves of the sword from the stone, and no other sword. A duplicate sword, a whole sword from the stone after it has broken, or a second weapon copied from a reference sheet is blocking, because the two swords and the one broken sword are the volume's causal objects. Angle and how much of a blade shows are nonblocking.
