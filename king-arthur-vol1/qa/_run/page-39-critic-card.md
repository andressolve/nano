## Page intent

## PAGE 39

Dramatic. A moonlit clearing with an old stone fountain, a battered green-and-white pavilion, and a dented shield hanging from an oak. King Pellinore rises from his fire, the biggest man in the book, both hands on a huge sword planted point-down, and states his rule: nobody passes without fighting him. Arthur, on horseback, plays his crown. Pellinore names himself, says he has never yielded to any man, and settles his great helm on his head; Arthur answers with one eager word. ### C1 — Transcription

Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. ### C2 — The giant

Pellinore plainly owns the page: far bigger than the boy, still, calm, a huge sword planted point-down. Blocking if he reads as no larger than Arthur or as a villain sneering.

### C3 — Who says what

Pellinore owns the rule and his own name; Arthur owns "Do you know who I am?" and "Gladly." Each must hang from its own speaker's mouth. Blocking if "Gladly." could belong to Pellinore, or if Arthur's question reads before Pellinore's rule.

### C4 — Identity

Pellinore: huge, cropped iron-grey hair, short square grey beard, scarred long face, faded green surcoat with a white boar on dented black armour. Arthur: fair-haired beardless boy in deep blue. Blocking if Pellinore's beard reads long or forked, or the two are confused.

### C5 — Weapons
