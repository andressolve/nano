## Page intent

## PAGE 44

A spectacle page. Arthur and Merlin come out of the forest onto the shore of a lake that looks like another world: still water, white mist, silver hills, no warmth anywhere. Out of the very middle of the lake rises a woman's arm in white silk, holding a long bright sword straight up in a dark-red scabbard. The arm and the sword own the page; Arthur and Merlin are tiny on the shore, and Arthur's blue is the only warm colour in all that grey. The dominant turn: the other sword is right there. The reader turns to find out who is holding it. What stays subordinate: the reeds, the far hills, and the two small figures, who give the scale and nothing more; the broken sword is not part of this picture.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 44 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text anywhere on the page; a caption that does not read is blocking, one that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The arm and the sword own the page
The eye goes first to the white-sleeved arm rising from the middle of the lake holding the sword upright; the page is a spectacle image, not a dialogue scene. If the figures on the shore, the reeds, or the hills take the eye first, the reader misses the turn: blocking. Judge by what the eye finds.

### C3 — A different world
The lake reads as cold silver, grey, and white mist, with Arthur's small blue the only warm note. A warm sunset, gold light, or a second warm figure breaks the book's signal that this place is not ordinary: blocking. Slight warmth in the reeds is nonblocking.

### C4 — The sword's state
The sword in the hand is sheathed in a dark-red scabbard and there is no Lady figure yet. A bare glowing blade or a woman already standing on the water spoils the next two pages: blocking. The exact look of the hilt at this distance is nonblocking.

### C5 — The two on the shore
Two small figures stand on the shore: a slight boy in blue and a tall, thin, white-bearded old man with a staff. Their faces need not be readable; if they cannot be told apart as boy and old man, or a third figure appears, it is blocking.

### C6 — Sword count
The page shows only Excalibur, sheathed, held up by the arm, and no other sword. A duplicate sword, a whole sword from the stone after it has broken, or a second weapon copied from a reference sheet is blocking, because the two swords and the one broken sword are the volume's causal objects. Angle and how much of a blade shows are nonblocking.
