## Page intent

## PAGE 34

Dramatic. Arthur kneels in the grass, holding a water-skin to the mouth of a wounded young smith, while Merlin leans on his staff above them. Arthur asks why winning feels like this, and Merlin says it is because he is looking at what it cost, and that this is good. Then, quietly and without looking up, Arthur admits he charged alone and would be dead if Kay hadn't come. Merlin tells him to remember it, because he may not always be there to remind him. Arthur and Merlin share the page, and its weight is Arthur's quiet confession. The dominant turn: Arthur has learned the lesson, and Merlin is afraid he won't keep it. The reader turns to see whether the lesson holds. The wounded smith is cared for and shows no wound.
