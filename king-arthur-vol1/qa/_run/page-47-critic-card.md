## Page intent

## PAGE 47

A dramatic page on the lakeshore. Arthur has drawn Excalibur and holds it up in pure delight; Merlin asks which he likes better, the sword or the scabbard, and Arthur picks the sword without a thought. Merlin calls him a fool: whoever wears the scabbard loses no blood. Then, slowly, Arthur lays the bright sword on the grass and buckles the empty scabbard on first, looking at it, while Merlin raises one eyebrow and allows that he may live to be old. Arthur's change of mind owns the page, and the joke is Merlin's. The dominant turn: Arthur has chosen the thing that keeps him safe, and on the way home he will have to choose again. What stays subordinate: the lake behind them, which is only a cold backdrop now.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 47 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the lakeshore.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 40%.** On the shore, Arthur has drawn Excalibur and
holds it up in delight: longer than the old sword, bright steel, a
gold-wired grip, a single green stone in the cross-guard. The empty red
scabbard hangs from his other hand. Merlin, left, leans on his staff.

> **MERLIN:** "Tell me. Which do you like better, the sword or the scabbard?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "The sword! Look at it, Merlin!"

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Merlin, tapping the scabbard with his staff.

> **MERLIN:** "Then you're a fool. Whoever wears that scabbard loses no blood, however badly he's hurt. It's worth ten swords. Keep it close."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Arthur, slowly, lays the bright sword down on the grass,
and buckles the empty scabbard onto his belt first, looking at it, not at
the sword. Merlin watches with one eyebrow up.

> **MERLIN:** "Hm. You may live to be old after all."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has chosen the thing that keeps him safe, and on the way home he will have to choose again.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction
guidance, never critic measurements.

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when
the reader harm and redraw justification are concrete.

A lettered speaker name, label, or tag anywhere on the page is extra text and
blocks under the transcription criterion, whatever else reads correctly:
finished pages carry dialogue, captions, prose, and sound cues only.
A sound cue whose letters and order match the script reads correctly; the run
length of a repeated letter in it is nonblocking and never counted.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 47 — card

### C1 — Transcription
Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. A line owned by placement (it names its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The dominant turn
Arthur's delight with the drawn sword owns the page, and the last image plainly shows him buckling on the empty scabbard while the sword lies on the grass. If he is still holding the sword at the end, the reader never sees him change his mind: blocking.

### C3 — Attribution
Merlin asks the question and Arthur answers "The sword!"; both later lines belong to Merlin. If Arthur's answer reads as Merlin's, or "Then you're a fool" hangs from Arthur, the joke and the lesson invert: blocking.

### C4 — The two objects
Excalibur is a bright long sword with a gold-wired grip and a green stone in the cross-guard; the scabbard is dark red. The plain iron sword from the stone must not appear. Confusing the two swords is blocking; exact gilt detail is not.

### C5 — Identity and focal integrity
Arthur: blond boy in blue. Merlin: thin, stooped, long forked white beard, staff. Arthur's two hands on the buckle are clean. A broken face or a mangled buckling hand is blocking.

### C6 — Sword count
The page shows only Excalibur and its scabbard, and no other sword. A duplicate sword, a whole sword from the stone after it has broken, or a second weapon copied from a reference sheet is blocking, because the two swords and the one broken sword are the volume's causal objects. Angle and how much of a blade shows are nonblocking.
