## Page intent

## PAGE 40

Dramatic, nearly silent. The joust: two riders crash together and both lances shatter. Then the two men on foot by the pool, Arthur smaller and attacking, Pellinore planted. Then the moment that owns the page: the sword from the stone meets Pellinore's huge blade and snaps in two, the broken half spinning away through the moonlight toward the pool, and Arthur's face goes blank with disbelief as he cries out. The one dominant turn: the sword that made Arthur king is broken, and he has nothing left to fight with. The joust and the circling are quick lead-ins; the break and Arthur's face are what the reader must see. Nobody is wounded.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 40 — card

### C1 — Transcription

Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, with no extra text; Arthur's line hangs from Arthur's mouth. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. The run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue, exclamation marks present or missing, and curly against straight quotes are nonblocking; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The break owns the page

The eye goes first to the plain iron sword snapping in two against Pellinore's blade, a broken half flying toward the pool, and Arthur's face in disbelief. Blocking if the break is unclear, if the sword looks bent rather than broken, or if Arthur's face cannot be read.

### C3 — The right sword breaks

The broken sword is the plain dull-iron one; nothing bright, gold-gripped, or green-stoned appears anywhere. Blocking if Pellinore's blade breaks instead, or a second whole copy of Arthur's sword is visible after the break.

### C4 — Identity and integrity

Arthur is the fair-haired boy in deep blue; Pellinore is the huge helmed knight in faded green and black. Blocking if the two are confused, if hands on the hilt are malformed, or if anyone bleeds. Splinter and horse detail is nonblocking.

### C5 — Sword count
The page shows the sword from the stone, whole in the joust and then snapping into two pieces (never a third sword), and no other sword. A duplicate sword, a whole sword from the stone after it has broken, or a second weapon copied from a reference sheet is blocking, because the two swords and the one broken sword are the volume's causal objects. Angle and how much of a blade shows are nonblocking.
