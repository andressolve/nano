## Page intent

## PAGE 41

Dramatic. Arthur lies on his back in the wet grass, the broken hilt still in his fist, with Pellinore's sword point at his throat. Pellinore offers him his life for one word; Arthur refuses, because kings don't yield. Pellinore, sorry for it, lifts the sword in both hands. Then, out of the dark behind him, come a long hand with a silver ring, two fingers pointing, the tip of a crooked staff, and the end of a forked white beard, and a single quiet command. The giant topples like a felled oak, his eyes closed. The one dominant turn: Merlin has stopped the killing blow, and Arthur doesn't yet know how. The pinned boy owns the page; Merlin is only a hand and a voice; no blow lands.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 41 — card

### C1 — Transcription

Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in causal order, owned by the right speaker by tail and placement, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Curly against straight quotes, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. "Sleep, Pellinore." is owned by placement at the hand, beard, and staff even though no mouth shows.

### C2 — The killing blow is stopped

The page reads as a boy pinned under a sword, the sword raised to kill, a ringed hand from the dark, and the giant falling asleep. Blocking if a blow lands, if there is blood, or if Pellinore's eyes are open as he falls.

### C3 — Who says what

Pellinore owns the offer and the apology; Arthur owns the refusal. Each hangs from its own speaker's mouth. Blocking if the refusal reads as Pellinore's.

### C4 — Merlin unseen, recognisable

Only the long hand, the green-stoned ring, two pointing fingers, the staff tip, and a forked white beard end show. Blocking if Merlin's face appears, if a glow comes from the hand, or if the beard reads square.

### C5 — Object state

Arthur grips only a broken hilt. Blocking if he holds a whole sword or a bright green-stoned one.

### C6 — Sword count
The page shows only the broken hilt in Arthur's fist, and no other sword. A duplicate sword, a whole sword from the stone after it has broken, or a second weapon copied from a reference sheet is blocking, because the two swords and the one broken sword are the volume's causal objects. Angle and how much of a blade shows are nonblocking.
