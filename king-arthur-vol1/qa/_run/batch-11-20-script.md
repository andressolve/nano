# Exact script — pages 11–20

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 11 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the churchyard, empty, snow falling.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The empty churchyard in falling snow, nobody
else anywhere. Arthur, alone, one foot on the stone, has drawn the sword
straight up out of the anvil in one easy pull; his body is still in the
motion, more surprised than strong. The blade catches a single shaft of pale
winter light that falls through the clouds onto the stone. Snow flies off the
anvil. The gold letters glow at the bottom of the page.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the snowy sky)*
> "It came out as easily as a stick out of snow."

**Why you turn the page:** he has done the thing no lord in Britain could do, and nobody saw him.

---

## PAGE 12 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the tournament field.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** By Kay's tent. Arthur, out of breath, holds the sword out
to Kay, hilt first. Arthur on the left, Kay on the right.

> **ARTHUR:** "The inn was locked. I got you this one instead."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close on Kay, holding the sword, staring at it. The
colour drains from his face.

> **KAY:** "Arthur. Where did you get this?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "From the stone in the churchyard. Nobody was using it."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 40%.** Kay has turned his back on Arthur and is
holding the sword high, shouting across the field toward Ector, who is
turning in the crowd. Behind Kay, small and baffled, Arthur.

> **KAY:** "Father! Father, come here! I'm the king of Britain! Look, I have the sword from the stone!"

**Why you turn the page:** Kay is claiming the crown for himself.

---

## PAGE 13 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the churchyard, the empty stone.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The stone with an empty slot in the anvil.
Ector, grim, stands on the left with a big book bound in leather from the
church in his hands; Kay, holding the sword, faces him with one hand laid on
the book. Arthur stands a little apart, right.

> **ECTOR:** "Kay. Put your hand on this book, and swear to me. How did you get that sword?"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Close on Kay. He looks at Arthur. A long pause.

> **KAY:** "Arthur gave it to me."

**Panel 3 — 35%.** Ector turns to Arthur. Arthur, worried now, on the
right.

> **ECTOR:** "Arthur. How did you get it?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "I pulled it out of the stone. The inn was locked, and Kay needed a sword. Was that wrong?"
>
> **ECTOR:** "Put it back in the stone. Then pull it out again, where I can see you."

**Why you turn the page:** Ector is going to watch Arthur do it.

## PAGE 14 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the churchyard.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Arthur stands on the stone with the sword
drawn again, holding it up, astonished at himself. Below him, in the snow,
Ector and Kay are down on their knees, heads bowed. Ector on the left, Kay on
the right, Arthur above them.

> **ARTHUR:** "Father, what are you doing? Get up! Kay, get up! Both of you!"
>
> **ECTOR:** "No, my lord. I won't. I'm not your father."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close on Arthur's face, the sword forgotten in his hand.

> **ARTHUR:** "What?"

**Why you turn the page:** the man Arthur has called Father all his life has just told him he isn't.

---

## PAGE 15 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the churchyard.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** Ector, still kneeling, has taken Arthur's free hand in
both of his.

> **ECTOR:** "Fifteen years ago, Merlin came to my door in the night with a baby in his arms."
>
> **ECTOR:** "He said: raise this boy as your own son, and never ask whose he is."
>
> **ECTOR:** "So I did, and I never asked."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Arthur, very still.

> **ARTHUR:** "Then I'm not anybody's son."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Arthur sits down hard on the edge of the stone,
the sword across his knees, crying the way a boy cries when he's trying not
to. Kay, behind him, looks away.

> **ARTHUR:** "This morning I had a father and a brother. Now all I've got is a sword."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has won a kingdom and lost his family in one breath, and someone has to answer him.

## PAGE 16 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the churchyard.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Ector, getting up off his knees, puts his big
hand on the back of Arthur's neck, the way a father does. Arthur, sitting on
the edge of the stone, looks up at him. Ector on the left, Arthur on the
right.

> **ECTOR:** "You'll always have us, lad, if you want us."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "I do want you. Can I still call you Father?"
>
> **ECTOR:** "When nobody's listening, lad. Always."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Ector glances across at Kay, who stands apart in the
snow, red in the face.

> **ECTOR:** "And I'll ask one thing of the new king. Make Kay your steward, the keeper of your house."
>
> **ECTOR:** "He's got a sharp tongue, but no man alive will fight harder for you."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Arthur nods, his eyes on Kay.

> **ARTHUR:** "Then he will be. But first I need to talk to him."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur still has a father, and now he has to face the brother who tried to take the crown.

## PAGE 17 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the churchyard.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** The brothers face each other in the snow by the stone.
Kay can't meet Arthur's eyes. Kay on the left, Arthur on the right.

> **KAY:** "I told Father it was mine. For about a minute, I wanted it to be."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "I know. Then he made you swear, and you told the truth."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Kay, looking away, embarrassed.

> **KAY:** "Don't make a song out of it."

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Arthur grips Kay's forearm, the knight's grip;
Kay, after a moment, grips back, and looks at him at last. Ector watches
from behind them.

> **ARTHUR:** "You'll be my steward for as long as we both live, Kay. I swear it."

**Why you turn the page:** the brothers are still brothers, and now the rest of Britain has to hear.

## PAGE 18 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** inside the cold church, night.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** The inside of the dark church, one candle.
Arthur sits alone on a bench with the sword across his knees, looking down
at it. Snow-light through a high window. He looks very small.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the plain dark wall above the window)*
> "That night Arthur could not sleep. He sat alone in the church with the sword across his knees and looked at it for a long time.
>
> This morning he had been a squire who owned nothing, and he had known exactly who he was. By evening Sir Ector had told the churchmen, and the churchmen had told everyone. Now all London was whispering his name, and he did not know who his father was, or why an old wizard had carried him away in the dark."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Close on Arthur's hand on the blade.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(over the plain shadow beside the candle)*
> "In the morning he would have to show the lords of Britain. He wondered if they would kneel, the way Ector had.
>
> He did not think they would."

**Why you turn the page:** tomorrow the lords decide what to do with him.

## PAGE 19 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Did the sword in the stone really happen?

**Sources:** Robert de Boron, *Merlin* (verse, c. 1200; the prose *Merlin*, c. 1210) — first appearance of the sword in the stone; *Culhwch and Olwen* (Welsh, c. 1100) — Arthur's sword Caledfwlch, no stone; Geoffrey of Monmouth, *History of the Kings of Britain* (c. 1136) — no stone.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant
image is a long ink timeline drawn as an unrolled scroll running down the
page from top to bottom, from the year 500 to the year 1500. Almost all of
it is empty vellum. Near the top, a small question mark and a tiny warrior.
Far down, a small ink drawing of the stone with its anvil and sword, painted
in watercolour, at about 1200. An illuminated initial in red, lapis, and
gold begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(right of the scroll, upper half)*
> "Did the sword in the stone really happen? Almost certainly not, and here is how we can tell. The oldest stories about Arthur have battles, giants, and a famous sword, but no stone. Even a book of 1136, which called itself a history and first made him a great king of all Britain, has no stone. Nobody pulls anything out of anything."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(right of the scroll, lower half)*
> "The sword in the stone first appears in a story called Merlin, by a French writer named Robert de Boron, around the year 1200. That is about seven hundred years after a real Arthur could have lived. Storytellers loved it at once: a king chosen not by an army, or by his father, but by a sign that only the right person could answer."

> **LABEL** *(at the top of the scroll)*
> "About 500: Arthur?"

> **LABEL** *(halfway down the scroll)*
> "1136: a great king"

> **LABEL** *(beside the stone)*
> "About 1200: the stone"

**Why you turn the page:** the stone may be a legend, but in the story, the lords of Britain are about to see it.

# MOVEMENT III — THE KINGS WHO LAUGHED · pp 20–27

---

## PAGE 20 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the churchyard, morning, crowded.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 50%.** The churchyard packed with lords in furs and
mail. Arthur, still in his patched brown tunic, stands at the stone holding
the sword up, drawn for the third time. The lords' faces: not wonder,
anger. In the church doorway behind them, Merlin watches and says nothing,
waiting.

> **LOT:** "So the wizard's trick works for a farm boy. I don't care if he pulls it out a hundred times."

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Lot, in front of the lords, pointing at Arthur.

> **LOT:** "Who is he? Whose son? Nobody knows! Am I to kneel to a squire who grew up in a pigsty?"

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Arthur, red to the ears, on the right, shouting back;
Lot, left, laughing to the lords.

> **ARTHUR:** "It wasn't a pigsty! It was a good farm!"
>
> **LOT:** "Hear that, my lords? The king of the hens!"

**Why you turn the page:** the lords are laughing him out of the churchyard.
