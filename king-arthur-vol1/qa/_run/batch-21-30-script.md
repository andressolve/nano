# Exact script — pages 21–30

Open only after the blind sequence read.

## PAGE 21 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the churchyard gate and the street beyond.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** The churchyard gate has burst open and the
people of London are pouring in: smiths in leather aprons, farmers, bakers,
washerwomen, boys on their fathers' shoulders, fists in the air, mouths
open, an ocean of ordinary faces filling the lower two-thirds of the page.
At the top, small, the lords turning round, shocked; Arthur on the stone
above them all, staring at the crowd; as the roar goes up, the sword in his hand flares with a sudden white light, and he looks down at it, startled.

> **SOUND** *(huge, rising out of the crowd)*
> "WE WILL HAVE ARTHUR!"

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the church wall)*
> "The people of London had been listening."

**Why you turn the page:** the lords said no, and the people said yes.

## PAGE 22 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the great church, the crowning.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** A tall stone church full of candles and
crowds. Arthur kneels before the altar in a new deep-blue surcoat, a thin
gold crown just placed on his head by unnamed churchmen in white. His hand
is on the sword, laid on the altar. At the front of the crowd, arms folded,
unsmiling, a few lords in furs.

> **ARTHUR:** "I swear to be a true king to you."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "To do justice, and to protect you, all of you, for as long as I live."

**Panel 2 — 45%.** Close: Arthur standing, the crown a little too big and
tipped, sweat on his lip. Merlin has stepped up beside him and speaks low
into his ear. Arthur on the left, Merlin on the right.

> **ARTHUR:** "You're the one who took me to Ector. You know who my father was. Tell me."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Not here, with a church full of ears. You should hear it before they do. Tonight, I swear it."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur is king, and tonight he'll learn whose son he is.

## PAGE 23 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the great hall, the crowning feast.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** A long hall at a feast, tables full, torches.
At the high table, Arthur in blue with his thin crown, Kay and Ector beside
him, Merlin behind. The doors at the far end are flung open: King Lot
strides in with a crowd of lords in furs behind him. At Lot's feet, an open
wooden chest spills gold cups and silver onto the floor.

> **ARTHUR:** "King Lot! You're welcome at my table. Did my gifts reach you?"

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Lot kicks the chest; cups roll.

> **LOT:** "They did, and we've brought them back. We don't take presents from a boy nobody's ever heard of."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Lot, grinning, his hand on his sword hilt.

> **LOT:** "But we've brought you a gift of our own, King Arthur. It's a sharp sword, and it's coming for your neck."

**Why you turn the page:** the most powerful lord in Britain has just threatened the king at his own feast.

## PAGE 24 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the great hall.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** Merlin has stepped forward from behind the
high table, staff raised, and the whole hall has gone silent. Lot in the
centre of the floor, left; Merlin, right. Behind Merlin at the high table,
Arthur half-risen from his chair.

> **MERLIN:** "Be quiet, Lot, and listen. This boy is the son of King Uther."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Some of you heard Uther say he had a son. I was there."

**Panel 2 — 45%.** Lot at the doors, laughing, turning back with his lords
to go.

> **LOT:** "Says the wizard who does tricks with swords! Come down from that chair, boy, before we pull you down."
>
> **LOT:** "We'll see you on the battlefield."

**Why you turn the page:** the whole hall has just heard whose son Arthur is, before Arthur heard it himself.

## PAGE 25 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the empty hall, after the feast.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** The hall empty, the torches low, cups knocked over.
Arthur stands facing Merlin across the high table, the crown pulled off and
gripped in his fist. Arthur on the left, Merlin on the right.

> **ARTHUR:** "You swore I'd hear it first! Then you shouted it at Lot, in front of everyone!"
>
> **MERLIN:** "In the churchyard I kept quiet, so the people would choose you for yourself."
>
> **MERLIN:** "At the feast, Lot was telling the whole hall you were nobody's son. I wasn't going to let him finish."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Arthur, still angry; Merlin, not sorry,
leaning on his staff, meeting his eyes.

> **ARTHUR:** "You took me away from my father. You let Ector raise me on a lie."

> **MERLIN:** "Yes. If those lords had found out where you were, you'd have been drowned in a well before you could walk."
>
> **MERLIN:** "I'd do it again."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Arthur, quieter, the anger going out of him.

> **ARTHUR:** "Did he ever see me?"
>
> **MERLIN:** "Once. The night you were born. He named you."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has his father's name now, and a war to fight for it.

## PAGE 26 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the empty hall.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** Arthur has sat down at the high table, the crown in his
lap, hands shaking. Merlin stands, right. Kay has just come in and leans on
the table, left.

> **ARTHUR:** "And my mother?"
>
> **MERLIN:** "That's another night's story. One father is enough for tonight."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "But Lot's right about one thing. I'm a squire. I've never been in a real fight."
>
> **KAY:** "It's true. I've seen him fight. It's terrible."
>
> **KAY:** "But I'll be next to him, which makes it less terrible."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Merlin leans down close to Arthur, his long
finger tapping the crown in Arthur's lap.

> **MERLIN:** "When the people shouted your name in the snow, did anyone ask how many fights you'd won?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "No. They didn't even know who my father was."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Merlin straightens; Arthur looks up at him, then at Kay.

> **MERLIN:** "Then don't you ask it either. Lot is coming for them as well as for you."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Then we fight him. All of us, together."

**Why you turn the page:** the boy has decided to go to war.

## PAGE 27 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Was there a real King Arthur? Did he really fight wars like this one?

**Sources:** Gildas, *De Excidio et Conquestu Britanniae* (c. 540) — the siege of Mount Badon, Ambrosius Aurelianus, no Arthur; *Historia Brittonum* (c. 829–830), ch. 56 — Arthur's twelve battles, the last at Badon; Badon's location unknown.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant
image: two old manuscript pages painted side by side as museum studies,
yellowed and stained, with dense brown Latin script too small to read and a
red initial each. Between them, a long empty space crossed by a thin ruled
line. Below them, a small ink drawing of a lonely green hill with a question
mark over it. An illuminated initial begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(top of the page)*
> "Was there a real King Arthur? Nobody knows. Here is what we have. Around the year 540, a British monk named Gildas wrote an angry book about his own times. He tells of a great battle at a place called Mount Badon, where the Britons beat the Saxons. He names a British war-leader, Ambrosius, who led the fight back against the Saxons. He never names Arthur."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(bottom of the page)*
> "Nearly three hundred years later, around 830, a book called the History of the Britons lists twelve battles won by a war-leader named Arthur, the last one at Badon. Was he real, and Gildas just didn't mention him? Or a hero people made up to remember better days? Historians are still arguing."

> **LABEL** *(under the left manuscript)*
> "Gildas, about 540"

> **LABEL** *(under the right manuscript)*
> "History of the Britons, about 830"

> **LABEL** *(under the hill)*
> "Badon: nobody knows where"

**Why you turn the page:** real or not, in the story Arthur has to get through the night before his first battle.

## PAGE 28 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** Arthur's camp, the night before the battle.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A dark valley at night. On this side, a small
camp of a few tents and many campfires where ordinary people sit with spears,
axes, and pitchforks. Across the valley, rows and rows of enemy campfires,
far more, going over the hill. In the foreground, the open flap of a small
tent, and Arthur sitting in it, awake, the sword beside him.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the black sky)*
> "Arthur's army was small. A few knights had come over to him, and a great many ordinary people, smiths and farmers and bakers' boys, carrying whatever they had. Across the valley, King Lot's campfires burned in rows, more than Arthur could count.
>
> He lay awake all night and thought about the people who had shouted his name in the snow. They had chosen him. Tomorrow, some of them would not come home."

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Grey before dawn. Arthur, alone, pulling his mail shirt
over his head by himself.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(over the plain grey of the tent wall)*
> "Just before dawn, he got up and put on his mail by himself, the way a squire does."

**Why you turn the page:** the battle is at dawn.

## PAGE 29 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the valley of Bedegraine, dawn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A forest valley at dawn, frost on the grass,
mist lying along a stream in the bottom. On the far slope, Lot's army: a
wall of mailed riders under dozens of banners, Lot's black bear banner in the
middle, gold glinting. On the near slope, Arthur's army: a thin line of a
few knights and a great many townspeople with spears and pitchforks. At the
front of it, small but clear, Arthur on a grey horse in his blue surcoat,
helmet on, the sword drawn; Kay on a black horse beside him, in black and
yellow. The low sun is behind Arthur's army.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the mist)*
> "Dawn, in a valley called Bedegraine."

**Why you turn the page:** two armies are about to collide, and one of them is mostly farmers.

## PAGE 30 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the battlefield.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Arthur raises the sword and spurs his horse; Kay, beside
him on the left, grabs for his reins and misses.

> **ARTHUR:** "For Britain! Follow me!"
>
> **KAY:** "Arthur, wait! Not alone! Wait for the line!"

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Seen from high above: Arthur, a single blue
rider, galloping down the slope and across the stream far, far ahead of his
own army, straight into the thick of Lot's riders, who are closing around
him like water.

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Arthur's horse going down in the stream with a great
splash; Arthur thrown, the sword still in his fist.

> **SOUND** *(over the splash)*
> "SPLASH!"

**Why you turn the page:** the king is on the ground in the middle of the enemy, alone.
