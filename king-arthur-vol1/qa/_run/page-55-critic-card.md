## Page intent

## PAGE 55

Dramatic. King Lot appears in the hall doors under a white flag of truce, two lords behind him, to see the boy's wonder for himself. Arthur, risen, holds out his own chair to him and says he would rather have Lot at this table than across a valley. Lot sneers at sitting level with a farm boy's steward and an old boar who sleeps in a tent; across the table Kay fires back that this steward chased him off a battlefield, and Pellinore says simply that he sat down and is not ashamed. Lot turns on his heel and walks out, refusing to sit level with anybody. The dominant turn is Lot walking out while the knights who stayed are about to swear. The page belongs to the offer and the refusal; the lords behind Lot stay silent background.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 55 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The hall doors open; King Lot stands in them
with two lords behind him under a white flag of truce, staring at the round
table. Arthur has risen from his own chair and holds it out to Lot, his hand on
its back. Arthur left, Lot right.

> **LOT:** "You sent for me under a white flag, boy. I came to see this wonder of yours with my own eyes."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "I'd rather have you at this table than across a valley, King Lot."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Take my chair, if you like. It's no higher than anyone's."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Lot, sneering, in the foreground on the left, looking round
the table.

> **LOT:** "No higher? Level with a farm boy's steward? Level with an old boar who sleeps in a tent?"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Across the table on the right, seated side by side: Kay
leaning forward with his chin up, on the left of the pair; Pellinore huge and
still, on the right.

> **KAY:** "This steward chased you off a battlefield."
>
> **PELLINORE:** "Level with me. I sat down. I'm not ashamed of it."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Lot turning on his heel in the doorway, cloak swinging.

> **LOT:** "I am the King of Orkney. I sit level with nobody. Keep your tabletop, boy. When you need a real king, send to Orkney."

**Why you turn the page:** Lot has walked out, and the knights who stayed are about to swear.

### C2 — The refusal does not land
The page must read as a dramatic page: Arthur holding out his own chair, Lot sneering, and Lot walking out. If Lot appears to accept, or his exit cannot be found, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the exchange
Lot's arrival line must read before Arthur's two replies, and both replies hang from Arthur. In the seated pair, the steward line hangs from Kay and the "Level with me" line from Pellinore; swapping them is blocking. Lot's last line hangs from Lot in the doorway.
