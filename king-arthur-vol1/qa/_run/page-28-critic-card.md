## Page intent

## PAGE 28

Illustrated prose. The night before the first battle, Arthur sits awake in the mouth of a small tent above a dark valley. On his side burn a handful of campfires where smiths, farmers, and bakers' boys sit with spears and pitchforks; across the valley King Lot's fires burn in rows over the hill, far more of them. The prose carries what he is thinking: these people chose him, and some will not come home. Then, in grey light before dawn, he pulls on his own mail with nobody to help him. Arthur owns the page, and he is alone in it. The dominant turn: the battle is at dawn. The reader turns to see it begin. The tents, the weapons, and the far enemy stay subordinate to the boy awake in the tent; the scale of the two camps is felt, not counted.

### C1 — Transcription
Every scripted string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no extra text: the two paragraphs of the first prose field, then the second prose field. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — Arthur awake and alone owns the page
The eye must find Arthur awake in the tent mouth, facing the far fires, as the page's centre, with the small camp and the far larger enemy camp both readable. If the reader cannot see that his side is small and the other is huge, or if Arthur is lost in the landscape, the night before does not land. Illustrated-prose mode: prose fields over plain ground, never over his face.

### C3 — Arming himself
The second image must read as Arthur alone, before dawn, putting his own mail on. A second person helping him breaks the scripted point. The exact pose is nonblocking.

### C4 — Identity and integrity
Arthur is a slight blond boy with no beard, the only one in deep blue. The sword beside him is plain iron and does not glow. Gross anatomy in his focal hands is blocking; background figures' hands and repeated faces are not.
