## Page intent

## PAGE 59

Chronicle. The reader steps out of the story onto a page from a chronicle, which asks who made up the story of King Arthur and whether the Round Table is real. The answer is the dominant image: a snowball rolling down a long hill and growing, a small date flag at each step where a storyteller added something, from Geoffrey of Monmouth's king and wizard, through Wace's round table and Chrétien's Camelot, Lancelot and Grail, down to Malory's one enormous book. At the bottom of the hill hangs the real Winchester Round Table, made long after any real Arthur could have lived. The dominant turn is that the story is not over: the storytellers had many more knights to add. No story character appears; the vellum, ink and wash register owns the page, and the snowball carries the idea.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 59 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Who made up this whole story, and is the Round Table real?

**Sources:** Gildas (c. 540); *Historia Brittonum* (c. 829–830); Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1136) — Arthur as king, Merlin introduced (he serves Uther; Merlin as Arthur's counsellor comes later, with Robert de Boron and the French prose romances); Wace, *Roman de Brut* (1155) — the Round Table, made so no baron could claim a higher seat; Chrétien de Troyes (c. 1170s–1180s) — Camelot named, Lancelot, the Grail; Malory, *Le Morte d'Arthur* (finished 1469–70, printed by Caxton 1485), self-described "knight prisoner"; the Winchester Round Table — oak, dendrochronology c. 1250–1280, probably for Edward I's tournament of 1290, painted for Henry VIII in 1522.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant
image: a great snowball drawn in ink and watercolour rolling down a long
hill from the top left to the bottom right, growing as it goes, a small
date flag planted beside the track at each step. At the bottom right, as a
museum study, the Winchester Round Table hanging on a stone wall: a huge
painted wooden disc, green and white segments, a Tudor rose in the middle, a
painted king at the top. An illuminated initial begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(top right, on plain vellum)*
> "Who made up the story of King Arthur, and is the Round Table real? Nobody made it up alone. It grew like a snowball. In 1136, Geoffrey of Monmouth made Arthur a great king, and brought in a wizard named Merlin. In 1155, a poet named Wace gave him a round table, so no knight could boast of a better seat."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(middle left, on plain vellum)*
> "Around 1180, a French poet, Chrétien de Troyes, added Camelot, new knights such as Lancelot, and the Grail, a mysterious holy dish that later storytellers turned into a cup the knights would search for. Around 1470, an English knight, Thomas Malory, writing in prison, gathered all of it into one enormous book. And the Round Table you can still see in Winchester? It was made around 1290, nearly eight hundred years after a real Arthur could have lived."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 3** *(bottom left, large)*
> "The storytellers weren't finished. They had a great many more knights to add."
