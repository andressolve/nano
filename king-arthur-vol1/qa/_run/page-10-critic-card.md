## Page intent

## PAGE 10

Illustrated-prose page. Arthur reaches the inn and finds it locked; the whole city has gone to the tournament, and the street is completely empty. He hammers on the door until his fists hurt, thinking of Kay being laughed at. Then his face changes as he looks up the street at the church tower over the roofs: there is a sword in the churchyard that nobody is using. The prose carries his thinking; the pictures carry the empty street and the moment the idea arrives. The turn is that Arthur is going to take the sword from the stone without knowing what that means. He does not think about the gold letters at all, and the page must not make him look solemn or chosen, only a boy solving a problem for his brother.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 10 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** an empty London street, outside the inn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A narrow snowy street, completely empty. Arthur
pounding on a heavy locked inn door with both fists. Every window shuttered.
At the end of the street, over the roofs, the square tower of the church.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the pale sky above the roofs)*
> "The inn was locked. Arthur hammered on the door until his fists hurt, but everyone in London had gone to the tournament, even the innkeeper's wife.
>
> He thought of Kay riding out in front of every lord in Britain with an empty scabbard. He thought of them laughing."

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Arthur, turned from the door, his face changing, looking
up the street at the church tower.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(over the plain snowy wall beside him)*
> "Then he remembered that there was a sword in the churchyard that nobody was using.
>
> He did not think about the gold letters at all. He only thought: Kay needs a sword."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur is going to take the sword from the stone, and he doesn't even know what that means.

### C2 — The idea arrives
The page reads as a locked door and an empty street, then Arthur turning toward the church tower as the idea comes. Blocking only if the reader cannot tell he is turning toward the churchyard and its sword.

### C3 — Declared mode
This is an illustrated-prose page: the prose sits on plain ground (sky, wall), never over Arthur's face or hands. Prose over the face, or the page rendered as balloons, is blocking.

### C4 — The empty city
Nobody else is in the street. A prominent bystander undercuts the prose and is blocking; a distant smudge is nonblocking.

### C5 — Arthur's identity and integrity
Arthur is the fair-haired boy in patched brown wool, no armour, no blue, no sword. His fists and face are whole.
