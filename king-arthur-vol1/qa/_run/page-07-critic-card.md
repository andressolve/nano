## Page intent

## PAGE 7

Chronicle. The reader steps out of the story onto a page from an old chronicle: warm ivory vellum, brown ink, watercolour wash, one red-and-lapis illuminated initial. The dominant image is a hand-drawn map of Britain about the year 500, Roman roads as straight red lines meeting at London, Hadrian's Wall across the north, green western hills, small oared ships crossing from the east. The first field opens with the page's question, who built the old walls and why Britain has no king, and answers it: four hundred years of Rome, then the soldiers left. The second field says Britain broke apart, new peoples came, hardly anyone wrote it down, and storytellers later put Uther, Merlin and a lost boy into that darkness. The dominant turn: the walls are real, the lost boy is legend, and the boy is about to ride through those walls. No story character appears.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 7 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Who built those old walls, and why is Britain full of lords fighting each other instead of having one king?

**Sources:** Roman rule of Britain AD 43–c. 410 (Zosimus on the letter of Honorius, c. 410); the Roman walls of Londinium (c. AD 200); Hadrian's Wall (AD 122); Gildas, *De Excidio Britanniae* (c. 540) on the coming of the Saxons; Bede, *Ecclesiastical History* (731) for Angles, Saxons, and Jutes.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A page of a chronicle on warm ivory vellum with
a ruled margin. The dominant image is a hand-drawn ink-and-watercolour map of
the island of Britain about the year 500: Roman roads as straight red lines
meeting at London; Hadrian's Wall drawn across the north; the western hills
shaded green; small oared ships crossing from the east.
A red-and-lapis illuminated initial with a touch of gold begins the first
field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(left of the map, on plain vellum)*
> "Who built those old walls, and why does Britain have no king? Here is something the story doesn't tell you. For almost four hundred years, Britain was part of the Roman Empire. The Romans built its roads, its towns, and London's walls. Then, around the year 410, Rome was in trouble at home. The soldiers left Britain, and they never came back."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(below the map, on plain vellum)*
> "Without Rome, Britain broke into small kingdoms, each fighting the others. Across the sea came new people, the Angles, Saxons, and Jutes, first to raid and then to stay. Hardly anyone wrote down what happened next. Into that real darkness, storytellers later put a king called Uther, a wizard called Merlin, and a lost boy. That part is legend."

> **LABEL** *(on the map, along the red lines)*
> "Roman roads"

> **LABEL** *(on the map, across the north)*
> "Hadrian's Wall"

> **LABEL** *(on the map, at the meeting of the roads)*
> "London"

> **LABEL** *(on the map, beside the ships)*
> "Saxon ships"

**Why you turn the page:** the reader knows now that the crumbling walls are real and the lost boy is legend, and the boy is about to ride through those walls.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 7 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The two chronicle fields and four labels must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Each label must sit by its map feature: roads, the northern wall, London, the ships. The illuminated W is the first letter of "Who", not an extra letter. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — Every claim reads as written
The page's facts (Rome for almost four hundred years, the soldiers leaving around 410, Angles, Saxons, and Jutes, Uther, Merlin and the lost boy as legend) must read exactly as written. A changed date, number, or name is blocking.

### C3 — Not visibly a chronicle
The whole page must read at a glance as a page from an old chronicle: ivory vellum, ruled margin, brown ink and watercolour, an illuminated initial in red and blue with gold. A painted oil scene, a story character, a balloon, or a caption box is blocking.

### C4 — The map does not carry the idea
The map of Britain must own the page, with red roads meeting at London, a wall across the north, and ships coming from the east. An unrecognizable island or a missing wall is blocking; minor coastline inaccuracy is nonblocking.
