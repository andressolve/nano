## Page intent

## PAGE 3

Illustrated prose. The prose tells thirteen years in two fields: nobody found Uther's son, the lords fought over the crown, the fields went to weeds, Britain had a hundred swords and no king. The large image is a winter landscape from a hill, two armies clashing at a ford under different banners, a village burning, crows over empty fields, every figure small and faceless. War stays distant; the boy stays small and untouched. The reader turns to meet him.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 3 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** Britain, over thirteen years.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A wide winter landscape seen from a hill:
two lords' armies clashing at a river ford under different banners, a
village burning behind them, crows over empty fields gone to weeds. Small,
far, nobody's face.

**Panel 2 — 35%.** Far below and far away, in the lower right, a small
timber-and-stone farm in a muddy yard, smoke curling from its chimney, a
boy carrying two buckets across the yard. Peaceful. Untouched.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over pale winter sky)*
> "Nobody ever found King Uther's son. Merlin would not say where he was, and after a while the lords stopped asking. Each of them wanted the crown for himself.
>
> For thirteen years they fought over it, lord against lord and castle against castle, while the fields went to weeds and the roads filled with robbers. Britain had a hundred swords and no king."

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(lower left, over the plain winter field beside the farm)*
> "And far from all of it, on a muddy little farm that belonged to an old knight called Sir Ector, a boy grew up who had never once wondered about King Uther's lost son."

**Why you turn the page:** the lost heir is out there somewhere, and the page has just shown us a boy.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 3 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
The three prose paragraphs must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no other text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The contrast does not land
The page must read as illustrated prose: a large distant war landscape, then far below a small peaceful farm with a boy carrying two buckets. If the farm or the boy cannot be found, or the war dominates both images, the turn fails.

### C3 — Prose over busy art
Each prose field must sit on calm ground. A field laid over the battle, the boy, or the farmhouse so that words or the boy are lost is blocking.

### C4 — War drawn too close
The war stays distant: no blood, no wounds, no bodies, no readable faces. A visible body or wound is blocking; small anonymous figures are nonblocking.
