## Page intent

## PAGE 8

Dramatic page. The great stone stands in a snowy London churchyard with a sword driven through an anvil and gold letters promising the crown to whoever pulls it out. King Lot, the strongest lord in the north, strains at the hilt and fails, then laughs it off as one of Merlin's tricks. Kay explains to Arthur who Lot is, both of them small at the edge of the crowd. The page belongs to the stone and its promise, but its turn is the last beat: Merlin, in the church doorway, answers Lot that the sword is waiting for someone else, and he is looking past Lot straight at the farm boy. The reader turns because the wizard has picked out Arthur. The crowd, the church, and the snow stay subordinate; nobody but Merlin should seem to notice Arthur.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 8 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the churchyard in London, snow.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 55%.** A snowy churchyard in front of a squat stone
church. In the middle, a great grey block of stone with an iron anvil on top,
and driven down through the anvil into the stone, a long plain sword with a
dark iron cross-guard and a round pommel. Letters of gold are cut into the
front of the stone. A ring of lords and townspeople watches. At the stone,
straining at the hilt with both hands, his face red: KING LOT OF ORKNEY, big
and heavy-shouldered, a long copper-red beard in two braids with iron rings,
a black bearskin cloak, a gold torc at his neck. At the far left edge of the
crowd, small: Ector, Kay, and Arthur, watching.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the church wall)*
> "On Christmas morning, a great stone had appeared in the churchyard with a sword through it. Every lord who came for the tournament had tried to pull it out."

> **PRINTED STONE** *(the gold letters on the stone, large and plain)*
> "WHOEVER PULLS THIS SWORD OUT OF THIS STONE IS THE TRUE-BORN KING OF ALL BRITAIN"

**Panel 2 — 15%.** Kay, leaning to Arthur, both on the left.

> **KAY:** "That's King Lot of Orkney. The strongest lord in the north. If he can't move it, nobody can."

**Panel 3 — 15%.** Lot lets go of the hilt and laughs, arms wide to the
crowd.

> **LOT:** "Ha! It's one of Merlin's tricks. That old wizard loves a trick with a sword. There's no man alive who could pull that out."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** In the church doorway, MERLIN, leaning on his staff. He
is not looking at Lot. He is looking past him, at the edge of the crowd,
where Arthur is.

> **MERLIN:** "There is one, King Lot. The sword is waiting for him, not for you."

**Why you turn the page:** the wizard is looking straight at the farm boy.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 8 — card

### C1 — Transcription, order, and ownership
Every one of the 5 listed strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, the caption tail-free, Kay's, Lot's, and Merlin's lines each hanging from that speaker's own mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text anywhere; a string that does not read, is missing, doubled, reordered, or misowned is blocking. The gold words on the stone are scripted object text and must read as written; the same stone legible in a second panel is continuity, not a doubled string. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word, and the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; the words and their order are what must match. A line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee, is owned even if its tail wanders. A string that reads is not to be measured.

### C2 — Merlin's look does not land
The page must end on Merlin in the doorway answering Lot while looking past him toward the boy at the crowd's edge. If Merlin looks at Lot, at the reader, or at nothing, the turn is lost; blocking. The churchyard image with Lot at the hilt must still own the page by eye.

### C3 — Lot and Merlin identity
Lot reads as the big copper-braided lord in bearskin and gold torc; Merlin as the tall thin old man with the long forked white beard and staff. A crowd figure wearing either identity is blocking only if it confuses who speaks.

### C4 — Kay and Arthur in the small panel
Kay, black-haired in black and yellow, speaks; Arthur, the fair-haired boy in brown wool, listens. If the reader cannot tell which of the two speaks, blocking. Arthur wears no blue.

### C5 — The sword is in the stone
The plain iron-grey sword stays driven through the anvil on this page; a sword drawn out or in Lot's hands is blocking. Exact hilt detail is nonblocking.

### C6 — Focal generation integrity
Lot's straining hands and the speakers' faces are whole and readable. Crowd anatomy and repeated background faces are nonblocking.
