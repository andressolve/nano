## Page intent

## PAGE 27

Chronicle. The book steps out of the story onto a page of warm ivory vellum to answer a question the reader has been holding: was there a real King Arthur, and did he really fight wars like this? The dominant image is two old manuscripts painted side by side as museum studies, a long empty gap between them crossed by a thin ruled line, and below them a small ink drawing of a lonely green hill with a question mark over it. One field tells what the monk Gildas wrote around 540, a great battle at Mount Badon and no Arthur named; the other tells what a book written nearly three hundred years later says, twelve battles won by Arthur. Short labels name each manuscript and the hill nobody can find. The dominant turn: real or not, in the story Arthur has to get through the night before his first battle.

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Was there a real King Arthur? Did he really fight wars like this one?

**Sources:** Gildas, *De Excidio et Conquestu Britanniae* (c. 540) — the siege of Mount Badon, Ambrosius Aurelianus, no Arthur; *Historia Brittonum* (c. 829–830), ch. 56 — Arthur's twelve battles, the last at Badon; Badon's location unknown.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant

yellowed and stained, with dense brown Latin script too small to read and a

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(top of the page)*
> "Was there a real King Arthur? Nobody knows. Here is what we have. Around the year 540, a British monk named Gildas wrote an angry book about his own times. He tells of a great battle at a place called Mount Badon, where the Britons beat the Saxons. He names a British war-leader, Ambrosius, who led the fight back against the Saxons. He never names Arthur."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(bottom of the page)*
> "Nearly three hundred years later, around 830, a book called the History of the Britons lists twelve battles won by a war-leader named Arthur, the last one at Badon. Was he real, and Gildas just didn't mention him? Or a hero people made up to remember better days? Historians are still arguing."

> **LABEL** *(under the left manuscript)*
> "Gildas, about 540"

> **LABEL** *(under the right manuscript)*
> "History of the Britons, about 830"

> **LABEL** *(under the hill)*
> "Badon: nobody knows where"

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 27 — card

### C1 — Transcription fails
Both chronicle fields and all three labels must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, with no extra text; each label sits by its manuscript or the hill. The illuminated initial counts as the first letter of "Was", not an extra letter. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. The tiny script inside the painted manuscripts is texture, not a string, and is nonblocking unless it forms legible words. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, a regional spelling variant; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — Not visibly the Chronicle
The page must be visibly the vellum Chronicle register, ink and watercolour wash on ivory vellum with a ruled margin and an illuminated initial, not an oil story page. Any story character, balloon, or caption box is blocking.

### C3 — Claims do not read as written
Every claim must read as written: about 540, Gildas, Mount Badon, Ambrosius, no Arthur; about 830, the History of the Britons, twelve battles, Badon unknown. A changed date or name is blocking.

### C4 — The image does not carry the idea
Two old manuscripts side by side with an empty gap between them, and the lonely hill with its question mark, must own the page and be matched to their labels. A swapped label (Gildas under the later book) is blocking.
