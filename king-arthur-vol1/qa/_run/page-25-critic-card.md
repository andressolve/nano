## Page intent

## PAGE 25

Dramatic. After the feast, the hall is empty, the torches low, cups knocked over. Arthur, crown pulled off and gripped in his fist, faces Merlin across the high table and accuses him: you swore I'd hear it first. Merlin explains why he kept quiet in the churchyard and why he spoke at the feast. Arthur's anger deepens: you took me from my father and let Ector raise me on a lie. Merlin, not sorry, leaning on his staff and meeting his eyes, says the lords would have drowned him in a well, and he would do it again. That exchange is the heart of the page. Then the anger goes out of Arthur and he asks, quietly, whether his father ever saw him. Once, the night he was born; he named him. The dominant turn: Arthur has his father's name now, and a war to fight for it.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 25 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the empty hall, after the feast.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** The hall empty, the torches low, cups knocked over.
Arthur stands facing Merlin across the high table, the crown pulled off and
gripped in his fist. Arthur on the left, Merlin on the right.

> **ARTHUR:** "You swore I'd hear it first! Then you shouted it at Lot, in front of everyone!"
>
> **MERLIN:** "In the churchyard I kept quiet, so the people would choose you for yourself."
>
> **MERLIN:** "At the feast, Lot was telling the whole hall you were nobody's son. I wasn't going to let him finish."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Arthur, still angry; Merlin, not sorry,
leaning on his staff, meeting his eyes.

> **ARTHUR:** "You took me away from my father. You let Ector raise me on a lie."

> **MERLIN:** "Yes. If those lords had found out where you were, you'd have been drowned in a well before you could walk."
>
> **MERLIN:** "I'd do it again."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Arthur, quieter, the anger going out of him.

> **ARTHUR:** "Did he ever see me?"
>
> **MERLIN:** "Once. The night you were born. He named you."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has his father's name now, and a war to fight for it.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 25 — card

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
All eight lines (three Arthur, five Merlin) must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, each owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. A line owned by placement (directly under its speaker's previous balloon, or naming its addressee) is owned even if its tail wanders. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant of the same word; the words and their order are what must match.

### C2 — The confrontation does not own the page
The heart of the page is Arthur's accusation about the lie and Merlin's unrepentant "I'd do it again," the two meeting each other's eyes. If the argument reads as calm conversation, or Merlin reads as sorry, the page loses its heat.

### C3 — Order across the exchanges
In each beat Arthur's line must read before Merlin's reply, and Merlin's two lines in a beat must read as his, in order. Any reply readable before its prompt is blocking.

### C4 — Identity and objects
Arthur is the blond boy in deep blue with the crown off, gripped in his fist; Merlin is tall, thin, stooped, with the forked white beard and staff. No one else is in the hall.

### C5 — The softening fails
The last beat must show Arthur's anger gone out of him as he asks whether his father ever saw him.
