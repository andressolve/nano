## Page intent

## PAGE 51

Dramatic. At the Pentecost feast at Camelot the knights are on their feet along a long table, shoving and shouting over which seats sit nearest the king, and Kay, now the harried steward, rushes to Arthur to say it is the seats again. Down the table a knight has drawn a knife on another. Then the page belongs to Arthur: on his feet at the head of the table, he brings Excalibur's pommel down on the boards so hard the cups jump, and every knight freezes while he forbids anyone to bleed in his hall over a chair. The dominant turn is the king stopping the fight, but not the reason for it. The reader turns to see how he will fix the reason. The quarrelling crowd and the knife stay small and subordinate; the blow and Arthur's face own the page.

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
All five strings (the caption, Kay's two lines, the sound cue, Arthur's line) must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant, the run length of a repeated letter in a sound cue; a line placed directly under its speaker's previous balloon is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The blow does not own the page
The page must read as a dramatic page in which the king stopping the fight is the dominant image: Arthur on his feet striking the table, the knights frozen. If the brawl or the knife reads as the main event, or the blow cannot be found, the turn fails. Judge by what the eye finds first.

### C3 — Attribution
Kay's two lines hang from Kay, not from Arthur or a brawling knight; Arthur's line hangs from Arthur in the blow panel.

### C4 — Arthur and Kay collide
Arthur a slight, blond, soft-faced boy, the only figure in deep blue; Kay a sharp-faced, black-haired young man in black and yellow. If they cannot be told apart, it is blocking.

### C5 — The sword or the table is wrong
The table is long, not round. Arthur strikes with Excalibur in its dark-red scabbard, not a plain iron sword. Blood or a wound is blocking; the small knife is scripted.

### C6 — Focal generation failure
Arthur's striking hands must be exactly two on one sword; a third hand or fused grip is blocking. Background hands and repeated knight faces are nonblocking.

### C7 — Kay's steward state
Kay appears in his steward's black: a plain black tunic with a yellow border, no mail and no black-and-yellow surcoat. Kay in his old mail and quartered surcoat is blocking: it sends him back to the tournament and battle pages just when his place at Camelot should read. The exact width of the yellow border or whether the keys show is nonblocking.
