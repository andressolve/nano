## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 36 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** In the songs, Arthur wore shining armour. What did warriors in his time really look like?

**Sources:** late-Roman and post-Roman military equipment in Britain (mail, round shields, spears; Roman ridge helmets such as the 4th-century finds from Burgh Castle and Richborough); mail hauberks, surcoats, and great helms of c. 1200–1250 (e.g. the Maciejowski Bible, c. 1250); full plate armour of the 15th century, Malory's own time.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant
image is two full-length warriors painted side by side as costume studies in
ink and watercolour, on plain vellum. Left: a warrior of about the year 500
in a long mail shirt, a dark cloak, a simple Roman-style iron helmet with a
ridge over the top, a round painted wooden shield, and a spear. Right: a
knight of about 1200, as in this story, in mail from head to foot, a long
red surcoat, and a flat-topped great helm, holding a long sword and a kite
shield. Behind the right-hand knight, faint, a ghost outline in pale ink of a
knight in full shining plate armour. An illuminated initial begins the first
field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(top of the page)*
> "What did warriors in Arthur's time really look like? The songs made Arthur bigger, and so did the pictures. If a real Arthur lived around the year 500, he and his warriors probably looked like the man on the left: a long mail shirt if they were rich, a round wooden shield, a spear, and perhaps a helmet like the Romans wore. Knights in shining armour were still nearly a thousand years away."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(bottom of the page)*
> "So why is Arthur almost never drawn like the man on the left? Because every storyteller dressed him in the armour of their own time. The storytellers and painters of about 1200 pictured him in mail and a great helm, so that is how this book draws him. Sir Thomas Malory, who wrote the most famous book of Arthur stories, lived in the 1400s among knights in steel plate, and that is how his readers pictured Arthur."

> **LABEL** *(under the left warrior)*
> "About 500"

> **LABEL** *(under the right knight)*
> "About 1200"

> **LABEL** *(beside the ghost outline)*
> "The 1400s"

**Why you turn the page:** back in the story, the boy king in mail is about to go looking for trouble.
