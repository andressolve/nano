## PAGE 41 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the fountain.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** Arthur on his back in the wet grass by the
pool, the broken hilt in his fist. Pellinore stands over him, the point of
his huge sword at Arthur's throat. Pellinore left, Arthur right.

> **PELLINORE:** "Yield, boy. Say the word, and you walk home."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "No. I'm the king. Kings don't yield."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Pellinore, sadly, lifting the sword in both hands.

> **PELLINORE:** "Then I'm sorry for it."

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Out of the dark behind Pellinore, Merlin's long hand,
the silver ring with its green stone, two fingers pointing, the tip of his
crooked staff and the end of his forked white beard at the panel's edge.

> **MERLIN:** "Sleep, Pellinore."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Pellinore toppling sideways like a felled oak, his eyes
closed, the sword falling from his hands.

**Why you turn the page:** Merlin has stopped the killing blow, and Arthur doesn't yet know how.

## PAGE 42 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the fountain.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Arthur scrambling up, furious, pointing at the fallen
giant; Merlin walks into the firelight. Arthur left, Merlin right.

> **ARTHUR:** "Merlin! You've killed him! The bravest knight I ever fought, and you killed him with a spell!"
>
> **MERLIN:** "He's asleep, you young fool. Listen. He's snoring."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** Arthur sinks to his knees in the wet grass,
holding the two pieces of the sword from the stone in his hands. Merlin
stands over him.

> **MERLIN:** "Who told you not to come here?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Kay."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Who offered to let you walk home?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Pellinore."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on Arthur, looking down at the broken halves.

> **MERLIN:** "And who was too proud to take it?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Me."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has lost the sword from the stone, and he knows whose fault it is.

## PAGE 43 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the forest edge, dawn.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** Dawn mist at the edge of the forest. Arthur
walking, leading his horse, the broken sword held in both hands against his
chest; Merlin beside him with his staff, a few steps ahead. Behind them, in
the trees, the glow of Pellinore's dying fire.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the pale sky)*
> "They walked out of the forest as the sun came up, Arthur leading his horse and holding the broken sword. He would not put it down.
>
> Merlin had followed him from the castle gate, which was the only reason he was alive. He had nearly died for a song. Kay had told him no, and he had crept out like a boy stealing apples, and the sword that had chosen him out of all Britain lay in two pieces because of it."

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Merlin, half turned, speaking over his shoulder; ahead
of them, through the trees, a white mist over open water.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(over the plain white mist)*
> "'There is another sword,' Merlin said at last. 'But the one who keeps it does not give it to just anyone.'"

**Why you turn the page:** there's another sword, and it's somewhere in that mist.

# MOVEMENT VI — THE LADY OF THE LAKE · pp 44–50

## PAGE 44 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the lake in the mist.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** A wide lake, still as glass, in white mist;
reeds in the foreground; far hills faded to silver. Out of the very middle
of the lake rises a woman's arm in a sleeve of shimmering white silk,
holding straight up a long bright sword in a dark-red scabbard. The only
warm colour on the page is Arthur's blue, tiny, on the shore in the lower
corner beside Merlin.

> **CAPTION** *(top, over the mist)*
> "In the middle of the lake, an arm in white silk rose out of the water, holding up a sword."

**Why you turn the page:** the other sword is right there.

## PAGE 45 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the lakeshore.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** On the shore, Merlin leans close to Arthur. Merlin left,
Arthur right. A small wooden boat is drawn up in the reeds beside them.

> **MERLIN:** "That arm belongs to the lake. And here comes the Lady of the Lake herself. Be polite. Be very polite."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** Walking toward them across the surface of the
lake as if it were a floor, the LADY OF THE LAKE: tall and still, a long
pale face, calm grey eyes, very long straight black hair wet to her knees,
a long plain gown of white samite, a thin silver circlet. Ripples spread
from her bare feet. Far behind her, out in the middle of the lake, the arm in
white silk still holds up the sword.

> **LADY OF THE LAKE:** "Arthur, son of Uther. That sword is Excalibur. It is yours, if you will give me a gift one day. Whatever I ask."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Arthur, face to face with her at the water's edge;
Merlin, behind him, has gripped his arm, too late.

> **ARTHUR:** "Then I promise. Whatever you ask."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Anything, Arthur? You've promised her anything at all, and you don't even know what she'll want."
>
> **LADY OF THE LAKE:** "Take the boat, and take the sword from the hand."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has made a promise he doesn't understand, and the sword is waiting.

## PAGE 46 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the lake.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 75%.** Arthur in the small wooden boat in the middle
of the misty lake, standing, reaching up with both hands to take the sword
and its dark-red scabbard from the white-sleeved hand. Ripples ring out

**Panel 2 — 25%.** The hand, empty, sinking back into the still water.

> **CAPTION** *(over the water, beside the sinking hand)*

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has Excalibur.

## PAGE 47 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the lakeshore.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 40%.** On the shore, Arthur has drawn Excalibur and
holds it up in delight: longer than the old sword, bright steel, a
gold-wired grip, a single green stone in the cross-guard. The empty red
scabbard hangs from his other hand. Merlin, left, leans on his staff.

> **MERLIN:** "Tell me. Which do you like better, the sword or the scabbard?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "The sword! Look at it, Merlin!"

**Panel 2 — 30%.** Merlin, tapping the scabbard with his staff.

> **MERLIN:** "Then you're a fool. Whoever wears that scabbard loses no blood, however badly he's hurt. It's worth ten swords. Keep it close."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Arthur, slowly, lays the bright sword down on the grass,
and buckles the empty scabbard onto his belt first, looking at it, not at
the sword. Merlin watches with one eyebrow up.

> **MERLIN:** "Hm. You may live to be old after all."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur has chosen the thing that keeps him safe, and on the way home he will have to choose again.

## PAGE 48 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the fountain in the forest, day.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The fountain clearing in daylight. Pellinore,
awake, huge, stands in the road with his sword planted point-down, blocking
the way. Arthur on his horse, left, Excalibur in its red scabbard at his
hip; Merlin behind him.

> **PELLINORE:** "You again. You've got a new sword, I see. Shall we try it?"

**Panel 2 — 25%.** Arthur's hand on Excalibur's hilt; then, deliberately,
letting it go and touching the scabbard instead.

> **ARTHUR:** "No. With this scabbard on, I can't bleed. That isn't a fight. That's cheating."

**Panel 3 — 30%.** Arthur holding out his open hand to Pellinore. Pellinore
staring at it, suspicious.

> **ARTHUR:** "Come to Camelot instead, King Pellinore. I need knights who are stronger than me, and who aren't afraid to tell me no."
>
> **PELLINORE:** "I'll think about it."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur turned down a fight he couldn't lose, and asked his enemy to join him.

## PAGE 49 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the gate of Camelot, dusk.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** Dusk at the castle gate, torches being lit. Kay stands in
the gateway, unshaven, his steward's black rumpled, a torch in his hand; he
has clearly not slept since last night. Arthur rides in, Merlin behind him, the red
scabbard at Arthur's hip. Kay on the left, Arthur on the right.

> **KAY:** "The guard woke in time to see your horse's tail. Merlin went after you and told me to wait. I hate waiting."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Arthur, down from his horse, holds out the two
halves of the sword from the stone to Kay, hilt first, the way he once
handed Kay a sword at the tournament.

> **ARTHUR:** "You told me no, and I went anyway. You were right."
>
> **KAY:** "Of course I was right. I'm always right. It's exhausting."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Kay takes the broken halves and tucks them under his
arm to carry them for him, then frowns at the red scabbard.

> **KAY:** "And where did you get that one?"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "A lake. Will you tell me no again, next time?"
>
> **KAY:** "Every day."

**Why you turn the page:** the brothers are square again, and Arthur has a new sword nobody at court has seen.

## PAGE 50 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Why does Arthur get a second magic sword, and where does the name Excalibur come from?

**Sources:** *Culhwch and Olwen* (Welsh, c. 1100) — Caledfwlch; Geoffrey of Monmouth, *History of the Kings of Britain* (c. 1136) — Caliburnus; Old French romances (late 12th–13th c.) — Escalibor; the Vulgate *Merlin* identifies the stone sword with Excalibur; Malory, *Le Morte d'Arthur* I.9 calls the stone sword Excalibur, breaks Arthur's sword against Pellinore (I.23), and has the Lady give Excalibur (I.25).

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant
image is a family tree of names drawn in brown ink: at its root, a long
sword painted in watercolour lying across the page; branches growing up from
it, each ending in a small round frame holding one name; one branch crosses
over and tangles with a little drawing of the stone and anvil. An illuminated
initial begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(top of the page)*
> "Two magic swords? In this story, yes: the sword from the stone, and Excalibur. Arthur's famous sword is older than his stone. In a Welsh tale from around 1100, it is called Caledfwlch. Geoffrey of Monmouth, writing in 1136, called it Caliburnus. French poets made it Escalibor, and English storytellers made that Excalibur."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(bottom of the page)*
> "When the sword in the stone came along, some old books made it the same sword as Excalibur, and some made them two; storytellers have told it both ways for eight hundred years. Sir Thomas Malory does both! This book chose two swords, so that Arthur could lose the first one."

> **LABEL** *(in the lowest frame)*
> "Caledfwlch, about 1100"

> **LABEL** *(in the middle frame)*
> "Caliburnus, 1136"

> **LABEL** *(in the top frame)*
> "Excalibur"

**Why you turn the page:** whatever its name, the king now has his sword, and a hall full of knights to keep in order.
