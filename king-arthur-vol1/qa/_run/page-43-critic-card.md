## Page intent

## PAGE 43

Illustrated prose. Dawn mist at the forest's edge. Arthur walks out leading his horse, the two halves of the broken sword held against his chest; Merlin walks a few steps ahead with his staff; behind them, Pellinore's fire is dying in the trees. The prose carries Arthur's shame plainly: Merlin's following him was the only reason he is alive, he nearly died for a song, and the sword that chose him lies broken because he crept out. Then Merlin, half turned, says there is another sword, and ahead of them white mist lies over open water. The one dominant turn: there's another sword, and it's somewhere in that mist. The walk owns the page; the mist is the hook.

### C1 — Transcription

Every listed string reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, in order, with no extra text; a string that does not read is blocking, a string that reads is not to be measured. Curly against straight quotes, single against double quotation marks around Merlin's words, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant, and line breaks inside a paragraph are nonblocking; the words and their order must match. Merlin's words live in the prose, not in a balloon; a balloon repeating them is a doubled string.

### C2 — The walk and the hook

The page reads as the boy and the old man walking out of the forest at dawn, the boy carrying the broken sword, and then white mist over water ahead. Blocking if the mist and water are missing, or if a sword, arm, or figure already shows in the mist.

### C3 — Object state

Arthur holds two pieces of a plain iron sword against his chest. Blocking if it reads whole or bright with a green stone.

### C4 — Identity

Arthur is the fair-haired boy in deep blue, head down; Merlin is tall, thin, forked white beard, staff. Blocking if they are confused or Pellinore appears.

### C5 — Prose over plain ground

Both fields sit over sky or mist, never over faces or the sword. Blocking only if they hide a face or the broken sword.
