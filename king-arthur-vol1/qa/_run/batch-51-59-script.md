## PAGE 51 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot, Pentecost feast.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** The hall in spring, decked with green branches. Along a
long rectangular table, knights are on their feet shoving and shouting over
the benches nearest the king's chair at its head; cups spill. Kay, in
steward's black and yellow, hurries up to Arthur's chair, exasperated. Kay
left, Arthur right.

> **CAPTION** *(top left)*
> "At the spring feast of Pentecost, at Camelot."
>
> **KAY:** "It's the seats again! Every one of them says the places nearest you belong to him."
>
> **KAY:** "And every one of them says the bottom of the table is an insult."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Down the table, one knight has drawn a knife on
another.

**Panel 3 — dominant, 45%.** Arthur on his feet at the head of the table,
bringing Excalibur's pommel down on the boards so hard the cups jump. Every
knight freezes.

> **SOUND** *(at the blow)*
> "BANG!"
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Enough! Put that knife away. Nobody bleeds in my hall over a chair!"

**Why you turn the page:** the king has stopped the fight, but not the reason for it.

## PAGE 52 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot, later that night.

**Panel 1 — 35%.** The empty hall. Arthur, Merlin, and Kay by the hearth.
Arthur, left, stares at the long table; Merlin, right, warms his hands; Kay
leans on the mantel behind Arthur, arms folded.

> **ARTHUR:** "They're fighting over who sits closest to me. Over who's higher than who."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Knights always have, and they always will, at a table with a head and a foot."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 40%.** Arthur looking at a round shield hanging on
the wall above the hearth, an idea dawning on his face.

> **ARTHUR:** "Then we'll have a table with no head. No top and no bottom."
>
> **KAY:** "That's not a table. That's a cartwheel."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Arthur, grinning now, turning to Merlin.

> **ARTHUR:** "It's round. Everyone is the same distance from the middle. Even me."
>
> **MERLIN:** "Now that has never been tried."

**Why you turn the page:** Arthur is going to build the table.

## PAGE 53 · *illustrated prose*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot, through the year.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 65%.** The great hall turned into a workshop: sawdust,
trestles, oak planks, a dozen carpenters (unnamed, in leather aprons) fitting
together the curved sections of an enormous round tabletop. Arthur, in his
blue surcoat with the sleeves rolled up, holds one end of a long curved
plank; Merlin sits on a bench by the hearth watching, his chin on his staff.

> **PROSE FIELD 1** *(top of the page, over the plain dark of the high roof)*
> "For most of a year the carpenters of Camelot worked on the table. It took nine oak trees and three hundred days, and it was so big that they had to build it inside the hall, because it would never have fitted through the doors.
>
> Arthur helped with the sawing whenever Kay wasn't looking. Merlin watched and said nothing, which, for Merlin, was a kind of praise."

**Panel 2 — 35%.** The hall doors open on a late-spring evening, green
branches and blossom outside. In them stands Pellinore, huge, his old green
surcoat dusty and stained from the road, his helmet under his arm, looking in
at the finished table, bare and waiting.

> **PROSE FIELD 2** *(over the plain dark wall beside the doors)*
> "On the eve of the next Pentecost, someone knocked at the hall door. It was King Pellinore, who had thought about it after all."

**Why you turn the page:** the table is finished, and the knights are about to sit down at it.

## PAGE 54 · *spectacle*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot, the new table.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 100%.** Seen from high in the rafters: an enormous
round table of pale oak filling the hall, ringed with tall chairs. Knights
are taking their seats all around it. On the table's rim in front of each
seated knight, his name glows in letters of gold, too small and far away to
read. Ector sits among the knights, beaming. Arthur sits at one place on the ring, no higher than any other, in
blue. Across the table and a little to his left, the biggest man at the table, sitting down
for the first time with a hand on his heart: Pellinore. One chair, empty,
has no glow in front of it.

> **CAPTION** *(top left, over the rafters)*
> "As each knight sat down, his name appeared on the table in letters of gold. The first to sit was King Pellinore."

**Why you turn the page:** the table is full of names, except for one place.

## PAGE 55 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 45%.** The hall doors open; King Lot stands in them
with two lords behind him under a white flag of truce, staring at the round
table. Arthur has risen from his own chair and holds it out to Lot, his hand on
its back. Arthur left, Lot right.

> **LOT:** "You sent for me under a white flag, boy. I came to see this wonder of yours with my own eyes."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "I'd rather have you at this table than across a valley, King Lot."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Take my chair, if you like. It's no higher than anyone's."

**Panel 2 — 20%.** Lot, sneering, in the foreground on the left, looking round
the table.

> **LOT:** "No higher? Level with a farm boy's steward? Level with an old boar who sleeps in a tent?"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Across the table on the right, seated side by side: Kay
leaning forward with his chin up, on the left of the pair; Pellinore huge and
still, on the right.

> **KAY:** "This steward chased you off a battlefield."
>
> **PELLINORE:** "Level with me. I sat down. I'm not ashamed of it."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Lot turning on his heel in the doorway, cloak swinging.

> **LOT:** "I am the King of Orkney. I sit level with nobody. Keep your tabletop, boy. When you need a real king, send to Orkney."

**Why you turn the page:** Lot has walked out, and the knights who stayed are about to swear.

## PAGE 56 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot, the oath.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 60%.** All the knights standing around the round
table, swords drawn and held upright by the blades so the hilts make
crosses. Arthur is the only one kneeling, at his place, Excalibur held up
before him the same way, looking across the table at Ector. Behind him,
the great doors Lot walked out of stand open on the dark.

> **ARTHUR:** "I'll swear first, because I need it most."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "I swear never to be cruel, and to give mercy to anyone who asks for it."

**Panel 2 — 40%.** Close round the table: Ector, Kay, Pellinore, and other
knights' faces in the torchlight, listening; Ector's eyes are wet.

> **ARTHUR:** "To protect anyone who can't protect himself. And never to fight for a wrong cause, not for money, and not for pride."
>
> **SOUND** *(all the knights together, large)*
> "WE SWEAR!"

**Why you turn the page:** the fellowship is sworn, and someone is looking at the empty chair.

## PAGE 57 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot.

**Panel 1 — 30%.** Kay, at his place, pointing across the table. Kay left,
Arthur right.

> **KAY:** "Arthur, that seat has no name on it. An empty chair looks like somebody refused you. I'll sit in it."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 45%.** The empty chair, tall and plain, with no gold
before it, in a pool of light from a high window. Merlin stands behind it
with his hand on its back, on the left. Arthur, at the right edge of the
panel in the foreground, looks from the chair to Merlin.

> **MERLIN:** "There's no name on it, Kay, and there won't be. Not yet. It is kept for someone who hasn't come."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Who?"

**Panel 3 — 20%.** Merlin, gravely, to the whole table.

> **MERLIN:** "You'll know him when he comes. And anyone else who sits in it will be very sorry."

**Panel 4 — 15%.** Kay, who had half-risen, sits back down fast with both
hands raised.

> **KAY:** "I'll keep my own, then."

**Why you turn the page:** the table is waiting for someone, and nobody knows who.

## PAGE 58 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot, late at night.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The far doorway of the dark hall. Ector, a candle in his
hand, is leaving for bed and looks back; Arthur, by the hearth, looks after
him. Arthur on the left, Ector on the right.

> **ARTHUR:** "Goodnight, Father."
>
> **ECTOR:** "Goodnight, lad."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The hall at night, empty but for two figures.
The great round table in moonlight from the high windows; Excalibur laid on
it in its red scabbard; the empty chair across the table lit by a shaft of
moonlight. By the dying hearth, small, Arthur and Merlin stand side by side
looking at the table. Merlin on the left, Arthur on the right.

> **MERLIN:** "You still owe the Lady of the Lake a gift. And I won't always be beside you, Arthur."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Then who'll tell me when I'm being a fool?"
>
> **MERLIN:** "Kay. He's had years of practice."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on the empty chair in the moonlight.

> **CAPTION** *(over the dark beside the chair)*
> "The seat stayed empty. The knight it was waiting for had not even been born."

**Why you turn the page:** Merlin is going somewhere, the Lady will come for her gift, and the empty seat is waiting for someone; all of those are a story still to come.

## PAGE 59 · *chronicle*

**Location:** the chronicle frame.

**Question:** Who made up this whole story, and is the Round Table real?

**Sources:** Gildas (c. 540); *Historia Brittonum* (c. 829–830); Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1136) — Arthur as king, Merlin introduced (he serves Uther; Merlin as Arthur's counsellor comes later, with Robert de Boron and the French prose romances); Wace, *Roman de Brut* (1155) — the Round Table, made so no baron could claim a higher seat; Chrétien de Troyes (c. 1170s–1180s) — Camelot named, Lancelot, the Grail; Malory, *Le Morte d'Arthur* (finished 1469–70, printed by Caxton 1485), self-described "knight prisoner"; the Winchester Round Table — oak, dendrochronology c. 1250–1280, probably for Edward I's tournament of 1290, painted for Henry VIII in 1522.

**Panel 1 — dominant, 70%.** A chronicle page on ivory vellum. The dominant
image: a great snowball drawn in ink and watercolour rolling down a long
hill from the top left to the bottom right, growing as it goes, a small
date flag planted beside the track at each step. At the bottom right, as a
museum study, the Winchester Round Table hanging on a stone wall: a huge
painted wooden disc, green and white segments, a Tudor rose in the middle, a
painted king at the top. An illuminated initial begins the first field.

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 1** *(top right, on plain vellum)*
> "Who made up the story of King Arthur, and is the Round Table real? Nobody made it up alone. It grew like a snowball. In 1136, Geoffrey of Monmouth made Arthur a great king, and brought in a wizard named Merlin. In 1155, a poet named Wace gave him a round table, so no knight could boast of a better seat."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 2** *(middle left, on plain vellum)*
> "Around 1180, a French poet, Chrétien de Troyes, added Camelot, new knights such as Lancelot, and the Grail, a mysterious holy dish that later storytellers turned into a cup the knights would search for. Around 1470, an English knight, Thomas Malory, writing in prison, gathered all of it into one enormous book. And the Round Table you can still see in Winchester? It was made around 1290, nearly eight hundred years after a real Arthur could have lived."

> **CHRONICLE FIELD 3** *(bottom left, large)*
> "The storytellers weren't finished. They had a great many more knights to add."

> **LABEL** *(flag at the top of the hill)*
> "1136"

> **LABEL** *(flag on the slope)*
> "1155"

> **LABEL** *(flag lower down)*
> "1180"

> **LABEL** *(flag near the bottom)*
> "1470"

> **LABEL** *(under the table on the wall)*
> "Made about 1290, painted about 1522"

**Why you turn the page:** the story isn't over; the next storytellers are already riding toward Camelot.
