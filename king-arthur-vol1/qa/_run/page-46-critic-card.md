## Page intent

## PAGE 46

A spectacle page, almost silent. Arthur stands in the small wooden boat in the middle of the misty lake and reaches up with both hands to take the sword and its dark-red scabbard from the white-sleeved hand; far off, the Lady stands on the water, small and white, watching. Then the empty hand lets go and sinks into the still water. Arthur owns the page in the moment of taking. The dominant turn: Arthur has Excalibur. The reader turns to see what he does with it. What stays subordinate: the boat, the ripples, and the Lady, who is a distant witness; nobody speaks, and the broken sword plays no part here.

## Numbered blocking criteria

## Page 46 — card

### C1 — Transcription
The one caption reads exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once, with no other text anywhere on the page; a caption that does not read is blocking, one that reads is not to be measured. Spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a hyphen for a dash, a comma present or missing, and a regional spelling variant are nonblocking; the words and their order must match.

### C2 — The taking owns the page
The eye finds Arthur in the boat reaching up to take the sword from the hand; the page is a near-silent spectacle. If the taking is not the dominant image, or Arthur is not plainly reaching for it, the reader loses the turn: blocking.

### C3 — The hand lets go
A second, smaller image shows the hand empty and sinking. If the hand still holds a sword there, or this beat is missing, the caption contradicts the picture: blocking.

### C4 — Identity and witnesses
Arthur is the blond boy in blue; the Lady, if visible, is a small white figure far off on the water. No speech, no third person in the boat. Arthur unrecognisable, or a balloon on anyone, is blocking.

### C5 — Focal integrity
Arthur takes the sword and its dark-red scabbard with exactly two hands. A third hand, a fused hand on the hilt, or a broken face is blocking; ripple pattern and boat detail are nonblocking.

### C6 — Sword count
The page shows only Excalibur in its scabbard, taken from the hand, and no other sword. A duplicate sword, a whole sword from the stone after it has broken, or a second weapon copied from a reference sheet is blocking, because the two swords and the one broken sword are the volume's causal objects. Angle and how much of a blade shows are nonblocking.
