## Page intent

## PAGE 58

Dramatic. Late at night Ector, candle in hand, leaves the hall for bed and looks back; Arthur, by the hearth, wishes his father goodnight and is answered. Then the page opens into its dominant image: the great round table in moonlight, Excalibur laid on it in its red scabbard, the empty chair lit by a shaft of moonlight, and, small by the dying hearth, Merlin and Arthur side by side. Merlin reminds Arthur that he still owes the Lady of the Lake a gift and that he will not always be beside him; Arthur asks who will tell him when he is being a fool, and Merlin answers, Kay. Last, the empty chair alone, and a caption: the knight it waits for has not been born. The dominant turn is that every thread is a story still to come.

## Exact owner-controlled script

## PAGE 58 · *dramatic*

**Location:** the hall at Camelot, late at night.

**Panel 1 — 25%.** The far doorway of the dark hall. Ector, a candle in his
hand, is leaving for bed and looks back; Arthur, by the hearth, looks after
him. Arthur on the left, Ector on the right.

> **ARTHUR:** "Goodnight, Father."
>
> **ECTOR:** "Goodnight, lad."

**Panel 2 — dominant, 50%.** The hall at night, empty but for two figures.
The great round table in moonlight from the high windows; Excalibur laid on
it in its red scabbard; the empty chair across the table lit by a shaft of
moonlight. By the dying hearth, small, Arthur and Merlin stand side by side
looking at the table. Merlin on the left, Arthur on the right.

> **MERLIN:** "You still owe the Lady of the Lake a gift. And I won't always be beside you, Arthur."
>
> **ARTHUR:** "Then who'll tell me when I'm being a fool?"
>
> **MERLIN:** "Kay. He's had years of practice."

**Panel 3 — 25%.** Close on the empty chair in the moonlight.

> **CAPTION** *(over the dark beside the chair)*
> "The seat stayed empty. The knight it was waiting for had not even been born."

**Why you turn the page:** Merlin is going somewhere, the Lady will come for her gift, and the empty seat is waiting for someone; all of those are a story still to come.

## Materiality threshold

The critic is a release gate, not a defect collector. A visible issue is
blocking only when it materially harms the reader event, exact text or
attribution, named or focal identity, consequential continuity or object state,
focal generation integrity, or the dominant dramatic relationship. Every
correction replaces the complete page. Minor background, cosmetic, tiny-prop,
geometry, colour, texture, and prompt-fidelity observations are nonblocking.
Omit them. Numeric panel shares and lettering sizes are builder construction

Each numbered criterion is also its stable retry signature. Cite it only when

### C1 — Transcription or ownership fails
All six strings must read exactly from the 600 × 900 proof, once each, in causal order, owned by the right mouth by tail and placement, the caption tail-free, with no extra text. A string that does not read is blocking; a string that reads is not to be measured. Nonblocking: spacing around a dash, curly against straight quotes, a comma present or missing inside a line, a regional spelling variant; a line naming its addressee is owned even if its tail wanders.

### C2 — The quiet ending does not land
The page must read as a dramatic page ending in stillness: the goodnight at the doorway, then the moonlit round table with Excalibur on it and the empty chair, two small figures by the hearth, then the chair alone. If the night image is crowded or the chair cannot be found, the turn fails.

### C3 — Attribution across the hearth exchange
Merlin's warning first, Arthur's question second, Merlin's "Kay" answer third; the answer given to Arthur, or the question to Merlin, is blocking. The goodnights: "Father" from Arthur, "lad" from Ector in the doorway.

### C4 — Identity
Arthur a slight blond boy in deep blue; Merlin tall, thin, forked white beard; Ector short, broad, bald, clean-shaven, with a candle. Merlin and Ector confused is blocking. Small-scale faces are nonblocking when build and hair read.

### C5 — Consequential object state
Excalibur lies on the table in its dark-red scabbard; the empty chair is lit and empty. A second sword, or the plain iron sword whole, is blocking.
