# Sequence gate — canonical pages 11–20

## Uninterrupted sequence

Arthur pulls the sword from the churchyard stone with startling ease, then carries it to Kay as a practical replacement for the sword Kay left behind. Kay recognizes it, briefly claims the kingship for himself, and calls Ector over. At the stone, Ector makes Kay swear on a holy book; Kay admits Arthur gave him the sword. Arthur innocently explains why he took it, and Ector asks him to repeat the feat in front of them.

Arthur draws the sword again. Ector and Kay kneel, and Ector tells the bewildered boy that he is not Arthur's father. Ector explains that Merlin brought Arthur to him as a baby fifteen years earlier. Arthur experiences the revelation as the sudden loss of both father and brother, but Ector assures him that their family bond remains. Ector asks the new king to make Kay his steward. Arthur first confronts Kay; Kay admits his moment of dishonesty, Arthur recognizes that he ultimately told the truth, and the brothers reconcile with Arthur's lifelong promise of stewardship.

That night Arthur sits alone in the church, unable to sleep, facing both his unknown parentage and the public burden arriving the next morning. A chronicle interlude distinguishes the late sword-in-the-stone legend from the much older Arthurian tradition. The story then returns to the crowded churchyard, where Arthur demonstrates the sword before Britain's lords. Lot rejects the sign, attacks Arthur's unknown birth and farm upbringing, and turns Arthur's defensive reply into public ridicule.
