**A source map with an empty cut list is not finished.** Cutting comes first:
before combining, reordering, or inventing, ask what can simply go.

## The cut list — named, unapologetic, famous material included

### Whole sequences cut

- **Uther, Igraine, Gorlois and Tintagel** — the story of how Arthur was
  conceived. It is a story about a disguise used to deceive a wife, it does
  nothing for a boy who will never know it, and it is the only way the book
  could open with grown-up machinery instead of a child. Arthur's birth is a
  secret, never a scandal.
- **The May babies** — Arthur's order to set the newborns adrift. Cut whole.
- **Morgause, and the birth of Mordred.** Cut whole; Mordred belongs to the
  last volume if anywhere.
- **Balin and Balan**, the Dolorous Stroke, the Knight with Two Swords. A
  great tragedy and a separate book.
- **The French alliance**: Kings Ban and Bors, the night crossing, the battle
  they win for Arthur. Arthur's first war is won by Arthur and Kay.
- **Arthur's marriage to Guinevere**, Leodegrance, and the Round Table as her
  dowry. The table is Arthur's own idea, which is the point of the book.
- **The later Lady of the Lake**: her beheading by Balin, her request (the
  gift is promised in this volume and not asked for).
- **Nimue and the end of Merlin.** Foreshadowed in one line, never shown.
- **Morgan le Fay, Accolon, the stolen scabbard.** Later volume.
- **The Questing Beast** that Pellinore hunts. A wonderful image that is a
  whole second story; Pellinore here is a champion who guards a fountain.
- **Three of the four trials of the sword** (Candlemas, Easter, Pentecost).
  One public redraw is enough to show the lords will never accept it.

### Characters cut

- The Archbishop of Canterbury · Sir Ulfius · Sir Brastias · Sir Griflet ·
  King Leodegrance · Guinevere · Gawain, Gaheris, Agravaine (Lot's sons) ·
  Igraine · Gorlois · Morgause · Morgan le Fay · Balin · Ban and Bors ·
  Lancelot · Nimue.

### Characters merged

- **Lot + Urien + Nentres + Carados + the King of the Hundred Knights + the
  rest of the eleven → Lot.** One face is every lord who will not kneel to a
  farm boy.
- **The Archbishop + Merlin → Merlin**, for the stone; the crowning is done by
  unnamed churchmen.
- **Griflet + the wounded messenger → Kay**, who brings the news of the
  champion at the fountain.

### Why the biggest cut pays for the volume

Cutting the conception and the French kings means the book has no adult
politics and no second set of heroes. Arthur's war is fought by Arthur, his
brother, and the people who shouted for him; everything that saves him in
Movement IV is someone he loves, which is exactly what he has to learn to
build a table around. It also deletes four characters who would need locks and
the only material in the source that is unsuitable without being worth it.

## Protected claims

- **On Chronicle pages only, the truth.** Every claim traceable to a named
  source on the page's `**Sources:**` line. Legend called legend; history
  called history; "nobody knows" said plainly. Dates as historians give them,
  with "about" where they are uncertain.
- The owner asked for "rich context: history and myth." The six Chronicle
  pages are not optional and are not the first cut if the book runs long.

## Sensitive material and its treatment

- **War deaths:** real, never drawn (see the mandate).
- **Arthur's parentage:** a secret kept to protect a baby. No word about how he
  was conceived.
- **Religion:** the stone stands in a churchyard and the knights swear on
  their swords at a feast; the book neither preaches nor erases the medieval
  church. Chronicle pages say that most of the people who wrote the legend down
  were monks, because they were.
- **Malory in prison:** Malory wrote the book as a "knight prisoner"; Chronicle
  6 may say so, plainly and without detail.
